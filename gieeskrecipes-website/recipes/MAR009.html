🍐 Lamb Tagine with Quince

Tajine d'agneau aux coings — lamb mkalli with quince (لحم بالسفرجل)

Lamb cooked the mqualli way with garlic, turmeric and ginger, served with quince poached until just soft and glazed golden in butter and honey.

MediumDifficulty

Quince is cooked separately here as well. The lamb neck fillets simmer with onions, stock, garlic, spices and saffron under a coriander bouquet for 1h30 to 2 hours. The quince is boiled 40 minutes (start checking at 35) until a knife slips in, then each piece is glazed 2 minutes per side in butter and honey until lightly golden. The meat and its onion sauce are spooned over, with the quince on top and more coriander.

Ingredients

Equipment: Casserole or tagine, Saucepan for the quince, Frying pan for glazing

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 4 large onions (about 800g), chopped
  • 300ml vegetable stock
  • 1 tablespoon finely chopped garlic (about 4 cloves)
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1 teaspoon salt or more to taste
  • Generous pinch saffron
  • 1,2kg lamb neck fillets (or any type of stewing meat) trimmed and cut in 5 cm pieces
  • 1 large coriander bouquet, tied and more for garnish

Quince

  • 3 large quinces
  • 40 gr unsalted butter
  • 4 tablespoons honey

Method

  1. Heat the oil in the casserole, add the onions and cover; leave until soft and translucent, about 15 minutes.
  2. Add the garlic, spices and saffron, then the lamb; cook 5 to 7 minutes, turning the meat occasionally.
  3. Pour in the stock, add the coriander bouquet, and simmer gently for 1h30 to 2 hours or until the meat is soft and separates easily.
  4. Meanwhile, peel, core and quarter the quinces; cover with water in a pan, bring to the boil, reduce to medium-low, cover and simmer for about 40 minutes. Start checking after 35 minutes.
  5. Drain the quince once soft enough for a knife to go in. Melt the butter with the honey and glaze each piece on each side for 2 minutes until lightly golden.
  6. Serve the lamb with its onion sauce, topped with the quince and fresh coriander.

Chef Tips

  • The page warns against overcooking the quince; check from 35 minutes.
  • The author compares the glazed fruit to Spanish membrillo.
  • Cook the lamb until it separates easily; neck fillets are suggested but any stewing meat will do.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the quince to mush before the glazing step.
  • Peeling too thick; quinces are hard and need care.
  • Adding the quince directly to the meat pot; the author cooks it apart.

Health Benefits

  • Quince adds fibre and pectin; the dish is lean if you trim the lamb.
  • Honey and butter are used lightly, only for the glaze.

Cultural Note

The page describes the dish as cooked "the mqualli way" — garlic, turmeric and ginger — and likens glazed quince to the Spanish membrillo, hinting at the Andalusian thread in Moroccan cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 3 days in the fridge, quince stored with the sauce.

Reheating: Reheat covered over low heat.

Serve with: Khobz
