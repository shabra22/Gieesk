🍟 Kenyan Chips Masala

Chips Masala

Kenyan Chips Masala — crispy deep-fried chips tossed immediately after frying in a fragrant spice mix of curry powder, paprika, cumin, chilli, and salt. The most beloved street food upgrade in Kenya — transforming plain chips into something bold, addictive, and completely Kenyan.

Chips masala is the Kenyan genius of taking something good — crispy fried chips — and making it extraordinary with a simple spice blend applied at the moment they come out of the fryer. The heat of the freshly fried chips helps the spices bloom and adhere. Every bite delivers crunch, heat, and fragrance. At every Kenyan food stall, the question is not 'chips or masala chips' — it is just masala chips.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep frying pan or fryer, Large bowl, Paper towels

  • 800g potatoes — peeled and cut into thick chips
  • Oil for deep frying
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp chilli powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice — optional

Method

  1. Peel and cut potatoes into thick chips. Soak in cold water for 30 minutes — this removes excess starch for crispier chips.
  2. Drain and dry the chips thoroughly — water causes dangerous oil splatter.
  3. Mix all spices and salt together in a large bowl. Set aside.
  4. Heat oil to 170°C.
  5. Fry chips in batches for 5—7 minutes until golden and crispy. Do not overcrowd.
  6. Remove immediately from the oil and drain briefly.
  7. While still piping hot, toss the chips in the spice mix bowl. Coat thoroughly.
  8. Add fresh coriander and lemon juice if using. Toss once more.
  9. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The spice mix must go on immediately after frying — the heat of the chips is what makes the spices bloom and adhere.
  • Soaking chips in cold water before frying makes them significantly crispier.
  • Dry the chips completely before frying — moisture makes them soggy and causes dangerous oil splatter.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding spices to cold chips — they do not bloom or stick properly.
  • Wet chips in the fryer — splatter and soggy result.
  • Overcrowding the fryer — lowers temperature and produces oily chips.

The Science

The high surface temperature of freshly fried chips (around 150—160°C) instantly volatilises the aromatic compounds in the spice mix, creating the characteristic fragrant bloom. At room temperature, these compounds would not volatilise and the spices would taste flat and raw.

Cultural Note

Chips masala is arguably the most universally loved Kenyan street food — found at every kiosk, food stall, and roadside restaurant across the country. The simple genius of tossing hot chips in a fragrant spice mix has made it one of the defining flavours of Kenyan urban street food culture.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately — chips lose crispiness rapidly.

Reheating: Not recommended — reheat in oven at 200°C for 5 minutes if necessary.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Pili Pili Sauce, Tomato Sauce, Smokie Pasua, Fried Chicken
