🔥 Roosterkoek

Roosterkoek

Bread dough proved twice and cooked directly on the braai grid over medium coals, turned often, until it sounds hollow when you tap it — bread baked on a fire with no tin and no oven.

Roosterkoek means grid cake and that is exactly what it is: bread dough put straight onto the braai grid. The whole craft is heat management, because the coals have to be medium rather than hot or the outside burns while the middle is raw, and the rolls need turning regularly for the full fifteen to twenty minutes. Split and buttered while hot, they are the best thing on a braai table, and they are why nobody at a South African braai needs a bread tin.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Floured tray, Kitchen towel, Braai with medium coals and a grid

  • 500 g bread flour
  • 10 g instant yeast
  • 12 g salt
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 350 ml water
  • A little flour, for dusting

To serve

  • Butter

Method

  1. Whisk the flour, yeast, salt, olive oil and sugar together in a large bowl.
  2. Pour in the water and bring the dough together with a wooden spoon.
  3. Tip it onto a work surface and knead for 10 minutes, until smooth and supple.
  4. Lightly oil the bowl, return the dough, cover, and prove until doubled in size.
  5. Turn out onto a lightly floured surface and divide into 8 or 10 equal pieces — use a scale if you want them even.
  6. Shape each into a ball: cup your hand over it, keep your little finger on the surface, and rotate quickly clockwise until it comes together.
  7. Space the balls out on a lightly floured tray, cover with a kitchen towel and prove 30 to 45 minutes. They double again, so leave room.
  8. Braai over medium coals for 15 to 20 minutes, turning regularly so they brown evenly.
  9. They are done when tapping one sounds hollow. Split and butter them while hot.

Chef Tips

  • Medium coals, not hot. This is the only thing that matters and it is the thing people get wrong — high heat gives a black crust over raw dough.
  • Turn them regularly rather than once. Fifteen minutes on a grid needs four or five turns.
  • The hollow tap is the test. A roosterkoek that thuds needs longer, whatever it looks like.
  • Space them well on the proving tray. They double again and will fuse if they touch.
  • They cook perfectly well in a dry pan on the stove if there is no fire, though you lose the smoke.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking them over hot coals, which burns them before they are cooked through.
  • Leaving them undisturbed on the grid, which chars one side.
  • Cutting the second prove short, giving dense rolls.
  • Judging doneness by colour rather than by the sound.

Cultural Note

Roosterkoek belongs to the braai rather than to any one community, and it is one of the reasons the braai is a meal rather than just grilled meat. It shares its logic with potbrood, isonka samanzi and ujeqe — bread made where there is fire but no oven, which for most of this country's history was most kitchens.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best straight off the fire. Keep a day wrapped; freeze for a month.

Reheating: Warm briefly on the grid or in a dry pan.

Serve with: Butter, Chakalaka, Boerewors or steak from the same fire
