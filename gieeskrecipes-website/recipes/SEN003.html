🍲 Thieboudienne with Tomato Sauce

Thieboudienne — ceebu jen, poisson au riz

The diaspora version built on tinned tomato sauce rather than a long paste reduction — the fish is fried three minutes a side first.

A more tractable thieboudienne for a kitchen without a fishmonger: about three pounds of firm white fish, whole or bone-in steaks, marinated in a cup each of chopped parsley and basil and chopped green onions with five cloves of garlic. The fish is fried until brown, around three minutes, then two minutes on the other side. The sauce uses a fifteen-ounce tin of tomato sauce with two tablespoons of paste rather than two cups of paste, and eight ounces of smoked fish stands in for guedj. Three cups of rice, and the whole thing in under two hours.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Frying pan, Blender or food processor, Slotted spoon

  • About 3 lb white, firm-fleshed fish, whole or bone-in steaks
  • 1 lemon
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp white pepper
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper
  • 1-2 tsp bouillon powder, or salt

Marinade

  • 1 cup chopped parsley and basil
  • 1 cup chopped green onions
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 1-2 habanero peppers (optional)

Sauce

  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 15 oz tomato sauce
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 8 oz smoked fish
  • 6 cups water or low-sodium broth

Vegetables

  • 2 lb potatoes and cassava
  • 2 lb mixed vegetables — carrots, green and red bell peppers, eggplant
  • 1 small head cabbage
  • 1 lb okra

Rice

  • 3 cups uncooked short-grain rice — the author notes she used basmati
  • 1 cup oil for frying the fish, plus 2 tbsp for the rice

Method

  1. Blend the parsley and basil, green onions, garlic and optional habanero into a marinade.
  2. Season the fish with the lemon, salt, white pepper, black pepper and bouillon powder, and stuff and coat it with the marinade.
  3. Heat the cup of oil and fry the fish until brown, around 3 minutes, then brown it for two minutes on the other side. Set aside.
  4. In the same pot soften the onion, then add the tomato sauce and tomato paste.
  5. Let it cook for about 8 minutes.
  6. Season, and let it simmer for about five minutes.
  7. Add the smoked fish and cook for another 5 minutes.
  8. Add the water or broth and bring to a simmer.
  9. Add the potatoes and cassava and cook about 5 minutes, then the rest of the vegetables for about 3 minutes.
  10. Return the fish, simmer until cooked through, then lift the fish and vegetables out and keep warm.
  11. Add the rice to the broth with the 2 tablespoons of oil, cover and cook until tender and the liquid is absorbed.
  12. Serve the rice with the fish and vegetables on top.

Chef Tips

  • Frying the fish first is the shortcut that makes this work — it firms the flesh so it survives the stew without the careful poaching the traditional method needs.
  • Three minutes then two. The uneven timing is deliberate: the first side gets the colour, the second just sets it.
  • A tin of tomato sauce plus two tablespoons of paste replaces two cups of paste cooked down. It is not the same depth, and the author is not pretending it is.
  • Smoked fish stands in for guedj here. It gives smoke rather than ferment, which is a real difference in the finished broth.
  • The author notes she used basmati despite calling for short-grain. Either works; basmati stays more separate.
  • Cook the vegetables in stages by density — potatoes and cassava first, the softer ones after.
  • If you want the traditional depth rather than the weeknight version, Thiebou Jenn is the long way round and worth the extra hour.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the fry and poaching the fish instead, which this method's timings do not allow for.
  • All the vegetables in at once.
  • Rushing the eight-minute sauce stage.
  • Expecting the fermented depth of a guedj-and-yeet version.
  • Too much broth left when the rice goes in.

Health Benefits

  • High in protein from both fresh and smoked fish.
  • Four-plus pounds of vegetables across eight servings.
  • Tomato-led, so a good source of lycopene.
  • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

Cultural Note

Senegalese cooking abroad runs into the guedj problem immediately — the fermented fish is hard to buy outside West African groceries, and smoked fish is what most diaspora kitchens reach for. This version is the honest result, and it is how a great many Senegalese households outside Senegal actually cook the national dish.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam the rice; warm the fish gently.

Serve with: Lime wedges, Kaani, Bissap
