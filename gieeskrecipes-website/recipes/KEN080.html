🦐 Kenyan Coconut Prawns

Uduvi wa Nazi

Kenyan Coconut Prawns — fresh prawns cooked in a creamy, fragrant coconut milk sauce with garlic, ginger, tomatoes, and Swahili coastal spices. The most luxurious quick dish of the Kenyan coast — ready in 20 minutes and tasting like a celebration.

Uduvi wa Nazi brings together two of the Kenyan coast's greatest ingredients — fresh Indian Ocean prawns and thick coconut milk — in a dish that is simultaneously elegant and effortless. The coconut milk creates a naturally sweet, creamy sauce that envelops the prawns without overwhelming their delicate flavour. Served with coconut rice, it is the coastal celebration meal at its most irresistible.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pan, Wooden spoon

  • 500g large prawns — peeled and deveined, tails on
  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 2 medium onions — finely chopped
  • 2 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • ½ tsp sugar — optional, to balance the coconut

Method

  1. Season prawns with salt, turmeric, and paprika.
  2. Cook onions in oil over medium heat until soft and golden — 6 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 30 seconds.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook for 5—6 minutes until softened.
  5. Add curry powder and remaining spices. Stir 30 seconds.
  6. Pour in coconut milk. Stir gently. Bring to a gentle simmer — do not boil hard.
  7. Add prawns. Cook for 3—5 minutes until pink and just cooked through.
  8. Add lemon juice, sugar if using, and coriander.
  9. Serve immediately with coconut rice.

Chef Tips

  • Never boil the coconut milk hard — it splits. A gentle simmer is the key.
  • Do not overcook the prawns — 3—5 minutes maximum.
  • A pinch of sugar balances the coconut milk beautifully without making the dish sweet.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling the coconut milk vigorously — it separates into oil and water.
  • Overcooking prawns — rubbery and tough.

The Science

The fat in coconut milk coats the prawn proteins and helps them retain moisture during brief cooking. The emulsified structure of coconut milk also creates a naturally thick, creamy sauce without any thickening agents — provided it is not boiled hard, which breaks the emulsion.

Cultural Note

Coconut prawns represent the pinnacle of everyday Swahili coastal cooking — two abundant coastal ingredients (prawns from the Indian Ocean, coconuts from the palm-lined shore) combined in a dish that is both quick and celebratory. In Mombasa, this dish symbolises the generosity and warmth of coastal Kenyan hospitality.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: Wali wa Nazi, Basmati Rice, Chapati, Kachumbari, Fresh Coriander
