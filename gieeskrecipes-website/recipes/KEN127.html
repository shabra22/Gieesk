🥥 Kenyan Coconut Bhajia

Bajias za Nazi

Coastal Coconut Bhajia — the Swahili coast's own version of bhajia, where coconut milk replaces water in the gram flour batter, adding a subtle sweetness and rich coconut fragrance to the crispy fried potato slices. A coastal elevation of the classic street snack.

Bajias za Nazi takes the beloved bhajia and gives it a distinctly coastal character — replacing the water in the batter with coconut milk, adding cardamom, and finishing with a tamarind coconut chutney. The result is a bhajia with a subtle sweetness and coconut aroma that perfectly complements the crispy potato and the tangy chutney.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Sharp knife or mandoline, Deep frying pan, Slotted spoon

  • 4 large potatoes — very thinly sliced
  • 2 cups gram flour (besan)
  • ¼ cup rice flour
  • 200ml coconut milk
  • ¼ cup water — as needed
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ¼ tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped
  • Oil for deep frying

Method

  1. Slice potatoes 2—3mm thin. Pat completely dry.
  2. Combine gram flour, rice flour, spices, cardamom, salt, and baking powder.
  3. Whisk in coconut milk gradually. Add water if needed to reach a medium-thick batter.
  4. Stir in coriander.
  5. Heat oil to 175°C.
  6. Dip potato slices in coconut batter. Fry in batches for 3—4 minutes until golden.
  7. Drain on a rack. Serve with tamarind coconut chutney.

Chef Tips

  • Coconut milk makes the batter slightly thicker — adjust water accordingly.
  • The cardamom is the coastal signature — do not skip it.
  • Serve with the tamarind coconut chutney from KEN017.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Wet potato slices — batter does not adhere.
  • Too-thick batter — heavy, doughy bhajia.

The Science

Replacing water with coconut milk adds fat to the batter, which produces a slightly more tender, less brittle crust than the classic water-based version — a pleasant coastal variation.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Bajias za Nazi represent the Swahili coast's instinct to add coconut to everything — transforming the already-beloved bhajia into something distinctly, fragrantly coastal.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Oven at 190°C for 5 minutes.

Serve with: Tamarind Coconut Chutney, Kenyan Masala Chai, Fresh Sugarcane Juice
