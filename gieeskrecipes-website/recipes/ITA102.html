🥐 Sfogliatelle Napoletane

Sfogliatelle Ricce

Neapolitan pastry famous for its many crisp, shell-like layers, encasing a filling of semolina, ricotta, and candied fruit — among the most technically demanding pastries in Italian baking.

The name means 'little leaves' or 'layers,' referring to the hundreds of paper-thin layers created by rolling the dough impossibly thin, brushing with lard, and coiling it before cutting and shaping each pastry into its distinctive clam-shell form.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pasta machine, Rolling pin, Baking sheet

  • 500 g (4 cups) 00 flour
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) water
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • Pinch of salt
  • 250 g (1⅛ cups) lard or shortening, softened, for laminating
  • 250 ml (1 cup) water, for the semolina filling
  • 100 g (½ cup) semolina
  • 250 g (1 cup) ricotta
  • 100 g (½ cup) granulated sugar
  • 1 egg
  • 50 g (⅓ cup) candied orange peel, finely chopped
  • Pinch of cinnamon
  • Powdered sugar, for dusting

Method

  1. Combine the flour, water, honey, and salt into a firm dough, kneading until smooth. Wrap and rest for 1 hour.
  2. Roll the dough as thin as possible using a pasta machine, working in sections, brushing each thin sheet generously with softened lard before rolling the next section directly on top.
  3. Once you have a long, laminated strip, roll it tightly into a log and wrap well; chill for at least 1 hour, or freeze for easier slicing.
  4. For the filling, bring the water to a boil and whisk in the semolina, cooking until thickened. Let cool, then beat in the ricotta, sugar, egg, candied orange peel, and cinnamon.
  5. Slice the chilled dough log into rounds about 1cm thick.
  6. Working with one round at a time, press and stretch it with your thumbs to open up the coiled layers into a cone shape, forming a pocket.
  7. Fill each cone generously with the semolina-ricotta filling and pinch to seal, shaping into the characteristic shell form.
  8. Bake at 220°C (425°F) for 20–25 minutes until deeply golden and the layers have visibly separated and crisped.
  9. Dust with powdered sugar and serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef Tips

  • Rolling the dough as thin as humanly possible is what creates the hundreds of characteristic crisp layers — this takes real patience.
  • Keeping the dough well-chilled makes it far easier to slice cleanly without the layers smearing together.
  • These are best eaten the day they're baked, while the layers are at their crispest.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not rolling the dough thin enough, resulting in fewer, thicker layers rather than the signature delicate shatter.
  • Overfilling the pastries, causing the filling to leak out during baking.
  • Skipping the chilling step, which makes the laminated dough very difficult to slice neatly.

Cultural Note

Sfogliatelle originated in a convent near Amalfi before becoming a signature pastry of Naples, where it remains a fixture of breakfast pastry cases across the city.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten the day they are made; keep at room temperature for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Warm briefly in the oven to help re-crisp the layers if slightly softened.

Serve with: Espresso
