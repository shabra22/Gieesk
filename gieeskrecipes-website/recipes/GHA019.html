🟠 Palm Nut Soup

Abenkwan — abɛ nkwan

Built on tinned palm nut cream — and the cue is exact: the soup is done when you no longer see foam on top and the oil is visible.

★ 4.9 (268 reviews)

20 minPrep

6Servings

MediumDifficulty

Abenkwan is the richest soup in Ghana and Osseo-Asare says it surpasses all others for colour, flavour and texture. This version uses tinned palm nut cream rather than pounding fresh palm fruit, which is what most cooks outside Ghana actually do. Beef neck bones and smoked fish simmer an hour and a half to two hours with garlic, ginger, scotch bonnet, tomato paste and bay. The palm cream is strained with six and a half cups of water, blended vegetables go in and cook half-covered fifteen minutes, the meats return, and it simmers thirty to forty-five minutes more.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Sieve, Blender, Ladle

    Meat

    • 2 lb beef neck bones
    • 3 pieces smoked fish
    • Beef tripe (optional)
    • 6 garlic cloves
    • 2 thumb-size pieces ginger
    • 1 habanero or scotch bonnet
    • 4 tbsp tomato paste
    • 3 bay leaves
    • 1/4 cup vegetable oil
    • 4 tbsp all-purpose seasoning

    Soup

    • 1 tin (1 lb 10 oz) palm nut cream
    • 6 1/2 cups water
    • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
    • 1 large onion
    • 4 large tomatoes
    • 1/2 red bell pepper
    • 1 thumb-size piece ginger

    Method

    1. Simmer the beef neck bones and smoked fish with the garlic, ginger, scotch bonnet, tomato paste, bay leaves, seasoning and vegetable oil for 1 1/2 to 2 hours.
    2. Strain the palm nut cream with the 6 1/2 cups of water into a clean pot.
    3. Blend the onion, tomatoes, red bell pepper and ginger.
    4. Add the blended vegetables to the palm liquid and cook half-covered 15 minutes.
    5. Add the cooked meats and fish.
    6. Simmer 30 to 45 minutes.
    7. The soup is done when you no longer see foam on top and the oil is visible on top.
    8. Serve with fufu or omo tuo.

    Chef Tips

    • The doneness cue is the best-written one in the Ghanaian soup literature: no foam on top, oil visible on top. Both conditions, not one.
    • Half-covered for the fifteen-minute vegetable stage. Fully covered and it will not reduce; uncovered and it spits palm oil across the kitchen.
    • Strain the palm cream with the water rather than stirring it in. The fibre that comes out of a tin is not pleasant in the finished soup.
    • An hour and a half to two hours on the neck bones. They are the whole point of the dish — collagen-heavy and cheap — and they will not give up their body in less.
    • Smoked fish goes in with the meat from the start, not at the end. It is a seasoning here as much as a protein.
    • Osseo-Asare's description is the best thing written about this soup: it surpasses all other soups for richness, colour, flavour and texture.
    • Every documented palm nut soup starts from a different base — fresh pounded nuts, tinned cream, a concentrate, or straight palm oil. They are genuinely different soups and the sources do not agree on which is correct.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Stirring the tinned cream in without straining.
    • Cutting the meat simmer short.
    • Fully covering the pot during the vegetable stage.
    • Serving while foam is still on the surface.
    • Adding the smoked fish at the end.

    Health Benefits

    • Palm fruit pulp is exceptionally rich in provitamin-A carotenoids and vitamin E tocotrienols.
    • Neck bones yield collagen, glycine and minerals.
    • Smoked fish adds protein, iodine and omega-3 fats.
    • High in lycopene from the long-cooked tomato.

    Cultural Note

    Abenkwan is the soup people travel for, and the version made from fresh pounded palm fruit is a different and better thing than any tin — which is precisely why the tinned version exists and is used everywhere outside Ghana. The foam-and-oil cue is the same whichever base you start from, because what it is really telling you is that the palm has cooked out.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. The palm oil sets solid when cold.

    Reheating: Simmer gently until the oil re-melts and rises.

    Serve with: Fufu, Omo Tuo, Banku
