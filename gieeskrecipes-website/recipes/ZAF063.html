🥛 Melkkos

Melkkos — frummels

Flour, salt and butter rubbed to crumbs between your fingers and sprinkled into a litre and a half of boiling cinnamon milk, whisked ten minutes to a loose porridge and buried under cinnamon sugar.

Melkkos means milk food and that is about the whole of it: milk, flour, butter, cinnamon sugar. The crumbs are called frummels and rubbing them by hand is the entire technique — a food processor works but the crumb is more even than it should be. The other traditional version, melksnysels, replaces the crumbs with a stiff egg dough rolled paper-thin and cut into ribbons. In the tradition this is supper, or a winter breakfast for grandchildren; the magazines file it under desserts, and both are right.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy-based pot, Balloon whisk, Mixing bowl

    The cinnamon sugar

    • 100 g sugar
    • 7.5 ml (1 1/2 tsp) ground cinnamon

    The frummels

    • 90 g cake flour
    • A pinch of salt
    • 45 ml (3 tbsp) butter, soft

    The milk

    • 1.5 litres milk
    • 1 cinnamon stick

    Method

    1. Mix the sugar and the ground cinnamon together and set it aside.
    2. Rub the flour, salt and butter together with your fingers until it is nice and crumbly — these are the frummels.
    3. Add 3 tablespoons of the cinnamon sugar to the crumbs.
    4. Heat the milk with the cinnamon stick until boiling and frothy.
    5. Reduce the heat to a simmer, then slowly sprinkle the frummels into the milk while whisking.
    6. Simmer gently, whisking continuously, for about 10 minutes.
    7. Take it off the heat and rest for 5 minutes.
    8. Serve with a generous cinnamon-sugar topping and a knob of butter.

    Chef Tips

    • Sprinkle the crumbs in slowly and never stop whisking. Tipped in at once they form one lump and no amount of whisking after that will fix it.
    • Be generous with the cinnamon sugar at the table. It is not a garnish — it is half the dish.
    • Real butter, not margarine. The Afrikaans sources are specific about it and they are right.
    • More flour gives a thicker melkkos — 90 g to 1.5 litres is loose and spoonable, and 180 g is closer to a pudding. Both are correct, so make the one you grew up with.
    • The five-minute rest off the heat is not padding. It thickens noticeably.
    • If it goes lumpy, work it hard with a balloon whisk while it is still hot. Most lumps will break down.
    • For melksnysels instead: a stiff dough of 240 g flour, 3 beaten eggs and a pinch of salt, kneaded 10 to 15 minutes, rolled very thin, rested 10 minutes, cut into 5 mm ribbons and simmered in 2 litres of milk for 10 to 15 minutes with a knob of butter.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding the crumbs all at once.
    • Letting the milk boil over while you are busy with the flour — it goes from nothing to everywhere in seconds.
    • Rubbing the butter in until it is a paste rather than crumbs.
    • Under-seasoning the cinnamon sugar.
    • Serving it straight off the heat, before it has thickened.

    Health Benefits

    • A litre and a half of milk makes this a substantial source of calcium, protein and vitamin B12.
    • Cinnamon has been studied for modest effects on blood sugar.
    • Cheap, filling and made from four things, which is why it fed so many people.

    Cultural Note

    Laurens van der Post called melkkos the drama of our supper, and the classification argument tells you something real: this was never a dessert in farm kitchens, it was what you ate when there was milk and flour and not much else. Now it appears in magazines under puddings, which is roughly what happens to any poor-kitchen dish that survives long enough.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days. It sets firm and is often eaten cold from the pot.

    Reheating: Warm gently with extra milk to loosen it.

    Serve with: Extra cinnamon sugar and butter, Beskuit, A cold winter evening
