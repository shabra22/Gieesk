🌿 Kenyan Festive Vegetable Pilau

Pilau ya Mboga ya Sherehe

Kenyan Festive Vegetable Pilau — the full celebration pilau experience, entirely plant-based. Fragrant whole spices, a rich tomato-onion base, and colourful mixed vegetables in spiced basmati rice. Proof that the finest Kenyan pilau can be vegetarian.

Vegetable pilau deserves to stand beside meat pilau as an equal — with the right spicing and technique, it is as fragrant, as satisfying, and as celebratory. Cashews add richness, mixed vegetables add colour and nutrition, and the whole spice base is exactly as generous as for the meat version.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Spice grinder, Wooden spoon

  • 3 cups basmati rice — soaked 30 minutes
  • 2 medium carrots — diced
  • 1 cup green beans — cut into 3cm pieces
  • 1 cup fresh or frozen peas
  • 1 large potato — cubed
  • ½ cup cashews
  • 2 large onions — thinly sliced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 6 cups vegetable stock
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander

Whole spices

  • 2 tsp cumin seeds
  • 6 cardamom pods
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 6 cloves
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns
  • 1 star anise

Method

  1. Toast all spices until fragrant. Grind half to a coarse powder, keep half whole.
  2. Heat oil. Cook onions until deeply golden — 10 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes and ground spices. Cook 8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add all vegetables and cashews. Stir and cook 5 minutes.
  6. Add vegetable stock. Bring to a boil.
  7. Add drained rice. Stir gently.
  8. Reduce to lowest heat. Cover tightly. Cook 18—20 minutes.
  9. Rest 10 minutes. Fluff gently. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • The spice technique is identical to meat pilau — do not reduce the spices just because it is vegetarian.
  • Cashews add the richness that meat would otherwise provide.
  • Add hardy vegetables (carrot, potato) first; tender vegetables (peas, beans) near the end.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-spicing — vegetable pilau needs just as much spice as meat pilau.
  • Overcrowding with too many vegetables — balance the rice-to-vegetable ratio.

The Science

The natural sugars in caramelised onions (from the Maillard reaction) provide much of the depth that meat would otherwise contribute — this is why deeply golden onions are even more important in vegetable pilau than in the meat version.

Cultural Note

Festive vegetable pilau ensures that every guest at the celebration table — regardless of dietary choice or requirement — can experience the full joy of Kenyan celebration food. It is also a statement that Kenyan cuisine is naturally abundant and celebratory even without meat.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze for 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat covered with a splash of water.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Coconut Chutney, Pili Pili Sauce, Avocado Salsa
