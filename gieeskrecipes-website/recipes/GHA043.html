🥔 Cassava Dough

Cassava dough — yucca, manioc dough

Cassava grated very fine, put in a sack under a stone and left to drain two or three days until it is a dry, tightly pressed clump.

This is the prerequisite for akple and the component half of banku, and it is almost nothing but patience. The original directions she worked from are four lines: peel cassava, wash it, grate it very fine, mix water if not too wet, put it in a sack with a stone or a heavy pot on top, and let it drain for two to three days. Her own version uses about two and a half cups of grated cassava and half a cup of water, grated on the fine side of a box grater, swished with water, and drained in a pillowcase under a weighted bowl. The endpoint is a dry, tightly pressed clump.

Ingredients

Equipment: Box grater — the fine side, Clean sack or pillowcase, A stone or heavy weighted pot or bowl, Catch pan

  • About 1 lb cassava, peeled
  • About 1/2 cup water, for swishing

Yields roughly 2 1/2 cups grated cassava before draining

    Method

    1. Peel the cassava and wash it. Ask the shop to cut a tuber across so you can check it is fresh.
    2. Grate it very fine — roll up your sleeves and use some elbow grease.
    3. Mix in water if it is not too wet, and swish it.
    4. Put it in a sack or pillowcase.
    5. Put a stone, a heavy pot or a weighted bowl on top, over a catch pan.
    6. Let it drain 2 to 3 days.

    ENDPOINT: a dry, tightly pressed-together clump of cassava.

    1. Blend with water to a dough or paste when you need it.

    Chef Tips

    • The endpoint is a dry, tightly pressed clump — that is the only cue given and it is enough. If it is still wet and loose it needs longer under the weight.
    • Grate on the FINE side. Coarse grating will not press down into a usable dough.
    • Two to three days under a real weight. A light bowl will not do it.
    • Ask the shop to cut a tuber across so you can see whether it is fresh. Her own advice, and cassava goes off invisibly from the inside.
    • This is draining, not fermenting. Neither of the two posts describing it calls it fermentation or gives a sour or smell cue — which matters for akple, where the author says the dough is unfermented.
    • Catch pan underneath. A surprising volume of starchy liquid comes out.
    • Blend with about a cup of water when you come to use it — that is the quantity in the akple recipe.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Coarse grating.
    • Too little weight on the sack.
    • Draining for one day and calling it done.
    • Treating the drain as a fermentation and waiting for sourness.
    • Using cassava that has gone off, which is not visible from the outside.

    Health Benefits

    • Draining removes a portion of cassava's water-soluble cyanogenic compounds.
    • Gluten-free and fat-free.
    • Supplies resistant starch.
    • No additives of any kind.

    Cultural Note

    Cassava dough under a stone in a sack for three days is the kind of preparation that sits behind half of West African cooking and almost never gets written down, because it is not a recipe so much as a household routine. That this version was reconstructed from sketchy notes, with no teacher named, is itself a small record of how nearly it went undocumented.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Use within a day or two of draining, or freeze.

    Reheating: Not applicable — it is a raw component.

    Serve with: Akple, Banku, Used as a component, not eaten alone
