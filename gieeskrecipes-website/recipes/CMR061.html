🌿 Bobolo (Fermented Cassava Sticks — Pilot-Scale Process)

Bobolo / bâton de manioc

Cassava roots soaked in drums until softened, drained, crushed, defibred, sieved, settled, pressed about 2 hours, ground, wrapped in leaves, tied and steamed 1 hour. The paper compares a starter against a traditional control.

The process documented in Emirates Journal of Food and Agriculture 33(6):475–482 (received 5 April 2021, accepted 27 June 2021). Roots in large chunks are washed and soaked in drums ‘until softened’ — with a starter or, for the control, without. They are drained, crushed by hand, defibred, sieved, settled, decanted and pressed about 2 hours; the paste is ground, wrapped in leaves, bound with banana strings and steamed 1 hour; the bobolo drips 1 hour. The paper’s retting times: with starter 30.0 ± 3.5 hours, control 78.4 ± 7.4 hours (the paper also cites a traditional retting of 3–5 days). The STARTER (PCFCP) is made from roots cut into cylinders about 4 cm × 5 cm; 5 kg of washed roots are submerged in 5 L of water at 28–32 °C and fermentation is stopped after 96 hours, then sun-dried 2 days to below 15% water and ground to a flour; the dose is 1% (w/w). CYANIDE AS PRINTED: bobolo 3.1 ± 0.9 mg/kg with starter versus 7.9 ± 3.1 mg/kg for the control, both under the 10 mg/kg FAO/WHO reference the authors cite. GAPS: this is a pilot-scale study with no home-safety guidance; the starter is not a household ingredient; ‘softened’ is measured by penetrometer and no hand test is described.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large drums, Sieve, Press, Mill, Steamer

  • Cassava roots, large chunks, washed — weight not printed for the bobolo
  • Water to cover in drums — quantity not printed
  • Starter (optional): 1% (w/w) of the roots, made as described in the method
  • Leaves for wrapping
  • Banana strings to bind

Method

  1. Wash the roots in large chunks. Soak them in drums (with starter, or without for the traditional control) until softened.
  2. Drain, crush by hand, defibre and sieve. Let settle, decant and press about 2 hours.
  3. Grind the paste. Wrap in leaves and bind with banana strings.
  4. Steam 1 hour. Let the bobolo drip 1 hour.
  5. Starter (if used): cut washed roots into cylinders about 4 cm × 5 cm. Submerge 5 kg in 5 L of water at 28–32 °C for 96 hours. Sun-dry 2 days to below 15% water. Grind to flour; use at 1% (w/w).

Chef Tips

  • The only cue printed is that the roots are ‘softened’; the paper measures this with a penetrometer.
  • First reading of the paper: consumers described control products as strongly fermented-smelling, dark and acidic; second reading quotes only ‘control products smell bad’ — recorded, not reconciled.
  • The paper notes retting with starter finished in 30 hours against 78 for the control.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stopping the soak before the roots soften.
  • Using the starter at a different dose without data.
  • Treating this as a validated home method — the paper gives no home-safety guidance.

Cultural Note

The paper names Cameroon (bobolo) and DR Congo (chikwangue) and no ethnic group. Other Afrik articles describe bobolo as popular among the Bantu; those pages print no quantities and are not used.
