🥛 Dakh, Soured-Milk Butter

Dakh — beurre local

Freshly drawn cow’s milk left three or four days in a whole calabash with the neck cut off; when it has soured, the gourd is shaken for two or three hours until the dakh separates, distinctly, from the soured milk.

Dakh is the soured-milk and butter base that several lakh and thiakry recipes call for without explaining. Cow’s milk, freshly drawn, goes into a large calabash left whole with only the neck cut off to serve as a spout (mpkh in Serer, gamba in Wolof). It rests three or four days. When it has soured, the calabash is shaken very long — two or three hours — and the contents are poured into a container: the dakh is separated from the soured milk, and ‘very distinct’. It is collected in pots and kept until used. Editorial safety note, not from the source: the page starts from raw, unpasteurised milk fermented warm for days; raw milk can carry pathogens, and this entry does not recommend it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Calabash with the neck cut off, Collecting container, Pots for storage

  • Cow’s milk, freshly drawn — quantity not printed
  • 1 large calabash (mpkh in Serer, gamba in Wolof), left whole, with only the neck cut off for a spout

Method

  1. Put the freshly drawn cow’s milk into a large calabash left whole, with only the end cut off to serve as a spout.
  2. Leave it to rest three or four days.
  3. When it has soured, shake the calabash for a very long time — two or three hours.
  4. Pour the contents into a container: the dakh is separated from the soured milk, and very distinct.
  5. Collect the dakh in pots and keep it until use.

Chef Tips

  • The observable endpoint is the separation: the dakh appears distinctly apart from the soured milk.
  • ‘Quand il a aigri’ (when it has soured) names the state but not how to recognise it — no smell, taste or pH is printed.
  • The calabash is kept whole; only the neck is cut.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Judging the souring by days alone.
  • Stopping the shaking before a distinct separation appears.
  • Using raw milk without understanding the food-safety risk.

Cultural Note

Dakh is the unpasteurised butter-and-soured-milk base of Serer and Wolof dairy tradition, as the page presents it. The research found no published pH, titratable acidity or sensory description for Senegalese lait caillé in practice; one 2018 paper on northern Senegal states that producers’ end-of-fermentation criteria are not described.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Kept in pots until use; no storage time is printed.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Lakh, Millet porridge, Thiakry
