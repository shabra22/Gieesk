🍗 Thiebou Guinar

Thiebou guinar — thiébou guinar, riz au poulet

The chicken thieb, with the same browned-onion colour rule and a two-onion sauce.

Rice cooked in chicken broth with the onion-browning technique that runs through all this author's thieb recipes. Two onions for the sauce with vegetable oil, built the same way as her meat versions: the chicken browns first, the onions caramelise to a real brown, the water goes in, and the rice finishes in the broth with the liquid sitting just above the grain. She glosses the Wolof name explicitly on the page.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Cocotte, for the onion sauce, Knife

    For the onion sauce, as printed

    • 2 onions
    • Vegetable oil — quantity not printed

    The remaining quantities are not printed in the source. See the note.

    • Chicken, cut into pieces
    • Rice
    • Garlic
    • Bell pepper
    • Bird's eye chilli
    • Bay leaf
    • Vinegar
    • Salt and pepper
    • Water

    Method

    1. Brown the chicken pieces in hot oil.
    2. Add a sliced onion and diced bell pepper and keep caramelising, stirring occasionally.
    3. Brown the onions well — they must be well browned, even brown, to get a good rice colour.
    4. Add the water and bring to the boil.
    5. Salt, add a fresh chilli and a bay leaf, and simmer until the chicken is cooked through.
    6. Lift out the chicken and keep it warm.
    7. Wash the rice and add it to the broth, with the liquid sitting slightly above the rice without drowning it.
    8. Cover and cook until the rice is tender and the broth absorbed.

    The onion sauce

    1. Soften the remaining onions in a little oil with a bay leaf.
    2. When the onions turn translucent, add the chopped garlic, pepper and chilli, salt and pepper.
    3. Add a little water and the vinegar, and cook a few minutes on low heat.
    4. The sauce is ready when there is almost no water left.
    5. Serve the rice with the chicken and the onion sauce.

    Chef Tips

    • Same colour rule as her other thiebs and it is worth repeating: well browned, even brown, or the rice will be pale.
    • Chicken cooks faster than beef or lamb, so the hour that her meat versions simmer is too long here — go by the chicken.
    • The liquid just above the rice, never drowning it.
    • Vinegar in the onion sauce, as in all her versions. It is the balancing acid.
    • Be aware this is the thinnest-quantified of her thieb recipes: only the two onions for the sauce carry a printed amount. Nothing else has been estimated into the list above.
    • The sauce endpoint is almost no water left, not a number of minutes.
    • For a fully quantified chicken-and-rice dish from a different source, the red-palm-and-coconut version by another author is a separate tradition but better specified.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Simmering chicken as long as red meat.
    • Pale onions.
    • Too much broth with the rice.
    • Skipping the vinegar.
    • Treating the absent quantities above as an oversight rather than as the source's own gap.

    Health Benefits

    • High in protein from chicken.
    • No bouillon in the author's method, so lower in sodium than most published versions.
    • Onions supply quercetin, concentrated by long browning.
    • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

    Cultural Note

    Guinar is chicken in Wolof, and thiebou guinar is the Sunday version of the weekday meat thieb — cheaper than fish, quicker than lamb, and built on the same hour of patient onion-browning that every good thieb depends on.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

    Reheating: Steam the rice; warm the chicken gently.

    Serve with: Onion sauce, Kaani, Attaya
