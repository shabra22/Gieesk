🍅 Kenyan Roasted Tomato Relish

Mchuzi wa Nyanya ya Kuoka

Kenyan Roasted Tomato Relish — tomatoes, garlic, chilli, and onion roasted until charred and deeply caramelised, then blended into a smoky, rich, deeply flavoured condiment. The slow-roasting creates complexity that raw tomato sauces simply cannot achieve.

Roasted tomato relish is the sophisticated cousin of fresh pili pili — the high heat of the oven or charcoal transforms the tomatoes from bright and acidic to deeply sweet, smoky, and complex. The charred edges contribute bitterness that balances the natural sweetness of the caramelised tomatoes. It is an extraordinary all-purpose condiment that elevates everything from ugali to grilled meats.

Ingredients

Equipment: Baking tray, Blender, Oven or charcoal grill

  • 6 large ripe tomatoes — halved
  • 1 whole head of garlic — unpeeled
  • 2 medium onions — quartered
  • 3—4 fresh chillies
  • 3 tbsp olive oil or vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp sugar — optional
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Preheat oven to 220°C.
  2. Place tomato halves cut-side up, onion quarters, whole unpeeled garlic head, and chillies on a baking tray.
  3. Drizzle with oil. Season with salt and pepper.
  4. Roast for 25—30 minutes until the tomatoes are charred at the edges and collapsed, and the garlic is soft.
  5. Allow to cool slightly.
  6. Squeeze roasted garlic from its skin. Discard skin.
  7. Blend all roasted vegetables with coriander and lemon juice to desired consistency.
  8. Taste and adjust seasoning. Add sugar if needed to balance.

Chef Tips

  • The charring on the tomatoes is the whole point — do not be afraid of the dark, caramelised edges.
  • Roasted garlic becomes sweet and mellow — use the whole head for maximum richness.
  • Blend to a slightly chunky consistency rather than a smooth puree for the best texture.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-roasting — the tomatoes must be properly charred, not just softened.
  • Discarding the charred bits — they contain all the smoky flavour.

The Science

Roasting tomatoes at high temperature drives off most of their water content (tomatoes are 95% water) and concentrates their flavour. The Maillard reaction on the tomato sugars and the caramelisation of the natural sugars at the cut edges creates the characteristic deep, complex flavour impossible to achieve any other way.

Health Benefits

  • Roasting concentrates lycopene in tomatoes — a powerful antioxidant more bioavailable when cooked.
  • Garlic provides allicin.
  • Very low in calories.

Cultural Note

Roasted tomato relish represents the depth of Kenyan condiment tradition — the patience to roast rather than blend raw, creating a condiment with complexity and character that reflects the best of Kenyan home cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 2 weeks. Freeze for 3 months.

Reheating: Warm gently or serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Ugali, Grilled Chicken, Nyama Choma, Chapati, Eggs, Grilled Fish
