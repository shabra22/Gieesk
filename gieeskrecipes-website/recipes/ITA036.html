🌿 Pesto Genovese Pasta

Trenette al Pesto Genovese

Pesto Genovese Pasta brings together Liguria's fragrant basil sauce — pounded with pine nuts, garlic, Parmigiano, and Pecorino — with pasta, green beans, and potatoes in one classic pot.

True Genovese pesto is traditionally made with a mortar and pestle, which bruises rather than purees the basil, preserving its bright flavor without the bitterness a food processor's blade can introduce through heat and oxidation.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle or food processor, Large pot

  • 400 g (14 oz) trenette or linguine
  • 2 medium potatoes, peeled and diced
  • 150 g (5 oz) green beans, trimmed and halved

Pesto

  • 60 g (2 cups packed) fresh basil leaves
  • 40 g (¼ cup) pine nuts
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 60 g (¾ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 20 g (¼ cup) grated Pecorino Romano
  • 120 ml (½ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Make the pesto: in a mortar and pestle (or food processor), pound or pulse the basil, pine nuts, and garlic with a pinch of salt until a coarse paste forms.
  2. Gradually work in the Parmigiano and Pecorino, then slowly drizzle in the olive oil, mixing until you have a thick, vibrant green sauce. Set aside.
  3. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add the diced potatoes and cook for 5 minutes.
  4. Add the green beans and pasta to the same pot, cooking together until the pasta is al dente.
  5. Reserve a cup of pasta water, then drain the pasta, potatoes, and beans together.
  6. Toss everything with the pesto in a large bowl, off the heat (never over direct heat, which dulls the basil's flavor and color), adding pasta water as needed to loosen.
  7. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Never cook pesto directly — always toss it with hot pasta off the heat to preserve its bright color and fresh flavor.
  • A traditional mortar and pestle genuinely produces a better texture and flavor than a food processor, which can heat and oxidize the basil.
  • The potatoes and green beans are a distinctly Ligurian touch — don't skip them for an authentic version of this dish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Heating the pesto directly, which turns it dull and can make it taste bitter.
  • Over-processing in a food processor, generating heat that darkens the basil.
  • Omitting the potatoes and green beans, which are a traditional and integral part of this specific dish.

Cultural Note

Genovese pesto has Protected Geographical Indication status, with strict traditional requirements including small-leafed Genovese basil (DOP) and a stone mortar for the most authentic preparation.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Fresh pesto keeps refrigerated with a layer of olive oil on top for up to 5 days, or freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Best served at room temperature or gently warmed pasta tossed with cold pesto — avoid direct heating.

Serve with: A crisp Ligurian white wine (Vermentino), Crusty bread
