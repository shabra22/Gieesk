🐐 Goat and Vegetable Pilau

Pilau ya mbuzi

Goat marinated two hours in garam masala and rosemary, cooked in stock from its own bones, then finished with rice in exactly three cups of its sauce and five tablespoons of yoghurt.

This is the most carefully engineered rice dish in the Ugandan press archive. The bones go into salted water to make stock while the meat marinates two hours in garam masala, rosemary, salt and pepper. The meat then cooks in its own stock until tender. And the finish is precise in a way recipes rarely are: you measure the sauce off the goat so that it makes exactly three cups, topping up with stock if there is not enough, and that is the liquid the rice cooks in.

Ingredients

Equipment: Covered pan, for the stock, Large bowl, Large saucepan, Second pan, Measuring cup

    The goat

    • 500 g goat meat
    • 3 cloves garlic, grated
    • 2 large onions, chopped
    • 5 large tomatoes, grated
    • 5 ml (1 tsp) garam masala
    • 22 ml (1 1/2 tbsp) oil, goat stock or water
    • 10 ml (2 tsp) fresh rosemary, chopped
    • Salt and black pepper
    • Chillies (optional)

    The rice

    • 350 g (1 1/2 cups) rice
    • 30 ml (2 tbsp) ghee or butter
    • 200 g potatoes, diced
    • 120 g cabbage or greens, shredded
    • 1 large onion
    • 2 large carrots, sliced
    • 75 ml (5 tbsp) plain yoghurt
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) pilau masala
    • 1 large red bell pepper, chopped

    Called for in the rice method but listed only under the goat

    • Garlic

    Listed in the source but never used in its method

    • 50 g (1/2 cup) roasted nuts (optional)

    Used in the method but absent from the lists

    • 1 litre goat stock, and 3 cups of sauce measured off

    Method

    1. Cut the meat off the bones and trim off the fat.
    2. Boil the bones in a covered pan of salted water to make the stock.
    3. Cut the meat into bite-sized pieces and mix in a large bowl with the garam masala, rosemary, salt, pepper and chillies if using. Leave covered for 2 hours.

    The goat

    1. Fry the meat in a large saucepan until well browned.
    2. Add the onions and garlic and keep stirring until the meat begins to stick to the bottom of the pan.
    3. Add the tomatoes and stir, scraping the bottom. Cover for 10 minutes.
    4. Stirring occasionally, add 1 litre of stock, cover, and cook until the meat is tender.

    The rice

    1. In a separate pan, heat the ghee or butter and fry the onion, potatoes and carrots for 10 minutes.
    2. Add the red bell pepper, greens, garlic and pilau masala, and the yoghurt. Cover and simmer gently 10 minutes.
    3. Stir in the rice and the goat meat.

    The three cups

    1. Measure the sauce from the goat so that it makes three cups, mixing it with a little stock if there is not enough, and pour it into the pan.
    2. Give it a good stir, then cover and cook on very low heat for 20 minutes, or until the rice is cooked.
    3. Serve hot with fresh fruit juice or chilled water.

    Chef Tips

    • Measure the sauce to exactly three cups. That is the rice-to-liquid ratio and it is the most precise instruction in the recipe — top up with stock rather than water so you keep the flavour.
    • Make stock from the bones while the meat marinates. Two jobs in the same two hours, and the stock is what the meat then cooks in.
    • Two hours of marinating in garam masala and rosemary. Rosemary in a Ugandan goat dish looks unexpected and it works.
    • Cook the meat until it sticks to the bottom of the pan, then add tomatoes and scrape. That fond is where the depth comes from.
    • Five tablespoons of yoghurt into the vegetables is the other unusual move, and it makes the rice richer without cream.
    • Very low heat for the final twenty minutes, covered. Same principle as any absorption rice.
    • The rice method calls for garlic, but garlic appears only in the goat ingredient list — the rice sub-list has none, so you cannot tell from the page how much the rice wants. Use a clove.
    • Fifty grams of roasted nuts are listed as optional and never appear in the method. Scattered over at serving is where they would go.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Guessing the liquid instead of measuring three cups.
    • Discarding the bones instead of making stock.
    • Cutting the two-hour marinade.
    • Adding the tomatoes before the meat has caught on the pan.
    • Cooking the final stage over anything but very low heat.

    Health Benefits

    • Goat is leaner than beef or lamb and high in protein, iron and vitamin B12.
    • Cabbage, carrots, peppers and potatoes make it a balanced plate.
    • Yoghurt adds calcium and protein in place of cream.
    • Contains dairy in the ghee and the yoghurt.

    Cultural Note

    The source frames this as a perfectly balanced meal — goat for protein, rice for carbohydrate, vegetables for vitamins and minerals — which tells you something about how the Ugandan press wrote about food in 2010. It is also a good dish: the bone stock and the measured three cups are proper technique, whatever the framing.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Steam or warm covered with a splash of stock.

    Serve with: Kachumbari, Fresh fruit juice, Sauteed Spicy Sukuma Wiki
