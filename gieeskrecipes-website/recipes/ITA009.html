Pizza Bianca Romana

Pizza Bianca is Rome's beloved 'white pizza' — no tomato, no cheese, just a thin, crackling, olive-oil-brushed flatbread with flaky salt, sold by the slice in bakeries across the city.

Traditionally the first thing baked to test the oven's temperature before a full day of bread baking, pizza bianca has become a Roman institution in its own right, eaten plain, split and filled with mortadella, or dunked into coffee for breakfast.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, Baking sheet, Baking stone (optional)

  • 500 g (4 cups) bread flour
  • 350 ml (1½ cups) warm water
  • 7 g (2¼ tsp) active dry yeast
  • 10 g (2 tsp) fine sea salt
  • 45 ml (3 tbsp) extra virgin olive oil, plus more for brushing
  • Flaky sea salt, for topping
  • Fresh rosemary (optional)

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in warm water and let stand 5 minutes until foamy.
  2. Combine the flour and salt in a large bowl. Add the yeast water and olive oil, mixing to a wet, shaggy dough.
  3. Knead for 8–10 minutes until smooth and elastic — the dough should remain quite soft.
  4. Cover and let rise for 2–3 hours until doubled and airy.
  5. Preheat the oven to 250°C (480°F), with a baking stone inside if you have one.
  6. Generously oil a baking sheet. Turn the dough out and stretch it thin directly on the sheet, dimpling with fingertips as you go.
  7. Brush generously with olive oil and scatter with flaky salt (and rosemary, if using).
  8. Bake for 15–20 minutes until deeply golden and blistered in spots.
  9. Slide onto a rack briefly, then cut into rectangles and serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • A very hot oven is essential — this is what gives pizza bianca its characteristic blistered, crisp exterior.
  • Stretch the dough as thin as it will comfortably go without tearing.
  • Generous olive oil both in and on top of the dough is non-negotiable — it's the whole point of "white" pizza.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rolling the dough with a rolling pin instead of stretching by hand — this deflates the air bubbles that give it texture.
  • Skimping on olive oil, resulting in a dry, dull crust.
  • Not preheating the oven hot enough, leading to a pale, unblistered surface.

Health Benefits

  • Lower in fat than cheese-topped pizzas while still being rich in heart-healthy olive oil.
  • Simple ingredient list with no processed additives.

Cultural Note

Romans traditionally eat pizza bianca split open and stuffed with a few slices of mortadella as an afternoon snack — a combination so beloved it has its own name, 'pizza e mortazza.'

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten the day it's made. Store at room temperature wrapped in a towel for 1 day.

Reheating: Refresh in a hot oven for 3–5 minutes.

Serve with: Sliced mortadella (split and filled, as eaten in Rome), Prosciutto, Cappuccino for breakfast
