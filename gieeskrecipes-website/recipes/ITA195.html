🍲 Ciambotta Lucana

Ciambotta

A rustic Basilicata vegetable stew of eggplant, peppers, potatoes, and tomato, slowly cooked together until soft and richly flavored — southern Italy's answer to ratatouille.

Ciambotta is a genuinely humble dish, built from whatever summer vegetables were most abundant and inexpensive, slow-cooked together until each component softens into a cohesive, deeply savory stew rather than remaining distinctly separate.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot with lid

  • 2 eggplants, cubed
  • 3 bell peppers, sliced
  • 3 potatoes, peeled and cubed
  • 2 zucchini, sliced
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 2 cloves garlic, sliced
  • Fresh basil
  • 100 ml (scant ½ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and chili flakes, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the olive oil in a large heavy pot and sauté the onion and garlic until softened.
  2. Add the potatoes first, as they take longest to cook, and cook for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the eggplant and peppers, and cook for another 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes and a splash of water, season with salt and chili flakes, and bring to a simmer.
  5. Cover and cook gently for 20 minutes.
  6. Add the zucchini and continue cooking, uncovered, for another 10–15 minutes until all the vegetables are tender and the stew has thickened.
  7. Finish with fresh basil and serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef Tips

  • Adding the vegetables in stages, starting with the longest-cooking potatoes, ensures everything finishes tender at the same time.
  • This dish is excellent made a day ahead, as the flavors continue to meld and deepen overnight.
  • A generous hand with olive oil is traditional and contributes significantly to the dish's richness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the vegetables at once, resulting in uneven cooking where some pieces are mushy and others undercooked.
  • Rushing the cooking time, which prevents the vegetables from properly softening and melding together.
  • Being too sparing with the olive oil, which the vegetables need to develop real flavor.

Cultural Note

Ciambotta is a defining dish of Basilicata's cucina povera tradition, its humble, vegetable-based simplicity reflecting the region's historically modest, resourceful cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days; flavor improves the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, or serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Crusty bread, Grilled meats or fish as a side
