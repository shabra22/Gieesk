🍅 Basic Tomato Gravy

Tomato gravy

Eight tomatoes, a large onion and a quarter cup of oil — the plain gravy that other dishes are measured against by the cupful.

This is the plainest sauce in the Ghanaian repertoire and it exists to be measured into other things. Eight tomatoes, which come to about two cups with their juice, a large onion, a quarter cup of oil, half to one teaspoon of salt, and a few tablespoons of tomato paste if you want it. It is the gravy that ayikple calls for by volume — a cup and a third to a cup and a half — and it is the thing a Ghanaian kitchen keeps in a bowl to spoon over rice, beans or boiled yam without further thought.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Blender or grater, Wooden spoon

  • 8 tomatoes, about 2 cups with juice
  • 1 large onion
  • 1/4 cup oil
  • 1/2-1 tsp salt
  • A few tbsp tomato paste (optional)

Method

  1. Chop or blend the tomatoes to about 2 cups with their juice.
  2. Slice or chop the onion.
  3. Heat the oil and soften the onion.
  4. Add the tomatoes and the optional tomato paste.
  5. Add the salt.
  6. Cook down until the gravy thickens and the oil comes back to the surface.
  7. Use at once, or keep for spooning over rice, beans or yam.

Chef Tips

  • Eight tomatoes to about two cups with juice is the useful conversion here, and it tells you to keep the juice rather than draining it.
  • A quarter cup of oil for six servings is modest. The oil coming back to the surface is the standard signal that a West African tomato base is cooked.
  • The tomato paste is optional and it is there for colour and depth rather than volume. A few tablespoons is the printed amount.
  • Salt range of half to one teaspoon — start low, since this gravy usually ends up in something else that is already seasoned.
  • Make it to go into ayikple, which calls for a cup and a third to a cup and a half by volume. That is roughly a batch and a half of this recipe's yield, so scale up if that is the plan.
  • No cooking time or doneness cue is printed in the source. The twenty-five minutes above and the oil cue are both editorial.
  • Keep a bowl in the fridge. This is the Ghanaian equivalent of a basic tomato sauce and it makes a plate of plain rice into a meal.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Draining the tomato juice.
  • A full teaspoon of salt when it is going into a seasoned dish.
  • Not cooking it long enough for the oil to separate.
  • Treating the tomato paste as essential.
  • Scaling it one-for-one for ayikple, which needs more.

Cultural Note

Every cuisine has its default tomato sauce and this is Ghana's — unremarkable on its own, and the reason a plate of rice, a boiled yam or a pot of beans is never dry. That it was written down at all, and quantified well enough for another recipe to measure it by the cup, is more than most household basics get.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat.

Serve with: Ayikple, Rice, Boiled yam
