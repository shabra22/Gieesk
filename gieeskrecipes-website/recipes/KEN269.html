🦐 Kenyan Seafood Salad

Saladi ya Samaki

Kenyan Seafood Salad — chilled prawns, calamari, and flaked tilapia over mixed greens with mango, cucumber, cherry tomatoes, and a tamarind-lime dressing. The coastal hotel's signature seafood starter — light, refreshing, and extraordinary.

The Kenyan seafood salad at a Mombasa beach hotel is the perfect first course — the chilled seafood, tropical fruit, and tamarind dressing create a refreshing start to a meal that is simultaneously luxurious and light.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Whisk, Serving plates

  • 200g cooked prawns — chilled
  • 150g calamari — lightly cooked and chilled
  • 200g tilapia fillet — poached and flaked
  • 4 cups mixed salad greens
  • 1 cup cherry tomatoes — halved
  • 1 small cucumber — sliced
  • 1 mango — diced
  • ½ avocado — diced

Tamarind-Lime Dressing

  • 2 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 3 tbsp lime juice
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp ginger — grated
  • ½ tsp salt

Method

  1. Ensure all seafood is cooked, cooled completely, and refrigerated.
  2. Whisk tamarind, lime, honey, olive oil, ginger, and salt into a dressing.
  3. Arrange greens on plates.
  4. Top with cucumber, cherry tomatoes, mango, and avocado.
  5. Arrange chilled seafood over the vegetables.
  6. Drizzle dressing over just before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Cold seafood is essential — everything must be properly chilled.
  • Add dressing only at service — it wilts greens quickly.
  • Arrange seafood by type for visual impact.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Warm seafood — unpleasant texture and food safety concern.
  • Pre-dressing — wilted greens.

The Science

Tamarind's tartaric acid combined with lime's citric acid creates a uniquely complex acidity that cuts through the seafood's richness. The honey provides fructose that caramelises slightly on the seafood surface, creating a glaze effect.

Cultural Note

The Kenyan seafood salad represents the coastal restaurant experience at its most elegant — the Indian Ocean's gifts served cold and simply, with a dressing that draws from the same coastal trade routes that first brought tamarind to East Africa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Assemble immediately before serving.

Reheating: Not applicable — serve cold.

Serve with: As a luxury starter, With the Seafood Platter (KEN252)
