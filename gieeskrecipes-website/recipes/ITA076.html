Troccoli al Ragù di Pesce

A thick, square-cut pasta unique to the Gargano area of Puglia, dressed in a slow-cooked tomato sauce built from mixed local fish, richer and more textured than a typical seafood marinara.

Troccoli are cut with a special ridged rolling pin called a troccolaturo, producing a substantial square-edged noodle that stands up well to hearty sauces — here, a fish ragù that simmers whole pieces of fish down into the tomato base until it becomes a deeply savory sauce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Troccolaturo or ridged rolling pin, Large pot, Wide sauté pan

  • 400 g (3 cups) semolina flour
  • 4 eggs
  • Pinch of salt
  • 800 g (1.75 lb) mixed firm white fish (such as grouper, monkfish, or similar), cut into chunks
  • 3 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 1 dried chili, crumbled
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry white wine
  • 600 g (1.3 lb) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Make the pasta dough by combining flour, eggs, and salt, kneading until smooth. Wrap and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Roll the dough into sheets about 3mm thick and pass through a troccolaturo, or cut by hand into thick square strands.
  3. Warm the olive oil in a wide pan and sauté the garlic and chili until fragrant.
  4. Add the fish pieces and sear briefly on all sides, then pour in the wine and let it reduce by half.
  5. Add the crushed tomatoes, season, and simmer gently for 30–35 minutes, breaking the fish apart gradually with a spoon as it cooks until the sauce becomes rich and cohesive.
  6. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the troccoli for 4–5 minutes.
  7. Drain the pasta and toss with the fish ragù, finishing with fresh parsley before serving.

Chef Tips

  • A mix of two or three different firm white fish gives the ragù more depth than a single variety.
  • Let the fish break down gradually into the sauce rather than removing and flaking it separately — this is what builds the ragù's texture.
  • The pasta should be substantial and toothsome to stand up to the chunky sauce.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using delicate, flaky fish that disintegrates too quickly rather than a firm variety that holds up to simmering.
  • Rushing the sauce, which needs time for the fish to fully break down and flavor the tomato base.
  • Adding cheese, which is never paired with seafood pasta in traditional Italian cooking.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of lean protein and omega-3 fatty acids from the fish.
  • Rich in lycopene from the slow-cooked tomato base.

Cultural Note

Troccoli are most closely associated with San Giovanni Rotondo and the Gargano promontory of Puglia, a peninsula with a long fishing tradition along the Adriatic coast.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The ragù keeps refrigerated for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat the sauce gently on the stovetop; cook fresh pasta just before serving.

Serve with: A dry white wine from Puglia, Crusty bread
