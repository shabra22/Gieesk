🥚 Kenyan Egg Curry

Mayai ya Curry

Kenyan Egg Curry — hard-boiled eggs simmered in a richly spiced tomato and onion curry sauce with garlic, ginger, and garam masala. One of Kenya's most satisfying budget-friendly meals — the combination of creamy eggs and bold curry sauce is extraordinary.

Egg curry is one of those dishes that seems simple until you eat it — and then you understand why it is beloved across the Indian-Kenyan culinary tradition and has become a staple in Kenyan homes. Hard-boiled eggs, halved or scored and simmered in a deeply spiced tomato sauce, absorb the curry beautifully. The result is rich, warming, and deeply satisfying at a fraction of the cost of meat curries.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pan, Wooden spoon, Pot for boiling eggs

  • 6 large eggs — hard-boiled and peeled
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • ½ cup water
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 1½ tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Hard-boil the eggs: place in cold water, bring to a boil, cook for 10 minutes. Transfer to cold water immediately, then peel.
  2. Score the peeled eggs 3—4 times with a knife — this allows the curry sauce to penetrate and flavour the eggs.
  3. Optional: shallow-fry the peeled eggs in a little oil for 2—3 minutes until lightly golden all over. This adds a pleasant texture.
  4. Heat oil in a pan. Cook onions until deeply golden — 10 minutes.
  5. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  6. Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 8 minutes until thick.
  7. Add all spices except garam masala. Stir 1 minute.
  8. Add water. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  9. Add eggs halved or whole. Spoon sauce over them gently.
  10. Simmer for 8—10 minutes until the eggs have absorbed some of the curry flavour.
  11. Stir in garam masala. Scatter coriander and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Scoring the eggs is the most important step — it allows the curry sauce to penetrate the white, infusing every bite.
  • Shallow-frying the eggs before adding them to the curry creates a lightly crispy exterior that absorbs sauce differently — the texture contrast is excellent.
  • Do not rush the onion and tomato base — the deeper it cooks, the richer the curry.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not scoring the eggs — they stay plain white while the sauce stays on the outside.
  • Under-cooking the base — a pale, thin sauce produces a flat curry.
  • Overcooking in the curry — the eggs become rubbery.

The Science

Scoring the egg white creates channels through which the curry sauce can diffuse via osmosis during simmering. The hot sauce gradually displaces some of the water in the egg white, carrying flavour compounds with it — this is why scored eggs taste so much better than unscored ones in curry.

Cultural Note

Egg curry reflects the deep Indian-Kenyan culinary heritage — one of the most beloved budget-friendly meals that delivers all the satisfaction of a meat curry at a fraction of the cost. It is a staple in Kenyan homes and restaurants nationwide.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently on stovetop over low heat.

Serve with: Basmati Rice, Chapati, Ugali, Naan, Kachumbari, Natural Yogurt
