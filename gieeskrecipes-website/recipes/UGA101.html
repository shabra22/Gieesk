Mukati — Maracha bread

Maize ground on a stone or in a mortar, tied up in a leaf and boiled into a solid block or oval — bread made without an oven or a pan.

Mukati, known as Maracha bread after the West Nile district, is a maize snack that is boiled rather than baked or fried. The maize is ground on a grinding stone or pounded in a mortar, tied in a leaf, and boiled until it sets into a block or an oval. It is the same principle as a tamale or a Ghanaian kenkey: the leaf is the mould and the pot is the oven, which is exactly what you do in a kitchen with a fire and no oven.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grinding stone, or mortar and pestle, Large leaves, String or leaf fibre, Large pan of water

  • Maize, ground on a stone or pounded in a mortar
  • Leaves, for tying

No quantities are documented

    Method

    1. Grind the maize on a stone, or pound it in a mortar.
    2. Tie the ground maize up in a leaf, shaping it into a block or an oval.
    3. Boil the parcels until firm and set.
    4. Unwrap and serve.

    Chef Tips

    • The leaf is the mould. Tie it tightly and the bread takes the shape you gave it; tie it loose and you get a shapeless mass.
    • A block or an oval is the documented shape, and the shaping happens at the tying stage rather than afterwards.
    • Boiled, not baked or fried. This is bread made in a pot, which is what you do without an oven.
    • Grind it on a stone or in a mortar as the source says. The coarseness of stone-ground maize is part of the texture.
    • No quantities, no water ratio and no boiling time are documented in any source. Those are real gaps and nothing here fills them.
    • It is named after Maracha district in West Nile, which is where it belongs.
    • The nearest documented relatives in this collection are ebwanga, Ebwanga, which is steamed rather than boiled, and the banana-leaf parcels of luwombo. All three use the leaf as a vessel.
    • Akarafi, Akarafi, is the other Lugbara tea-time item and the two go together.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Tying the parcel loosely.
    • Using leaves that split in boiling water.
    • Under-boiling, so the centre stays raw.
    • Using fine commercial maize meal and expecting the same texture.
    • Unwrapping it while it is still soft.

    Health Benefits

    • Maize supplies fibre, folate and thiamine.
    • Boiled in leaf with no added fat at all.
    • Naturally gluten-free and dairy-free.
    • Stone-ground maize retains more of the germ than commercial flour.

    Cultural Note

    Boiling a starch inside a leaf turns up independently in West Africa, Central America and East Asia, and it is the solution a kitchen reaches when it has a fire, a pot and no oven. That West Nile's version is named after a district rather than a technique suggests it is considered local and particular rather than generic.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: In its leaf, 2 days at room temperature or 4 refrigerated.

    Reheating: Boil or steam the parcel again for a few minutes.

    Serve with: Kuruku Chai, Maize Milk, Akarafi
