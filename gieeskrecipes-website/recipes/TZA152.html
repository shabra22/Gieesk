🍖 Swahili Spiced Lamb

Kondoo wa Viungo vya Kiswahili

Tanzanian Swahili Spiced Lamb — lamb slow-cooked in a Zanzibar spice sauce with coconut milk, tamarind, and fresh coriander. The most aromatic lamb preparation of the coast — every spice the island grows woven into one magnificent stew.

Swahili spiced lamb is the coastal equivalent of the grandest inland lamb stew — every Zanzibar spice applied generously, coconut milk for richness, tamarind for brightness. The result is a lamb stew of extraordinary complexity.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot

  • 1 kg lamb — bone-in pieces
  • 400ml coconut milk
  • 2 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 4 tomatoes — diced
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp coriander
  • ½ tsp ground cloves
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup coriander

Method

  1. Brown lamb. Remove.
  2. Cook onions until very golden — 12 minutes.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, and all spices. Stir 1—2 minutes.
  4. Add tomatoes and tamarind. Cook 10 minutes until thick.
  5. Return lamb. Add 1 cup water. Simmer covered 55—65 minutes until very tender.
  6. Add coconut milk. Simmer gently 12 minutes.
  7. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • The clove and cardamom are the coastal signature.
  • Tamarind brightens what would otherwise be a very rich stew.
  • Add coconut milk only when lamb is fully tender.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding coconut milk too early.
  • Insufficient coastal spices.

Cultural Note

Swahili spiced lamb is what happens when the world's finest spice tradition meets one of the world's great slow-cooking traditions — a stew of extraordinary depth that only the Zanzibar coast can produce.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Wali wa Nazi (TZA002), Chapati ya Nazi (TZA135), Kachumbari (TZA108)
