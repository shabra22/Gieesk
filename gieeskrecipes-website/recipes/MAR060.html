🍞 Khobz (Moroccan Round Loaf)

Khobz / khobz dyal dar (خبز)

The everyday Moroccan loaf: a flat round of white-flour dough about a half inch thick, risen and baked until golden and hollow-sounding, used to scoop up tagines.

Khobz is served at nearly every Moroccan meal and doubles as a utensil. The dough is made from bread flour, yeast, salt, sugar, oil and warm water, kneaded until smooth and elastic, divided into two mounds, rested, patted into rounds about 1/4 inch thick and left to rise before baking at 225°C. The author notes Moroccans keep it to about 1.5 cm thick, that it can be baked at home or at a public street oven called a ferran, and that it does not stay fresh beyond a day.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Baking sheet, Oven

  • 4 cups white flour (preferably high-gluten or bread flour)
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 2 tsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp yeast
  • 2 tbsp oil (olive or vegetable)
  • 1 1/4 cups warm water (not hot)
  • Oil, semolina or cornmeal for the pan (optional)

Method

  1. Combine the flour, salt and sugar. Make a well and add the yeast, then the water and oil.
  2. Knead 5 to 10 minutes until smooth, soft and elastic but not sticky.
  3. Divide into two mounds and rest 10 to 15 minutes.
  4. Pat each into a flat round about 1/4 inch thick. Let rise about an hour (longer in a cold room), until the dough springs back when lightly pressed.
  5. Heat the oven to 435°F (225°C). Score or prick the tops and bake about 20 minutes, until golden brown and hollow-sounding when tapped.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: yeast foamy within 10 minutes if proofed; rise until it springs back; baked until golden and hollow-sounding.
  • The wording of the springs-back cue was relayed secondhand; check the page.
  • The card says rising 1 hr 15 minutes and the method says about an hour.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Making the rounds too thick.
  • Using cold water.
  • Skipping the pricking, which lets the loaf balloon.

Health Benefits

  • A simple wheat bread with little fat.

Cultural Note

The author writes that khobz is served at nearly every meal, is used as a utensil, may be baked in a public street oven called a ferran, and is known as kesra in Tamazight and agroum in Tashelhit.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best the day it is baked; does not stay fresh beyond a day.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a hot oven or on a dry pan.

Serve with: Tagines and salads
