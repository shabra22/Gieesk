🐢 Skilpadjies

Skilpadjies

Liver soaked overnight in yoghurt and liqueur, hand-chopped with onion, garlic, thyme and clove, wrapped in squares of caul fat and braaied slowly until the fat crisps — little tortoises, for the shape.

Skilpadjies means little tortoises, which is what a parcel of liver wrapped in netvet looks like on the grid. The overnight soak is the step that makes them approachable — yoghurt on one half of the liver and a fruit liqueur on the other, both of which tame the iron edge. Then it is hand-chopped, never processed, because a food processor turns liver to purée in about two seconds and there is no way back.

Ingredients

Equipment: Two bowls, for the overnight soak, Sharp knife and board — not a food processor, Braai with medium coals, Toothpicks

    The liver, soaked overnight

    • 500 g cleaned liver — ask the butcher to clean it
    • 240 ml (1 cup) plain yoghurt
    • 240 ml (1 cup) ratafia or another fruit liqueur

    The mix

    • 50 g onion, finely chopped
    • 1 clove garlic, finely grated
    • 1 tsp fresh thyme, chopped
    • A pinch of ground cloves
    • Salt and pepper, to taste

    The wrapping

    • 12 pieces caul fat (netvet), cut into roughly 12 x 12 cm squares
    • 12 toothpicks
    • A little oil, for the grid

    Method

    1. Make sure the livers are properly cleaned, with all tubes and membranes removed. The butcher will do this.
    2. Divide the liver into two bowls of 250 g each. Cover one with the yoghurt and one with the ratafia. Cover both and leave overnight in the fridge. The colour will change, which is normal.
    3. The next day, drain off the yoghurt and the liqueur, rinse the livers in cold water and pat them dry.
    4. Chop the liver by hand to a rough mince. Do not use a food processor — it purees before you notice.
    5. Mix in the onion, garlic, thyme, ground cloves, salt and pepper, and divide the mixture into 12 equal balls.
    6. Set each ball in the centre of a square of caul fat and wrap it into a round parcel, fastening with a toothpick. Refrigerate until you cook them.
    7. Braai over medium coals — too hot and the outside burns while the inside stays raw. Brush a little oil on so they do not stick to the grid.
    8. Give them 15 to 20 minutes depending on thickness, turning. They are done when the surrounding fat has crisped and the inside still has a slight pink tinge.
    9. Eat them straight off the fire. Standing, they lose their juices and dry out.

    Chef Tips

    • Hand-chopped, always. This is the difference between skilpadjies and a liver pate in a fat wrapper.
    • Medium coals. The caul fat needs time to render and crisp, and high heat burns it before that happens.
    • Slightly pink in the middle is the target. Cooked through, liver goes grainy and bitter.
    • Caul fat is a special order from most butchers — ask a day or two ahead. There is no substitute; it is the wrapper and the basting fat at once.
    • Plain milk works in place of the ratafia for the soak if you would rather not use liqueur, and is what many households use.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using a food processor on the liver.
    • Braaiing them over hot coals.
    • Cooking them through to well done.
    • Letting them stand after they come off the fire.
    • Trying to make them without caul fat, which simply does not work.

    Health Benefits

    • Liver is among the most nutrient-dense foods there is, exceptionally high in vitamin A, iron, copper and B12.
    • Very high in protein and essentially free of carbohydrate.
    • Rich enough that two or three is a serving, which is how they are eaten.

    Cultural Note

    Skilpadjies belong to the same family as pofadders and vlermuise — Afrikaans names for parcels of offal wrapped in caul fat and cooked over fire, all named after animals they vaguely resemble. They are Karoo and farm food and they are a proper braai delicacy rather than a novelty, which anyone who has eaten a well-made one will confirm.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Raw, wrapped, up to 2 days refrigerated. Cooked, eat immediately.

    Reheating: Not recommended — reheating cooks them past the point they are good at.

    Serve with: An off-sweet pickle or preserve, Melba toast, as a starter, Roosterkoek
