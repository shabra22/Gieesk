🫕 Kenyan Sorghum Porridge

Uji wa Mtama

Kenyan Sorghum Porridge — uji wa mtama. Ground sorghum cooked into a warm, slightly reddish-brown porridge with a distinctive earthy, mildly smoky flavour. A deeply traditional Kenyan breakfast from communities where sorghum has been grown for thousands of years.

Sorghum porridge has a distinctive character all its own — the slightly reddish-brown colour from the sorghum tannins, a subtle earthiness, and a mild smokiness that sets it apart from millet or maize porridge. In communities that have grown sorghum for generations, this porridge is associated with strength, resilience, and deep agricultural heritage.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium saucepan, Wooden spoon

  • 1 cup sorghum flour (mtama flour)
  • 4 cups water — divided
  • 3—4 tbsp sugar or honey
  • ½ cup milk — optional

Method

  1. Mix sorghum flour with 1 cup cold water to a smooth slurry.
  2. Bring remaining 3 cups water to a boil.
  3. Pour slurry into boiling water while stirring constantly.
  4. Stir over medium heat for 6—8 minutes until smooth and thickened.
  5. Add sugar and milk if using. Simmer 2 more minutes.
  6. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Sorghum porridge is thinner than millet porridge at the same ratio — it has a slightly more liquid consistency that many prefer.
  • Brown sugar or jaggery pairs particularly well with sorghum's earthy notes.
  • A combination of sorghum and millet flours creates a more complex flavour.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not making a slurry first — lumps.
  • Overcooking — becomes too thick and gluey.

The Science

Sorghum flour has slightly coarser granules than finger millet, producing a slightly less smooth porridge at the same cooking time. The reddish colour comes from condensed tannins (proanthocyanidins) in the sorghum grain.

Cultural Note

Sorghum porridge is a breakfast that carries the memory of thousands of years of East African agriculture. In communities that have grown mtama for generations, a bowl of sorghum porridge in the morning is both nourishment and identity.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best consumed fresh.

Reheating: Add water and reheat gently, stirring.

Serve with: Mandazi, Sweet Potatoes, Boiled Eggs, Groundnuts
