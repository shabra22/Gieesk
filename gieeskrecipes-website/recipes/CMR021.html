🌽 Cameroon Koki Corn (Steamed Fresh-Corn Cake)

Koki corn / koki mais

Fresh corn and onion blended coarsely and mixed with corn flour, palm oil and spinach, then steamed about an hour on a bed of corn husks until the cake holds together.

A fresh-corn koki: four ears of corn and an onion are blended coarsely with about a quarter cup of water. The pot is lined with corn husks and the mixture steamed around an hour — the page says to check after 15–20 minutes that the water has not dried out — then rested 10 minutes. The cue given is that the cake should hold together and not fall apart, and it firms up as it cools. A second printing (Adamu, 24 Nov 2012) uses 5 ears of corn, 1 cup cornmeal and 3 cups of spinach and says the dish is made predominantly in Cameroon during the rainy season; its steam time differs between its article and its recipe card.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Steamer pot, Corn husks

  • 4 ears fresh corn
  • 1 onion
  • 3 tablespoons palm oil
  • 1 cup corn flour
  • Salt to taste
  • 2 cups spinach

Method

  1. Blend the corn and onion coarsely with about a quarter cup of water.
  2. Mix in the corn flour, palm oil, salt and chopped spinach.
  3. Line the pot with corn husks, add the mixture and steam for about 1 hour. Check after 15–20 minutes that the water has not dried out.
  4. Rest 10 minutes. The cake should hold together and not fall apart; it firms up as it cools.

Chef Tips

  • The printed cue is that the koki ‘should hold together and not fall apart’ (single-pass).
  • Check the steamer water after 15–20 minutes.
  • Leave it to firm up as it cools.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting the steamer dry out.
  • Blending the corn to a fine puree — the page says coarse.
  • Cutting it hot, before the rest.

Cultural Note
