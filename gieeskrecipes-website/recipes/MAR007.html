🍑 Lamb Tagine with Prunes

Tajine d'agneau aux pruneaux — lham bel barquq (لحم بالبرقوق)

Lamb shanks simmered with a mountain of onions until they fall from the bone, with prunes cooked separately in honey and cinnamon so they stay glossy and do not turn the sauce brown.

The method that makes this version special is that the prunes are not cooked with the meat. Benkabbou softens large quantities of chopped onion in olive oil, adds garlic, turmeric, ginger, salt, pepper and saffron, browns the shanks and simmers them gently for 2 hours or until the meat separates easily from the bone. Halfway through, 1/2 cup of the cooking liquid is set aside; later the prunes, softened for 5 minutes in boiling water, are cooked in that reserved liquid with cinnamon and honey for 5 minutes until the sauce starts to thicken, then spooned over the meat with sesame or almonds. She does it this way so the dish does not turn brown or blackish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large casserole or tagine with lid, Small pan for the prunes

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1.2kg lamb shanks trimmed of excess fat (about 4 small or 3 large shanks)
  • 4 large onions (about 800g), chopped
  • 1 tablespoon finely chopped garlic (about 4 cloves)
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1 teaspoon salt or more to taste
  • ¼ teaspoon ground black pepper
  • ¼ teaspoon saffron
  • 1 large coriander bouquet, tied
  • Toasted sesame seeds or chopped roasted almonds for garnish

For the prunes

  • 300g soft dried prunes
  • 3 tablespoons honey
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon

Method

  1. Heat the olive oil in the casserole, add the onions and garlic, cover and leave until the onions are soft and translucent, about 10 to 15 minutes.
  2. Add the turmeric, ginger, salt, pepper and saffron, then the lamb. Turn the meat to coat.
  3. Pour a cup and a half of water (as printed: 320 ml) into the casserole and place the coriander bouquet on top of the meat.
  4. Simmer gently for 2 hours or until the meat is soft and separates easily from the bone. Halfway through, after about 1 hour, reserve 1/2 cup of the liquid. Add a few tablespoons (or 1/4 cup) of water if the tagine is drying out.
  5. Put the prunes in boiling water for 5 minutes to soften, then drain.
  6. In a small pan, combine the reserved 1/2 cup liquid with the drained prunes, cinnamon and honey and simmer 5 minutes until the sauce starts to thicken.
  7. Serve the lamb with the onion sauce, the prunes spooned over, and a scatter of sesame seeds or almonds.

Chef Tips

  • Cooking the prunes separately keeps the dish from turning brown or blackish, says the author.
  • The author uses extra onions so the sauce thickens on its own. Alternative cuts she names: lamb shoulder, neck, or beef shin.
  • Doneness: the meat is soft and separates easily from the bone.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the prunes the whole time. They dissolve and darken the sauce.
  • Too much water; the onions give most of the sauce.
  • Skipping the reserved liquid, which carries the lamb flavour into the prune glaze.

Health Benefits

  • Prunes provide fibre and potassium; onions add sweetness without sugar.
  • A rich dish for occasions; serve with plenty of vegetables.

Cultural Note

Benkabbou introduces this as the legendary Moroccan meat tagine with caramelised prunes. MarocMama's version of the dish (see related) says it is served at births and weddings.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 3 days in the fridge. The prunes taste better the next day.

Reheating: Warm gently in a covered pot, adding a spoonful of water if needed.

Serve with: Khobz, Couscous with seven vegetables
