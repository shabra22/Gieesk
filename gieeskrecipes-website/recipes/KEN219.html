🫘 Kenyan Family Bean Stew

Mchuzi wa Maharagwe ya Familia

Kenyan Family Bean Stew — the large-pot bean stew prepared for family gatherings and celebrations. Rich, thick, generously spiced, and made in quantities that feed the whole extended family. The essential side dish at every Kenyan celebration table.

No Kenyan celebration is complete without a large pot of bean stew on the table — it is the dish that ensures everyone is fed, regardless of how many guests arrive, and it is the side that ties together the meat and rice dishes of the feast. Prepared at scale with more spice and more care than the everyday version, family bean stew is deeply satisfying and universally beloved.

Ingredients

Equipment: Very large pot (6L+), Wooden spoon

  • 800g dried mixed beans — red kidney, borlotti, and black-eyed peas
  • 3 large onions — finely chopped
  • 6 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 5 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 4 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 3 tsp salt
  • ½ cup fresh coriander

Method

  1. Soak beans overnight. Drain.
  2. Cook in fresh water for 60—75 minutes until tender. Drain, reserve 2 cups liquid.
  3. Heat oil in large pot. Cook onions until deeply golden — 10 minutes.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, tomatoes, tomato paste, and spices. Cook for 10 minutes until very thick.
  5. Add cooked beans and reserved liquid.
  6. Simmer for 20—25 minutes until thick and richly flavoured.
  7. Season generously. Garnish with fresh coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Mixed beans are better than single variety for celebration — more colour, flavour, and texture variation.
  • Prepare a day ahead — bean stew always tastes better the next day.
  • Cook a larger quantity than you think you need — it always runs out first.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-seasoning at scale — taste and adjust generously.
  • Not cooking the beans fully before the sauce stage.

The Science

Bean stew improves significantly when reheated — the overnight resting allows the spices to continue to permeate the beans and the sauce to thicken further as more starch leaches from the beans into the liquid.

Cultural Note

Family bean stew is the great equaliser of the Kenyan celebration table — it ensures that every guest, whether they eat meat or not, has a rich, satisfying, deeply flavourful dish. No celebration is properly catered without it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 5 days. Freeze for 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on stovetop — improves with reheating.

Serve with: Christmas Pilau, Wedding Beef Pilau, Ugali, Chapati, Nyama Choma
