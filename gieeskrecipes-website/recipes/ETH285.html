🍟 Ethiopian Berbere Fries

ቤርበሬ ፍሬ — Berbere Fries

Ethiopian Berbere Fries — thick-cut potato fries seasoned with a berbere-salt blend and mitmita immediately after frying. The Ethiopian answer to seasoned fries — where ordinary chips become something extraordinary with the berbere spice tradition.

Berbere fries are the simplest expression of the modern Ethiopian food identity — the globally ubiquitous French fry, transformed by the application of berbere-salt at the moment of coming out of the oil, into something distinctly and completely Ethiopian.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep pan, Bowl for tossing

  • 600g potatoes — peeled, cut into thick batons
  • Oil for deep frying

Berbere Salt: 1 tbsp berbere (ETH141) + 1 tsp salt + ¼ tsp mitmita (ETH142)

    Method

    1. Soak potato batons in cold water 30 minutes. Dry thoroughly.
    2. First fry: 160°C for 5 minutes. Remove. Cool 10 minutes.
    3. Second fry: 190°C for 3—4 minutes until very crispy.
    4. Drain. Immediately toss in berbere salt while hot.
    5. Serve immediately.

    Chef Tips

    • Double-frying is essential for crispy fries.
    • Season while hot — the berbere salt adheres to the oil surface.
    • Mitmita adds a fierce finishing heat — use judiciously.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Single fry — less crispy.
    • Seasoning cold fries.

    The Science

    Double-frying: the first low-temperature fry dehydrates the interior (gelatinising the starch and evaporating moisture). The second high-temperature fry creates the crispy shell through rapid surface dehydration and Maillard browning.

    Cultural Note

    Berbere fries are the Addis Ababa café's most ordered side dish — the combination of crispy potato with berbere and mitmita seasoning produces a flavour that is completely familiar (fries) and completely Ethiopian (berbere) simultaneously.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately.

    Reheating: Oven at 200°C briefly.

    Serve with: With Ethiopian Beef Burger (ETH284), With berbere mayo (ETH159), As a standalone snack
