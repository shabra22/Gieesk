🍆 Ebatu

Ebatu

Garden eggs and ewedu steamed alongside the ekuru for fifteen minutes, pounded rough in a mortar, then folded into a fried sauce.

Ebatu is the other sauce for ekuru and it is much the more interesting of the two. Four garden eggs and a small bunch of ewedu go into the steamer with the ekuru itself, wrapped in leaves, for about fifteen minutes — so the two halves of the meal cook in the same pot. The steamed vegetables are then pounded roughly in a mortar, not blended, and stirred into a palm-oil sauce of onion, tomato, pepper, crayfish, shawa fish and iru. It takes two minutes once they go in.

Ingredients

Equipment: Steamer or deep pot, Mortar and pestle, Saucepan, Banana or moin moin leaves, or foil

  • 4 pieces garden eggs
  • 1 small bunch ewedu
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 cooking spoon ground tomato and pepper
  • 1 cooking spoon palm oil
  • 1 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 3 pieces shawa fish
  • 1 tsp iru (locust beans)
  • Seasoning, to taste
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Wash and chop the garden eggs and ewedu, wrap them in moin moin or banana leaves, and steam with the ekuru for about 15 minutes. Foil works too.
  2. Remove the vegetables and mash them roughly in a mortar.
  3. Heat the palm oil and fry the onion, the ground tomato and pepper, and the crayfish.
  4. Add the fish, the iru, seasoning and salt, and fry until the sauce is cooked.
  5. Pour in the mashed vegetables, stir, and cook about 2 minutes.
  6. Serve with the ekuru.

Chef Tips

  • Steam the vegetables in the same pot as the ekuru. It saves a pan and it is how the recipe is built.
  • Pound, do not blend. Roughly mashed garden egg has texture; blended it turns to paste.
  • Garden eggs are the small bitter African aubergines, not the large purple kind. The bitterness is the point and it plays against the plain ekuru.
  • Two minutes once the vegetables go in. The sauce is already cooked; this is just to marry them.
  • Ewedu here is a vegetable rather than a thickener — steamed and pounded, not boiled and drawn out as in Ewedu.
  • Shawa is the small dried herring. Three pieces is enough; it is salty and assertive.
  • If you cannot get garden eggs, the dish does not really work. Aubergine has none of the bitterness that makes it ebatu.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Blending the steamed vegetables.
  • Using large purple aubergine instead of garden eggs.
  • Cooking it on after the vegetables go in.
  • Steaming the vegetables unwrapped so they waterlog.
  • Treating the ewedu as a thickener and boiling it separately.

Health Benefits

  • Garden eggs are high in fibre and very low in calories.
  • Jute leaf contributes vitamins A, C and E plus calcium and iron.
  • Light on oil — one cooking spoon for four servings.
  • Shawa fish supplies omega-3 fats and calcium.

Cultural Note

Garden egg and ewedu pounded together is an older, more vegetable-led way of dressing ekuru than the fried-pepper route, and it belongs to the same logic as the bitter-leaf and garden-egg preparations found across southern Nigeria. The author frames the whole dish in terms of eating well and portion control, which is a modern reason to arrive at a very traditional sauce.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat, briefly. The vegetables are already soft.

Serve with: Ekuru, Eko, Boiled yam
