🌭 Droewors

Droëwors

Coarse-ground beef at seventy-thirty with its fat, seasoned with toasted coriander, clove and nutmeg, loosely stuffed into thin sheep casings and dried three to five days until it has lost half its weight.

Droëwors is not biltong spice in a casing, though it is the same family of seasoning — what makes it its own thing is clove and nutmeg, almost no sugar, and the fat that has to be in it to keep a dried sausage from turning to string. The casings are thin, 22 to 24 mm sheep, because a thick sausage dries on the outside and stays wet in the middle. The rest is restraint: mix lightly, stuff loosely, and let air do the work.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mincer with an 8 to 10 mm coarse plate, Sausage stuffer, Kitchen scale, Biltong box or drying cabinet with warm airflow, A needle, for air pockets

    The meat, 1 kg total

    • 700 g lean beef
    • 300 g beef fat

    The seasoning, per kg

    • 20 g salt
    • 10 g coriander seeds, toasted and coarsely ground
    • 2 g black peppercorns, ground
    • 0.5 g ground nutmeg
    • 0.25 g ground cloves
    • 15 ml brown or malt vinegar
    • 30 ml Worcestershire sauce

    The casings

    • 22 to 24 mm sheep casings, soaked and rinsed

    Method

    1. Keep the meat, the fat and the equipment cold throughout — this matters more than any other single thing.
    2. Toast the coriander seeds in a dry pan until fragrant and grind them coarse.
    3. Grind the beef and the fat through an 8 to 10 mm coarse plate.
    4. Add the salt, coriander, pepper, nutmeg, cloves, vinegar and Worcestershire sauce and mix lightly. Do not work it — you do not want a protein bind.
    5. Refrigerate the mixture for at least 3 hours.
    6. Soak and rinse the sheep casings, then stuff loosely. Prick any air pockets with a needle.
    7. Weigh the sausage and write the weight down.
    8. Hang at 21 to 27C with 50 to 60 per cent humidity and constant warm airflow.
    9. Start checking at day 3. It is done at 50 to 60 per cent weight loss — usually 3 to 5 days.
    10. Flatten the sausage gently with your hand during the first day or two if you are using a thicker casing, to push out trapped air.

    Chef Tips

    • Beef only if you are not using a curing salt. Droëwors is never cooked, and the traditional sources are explicit about not using pork in an uncured dried sausage.
    • Thirty per cent fat is not excessive. Under about twenty and the dried sausage goes hard and crumbly.
    • Mix lightly and stuff loosely. Overworked mince makes a rubbery droëwors, and a tightly stuffed casing splits as it shrinks.
    • Thin casings, 22 to 26 mm. A boerewors-gauge casing will dry unevenly, and if you use one you must flatten it daily.
    • Weight loss, not days. Fifty to sixty per cent is the target; some makers stop at forty for a softer result.
    • Prick every air pocket you can find. Air trapped under the casing is where a dried sausage goes wrong.
    • Keep everything below 3C while you work. Warm fat smears instead of grinding and ruins the texture.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using pork or pork fat without a curing salt in a sausage that is never cooked.
    • Overmixing until it becomes a paste.
    • Stuffing the casings tight.
    • Leaving air pockets unpricked.
    • Drying it too cool and damp, where it will spoil rather than dry.
    • Making it too lean.

    Health Benefits

    • Very high in protein, iron, zinc and B12.
    • No carbohydrate to speak of.
    • High in both fat and salt — a snack in small quantities.

    Cultural Note

    Droëwors and biltong solve the same problem in two ways, and the commercial spice trade still treats them as separate products with separate dosages. The clove and nutmeg in it are a direct line back to the Cape spice route, the same ingredients that show up in bobotie and denningvleis — which is a reminder that boerekos and Cape Malay cooking are not two separate traditions so much as two branches of one.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Vacuum-sealed: 2 to 4 months in the fridge, indefinitely frozen. In a brown paper bag, 2 weeks at most.

    Reheating: Never — it is eaten dry.

    Serve with: Biltong, A cold beer, Beskuit
