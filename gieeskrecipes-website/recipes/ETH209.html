🍩 Ethiopian Fried Sweet Bread (Mandazi)

ማንዳዚ — Mandazi

Ethiopian Mandazi — triangular fried sweet dough with cardamom and coconut milk, shared with the Swahili East African tradition. Served at coastal and eastern Ethiopian community celebrations — a cross-cultural sweet that demonstrates Ethiopia's Indian Ocean connections.

Mandazi is the East African fried sweet bread that arrived in Ethiopia through the Swahili coastal connections of its eastern Muslim communities. Ethiopian mandazi uses coconut milk and cardamom for its distinctive flavour.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Rolling pin, Deep pan

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp yeast
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • ¼ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ½ cup coconut milk
  • ¼ cup warm water
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Dissolve yeast and 1 tsp sugar in warm water. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Combine flour, remaining sugar, cardamom, cinnamon, salt.
  3. Add yeast mixture, coconut milk, and oil. Mix to a soft dough.
  4. Knead 5 minutes. Cover. Rise 30 minutes.
  5. Roll to 5mm. Cut into triangles or squares.
  6. Deep fry at 170°C 2—3 minutes per side until golden and puffed.
  7. Drain. Dust with icing sugar if desired.

Chef Tips

  • Coconut milk is the distinctive flavour.
  • Do not roll too thin — mandazi should puff significantly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too thin — flat, not puffy.

The Science

See ETH166 for fried dough science. Coconut milk's MCTs (medium-chain triglycerides) produce a more tender crumb than vegetable oil — the fat molecules are shorter and coat gluten strands more completely, preventing toughening.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian mandazi represents the Indian Ocean food connection — the fried sweet bread tradition that moves along the Swahili coast from Mozambique to Somalia has found its Ethiopian expression in the eastern Muslim communities who have maintained these coastal connections for centuries.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Oven briefly.

Serve with: With chai, At East Ethiopian celebrations, As a sweet treat
