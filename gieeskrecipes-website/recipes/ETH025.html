🐑 Ethiopian Lamb Tibs

የበግ ጥብስ — Ye-Beg Tibs

Ethiopian Lamb Tibs — the celebration version of tibs using young lamb, cooked fast and hot in kibbeh with jalapeños, rosemary, onions, and tomatoes. Lamb tibs is served at the most important Ethiopian celebrations — weddings, Christmas, Easter, and Timkat.

Lamb tibs is the tibs of celebration — more expensive, more tender, and more flavourful than beef tibs. Young Ethiopian highland lamb has a mild sweetness that pairs magnificently with the kibbeh and berbere. Served at weddings and religious celebrations, it signals the generosity of the host.

Ingredients

Equipment: Cast iron pan or clay shekla pot

  • 600g young lamb — leg or shoulder, cubed 2cm
  • 3 tbsp kibbeh
  • 1 large onion — diced
  • 2 tomatoes — diced
  • 2 jalapeños — sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tsp berbere
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Pat lamb cubes completely dry.
  2. Heat kibbeh until smoking hot.
  3. Sear lamb in a single layer — 2 minutes per side.
  4. Add onion. Cook 2 minutes.
  5. Add garlic, jalapeño, and rosemary.
  6. Add tomatoes, berbere, pepper, and salt.
  7. Toss and cook 2—3 minutes more.
  8. Lamb should be medium — pink in the centre.

Chef Tips

  • Young lamb — under 1 year old — is essential for tenderness and mild flavour.
  • Lamb tibs should be slightly pinker than beef tibs — do not over-cook.
  • The rosemary and lamb combination is one of the great flavour pairings in Ethiopian cooking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-cooking lamb — becomes dry.
  • Using old lamb — strong and tough.

The Science

Young lamb has significantly less myoglobin than older animals — producing the characteristic pale pink colour and milder flavour. The fat in young lamb is primarily subcutaneous rather than intramuscular, melting quickly in the hot kibbeh and contributing to the sauce richness.

Cultural Note

Lamb tibs at an Ethiopian celebration is a mark of honour — the host who serves lamb tibs is saying 'you are worth the finest food we can offer'. At Ethiopian weddings, the lamb slaughtered for the feast is done so at dawn, and the tibs served by midday represents the full cycle of Ethiopian hospitality.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Briefly in hot kibbeh.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Ayib (ETH096), At celebrations alongside Doro Wat (ETH061)
