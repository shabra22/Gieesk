🥞 Malawax

Malawax

A soft, slightly sweet Somali pancake-bread, thicker and more tender than canjeero, traditionally rolled around a filling of sugar and ghee or dipped into savory stew.

Malawax sits somewhere between a pancake and a flatbread, its batter enriched with a touch of sugar that gives it a gentle sweetness, and it's traditionally rolled up while warm, either plain, with a smear of ghee and sugar, or alongside a savory suqaar for a satisfying contrast.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Non-stick pan

  • 300 g (2 1/4 cups) all-purpose flour
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp active dry yeast
  • 400 ml (1 2/3 cups) warm milk
  • 1 egg
  • 2 tbsp ghee or butter, melted, plus more for serving

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in a little of the warm milk with a pinch of the sugar and let sit for 10 minutes.
  2. Combine the flour, remaining sugar, and salt in a bowl.
  3. Whisk in the egg, yeast mixture, and remaining milk until you have a smooth, medium-thick batter.
  4. Cover and rest for 30 minutes.
  5. Heat a lightly oiled non-stick pan over medium heat.
  6. Pour a ladle of batter into the pan, swirling gently to spread into a round.
  7. Cook for 2-3 minutes until the top sets and bubbles appear, then flip and cook the other side briefly.
  8. Brush with melted ghee and serve warm, rolled or folded.

Chef Tips

  • Malawax is thicker than canjeero and, unlike canjeero, is traditionally flipped and cooked on both sides.
  • A touch of sugar in the batter is what gives malawax its gentle sweetness, distinguishing it from savory canjeero.
  • Serve warm with ghee and sugar for a classic breakfast, or alongside a savory stew for contrast.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Making the batter too thin, losing the soft, pancake-like thickness that defines malawax.
  • Overcooking, which can dry out the tender crumb.
  • Skipping the rest time, which allows the yeast and gluten to develop properly.

Cultural Note

Malawax is a comforting staple across Somali households, its gentle sweetness making it especially popular with children and a common feature of family breakfasts.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate stacked and wrapped for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a dry pan.

Serve with: Ghee and sugar, Suqaar, Somali tea
