Bicerin

Turin's celebrated layered drink of espresso, thick drinking chocolate, and whipped cream, served in a small glass so the distinct layers remain visible, historically enjoyed unstirred.

Bicerin has been a fixture of Turin's historic cafés since the 18th century, and tradition holds that it should be sipped slowly through its layers rather than stirred together, allowing each element — bitter coffee, rich chocolate, cool cream — to be tasted somewhat distinctly.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small saucepan, Small clear glasses

  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) whole milk
  • 80 g (2.8 oz) dark chocolate, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 2 shots freshly brewed espresso, hot
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) heavy cream, whipped to soft peaks

Method

  1. Warm the milk gently in a small saucepan, then add the chopped chocolate and sugar, stirring until fully melted and smooth.
  2. Continue heating gently, stirring, until the mixture thickens slightly into a rich, drinkable chocolate.
  3. Pour the hot chocolate mixture into the bottom of small clear glasses.
  4. Gently pour the hot espresso over the back of a spoon on top of the chocolate layer, to keep the layers distinct.
  5. Top with a generous dollop of softly whipped cream, again pouring gently to preserve the layers.
  6. Serve immediately, traditionally enjoyed without stirring.

Chef Tips

  • Pouring each layer gently over the back of a spoon helps keep the distinct layers from mixing together.
  • The chocolate layer should be thick and rich, more like a drinking chocolate than thin cocoa.
  • Traditionally sipped slowly through its layers rather than stirred, allowing each element to be tasted somewhat separately.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Pouring the layers too forcefully, causing them to mix together rather than staying distinct.
  • Making the chocolate layer too thin, losing the rich, velvety quality that defines a proper bicerin.
  • Stirring immediately, which is contrary to the traditional way of enjoying this layered drink.

Cultural Note

Bicerin has been served in Turin's historic cafés since the 18th century, and remains one of the city's most iconic culinary traditions, inseparable from its coffeehouse culture.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Not applicable; made and consumed immediately.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Enjoyed on its own as an after-dinner treat or afternoon pick-me-up
