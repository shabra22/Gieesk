Fonduta Valdostana

A rich, silky Alpine cheese fondue from Valle d'Aosta, built from Fontina melted with milk and egg yolks, traditionally topped with shaved white truffle when available.

Fonduta relies entirely on genuine Fontina from Valle d'Aosta, whose particular melting qualities and flavor cannot be easily replicated by other cheeses, resulting in a fondue that's silkier and less stringy than its Swiss and French cousins.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl for soaking, Double boiler or heatproof bowl, Whisk

  • 400 g (14 oz) Fontina Valle d'Aosta DOP, rind removed and cubed
  • 300 ml (1¼ cups) whole milk
  • 4 egg yolks
  • 30 g (2 tbsp) unsalted butter
  • White truffle, for shaving (optional)
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Crusty bread or grilled polenta, for serving

Method

  1. Cube the Fontina and soak it in the milk for at least 2 hours, or ideally overnight, in the refrigerator.
  2. Set the soaked cheese and milk over a double boiler, stirring gently as it begins to melt.
  3. Once the cheese has mostly melted into the milk, whisk in the egg yolks and butter.
  4. Continue whisking gently and constantly over the gentle heat until the mixture becomes smooth, thick, and glossy, about 8–10 minutes.
  5. Take care not to let it get too hot, which can cause the eggs to scramble or the mixture to separate.
  6. Pour into warmed bowls immediately, finishing with a generous shaving of white truffle if available, and a crack of black pepper.
  7. Serve immediately with crusty bread or grilled polenta for dipping.

Chef Tips

  • Soaking the cheese in milk beforehand is essential for achieving a smooth, silky texture rather than a stringy, clumpy one.
  • Keep the heat very gentle throughout — this is closer to a custard technique than a typical rapid cheese melt.
  • Genuine Fontina Valle d'Aosta DOP makes a real difference here; other cheeses won't achieve quite the same silky result.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the soaking step, resulting in a grainy, less cohesive fondue.
  • Cooking over heat that's too high, which can scramble the egg yolks or cause the cheese to separate.
  • Substituting a lower-quality or different cheese entirely, losing the dish's signature silky character.

Cultural Note

Fonduta is a signature dish of Valle d'Aosta, Italy's smallest and most mountainous region, deeply tied to its Alpine dairy tradition and prized Fontina cheese.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best made and eaten immediately; does not hold or reheat well.

Reheating: Not recommended; the texture suffers considerably upon reheating.

Serve with: Crusty bread, Grilled polenta wedges
