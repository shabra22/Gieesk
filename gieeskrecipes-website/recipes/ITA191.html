Torta al Testo

Umbria's beloved unleavened flatbread, traditionally cooked on a heavy flat stone called a testo, split open while hot and filled with cured meats, cheese, or sautéed greens.

Torta al testo takes its name from the terracotta or iron griddle it's traditionally cooked on, and its simple, quick-mixed dough makes it a practical, everyday bread that's just as often eaten split and stuffed as a sandwich as served alongside a meal.

Ingredients

Equipment: Testo (flat griddle) or heavy cast iron pan, Rolling pin

  • 400 g (3 cups) 00 flour
  • 250 ml (1 cup) warm water
  • 1 tsp baking soda or baking powder
  • 8 g (1½ tsp) salt
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Prosciutto, sausage, or sautéed greens, for filling

Method

  1. Combine the flour, baking soda, and salt in a bowl.
  2. Gradually add the warm water and olive oil, mixing into a smooth, slightly soft dough.
  3. Knead briefly, then rest, covered, for 20 minutes.
  4. Divide the dough into portions and roll each out into a round about 1cm thick.
  5. Heat a testo or heavy cast iron pan until very hot, with no oil.
  6. Cook each round for 5–7 minutes per side, until browned in spots and cooked through.
  7. Split the warm torta open like a pocket and fill with your choice of prosciutto, sausage, or sautéed greens.
  8. Serve immediately while warm.

Chef Tips

  • A very hot, dry pan is essential for achieving the right texture and characteristic browned spots.
  • The dough should be soft but not sticky — adjust water as needed to reach the right consistency.
  • This bread is best filled and eaten warm, straight off the griddle.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking over heat that's not hot enough, leaving the bread pale and doughy rather than properly browned.
  • Rolling the dough too thin, resulting in a bread too fragile to split and fill.
  • Letting the bread cool completely before filling, when it's best enjoyed warm.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Torta al testo is a defining bread of Umbrian cuisine, historically cooked on a stone testo placed directly over embers, and remains a staple street food and home meal throughout the region.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; store cooked, unfilled bread wrapped for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Reheat briefly on a hot dry pan before filling.

Serve with: Cured meats, Sautéed greens, Fresh cheeses
