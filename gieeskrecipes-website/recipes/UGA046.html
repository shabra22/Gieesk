Omunanansi — fermented pineapple juice

The peelings of one pineapple boiled half an hour, the juice hand-worked out of them a second time with fresh water, sweetened and left overnight at room temperature to ferment and gain a bite.

Omunanansi is made from the parts of the pineapple you would throw away. You eat the fruit, boil the peelings, and the overnight rest at room temperature is what makes it omunanansi rather than pineapple water — it ferments a little and picks up a distinct bite. It is drunk cold with ice or hot as a beverage straight off the stove, and with lemon, ginger, cinnamon and honey it is what Ugandan households reach for during a cold.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pan, Sieve, Jug or jar

  • 1 large ripe pineapple — the peelings
  • Water, to cover, plus 480 ml (2 cups) more
  • Sugar, to taste

Optional, and traditional

  • Lemongrass, cinnamon, cloves

For a cold

  • Lemon juice, ginger, cinnamon, honey

Method

  1. Clean the pineapple thoroughly before cutting it.
  2. Peel it and eat the fruit.
  3. Gather all the peelings, put them in a clean pan and add water to cover.
  4. Bring to a gentle boil over medium heat and boil about 30 minutes.
  5. Take it off the heat and let it cool completely.
  6. Sieve the juice off the peelings.
  7. Add about two more cups of water to the peelings and work them with your hands to get the rest of the juice out. Sieve that and add it to the first lot. Discard the peelings.
  8. Sweeten to taste.

The ferment

  1. Let it rest overnight at room temperature. This is what transforms the flavour and gives it its bite.
  2. Serve cold over ice — or hot as a beverage straight after cooking, before the rest.

Chef Tips

  • The overnight rest at room temperature is the recipe. Without it you have sweetened pineapple water; with it you have omunanansi, and the flavour is completely different.
  • The second extraction is worth doing. Two cups of water worked through the boiled peelings by hand gets out a surprising amount.
  • Over-ripe pineapples are ideal — this works especially well with fruit too ripe to eat, which is part of the point.
  • Clean the pineapple before you cut it. You are going to drink what comes off the skin.
  • Let it cool completely before sieving. Hot juice through a sieve carries pulp through with it.
  • No sugar quantity is given in the source, so none is stated. Sweeten before the rest, not after.
  • Lemongrass, cinnamon and cloves are common additions — some households go further.
  • Served hot with lemon, ginger, cinnamon and honey, this is the household cold remedy, and that use is the reason many people know it at all.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the overnight rest.
  • Refrigerating it overnight instead of leaving it at room temperature, which stops the ferment.
  • Throwing the peelings out after one extraction.
  • Sieving it hot.
  • Using an unwashed pineapple.

Health Benefits

  • Pineapple peel carries vitamin C, manganese and bromelain.
  • The overnight ferment produces a lightly fermented drink with some live culture.
  • Makes a drink out of what is normally waste.
  • Sweetened to taste, so the sugar is entirely under your control.

Cultural Note

Boiling the peelings is thrift and it is also just how the drink is made — nobody makes omunanansi from the flesh. The peelings afterwards go on the compost, which closes the loop completely. It is a family drink rather than a commercial one, made in large pots to last days.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days once it has had its overnight rest.

Reheating: Warm gently to drink hot.

Serve with: Rolex, Muchomo, Kabalagala
