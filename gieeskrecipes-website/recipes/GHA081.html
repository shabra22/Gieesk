🫕 Benne Soup

Benne soup — sesame soup

A cup of tahini standing in for ground sesame, heated three to four minutes until the oil begins to separate — the northern sesame soup of Tamale.

Benne soup is sesame soup, popular in Tamale, and traditionally made with guinea fowl. This version substitutes Cornish game hens and a cup of tahini for the ground sesame seed, which is an honest adaptation by a cook who learned the dish in Tamale while shadowing Mrs Comfort Awu Akor. The technique that matters is the same as groundnut soup's: heat the tahini three to four minutes until the oil begins to separate, and when the sesame oil rises, add a couple of ladles of the soup broth to the tahini mixture. Served with tuo zaafi, omo tuo or plain rice.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Separate pan, for the tahini, Ladle, Blender

  • 2 Cornish game hens, around 4 lb total — traditionally guinea fowl
  • 1 cup tahini, or less if you prefer
  • 1 1/2 tsp salt, or to taste
  • 3-4 cloves garlic
  • About a 2-inch chunk fresh peeled ginger
  • 1 onion, about 1 cup, red if available
  • About 4 habanero peppers
  • 6 small-to-medium tomatoes, or about half a large 28 oz tin
  • About 4-5 cups water in total, added throughout

Method

  1. Steam the fowl about 10 minutes with the aromatics.
  2. After 10 minutes, add water and bring to a simmer.
  3. Simmer until the bird is tender, adding water as needed to 4 or 5 cups in total.
  4. Separately heat the tahini 3 to 4 minutes, until the oil begins to separate.
  5. When the sesame oil rises, add a couple of ladles of the soup broth to the tahini mixture.
  6. Stir the loosened tahini back into the pot.
  7. Add the tomatoes and continue simmering until the soup is thick and the oil sits on the surface.
  8. Serve with tuo zaafi, omo tuo or plain rice.

Chef Tips

  • Heat the tahini separately until the oil begins to separate, three to four minutes. The same principle as groundnut soup — the paste has to break before it goes into liquid.
  • When the sesame oil rises, let the broth down into the tahini rather than the tahini into the broth. Two ladles at a time, and it will not seize.
  • A cup of tahini is a lot and she offers less as an option. Sesame is stronger and more bitter than groundnut.
  • Four habaneros for four servings is genuinely hot. Pierce them whole if you want the aroma without the full heat.
  • Guinea fowl is the traditional bird and it is a different animal from chicken — darker, gamier, tougher. Cornish game hens are her stated substitute.
  • The Tamale provenance is worth knowing: she learned it in the market there with Mrs Comfort Awu Akor, who also taught her the tuo zaafi this soup goes with.
  • Water is added throughout rather than at once — about four to five cups total, which is a loose instruction and deliberately so.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the tahini straight into the pot.
  • Moving on before the sesame oil rises.
  • Using chicken and expecting guinea fowl's depth.
  • All the water at the start.
  • Chopping the habaneros in if you wanted it mild.

Health Benefits

  • Sesame is exceptionally high in calcium, magnesium, copper and lignans.
  • Guinea fowl is leaner than chicken and higher in protein.
  • Tahini supplies plant protein and monounsaturated fat.
  • High in lycopene from the long-cooked tomato.

Cultural Note

Sesame soup is the northern counterpart to the south's groundnut soup, built on the same principle — an oily seed paste let down into broth until the oil breaks out — with sesame in place of peanut. That the recipe traces to a named Tamale cook and a trip to her local market makes it one of the better-provenanced northern dishes in print.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat with water to loosen.

Serve with: Tuo Zaafi, Omo Tuo, Plain rice
