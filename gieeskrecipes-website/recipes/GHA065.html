🐄 Wele

Wele — cow hide, cow skin

Singed, scraped, boiled six hours or more until moderately tender, then soaked overnight to leach the bitterness — a documented process with real safety caveats.

Wele is singed cow hide and it is what gives the waakye tray its chew. It is published here as a documented process rather than a recipe, because no acceptable source publishes household quantities. The hide is cut into portions, rolled hair side out, tied, singed over a fire, washed thoroughly with a sponge and water, boiled six hours or more until moderately tender, soaked in water overnight to leach the bitter taste, and rinsed. The commercial practice carries real hazards that the research itself flags: scrap tyres as fuel, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, high microbial contamination and coliforms.

Ingredients

Equipment: A clean fire or gas burner, for singeing — NOT scrap tyres, Sponge and scraper, Large pot, Large bowl, for the overnight soak

  • Cow hide, cut into portions about 30 cm in length — see the note on the printed figure
  • Scrap wire or string, for tying
  • Water, for washing, boiling and the overnight soak

No household quantities are published in any acceptable source. See the note.

    Method

    1. Cut the cow hide into portions and roll each one hair side out.
    2. Tie the rolled ends with wire or string.
    3. Singe over a fire until the hair is burnt off.

    SAFETY: use clean wood, charcoal or a gas burner. The documented commercial practice uses scrap tyres, which the research links to polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon contamination.

    1. Scrape, then wash thoroughly with a sponge and water.
    2. Boil in water in a large pot about six hours or more, until the hide becomes moderately tender.
    3. Soak in water overnight, to leach out the bitter taste.
    4. Rinse in clean water.
    5. Add to soups, or to the waakye tray.

    Chef Tips

    • Moderately tender is the cue after six hours or more of boiling. Not soft — wele is meant to have real chew.
    • The overnight soak is not optional. It leaches a bitter taste that the singeing leaves behind, and skipping it is the usual reason home-prepared wele tastes wrong.
    • Singe over clean wood, charcoal or gas. The documented commercial practice in Accra uses scrap tyres as a cheap fuel, and the research flags polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon exposure as a consequence.
    • Wash thoroughly with a sponge after singeing. The research also reports high microbial contamination and the presence of coliforms in commercially prepared hide, so the washing and the long boil are both hygiene steps.
    • Rolled hair side out and tied before singeing. That is how the hide holds its shape over the flame.
    • Six hours is a long boil and it cannot be shortened. Collagen takes that long to give.
    • Published as a process. No named source gives household quantities and none have been invented.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Singeing over scrap tyres or any burning plastic.
    • Skipping the overnight soak, which leaves it bitter.
    • Cutting the boil short, so it stays rubbery.
    • Inadequate washing after singeing.
    • Boiling it to softness, which loses the texture entirely.

    Health Benefits

    • Almost pure collagen, so very high in glycine and proline.
    • High in protein and essentially free of carbohydrate.
    • Lower in fat than most offal.
    • The overnight soak and long boil both reduce surface contamination.

    Cultural Note

    Wele is cheap protein that would otherwise be waste, and it is prized for texture rather than flavour — the chew is the point. That the published research on it is mostly about contamination from scrap-tyre fires says something uncomfortable about how the cheapest foods get made, and it is worth knowing before buying it ready-prepared.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 4 days refrigerated once cooked, or frozen.

    Reheating: Add straight to simmering soup.

    Serve with: Waakye, Light Soup, Palm Nut Soup
