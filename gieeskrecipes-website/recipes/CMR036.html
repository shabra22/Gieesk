🌶️ Pèpè Soup du Cameroun (Spicy Fish Pepper Soup)

Pèpè soup

Whole fish rubbed with lime and rinsed, simmered with a blend of tomato, onion, ginger and chilli and a crushed mixture of browned njansang, burnt pèbè pods and esèsè for about 20 minutes.

A spicy fish soup the page calls ‘soupe pimentée aux poissons à la camerounaise’. The fish is soaked in lukewarm water, rubbed with lime, and rinsed, repeated, then held in salted water. The njansang is browned lightly; the pèbè pods are burnt, shelled and crushed dry with the esèsè. Tomato, onion, ginger and half the chilli (seeds removed) are blended and combined with the fish and spices in a thick-bottomed pot over low heat for a few minutes. About 1/2 litre of water is added and it cooks ‘une bonne quinzaine de minutes’; the cube is added, the seasoning adjusted, and it cooks 5 more minutes, adding a little water if needed. Handle the fish gently so the pieces stay intact. Served with macabo, boiled yam or rice. SAFETY NOTE: the source’s ingredient line offers castor seeds as an alternative to njansang. Castor seeds are toxic and are NOT used or recommended here; only njansang is carried into the recipe.

Ingredients

Equipment: Thick-bottomed pot, Frying pan, Blender, Mortar

  • 3 medium fish (machoiron, bar, capitaine, snapper or carp)
  • 2 ripe tomatoes
  • 20–25 njansang seeds (about 60 g)
  • 3–4 pèbè pods
  • 10 g fresh ginger
  • 10 g esèsè
  • 1 large onion
  • 10 g bobimbi bark powder (optional)
  • 1 Maggi or shrimp stock cube
  • About 1/2 litre water
  • 1 chilli
  • Local condiments — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Soak the fish in lukewarm water, rub with lime and rinse; repeat. Cut it and keep it in salted water.
  2. Brown the njansang lightly in a pan. Burn the pèbè pods, shell them and crush them dry with the esèsè.
  3. Blend the tomato, onion, ginger and half the chilli (seeds removed).
  4. Combine the blend and the crushed spices with the fish in a thick-bottomed pot. Cook a few minutes on low heat.
  5. Add about 1/2 litre of water and cook a good fifteen minutes.
  6. Add the stock cube, taste and adjust. Cook 5 more minutes, adding a little water if needed. Handle gently so the fish stays intact. Serve with macabo, boiled yam or rice.

Chef Tips

  • The only printed cue is that the fish pieces stay intact.
  • Do not substitute castor seeds for njansang — they are toxic.
  • ‘A few minutes’ for the first stage is not quantified on the page.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using castor seeds as the source line offers.
  • Stirring hard so the fish breaks up.
  • Adding all the chilli with the seeds.
