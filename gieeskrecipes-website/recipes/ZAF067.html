🍗 Walkie Talkies

Walkie talkies — maotwana

Chicken feet and heads dipped two minutes in boiling water so the scales rub off and the nails pull out, then simmered with onion, curry powder and thyme for three quarters of an hour and fried crisp.

Walkie talkies are chicken feet and heads, sold at taxi ranks and spaza stands and eaten with pap or amagwinya, and the name is a joke about the parts of the bird that walked and talked. The work is all in the preparation: two minutes in boiling water loosens the yellow outer scale on the feet so it rubs away, the nails pull or snip off, and the heads get plucked and the beak removed. After that it is a simmer and a fry. They are extremely sticky to eat, which is collagen, and which is also why the broth sets like jelly.

Ingredients

Equipment: Two large bowls, for the hot and cold dips, Kitchen scissors, Pot with a lid, Frying pan

    To clean

    • 500 g chicken feet, and heads if you want them
    • Boiling water, to cover
    • Cold water, to cover

    The simmer

    • 1 onion, diced
    • 2 cloves garlic, minced
    • 10 ml (2 tsp) fresh or dried thyme
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) mild curry powder
    • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) chilli powder
    • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) turmeric
    • 10 ml (2 tsp) chicken stock powder
    • 500 ml (2 cups) water, plus more as needed
    • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

    To finish

    • 30 ml (2 tbsp) sunflower oil
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) butter
    • Juice of 1 lemon
    • Fresh coriander and sliced chilli, to garnish

    Method

    1. Immerse the feet and heads in a bowl of boiling water and leave them for about 2 minutes.
    2. Transfer them straight into cold water.
    3. Rub the loosened yellow scales off the feet, and pull or snip the nails off with kitchen scissors.
    4. Pluck any remaining feathers from the heads and remove the beaks.
    5. Rinse everything thoroughly.

    Cooking

    1. Fry the onion and garlic in the oil and butter over medium-high heat until translucent.
    2. Add the thyme, curry powder, chilli powder and turmeric and saute for 2 minutes.
    3. Add the cleaned feet and heads and stir to coat.
    4. Mix the stock powder into the water and pour it in.
    5. Reduce the heat and simmer for 45 to 60 minutes, stirring occasionally and adding hot water as needed, until the meat gives easily at the bone.
    6. Season, finish with the lemon juice, and scatter over the coriander and fresh chilli.

    For a crisp finish

    1. Lift the feet out, pat them dry, and fry them in hot oil until golden and crisp before saucing them.

    Chef Tips

    • Two minutes in boiling water, then straight into cold. That temperature shock is what makes the yellow scale layer rub off; without it you are peeling by hand for an hour.
    • Nails come off by pulling after the blanch, or by snipping with kitchen scissors. Both work; the scissors are faster and less satisfying.
    • Wash your hands properly afterwards. These are pure collagen and the stickiness is remarkable.
    • Simmer then fry is the better structure — the simmer makes them tender and the fry makes them worth eating.
    • Every quantified recipe for these either stews or fries them. I could not find a single properly sourced coal-braaied version, so if you have seen them done over a fire at a shisa nyama, that method is not written down anywhere I could reach.
    • Feet only, or feet and heads. The heads are the more challenging half and there is no shame in leaving them out.
    • The cooled broth will set to a firm jelly. That is the collagen, it is entirely normal, and it makes a very good stock base.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Skipping the blanch and trying to clean them raw.
    • Leaving the nails on.
    • Leaving the beaks on the heads.
    • Under-simmering, so the meat will not come off the bone.
    • Frying them wet, which spits dangerously.

    Health Benefits

    • Chicken feet are exceptionally high in collagen, which is what makes the broth gel.
    • A good source of protein, calcium and glucosamine.
    • Very cheap protein — the reason this food exists.
    • Fried, the fat content rises considerably.

    Cultural Note

    Walkie talkies are street food in the most literal sense: sold at taxi ranks, eaten standing up, and named with the sort of joke that only works if everyone already knows what they are. Like mogodu and skopo, they are food built on making full use of the bird, and like those they have been quietly moving upmarket.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days. The sauce will set solid.

    Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of water to loosen the set broth.

    Serve with: Amagwinya or Vetkoek, Pap or Uphuthu, Ujeqe
