🥟 Tanzanian Vegetable Samosa

Sambusa ya Mboga

Tanzanian Sambusa ya Mboga — crispy triangular pastries filled with spiced potato, peas, onion, and coriander, deep-fried golden. The vegetarian street samosa — as beloved as the meat version, and completely satisfying on its own.

Vegetable samosa is the option for the growing number of urban Tanzanians who prefer vegetarian street food, and for the population that eats no meat at certain times. The spiced potato and pea filling is as satisfying as the beef version.

Ingredients

Equipment: Rolling pin, Deep frying pan

    Pastry: same as TZA176

      Vegetable Filling

      • 4 medium potatoes — boiled and diced small
      • 1 cup frozen peas
      • 1 large onion — finely diced
      • 3 garlic cloves
      • 1 tbsp ginger
      • 1 green chilli
      • 3 tbsp coriander
      • 1 tsp curry powder
      • 1 tsp cumin
      • 1 tsp turmeric
      • 1 tsp garam masala
      • 2 tsp salt
      • 2 tbsp oil

      Method

      1. Make filling: heat oil. Cook onion, garlic, ginger, chilli until golden.
      2. Add all spices. Stir 30 seconds.
      3. Add diced potato and peas. Toss to coat. Cook 5 minutes. Add coriander. Cool completely.
      4. Make pastry as per TZA176.
      5. Fill and seal as per TZA176 instructions.
      6. Fry at 175°C for 4 minutes until deep golden.

      Chef Tips

      • Dice potatoes small — they must fit in the samosa without bulging.
      • Filling must be cool and dry before using.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Warm wet filling — soggy pastry.
      • Large potato pieces — samosa splits.

      Cultural Note

      Vegetable samosa fills the same role as meat samosa in Tanzania's street food economy — fast, affordable, portable — while serving the growing demand for plant-based options from Tanzania's diverse population.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Freeze uncooked 2 months.

      Reheating: Oven at 180°C.

      Serve with: Chatni ya Nazi (TZA118), Pili Pili (TZA117), Tamarind Sauce
