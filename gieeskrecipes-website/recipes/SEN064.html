🫘 Black-Eyed Pea Salad

Salatu niébé

Black-eyed peas boiled until tender, folded into a bowl of tomato, cucumber, pepper, parsley, lime and habanero with two-thirds cup of olive oil, and rested an hour.

Half a pound of black-eyed peas, soaked an hour, boil about 30 minutes in a quart of water until tender, with half a teaspoon of salt near the end. A large bowl holds the diced tomato, cucumber and red bell pepper, chopped green onions and parsley, lime juice, a seeded habanero, the other half teaspoon of salt and black pepper; olive oil is whisked in and the peas folded through. The salad rests an hour ‘so the flavors blend’ and is served in lettuce leaves.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Large bowl

  • 1/2 pound black-eyed peas, soaked in water for 1 hour
  • 1 quart water
  • 1 tsp salt, divided in two parts
  • 1 tomato, peeled, seeded and diced
  • 1 cucumber, seeded and diced
  • 1 red bell pepper, seeded and diced
  • 1 bunch green onions, chopped
  • 1/2 bunch flat-leaf parsley, roughly chopped
  • Juice of 2 limes
  • 1 habanero pepper, seeded and finely chopped
  • 1/8 tsp black pepper, freshly ground
  • 2/3 cup olive oil, best-quality
  • 8 lettuce leaves, for serving

Method

  1. Boil the soaked peas in the quart of water about 30 minutes, until tender. Add half a teaspoon of the salt near the end of the cooking time.
  2. Drain and set aside.
  3. In a large bowl combine the tomato, cucumber, bell pepper, green onions, parsley, lime juice, habanero, the remaining half teaspoon of salt and the black pepper.
  4. Whisk in the olive oil, then gently fold in the black-eyed peas. Cover and rest for an hour so the flavours blend. Serve in lettuce leaves.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Until tender’ is the cue for the peas; salt goes in near the end of cooking.
  • The hour’s rest is a printed instruction, not an option.
  • Fold, don’t stir, so the peas hold their shape.
  • Canned peas are used in the Yolélé derivative with a 10-minute rest; this version uses dried peas.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Salting the peas at the start of boiling.
  • Serving immediately without the rest.

Cultural Note

The page prints the Wolof-looking name ‘salatu niebe’ but does not identify the language. The Saveur May 2012 feature prints a related ‘Saladu Ñebbe’ at 5 cups of cooked peas, a cup each of canola oil and parsley, and a misprinted ‘1o’ scallions.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Serve cold or at room temperature.

Serve with: Grilled fish or chicken, Fonio chips
