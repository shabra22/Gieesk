🍃 Ombangulu

Ombangulu — bofolo; Alanda, Amboroko

Wingless white ants pounded with salt and cooked inside leaves — the name means toughened or compacted together, which is exactly what happens to them.

Ombangulu is pounded white ants cooked in leaves, and the Lugbara name means toughened or compacted together. It is made from egbere, the early-morning type that emerges between half past four and six. That precision is characteristic: the Lugbara sort their white ants by the hour they come out of the ground, and each type has a use. The related dish alanda, also called amboroko, is a cake made from the small white ants.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle, Large leaves, for wrapping, Pan or embers

  • White ants (onya, ona or una) — the egbere type, with wings removed
  • Salt
  • Leaves, for wrapping

No quantities are documented

    Method

    1. Remove the wings from the white ants.
    2. Pound them with salt.
    3. Wrap the pounded mixture in leaves.
    4. Cook the parcels until set and compacted together.

    Chef Tips

    • Egbere is the type used for ombangulu — the early-morning emergence, between half past four and six in the morning. That is the Lugbara distinction and it is a real one.
    • The name tells you the doneness cue: toughened, compacted together. It should hold as a mass rather than staying loose.
    • Wings off before pounding. They will not soften and they taste of paper.
    • Alanda, also called amboroko, is the ant cake made from the small white ants, and it is the same family of preparation.
    • The Lugbara clock for white ants is worth learning: ondreondre or andre emerge 7.30 to 8.30 in the evening, immediately after dusk; api or inia between 11 and midnight as the second round; egbere 4.30 to 6 in the morning; and otunyo means yesterday's white ants, fermented two days.
    • Other insects have their own times and methods too: akuruma are collected in the mornings with noise made from tins and jerrycans; ifu or ofu in the late afternoon, three to four, with children singing; ica last of all before dusk, six to seven. Ese are grasshoppers, migrating in April and November — which tracks the Ganda May and November nsenene seasons closely.
    • No quantities and no timings are documented. Nothing here is estimated to cover that.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Leaving the wings on.
    • Under-pounding, so it never compacts.
    • Wrapping in leaves that tear.
    • Cooking it over flames rather than embers.
    • Using the wrong emergence type, which the Lugbara would not do.

    Cultural Note

    Sorting an insect by the hour it emerges, and cooking each type differently, is the kind of knowledge that only exists where people have watched the same anthills for generations. Four named emergence windows in a single night is a more detailed observation than most published entomology bothers with.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

    Reheating: Warm the parcels through.

    Serve with: Enya, Karo, Kwete
