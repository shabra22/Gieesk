🍩 Ethiopian Fried Dough

ዳቦ ጥብስ — Dabo Tibs

Ethiopian Fried Dough — simple wheat dough balls or rings deep-fried until puffy and golden, dusted with cinnamon sugar or dipped in honey. The Ethiopian street breakfast — sold by vendors outside schools and markets, eaten while walking.

Ethiopian fried dough is the universal street breakfast — the warm, puffy fried dough that schoolchildren buy on their way to school, that market-goers eat while walking. Its variants range from plain (eaten with tea) to sweet (with honey or sugar) to slightly savoury.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Deep pan

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ¾ cup warm water
  • 1 tbsp oil

Serving: honey, cinnamon sugar, or plain

  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Dissolve yeast and sugar in warm water. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Add flour, salt, and oil. Mix to a soft sticky dough.
  3. Cover. Rise 30 minutes until slightly puffed.
  4. Heat oil to 175°C.
  5. Drop spoonfuls or small balls of dough into oil.
  6. Fry 2—3 minutes per side until golden and puffed.
  7. Drain. Serve with honey or cinnamon sugar immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The dough should be quite soft and sticky — this produces the characteristic puffiness.
  • Do not let dough rise too long — over-proofed dough collapses in oil.
  • Serve immediately — fried dough loses its crispness quickly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-proofed dough — dense rather than puffy.
  • Cold oil — greasy.

The Science

The CO2 produced by the yeast fermentation is trapped in the soft dough. When the dough is dropped into hot oil (175°C), the CO2 rapidly expands (Charles's Law) and the outer surface gelatinises and sets almost instantly — producing the characteristic hollow, puffy interior with a crispy exterior.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian fried dough is the smell of school mornings — the hot oil and yeast smell drifting from street vendors as children and workers pass on their way. It is the food of ordinary daily life at its most immediate and satisfying.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: With honey, With chai (ETH221 style), As a street breakfast
