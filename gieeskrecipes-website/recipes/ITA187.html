Cavatelli al Sugo di Salsiccia

Small hand-shaped pasta shells from Molise tossed in a rustic sausage and tomato ragù, a simple, satisfying dish typical of the region's home cooking.

Molise's version of cavatelli with sausage ragù leans on generous fennel-forward sausage and a slow-simmered tomato base, a straightforward but deeply satisfying dish found in home kitchens throughout the region.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Small knife for shaping, Large pot, Large sauté pan

  • 400 g (3 cups) semolina flour
  • 180 ml (¾ cup) warm water, plus more as needed
  • Pinch of salt
  • 400 g (14 oz) Italian sausage, casings removed and crumbled
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 100 ml (scant ½ cup) dry red wine
  • 500 g (1 lb) canned crushed tomatoes
  • Fresh basil
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Grated pecorino, for serving
  • Salt and chili flakes, to taste

Method

  1. Mix the semolina, water, and salt into a firm dough, kneading for 10 minutes. Rest, covered, for 20 minutes.
  2. Pinch off small pieces of dough and drag each across a work surface with a fingertip to curl into small shells, setting aside on a floured tray.
  3. Warm the olive oil in a pan and cook the crumbled sausage until browned, breaking it into small pieces.
  4. Add the onion and garlic and sauté until softened.
  5. Add the wine and let it reduce, then add the crushed tomatoes and chili flakes.
  6. Simmer gently for 25–30 minutes until thick and well combined.
  7. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the cavatelli until they float and are tender, about 4–5 minutes.
  8. Drain the pasta, reserving some cooking water, and toss into the sauce with a splash of the water.
  9. Finish with fresh basil and serve with grated pecorino.

Chef Tips

  • Break the sausage into small, even crumbles for a consistent sauce texture.
  • The dough should be firm — knead persistently until it comes together properly.
  • This sauce benefits from a slow simmer, letting the sausage flavor fully infuse the tomato base.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the sauce, which needs time to develop real depth from the sausage.
  • Making the dough too soft, leading to cavatelli that lose their shape when boiled.
  • Using bland sausage, which is the main flavor driver of this simple dish.

Cultural Note

This dish reflects Molise's straightforward, hearty home cooking, a lesser-known Italian region whose cuisine remains deeply tied to family recipes rather than restaurant traditions.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The sauce keeps refrigerated for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat the sauce gently on the stovetop; cook fresh pasta just before serving.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A robust red wine
