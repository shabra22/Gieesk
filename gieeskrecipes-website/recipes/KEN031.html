🧆 Traditional Kenyan Suqaar

Suqaar

Suqaar is one of Northern Kenya's most beloved dishes — tender cubes of beef or goat quickly stir-fried with onions, tomatoes, garlic, ginger, and a warming blend of cumin, cardamom, and coriander until perfectly cooked and fragrant. Fast, rich, and deeply satisfying.

Suqaar is the dish that teaches you how much flavour can be achieved in under 30 minutes. Unlike the slow braises of other Kenyan meat dishes, Suqaar is a quick, high-heat stir-fry — tender cubes of meat cooked fast in a spiced tomato base until just done. The result is remarkably juicy and fragrant. In Eastleigh, Nairobi's vibrant Somali neighbourhood, the smell of Suqaar frying in the evening is as defining as any in the city.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: North Eastern Kenya — Somali-Kenyan community

Suqaar is a cornerstone of Somali cuisine that has become deeply woven into the food culture of North Eastern Kenya. The dish reflects the nomadic pastoral traditions of the Somali people, who have herded camels, goats, and cattle across the Horn of Africa for centuries. Quick to prepare, rich in protein, and deeply satisfying, Suqaar was the ideal food for communities on the move. In Kenya, it is most associated with Eastleigh in Nairobi and the North Eastern counties, where Somali-Kenyan communities have built a vibrant food culture.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large frying pan or skillet, Sharp knife, Chopping board, Wooden spoon, Measuring spoons

  • 600g beef sirloin, rump, or goat — cut into 1–2 cm cubes
  • 2 medium onions — finely chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 1 green or red bell pepper — diced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1½ tsp salt — or to taste
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice

Method

  1. Pat the meat cubes completely dry with paper towels. Season with salt, black pepper, cumin, coriander, cardamom, paprika, and turmeric. Toss to coat evenly.
  2. Heat the oil in a large frying pan or skillet over high heat until shimmering.
  3. Add the seasoned meat cubes in a single layer. Do not stir immediately — allow to sear for 2 minutes until browned on the bottom.
  4. Toss and cook for another 2–3 minutes, stirring occasionally, until browned on all sides. The meat should be just cooked through — do not overcook. Remove and set aside.
  5. In the same pan over medium heat, add the onions. Cook for 4–5 minutes until soft.
  6. Add the garlic and ginger. Cook for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  7. Add the diced tomatoes and bell pepper. Cook for 4–6 minutes until the tomatoes break down into a light sauce.
  8. Return the cooked meat to the pan. Toss everything together and cook for 2–3 minutes, allowing the flavours to come together.
  9. Squeeze in the lemon juice. Taste and adjust seasoning.
  10. Scatter with fresh coriander and serve immediately with rice, canjeero, or chapati.

Chef Tips

  • High heat and a hot pan are essential — Suqaar is a stir-fry, not a stew. The meat should sear, not steam.
  • Cut the meat into small, uniform cubes so they cook evenly in the short cooking time.
  • Do not overcook the meat — the moment it is just cooked through, remove it from the pan. Overcooked Suqaar becomes tough and dry.
  • Season the raw meat first and let the spices coat it before it hits the hot pan.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcrowding the pan — lowers the temperature, causing the meat to steam and become tough.
  • Cooking on low heat — Suqaar must be cooked on high heat for the right texture and caramelisation.
  • Overcooking the meat — small cubes cook very quickly and should be removed the moment they are done.
  • Adding meat and vegetables to the pan at the same time — they cook at different rates and the result is uneven.

The Science

Suqaar uses high-heat stir-frying to achieve the Maillard reaction — the browning of proteins and sugars on the meat surface that creates hundreds of new flavour compounds. This is why high heat and a dry, uncrowded pan are essential. Water on the meat surface or a crowded pan drops the temperature and causes steaming instead of searing, producing grey, flavourless meat rather than the browned, caramelised result that defines great Suqaar.

Cultural Note

Suqaar is a defining dish of Somali-Kenyan food culture and is closely associated with North Eastern Kenya and Nairobi's Eastleigh neighbourhood. It reflects the pastoral traditions of the Somali people — a cuisine built on meat, spice, and the practical needs of communities who have moved across vast landscapes for centuries. In Kenya, Suqaar represents the rich cultural contribution of the Somali-Kenyan community to the country's diverse food heritage.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Freeze for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Reheat quickly in a hot pan with a splash of oil for 2–3 minutes. Avoid microwave if possible — it makes the meat tough.

Serve with: Basmati Rice, Canjeero (Somali Flatbread), Chapati, Pilau Rice, Kachumbari, Fresh Lemon Wedges
