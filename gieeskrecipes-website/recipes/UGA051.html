🫛 Fresh Bean Pasta with Katunkuma

Katunkuma — butakala

Fresh shelled beans boiled under two inches of water until they turn grey, aromatics added at that point, then pasta and bitter berries dropped in at the very end so the berries do not overcook.

Fresh beans — shelled from the pod, not dried — are sold by the cup in Ugandan markets and cook in a fraction of the time. The dish has two proper doneness cues and both are sensory: the beans turn grey when they are ready for the aromatics, and they release an unmistakable aroma when they have softened enough for the pasta. The bitter berries, katunkuma or butakala, go in with the pasta at the end, because added earlier they lose their bitterness and the point of them.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan with a lid, Wooden spoon

  • 240 ml (1 cup) fresh beans, shelled
  • 250 g pasta noodles
  • A handful of bitter berries (katunkuma or butakala)
  • 3 small red onions, chopped
  • 7.5 ml (1/2 tbsp) ginger, grated
  • 1 carrot, grated
  • A handful of fresh coriander
  • Salt and pepper
  • Water

Method

  1. Wash the fresh beans and put them in a saucepan.
  2. Add enough water to cover them by two inches.
  3. Put the pan on high heat and let the beans boil.
  4. When the beans start turning a grey colour, add the ginger, onions, carrot and salt.
  5. Cover the pan and let the beans keep cooking.
  6. Once the beans start releasing an inviting aroma and have softened, add the pasta and the bitter berries.
  7. Let the pasta cook, stirring so the noodles do not clump together.
  8. When the pasta is cooked, add the black pepper and coriander and take it off the heat.
  9. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Adding the bitter berries at the end is the point. Earlier and they overcook, lose their bite, and you may as well have left them out.
  • The beans turning grey is the signal for the aromatics. It is a real visual change and it happens partway through the boil.
  • The aroma is the second cue — you will know it when it happens.
  • Two inches of water over the beans to start, and keep topping it up as it reduces so you keep the aromatic broth.
  • More water makes it soupy, which is a legitimate way to serve it.
  • Stir once the pasta is in, continuously enough to stop it clumping in a shallow pan.
  • Fresh beans, not dried. They cook far faster and taste completely different, and they are sold by the cup in the market.
  • The berries go by different names in different Ugandan languages — katunkuma and butakala are two of them.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Putting the berries in with the beans.
  • Letting the water reduce away and losing the broth.
  • Adding the aromatics at the start rather than at the grey stage.
  • Using dried beans without adjusting the whole method.
  • Not stirring after the pasta goes in.

Health Benefits

  • Fresh beans are high in protein, folate and fibre, and cook faster than dried.
  • Bitter berries are a traditional African vegetable, rich in iron and micronutrients.
  • Carrot and coriander add carotenoids and vitamin K.
  • Almost no added fat.

Cultural Note

Bitter berries divide Ugandan opinion as sharply as bitter ntula do, and they come with a local name in every language — which is a good reminder of how many languages Ugandan food is actually spoken in. Fresh shelled beans sold by the cup are a seasonal treat rather than a staple.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days. The pasta softens further.

Reheating: Warm with extra water — the pasta will have absorbed the broth.

Serve with: Sauteed Garlic Dodo, Avocado, Chapati
