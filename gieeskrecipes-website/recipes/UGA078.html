🍛 Chicken Pilau

Pilau ya nkoko

Chicken browned in pilau and chicken masala then lifted out, potatoes cooked nearly dry in tomato, everything returned with the rice, a litre of hot water poured in — and then the lid goes on and you do not stir at all.

This is the absorption pilau, and it is built in a strict order for a reason. The chicken browns in the spices and comes straight back out, so it does not overcook while the rice does. The potatoes and tomatoes reduce until fairly dry. Then rice, chicken, grated carrot and four tablespoons of chopped coriander all go in, a litre of boiling water is poured over, and from that moment the lid stays on and the spoon stays out. Stirring rice while it absorbs is what makes it claggy.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium saucepan with a tight lid, Grater, Kettle or second pan

  • 500 g rice
  • 120 ml (1/2 cup) fresh peas, cooked
  • 1 large onion, sliced
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) oil
  • 2 large cloves garlic, grated fine
  • 1 cm piece fresh ginger, grated fine
  • 7.5 ml (1 1/2 tsp) pilau masala
  • 7.5 ml (1 1/2 tsp) chicken masala
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) tomato paste
  • 2 large red ripe tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 1 bunch fresh coriander — 60 ml (4 tbsp) chopped
  • 2 large carrots, coarsely grated
  • 2 medium potatoes, cubed
  • Chilli powder, optional — the source prints the range backwards, as 1 1/2 to 1 tsp
  • 300 g chicken breast, bones and skin removed, cubed
  • 7.5 ml (1 1/2 level tsp) salt

Used in the method but absent from the printed list

  • 1 litre water

Method

  1. Slice the onions. Grate the ginger and garlic on the smallest holes of the grater, and the carrots on the largest. Chop enough coriander to make 4 tablespoons. Peel and cube the potatoes. Finely chop the tomatoes.
  2. Cube the chicken.
  3. Heat the oil in a medium pan and fry the onions until they begin to colour. Add the garlic, ginger, pilau masala, chicken masala, salt and chilli powder if using, and stir briskly on medium heat 2 to 3 minutes.
  4. Add the chicken and cook, stirring, until well browned. Remove the chicken, cover it and set it aside.
  5. In the same pan add the tomatoes and cook, stirring, 1 to 2 minutes. Add the tomato paste and stir another 1 to 2 minutes. Add the potatoes and enough water to form a thick sauce, and cook, stirring occasionally, until fairly dry.
  6. Meanwhile boil 1 litre of water in a kettle or a separate pan.
  7. Add the rice to the potato mixture and stir it well. Return the chicken with the grated carrots and chopped coriander and mix well. The mixture should be quite dry.

From here, do not stir

  1. Pour in the litre of hot water and bring to the boil. Reduce the heat and cover the pan as soon as it starts simmering.
  2. Add the cooked peas halfway through the cooking.
  3. Keep the pan covered and do not stir at all.
  4. Once the rice is cooked, stir it up to distribute the chicken and vegetables evenly. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Do not stir once the water is in. That is the single instruction the recipe puts in its own emphatic terms, and it is why this comes out as separate grains.
  • Take the chicken out after browning. Left in, breast meat is dry by the time the rice is done.
  • Cook the potato and tomato mixture until fairly dry before the rice goes in. Wet at that stage and your liquid ratio is wrong.
  • Boil the litre of water separately and have it ready. Adding cold water stops the cooking and throws the timing.
  • Grate the garlic and ginger on the smallest holes and the carrots on the largest — the recipe is specific and it is right about both.
  • Four tablespoons of chopped coriander from the bunch. That is a real amount and it is meant to be.
  • The chilli powder quantity is printed as a range running backwards, 1½ to 1 teaspoon. It is reproduced as printed; a teaspoon is a sensible reading.
  • The litre of water appears only in the method, not the ingredient list.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring after the water goes in.
  • Leaving the chicken in throughout.
  • Adding the rice to a wet tomato mixture.
  • Using cold water.
  • Lifting the lid to check.

Health Benefits

  • Chicken breast is lean, high-protein and rich in niacin and selenium.
  • Carrots, peas, potato and tomato all go into the same pot.
  • Two tablespoons of oil across five servings.
  • Whole-spice masalas rather than a sauce base.

Cultural Note

Pilau is function food in Uganda — weddings, graduations, introductions — and everyone has an opinion about it. The two methods in this collection, the absorption one here and the stir-fry in Pilau, are both Ugandan and both published in the same newspaper, which is a good illustration of how a borrowed dish settles into more than one local form.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Steam or microwave with a splash of water. Do not stir it while hot.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Fresh fruit juice, Sauteed Spicy Sukuma Wiki
