🥥 Acha Pottage

Acha — fonio pottage

Acha cooked in coconut milk with beef, carrots and peas — two to three minutes only, because the pottage thickens quite quickly.

This acha pottage is built on coconut milk rather than broth, which is not traditional to Plateau cookery but makes a genuinely good dish. A large coconut is grated and squeezed for two cups of milk, the beef is steamed down with its stock reduced, and coconut oil, tomatoes, onion, pepper and seasoning go in with it. Carrots and peas get two minutes, then half a cup of washed acha goes in for two to three minutes. The author's own note is the useful one: the pottage will thicken quite quickly.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grater, Sieve or cloth, for the coconut milk, Medium pot, Wooden spoon

  • 1 large coconut (2 cups coconut milk)
  • 1 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1/2 cup acha
  • 1 cup boiled beef, chopped into small bits
  • 1/2 small onion, chopped
  • 1/4 cup chopped carrots
  • 1/4 cup green peas
  • 2 fresh tomatoes
  • 2 scotch bonnet peppers
  • Chicken seasoning, to taste
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Extract the coconut milk by grating the coconut and squeezing it — you want about 2 cups.
  2. Season and steam the beef until cooked and tender, with the stock reduced.
  3. Add the coconut oil, tomatoes, onion, pepper and seasoning to the meat and boil until the vegetables cook, adjusting the seasoning.
  4. Add the carrots and the peas and cook 2 minutes.
  5. Add the washed acha and cook 2 to 3 minutes.
  6. Serve hot. The pottage will thicken quite quickly.

Chef Tips

  • Two to three minutes on the acha, and the warning that follows matters — it thickens quite quickly, so serve it straight away.
  • Wash the acha before it goes in. The grain is tiny and dusty.
  • Fresh coconut milk from a grated coconut, two cups' worth. Tinned will work but it is sweeter and thicker.
  • Reduce the beef stock before the other ingredients go in. You want concentrated flavour, not a watery base.
  • Carrots and peas get only two minutes, before the acha. They should still have bite.
  • A peer-reviewed study puts acha's optimum at 7.5 minutes at 100°C, which is longer than this recipe's two to three. If you want it softer, go nearer the paper's figure.
  • Coconut milk is not traditional to Plateau acha cookery. The author does not flag it, but it functions as a deviation and is noted as one here.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the acha for more than three minutes.
  • Leaving it to stand after cooking, when it tightens fast.
  • Not reducing the beef stock.
  • Overcooking the carrots and peas.
  • Using unwashed acha.

Cultural Note

That the two best-documented acha recipes are both adaptations — one with smoked turkey and thyme, one with coconut milk — is a fair picture of where this grain sits. It is ancient, nutritionally excellent, grown on the Jos Plateau, and almost entirely absent from the written record in its traditional forms, so what gets published is what urban and diaspora cooks make of it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with water or coconut milk to loosen.

Serve with: On its own, as a pottage, Fried fish, Dodo (fried plantain)
