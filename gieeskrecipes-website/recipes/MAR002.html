🥩 L'ham M'hammar

L'ham m'hammar — braised and roasted lamb, Fassi (مْحَمّر)

Lamb or beef shoulder rubbed with garlic, paprika and spices, braised with mountains of onion until the onions collapse into a paste, then roasted hot and scattered with fried almonds. A Fassi celebration dish.

M'hammar means 'red', and the colour is the point: sweet paprika, a pinch of saffron and slowly reduced onions turn a shoulder of lamb mahogany. The meat marinates in the fridge for at least 6 hours or overnight, simmers with 18 oz of sliced onions and a little water for about two hours until you can pinch it off the bone, and then goes into a 400°F (200°C) oven for 20 to 25 minutes if serving right away. The onions end up as a paste-like sauce around the meat, topped with blanched, fried almonds.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot or pressure cooker, Roasting dish, Frying pan for the almonds

    Meat and spice rub

    • 3.3 lbs. lamb or beef shoulder with bones, cut into 5 large pieces (can also use leg or rack of lamb)
    • 2 to 4 cloves garlic, pressed or finely chopped
    • 1 tsp smen (or ghee or olive oil)
    • 1 1/2 tbsp sweet paprika
    • 1 tsp salt, or to taste
    • 1 tsp ground ginger
    • 1/2 tsp ground black pepper
    • 1/2 tsp ground white pepper

    Braising

    • 18 oz. of yellow onions, finely sliced or chopped
    • 1 pinch saffron threads
    • 1/3 to 1 cup water
    • 2 to 3 tbsp oil, mixed olive and vegetable oil

    Garnish

    • 3.5 oz. almonds, blanched and fried

    Method

    1. Combine the meat with the garlic, smen, paprika, salt, ginger, black and white pepper. Cover and leave in the fridge for at least 6 hours or overnight.
    2. Put the meat in the pot with the onions, saffron, water and oil. Use 1/3 cup water in a conventional pot and 1 cup in a pressure cooker.
    3. Cover and simmer for about 2 hours, or cook with medium-low pressure for about 80 minutes, until the meat is tender.
    4. Lift the meat out and cover it. Reduce the onions and pan juices uncovered until the onions have reduced to a paste-like texture.
    5. If serving within the hour, put the meat in a preheated 400°F (200°C) oven for about 20 to 25 minutes.
    6. Return the meat to the onion paste, top with the fried almonds and serve.

    Chef Tips

    • Doneness from the source: you can easily pinch the meat off the bone.
    • Cook the first stage a day ahead if you can. The overview page says it gives the meat another chance to absorb the spices.
    • The goal for colour is mahogany red, which comes from the paprika and the long reduction rather than from tomato.
    • Name check from the source: m'hammar means red; do not confuse it with h'maar, which means donkey.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Skipping the final onion reduction. The paste-like onions are the sauce.
    • Using too much water. The source keeps it to 1/3 cup in an ordinary pot.
    • Roasting too long. 20 to 25 minutes at 400°F (200°C) is only to colour and crisp an already tender piece of meat.

    Health Benefits

    • A rich source of protein, zinc and B vitamins from the meat.
    • Onions cooked down in the pan add fibre and natural sweetness without added sugar.

    Cultural Note

    The Taste of Maroc page calls it one of the first special-occasion recipes a Fassi girl learns to master, and links it to Eid al-Adha and to weddings and births. The author remembers the oven method from childhood; traditionally, she writes, the meat was browned in clarified butter.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Keeps 3 days in the fridge. Store the almonds separately.

    Reheating: Reheat covered over low heat with a spoonful of water; refresh the almonds in a hot pan.

    Serve with: Khobz, Zaalouk
