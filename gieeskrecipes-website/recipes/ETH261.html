Meskel is the post-fast meat celebration — after the Apostles' Fast when meat was prohibited, the feast breaks with the richest Ethiopian dishes. The bonfire night is followed by a feast that is both spiritual celebration and joyful indulgence.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: All of Ethiopia — Meskel is celebrated on September 27 every year

Meskel (Finding of the True Cross) is one of the most important holidays in the Ethiopian Orthodox calendar — celebrated with a bonfire (Demera) decorated with yellow daisies and lit on September 27. After the bonfire ceremony, the feast begins. The Meskel feast is distinctive for its abundance of meat (the end of the Apostles' Fast that precedes it), yellow colour symbolism (the daisies), and the honey wine that flows freely.

Chef Tips

  • Meskel must have both Doro Wat and Key Wat — both are non-negotiable.
  • Serve with the first tej of the season.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Making only one wat — Meskel demands abundance.

Cultural Note

Meskel is the feast after the bonfire — the smell of the Demera burning, the yellow daisies on the fire, and then the gathering to eat Doro Wat and Key Wat together. It is one of Ethiopia's most joyful religious celebrations.
