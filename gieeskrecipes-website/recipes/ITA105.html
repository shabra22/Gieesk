🍪 Cantucci di Prato

Cantucci di Prato

Twice-baked Tuscan almond biscuits, hard and crunchy by design, traditionally served alongside a glass of sweet Vin Santo for dunking.

The double-bake process — first as a log, then sliced and toasted again — is what gives cantucci their signature crunch and long shelf life, a practical Tuscan tradition that has become one of Italy's most beloved cookies.

Ingredients

Equipment: Baking sheet, Sharp serrated knife

  • 350 g (2⅔ cups) 00 flour
  • 250 g (1¼ cups) granulated sugar
  • 3 eggs, plus 1 extra for egg wash
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • Pinch of salt
  • Zest of 1 orange
  • 200 g (1½ cups) whole almonds, skin on

Method

  1. Combine the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt in a bowl.
  2. Add the eggs and orange zest, mixing until a soft, slightly sticky dough forms.
  3. Fold in the whole almonds.
  4. Divide the dough in half and shape each portion into a log about 5cm wide on a lined baking sheet.
  5. Brush the logs with beaten egg and bake at 180°C (350°F) for 25 minutes until golden and firm.
  6. Remove from the oven and let cool for 10–15 minutes, just until cool enough to handle.
  7. Using a sharp serrated knife, slice the logs on a diagonal into biscuits about 1.5cm thick.
  8. Lay the slices cut-side down on the baking sheet and bake again for 12–15 minutes, until dry and golden on both sides.
  9. Cool completely — the biscuits will continue to harden as they cool.

Chef Tips

  • Let the baked logs cool just slightly before slicing — too hot and they crumble, too cool and they're harder to cut cleanly.
  • A serrated knife with a firm, decisive cut gives the cleanest slices without crumbling.
  • These genuinely improve with age, keeping well for weeks in an airtight container.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Slicing the log while still too hot, causing the biscuits to crumble apart.
  • Underbaking the second round, leaving the biscuits too soft rather than achieving their signature crunch.
  • Skimping on the almonds, which are central to the texture and flavor of a proper cantuccio.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Cantucci take their alternate name, biscotti di Prato, from the Tuscan city where they originated, and their long shelf life made them a practical staple for travelers for centuries.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 weeks.

Reheating: Not applicable; these are meant to be crunchy and are not reheated.

Serve with: A glass of Vin Santo, for dunking, Espresso
