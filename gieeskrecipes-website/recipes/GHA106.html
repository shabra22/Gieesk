🍢 Chinchinga Beef Skewers

Chinchinga — chichinga

Rump steak in one-centimetre strips with a dry suya mix of cayenne, clove and cinnamon — thirty minutes marinating, three to four minutes a side.

The beef version from the same cook who publishes the goat kebabs, and the suya mix here is dry rather than a wet peanut rub: two teaspoons of cayenne, half a teaspoon of smoked paprika, a teaspoon each of ground ginger, nutmeg and garlic powder, half a teaspoon each of cloves and cinnamon, and a teaspoon of crushed sea salt. Five hundred grams of rump steak is cut into one-centimetre strips with fifty grams of grated ginger, marinated at least thirty minutes or up to a day refrigerated, and cooked three to four minutes a side until lightly charred. Her note is that the mix is versatile and can be applied to any meat.

Ingredients

Equipment: 8 to 12 skewers, Grill or griddle, Mixing bowl, Basting brush

  • 500 g rump steak, cut into 1 cm strips
  • 50 g fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1/2 tsp sea salt
  • 30 ml groundnut oil, plus extra for brushing
  • 100 g roasted peanuts, crushed (optional)
  • 2-3 red chillies, finely sliced
  • A handful of fresh coriander

Suya mix

  • 2 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • 1 tsp ground nutmeg
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1/2 tsp ground cloves
  • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tsp crushed sea salt

Method

  1. Combine the suya mix ingredients.
  2. Cut the rump steak into 1 cm strips.
  3. Toss the beef with the grated ginger, cayenne, salt, groundnut oil and the suya mix.
  4. Marinate at least 30 minutes, or up to 1 day refrigerated.
  5. Thread onto skewers.
  6. Brush with extra groundnut oil.
  7. Cook 3 to 4 minutes per side, until lightly charred.
  8. Scatter with the crushed peanuts, sliced red chillies and coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Lightly charred is the cue, at three to four minutes a side. Chinchinga wants char marks rather than an even brown.
  • One-centimetre strips, which is thicker than the quarter-inch of the documented tankora version. Thicker strips suit a shorter, hotter cook.
  • Fifty grams of grated ginger for five hundred grams of beef is a lot and it tenderises as well as flavours.
  • Thirty minutes is enough, and up to a day refrigerated is the maximum she gives. Beef does not need overnight the way goat does.
  • The crushed peanuts go on at the END here, as a garnish, rather than into a rub. That is the opposite of her own goat version, where the peanuts are ground into the marinade.
  • Cloves and cinnamon in the suya mix are what distinguish this blend. Half a teaspoon each and they are noticeable.
  • Her own note: the mix is so versatile it can be applied to any meat. Keep a jar of it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Strips much thinner than a centimetre, which overcook in three minutes.
  • Marinating beyond a day.
  • Grinding the peanuts into the marinade, which belongs to the goat version.
  • Cooking to an even brown rather than a light char.
  • Skipping the ginger, which is doing tenderising work.

Health Benefits

  • Rump steak is high in protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
  • Roasted peanuts add niacin, vitamin E and monounsaturated fat.
  • Cloves and cinnamon are among the highest-antioxidant spices measured.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.

Cultural Note

Four published versions of one street kebab, by three cooks, agreeing only that meat goes on a stick with a peanut-and-spice seasoning, is the honest state of chichinga in print. This one is the easiest to make on a weeknight and the suya mix keeps, which is probably why it exists in two outlets.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best at once. 2 days refrigerated cooked.

Reheating: Briefly on a hot grill.

Serve with: Shito, Jollof One-Pot Rice, Kelewele
