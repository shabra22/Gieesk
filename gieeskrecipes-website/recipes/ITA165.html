Carciofi alla Giudia

Whole artichokes deep-fried twice, first to cook through and then again to crisp, opening up into a golden flower with leaves that shatter like crisps — a jewel of Rome's historic Jewish quarter.

This centuries-old technique from Rome's Jewish community involves flattening the artichoke like a flower and frying it twice at different temperatures, the second, hotter fry being what gives the outer leaves their distinctive shattering crispness while the heart stays tender.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl with acidulated water, Deep pot for frying

  • 8 young, tender artichokes with long stems
  • Juice of 2 lemons
  • Vegetable oil, for frying (enough to submerge the artichokes)
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Trim the tough outer leaves of each artichoke, peel the stem, and trim the top.
  2. Place the trimmed artichokes in a bowl of water with lemon juice to prevent browning.
  3. Using your hands, gently open and flatten each artichoke by pressing down and spreading the leaves outward like a flower.
  4. Heat vegetable oil to 150°C (300°F) and fry the artichokes, stem-up, for about 10 minutes until tender when pierced.
  5. Remove and let drain and rest briefly.
  6. Increase the oil temperature to 190°C (375°F).
  7. Fry the artichokes again briefly, about 2–3 minutes, until the outer leaves turn deeply golden and crisp — you can gently dunk them in the hot oil to help the leaves open further.
  8. Drain, season generously with salt, and serve immediately while hot and crisp.

Chef Tips

  • Young, tender artichokes work best — older ones tend to have tougher, more fibrous centers.
  • The double-fry technique, at two distinct temperatures, is essential to achieving both a tender heart and shatteringly crisp outer leaves.
  • Serve immediately after the second fry, as the crispness fades relatively quickly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using overly mature artichokes, which have tough, inedible chokes that are difficult to work around.
  • Skipping the double-fry technique, resulting in artichokes that are either undercooked inside or not properly crisp outside.
  • Letting the artichokes sit too long before the second fry, allowing them to cool and become less receptive to crisping.

Cultural Note

Carciofi alla giudia is a treasured specialty of Rome's ancient Jewish community, one of the oldest in Europe, and remains a beloved feature of the city's historic Ghetto district.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately; these do not hold or reheat well.

Reheating: Not recommended; best enjoyed fresh and hot.

Serve with: Lemon wedges, As part of a Roman antipasto spread
