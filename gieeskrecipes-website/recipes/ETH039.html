🍛 Ethiopian Lamb Curry

የበግ ካሪ — Ye-Beg Kari

Ethiopian Lamb Curry — lamb slow-simmered with an Ethiopian-influenced curry sauce combining berbere, turmeric, cardamom, and coriander with tomato and onion. The cross-cultural dish of Ethiopia's urban kitchens — the curry that has absorbed the Ethiopian spice tradition completely.

Ethiopian lamb curry reflects the country's gradual absorption of South Asian culinary influences through Somali and Djibouti traders — the familiar curry format applied to lamb but seasoned with berbere and mekelesha instead of garam masala.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 800g lamb — bone-in, cubed
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 4 tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp berbere
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 cup water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Brown lamb. Remove.
  2. Cook onions until very golden — 12 minutes.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, all spices, and tomatoes. Cook 8 minutes.
  4. Return lamb. Add water.
  5. Simmer covered 60—70 minutes until very tender.
  6. Season.

Chef Tips

  • The berbere in a curry context gives it an unmistakably Ethiopian character.
  • Low and slow for the tenderness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing.

The Science

See ETH025 for lamb cooking science. The Ethiopian curry differs from South Asian curry in the absence of coconut milk or cream — producing a drier, more concentrated sauce.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian lamb curry is the meeting of Ethiopian highland cooking with the Indian Ocean trade routes — a dish that belongs to no single food tradition but to the crossroads Ethiopia has always occupied.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Ethiopian Style Rice (ETH013), Ayib (ETH096)
