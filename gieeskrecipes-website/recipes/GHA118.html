🍞 Ghana Sugar Bread

Sugar bread — butter bread, sweet bread

Soft, sweet and pillowy — proofed yeast must be foamy and bubbled up, and the dough rises till it doubles in size.

The sweetened loaf Ghanaians eat for breakfast, distinguished in the literature from unsweetened tea bread. Three hundred and ninety grams of bread flour with half a cup of sugar, three quarters of a cup of scalded flour, a sachet of active dry yeast, a teaspoon of salt and an eighth of a teaspoon of ascorbic acid. The yeast is proofed in a cup of water at 105 to 115 degrees with a teaspoon of sugar for ten minutes, and it should be foamy and should have bubbled up. Kneading takes fifteen minutes, until it is smooth to touch and not sticky, then it rises until doubled, about an hour. Six balls, a second rise, and thirty to thirty-five minutes at 350.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Thermometer, for the water, Loaf pan or baking tray, Oven

  • 390 g (2 2/3 cups) bread flour
  • 1/2 cup sugar, plus 1 tsp for proofing the yeast
  • 3/4 cup scalded flour — see the note, the source does not define this
  • 2 1/4 tsp (1 sachet) active dry yeast
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/8 tsp ascorbic acid
  • 1 cup water at 105-115 degrees Fahrenheit

Method

  1. Proof the yeast: combine 1 cup of water at 105 to 115 degrees Fahrenheit with 1 teaspoon of sugar and the yeast, and leave 10 minutes.
  2. It should be foamy and should have bubbled up. If it is not, the yeast is dead — start again.
  3. Combine the bread flour, the 1/2 cup of sugar, the scalded flour, the salt and the ascorbic acid.
  4. Add the proofed yeast mixture and bring together into a dough. Keep the yeast and the salt apart in the bowl until mixing, because the salt will weaken the yeast.
  5. Knead 15 minutes. It should be smooth to touch after kneading and not sticky.
  6. Leave to rise till it doubles in size — usually about 1 hour.
  7. Knead 5 minutes more.
  8. Preheat the oven to 350 degrees Fahrenheit.
  9. Divide into 6 balls and arrange them in the pan.
  10. Leave for the second rise, until doubled.
  11. Bake 30 to 35 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • Foamy and bubbled up is the proof cue, and it is the ten minutes that saves you three hours. Flat water after ten minutes means dead yeast.
  • Smooth to touch and not sticky is the kneading endpoint, after fifteen minutes. Another printing gives the same cue as the dough no longer sticking to the sides of the bowl.
  • Doubled in size, about an hour. Every documented printing of this bread gives doubling as the cue rather than relying on the clock, which is why it publishes.
  • Keep the yeast and salt apart in the bowl until you mix, because salt weakens yeast. That instruction comes from a second printing and is worth importing.
  • Water at 105 to 115 degrees. Hotter kills the yeast; colder will not wake it. Use a thermometer the first time.
  • Be aware that 'scalded flour' is never defined in the source and nothing on the page explains how to make it. It is a yudane- or tangzhong-style hot-water flour paste, but that reading is not the source's.
  • Ascorbic acid is a dough conditioner at an eighth of a teaspoon. Vitamin C powder works; leaving it out gives a slightly less springy crumb.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the ten-minute proof and discovering dead yeast later.
  • Water hotter than 115 degrees.
  • Mixing the salt straight into the yeast.
  • Baking after one rise.
  • Under-kneading, so the dough stays sticky.

Health Benefits

  • Bread flour's higher gluten content gives more protein than all-purpose.
  • No dairy in this printing, so it suits the lactose-intolerant.
  • Yeast fermentation improves the mineral availability of the flour.
  • Lower in fat than the butter-bread printings, which carry up to two thirds of a cup.

Cultural Note

Sugar bread with a Milo or a bowl of hausa koko is the Ghanaian breakfast, bought warm from a woman with a basin on her head, and the beehive wood-fired ovens Whitby described in 1968 are still going in parts of the country. That the Nigerian name Agege gets attached to it in print is a small example of how West African food gets flattened once it crosses the Atlantic.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days at room temperature in a bag, or 3 months frozen.

Reheating: A warm oven, or toast it. It is excellent toasted with butter.

Serve with: Hausa Koko, Egg Stew, Shito
