Pasta 'Ncasciata

A festive baked pasta from Messina, layers of short pasta packed with meatballs, eggplant, salami, hard-boiled eggs, and two cheeses, pressed and baked until it holds together like a cake.

Pasta 'ncasciata means 'boxed' or 'encased' pasta, reflecting how the dish is tightly packed into its baking pan before cooking, resulting in a dense, richly layered casserole that slices cleanly like a savory cake once cooled slightly.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep frying pan, Large pot, Deep round baking pan

  • 500 g (1.1 lb) short pasta such as maccheroni or rigatoni
  • 2 eggplants, sliced and fried
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) small meatballs, browned
  • 150 g (5.3 oz) salami, diced
  • 3 hard-boiled eggs, sliced
  • 700 g (1.5 lb) tomato sauce
  • 200 g (2 cups) caciocavallo or provolone, diced
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated pecorino
  • Fresh basil
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Prepare the fried eggplant, browned meatballs, and tomato sauce ahead of assembling.
  2. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the pasta until very al dente, a couple of minutes less than usual.
  3. Drain the pasta and toss with about half the tomato sauce.
  4. Oil a deep round baking pan and line with a layer of fried eggplant slices, letting some hang over the edge.
  5. Layer in a portion of pasta, followed by meatballs, salami, sliced egg, diced cheese, and a little more sauce.
  6. Repeat the layers, finishing with pasta and a final layer of eggplant, folding the overhanging slices over the top to enclose everything.
  7. Press down gently and top with the remaining sauce and pecorino.
  8. Bake at 190°C (375°F) for 35–40 minutes until golden and bubbling.
  9. Let rest for 15 minutes before inverting onto a plate or slicing directly from the pan.

Chef Tips

  • Undercooking the pasta slightly before assembling is important, as it continues cooking in the oven.
  • Lining the pan with eggplant slices that overhang the edges allows you to fold them over the top, fully enclosing the filling.
  • Letting the dish rest before slicing helps it hold together in clean pieces.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the pasta before assembling, resulting in a mushy final texture.
  • Skipping the resting time, causing the layers to fall apart when sliced.
  • Under-seasoning any individual layer, which can leave parts of the dish tasting flat.

Cultural Note

Pasta 'ncasciata is a festive specialty of Messina, traditionally prepared for special occasions given the effort its many layered components require.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat covered in the oven until warmed through.

Serve with: A simple green salad, A robust Sicilian red wine
