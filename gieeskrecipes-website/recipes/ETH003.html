🫓 Mixed Grain Injera

የቅይጥ ጤፍ እንጀራ — Ye-Qiyt Injera

Ethiopian Mixed Grain Injera — a blend of teff, barley, and sorghum flours fermented together, producing a lighter, less sour injera that is more affordable and accessible than pure teff. The everyday injera of much of Ethiopia — less expensive than pure teff, still deeply traditional.

Mixed grain injera reflects the economic and ecological reality of Ethiopia — teff is expensive, and in many regions and for many families, mixing it with barley, sorghum, or wheat produces a more accessible daily bread. The ratios vary by region and family preference; this recipe uses a balanced approach.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl for fermentation, 30cm non-stick pan or mitad, Lid

  • 1 cup teff flour
  • ½ cup barley flour
  • ½ cup sorghum flour (or wheat flour)
  • 2.5 cups water — room temperature
  • 2 tbsp ersho or fermentation starter
  • ½ tsp salt (optional)

Method

  1. Combine all flours. Add water and ersho. Whisk thoroughly until smooth.
  2. Cover with a cloth. Ferment 2 days at room temperature.
  3. On day 2, the batter should smell sour and have small bubbles.
  4. Thin with a little cold water to slightly thinner than pancake batter.
  5. Cook as per ETH001 — medium heat, covered, never flipped.

Chef Tips

  • The teff is still essential — it provides the structure and fermentation character.
  • More sorghum = slightly sweeter, less sour. More barley = slightly nuttier.
  • Mixed grain injera is slightly lighter in colour than pure teff.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Omitting teff entirely — the result is not injera.

The Science

The mixed grain fermentation produces a less complex lactic acid profile than pure teff — the barley and sorghum starches ferment at slightly different rates, producing a batter with a wider range of organic acid compounds. This creates a less intensely sour but more complex flavour.

Health Benefits

  • Still very high in iron and calcium from teff.
  • Added B vitamins from barley.
  • More affordable nutritious bread.

Cultural Note

Mixed grain injera is the everyday reality of Ethiopian bread making — the practical solution that most families use when pure teff is too expensive. It is just as traditional, just as loved, and made with the same care and fermentation knowledge as pure teff injera.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Room temperature 3 days. Refrigerate 1 week.

Reheating: Damp cloth. Microwave 30 seconds.

Serve with: All Ethiopian stews, Particularly paired with milder dishes to balance the gentler sourness
