🍮 Doolshe Banaaddir

Doolshe Banaaddir

Cardamom creme caramel — the dessert every good restaurant in Mogadishu served, which is why Somalis call it Banadir cake rather than anything French.

Doolshe comes from the Italian dolce and Somalis use it for cake of any kind; Banaadir is the region Mogadishu sits in. Creme caramel became so completely the dessert of the city's restaurants that it took the city's name. It was never everyday food at home — eggs were hard to come by — so it was made for invited guests, and in kitchens with no oven it was cooked in a pot set inside a larger pot of water over charcoal, with embers piled on the lid so the heat came from above as well.

Ingredients

Equipment: 6 ramekins, Small heavy saucepan, Roasting tin, for the bain marie, Aluminium foil

    For the caramel

    • 100 g (1/2 cup) sugar
    • 2 tbsp water

    For the custard

    • 100 g (1/2 cup) granulated sugar
    • 3 large eggs
    • 1 egg yolk
    • Pinch of salt (optional)
    • 500 ml whole milk, hot
    • 1/2 tsp ground cardamom

    Method

    1. Put the caramel sugar and the 2 tbsp water in a small heavy pan.
    2. Cook over high heat, without stirring, until the sugar caramelises to a dark brown.
    3. Pour the caramel into the ramekins and swirl to coat the bases. Work fast — it hardens within seconds. Leave them to cool completely.
    4. For the custard, combine the sugar, whole eggs, egg yolk and salt and beat lightly. Do not whisk air into it.
    5. Temper the mixture with 3 tbsp of the hot milk, whisking as you pour, then stir in the cardamom.
    6. Add the rest of the milk and mix, then pour the custard into the ramekins over the set caramel.
    7. Stand the ramekins in a roasting tin, pour in hot water to halfway up their sides, cover the tin with foil and bake at 177C / 350F for 45 minutes.
    8. Cool, then refrigerate for at least 4 hours.
    9. Run a knife round the inside of each ramekin and turn out onto a plate, so the caramel runs down the sides.

    Chef Tips

    • Temper the eggs with a few spoons of hot milk before the rest goes in. Pour it all in at once and you have sweet scrambled egg.
    • Beat the custard lightly. Air bubbles beaten in now become holes in the set custard.
    • The caramel sets hard in the ramekins and that is correct — it liquefies again during baking and becomes the sauce.
    • For the traditional stovetop method, cook it in a covered pot inside a larger pot of water on low heat for 45 minutes, weighing the lid down and wrapping everything in foil so no water gets in.
    • A cracking sound as the caramel cools is normal, not a fault.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding all the hot milk to the eggs at once, which curdles them.
    • Baking at too high a heat or for too long, which makes the custard grainy and full of holes.
    • Skipping the four hours in the fridge, after which it will not turn out cleanly.
    • Letting the water in the bain marie come more than halfway up the ramekins, so it slops in.

    Health Benefits

    • Eggs and milk give it a useful amount of protein and calcium for a dessert.
    • Naturally gluten-free.
    • Portioned in individual ramekins, which is its own restraint.

    Cultural Note

    That a French dessert ended up named after a Somali region says something about Mogadishu in the years when its restaurants set the standard. Somalis who grew up there order it by that name still, and the cardamom in it is the thing that makes it Somali rather than merely borrowed.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate, covered, up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Serve cold, straight from the fridge.

    Serve with: Somali coffee, Shaah Cadays, Fresh fruit
