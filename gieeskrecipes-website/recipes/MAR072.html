🍯 Chebakia (Griouech / Mkharka, Sesame and Honey Cookies)

Chebakia, griouech, mkharka (شباكية)

Folded, fried sesame-and-spice dough dipped in hot honey scented with orange flower water, until glossy amber.

2hTotal time
1hPrep
45mCook
20Servings
210Calories
HardDifficulty

A stiff but pliable dough of ground toasted sesame, flour, anise, cinnamon, saffron, turmeric, egg, butter, oil, vinegar and a little yeast is rolled thin, cut into rectangles with four cuts and folded into a flower. The cookies are fried slowly to a medium brown, moved straight into hot honey and soaked 5 to 7 minutes until a rich, glossy amber, then cooled for several hours. The page gives a yield of about 65 medium cookies.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor, Rolling pin, Fluted pastry cutter, Large deep frying pan, Large pot for the honey, Slotted spoon

  • 7 oz. unhulled sesame seeds - about 1.5cups
  • 4 cups flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons ground anise
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon saffron threads, crumbled to a powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 pinch mastic or gum arabic drops - mix with 1/4 teaspoon sugar and crush to a powder
  • 1 extra large egg
  • 1/4 cup unsalted butter - melted
  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • 1/4 cup vinegar
  • 1/4 cup orange flower water
  • 1 teaspoon yeast - dissolved in 1/4 cup warm water

For frying and soaking

  • 36 oz. honey - approx.
  • 2 tablespoons orange flower water
  • 6 cups vegetable oil, for frying - approx.
  • 1/2 cup unhulled sesame seeds - toasted, for garnish

Method

  1. Pick through the sesame and toast it until golden: in a skillet over medium heat, or in a 400°F (200°C) oven for 10 to 15 minutes.
  2. Grind 1.5 cups of the toasted sesame in a food processor until powdery, then a little longer until the powder is moist enough to pack.
  3. Mix the ground sesame with the flour, baking powder, salt and spices, then add the egg, butter, oil, vinegar, orange flower water and the dissolved yeast. Mix with your hands into a stiff but pliable dough.
  4. Knead 7 to 8 minutes by hand (4 to 5 with a dough hook). Divide into four mounds, bag them and rest 10 to 15 minutes.
  5. Roll each mound to the thickness of thin cardboard and cut palm-sized rectangles with a fluted cutter. Make four evenly spaced cuts almost the length of each rectangle, leaving five attached strips, then fold into a flower shape.
  6. Heat about an inch of oil in a deep pan over medium-low. At the same time heat the honey almost to boiling. When it is frothy but not bubbling, stir in the 2 tablespoons orange flower water and turn off the heat.
  7. Fry each batch slowly, adjusting the heat, to a medium brown colour; the page says about 10 minutes if the oil is at the right temperature. Too-hot oil colours the outside before the inside crisps.
  8. Lift the cookies out with a slotted spoon straight into the hot honey. Push them under and soak 5 to 7 minutes, until they turn a rich, glossy amber.
  9. Drain for only a few minutes, sprinkle with toasted sesame, and cool for several hours before storing airtight.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: honey frothy but not bubbling; fry to a medium brown; soaked cookies rich, glossy amber.
  • In a very warm kitchen the page says to cut the yeast to 1/2 teaspoon.
  • The yeast is only dissolved and mixed in; the page gives no rising step. The dough rest is NO CUE, TIME ONLY (10 to 15 minutes).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Oil too hot, so the chebakia colour fast but stay soft inside.
  • Boiling the honey hard instead of heating it until frothy.
  • Packing them away before they have cooled for several hours.

Health Benefits

  • Sesame adds minerals and fibre, but the cookies are fried and honey-soaked, so keep portions small.

Cultural Note

The author says griouech is the older term and chebakia the common one, and that chebakia is traditionally eaten with harira in Ramadan, with families making large batches beforehand.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The page says they keep at room temperature for a month or longer, airtight, and freeze well for four or five months.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Harira, Mint tea
