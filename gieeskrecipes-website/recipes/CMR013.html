🌿 Eru Soup (Spinach and Okazi Leaves)

Eru / okazi

Beef, tripe, cow skin or smoked turkey with dried fish, frozen spinach, shredded dried eru leaves, ground crayfish and a cup of palm oil, simmered until a fragrant aroma tells you it is ready.

Adamu uses the name eru for the leaf also called okazi, ukazi, afang and m’fumbua in different regions. Meats are boiled with salt, Maggi and onion until tender, aiming for 2 to 3 cups of stock. Dried chopped eru leaves are soaked 10 to 15 minutes in hot water, rinsed and drained. Meats and fish are combined with at least 2 cups of stock, boiled and simmered 5 minutes; thawed spinach, crayfish and 2 tablespoons of Maggi cook about 10 minutes; the shredded leaves and red palm oil go in for 10 more minutes, with stock or water added if needed. Served with fufu, water fufu or ugali.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 2 pounds (900 g) meat (beef, tripe, cow skin, smoked turkey)
  • Salt to taste (1/2–1 teaspoon per pound of meat)
  • 2 tablespoons (15–16 g) Maggi or bouillon powder
  • 1/4 onion, chopped
  • 1 1/2 pounds (750 g) dried fish (or smoked turkey)
  • 5–6 cups (125–180 g) dried chopped ukazi, afang or eru
  • 2–3 cups (475–700 ml) water
  • 2 pounds (900 g) frozen spinach, thawed
  • 1/2 cup (25 g) ground crayfish
  • 1 cup (240 ml) palm oil
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 1 Scotch bonnet pepper (optional)

Method

  1. Cook the cut beef with salt, Maggi and onion until tender (cow skin takes longer). Use little water, aiming for 2–3 cups of stock. The time is not printed.
  2. Soak the dried leaves in hot water for 10–15 minutes, then rinse and drain.
  3. Combine the meats and fish with at least 2 cups of stock. Bring to a boil, then simmer 5 minutes.
  4. Add the drained, thawed spinach, the crayfish and 2 tablespoons of Maggi. Cook on medium heat about 10 minutes.
  5. Add the shredded eru leaves and the red palm oil. Stir and simmer another 10 minutes, adding stock or water if needed. Adjust the seasoning.
  6. Serve with fufu, water fufu or ugali.

Chef Tips

  • The printed cue is a ‘fragrant aroma’.
  • The author suggests reducing the palm oil to 1/4 cup or replacing half with corn oil if it feels too rich.
  • The page notes the leaf is a threatened plant in some regions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the dried leaves without soaking them.
  • Leaving the water to boil away before the oil goes in.
  • Treating the 2–3 cups of listed water as separate from the stock — the page does not say what it is for.

Health Benefits

  • Leafy greens and fish add minerals and protein; palm oil is rich in fat.

Cultural Note

Adamu says the soup is very popular in Cameroon, where the Bayangi of the South West love it. A second printing (Demand Africa, 6 July 2018) uses 3 cups of palm oil and 2 onions and says ‘red oil’ in the method although its list says palm oil; the two printings are not reconciled.
