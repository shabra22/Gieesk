Casoncelli alla Bergamasca

Casoncelli alla Bergamasca are Bergamo's beloved stuffed pasta pockets, filled with a savory-sweet mix of meat, breadcrumbs, and amaretti, finished with butter, sage, and crisp pancetta.

This dish showcases Lombardy's love of blending sweet and savory — amaretti cookies and sometimes raisins or pear find their way into the meaty filling, a Renaissance-era culinary habit that has endured in this specific corner of the region.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Pasta machine or rolling pin, Round or square cutter, Skillet

  • 1 batch Fresh Egg Pasta Dough

Filling

  • 250 g (9 oz) leftover roasted or braised beef, finely chopped
  • 80 g (1 cup) fresh breadcrumbs
  • 40 g (¼ cup) crushed amaretti cookies
  • 1 egg
  • 40 g (½ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped fresh parsley
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a pinch
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

To serve

  • 100 g (½ cup) butter
  • 100 g (3.5 oz) pancetta, diced
  • Fresh sage leaves
  • Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for serving

Method

  1. Combine the chopped beef, breadcrumbs, amaretti, egg, Parmigiano, parsley, nutmeg, salt, and pepper in a bowl, mixing until well combined and firm enough to hold shape.
  2. Roll the pasta dough thin and cut into squares or rounds about 8cm.
  3. Place a small spoonful of filling in the center of each piece.
  4. Fold into a half-moon or triangle, pressing to seal, then bring the two outer corners together and pinch to form the classic casoncelli shape.
  5. Bring a large pot of salted water to a gentle boil and cook the casoncelli in batches for 3–5 minutes until they float and the pasta is tender.
  6. Meanwhile, melt the butter in a skillet with the pancetta, cooking until the pancetta is crisp.
  7. Add the sage leaves to the butter for the last minute of cooking.
  8. Lift the casoncelli directly into the butter-pancetta mixture, tossing gently to coat.
  9. Serve immediately with grated Parmigiano.

Chef Tips

  • The amaretti cookies add a distinctive sweet note that balances the savory meat — a hallmark of this specific Bergamo tradition.
  • Using leftover roasted meat gives the best depth of flavor, echoing the dish's origins in resourceful cooking.
  • Keep the filling firm rather than wet to make sealing and shaping easier.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Omitting the amaretti, losing the dish's distinctive Lombard character.
  • Overfilling, making the casoncelli difficult to seal properly.
  • Cooking the pancetta too long before adding sage, causing the sage to burn in the residual heat.

Cultural Note

Casoncelli varies noticeably even within Lombardy — the Bergamo style with amaretti differs from the Brescia version, which sometimes includes pear or raisins, each town fiercely proud of its particular recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Uncooked casoncelli freeze well in a single layer for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Cook frozen directly from frozen in boiling water, adding 1–2 extra minutes.

Serve with: A crisp Franciacorta wine, A simple green salad
