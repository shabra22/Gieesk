🍪 Buskud

Buskud — Buskut

Piped coconut biscuits leavened with baker's ammonia as well as baking powder and soda — which is the whole secret, and the reason home versions without it are never crisp enough.

These are Brava biscuits, eaten with morning coffee before school, pressed out through a hand-cranked biscuit press stamped Made in Somalia. What separates the real thing from a merely nice cookie is baker's ammonia, ammonium carbonate, which gasses off completely in the oven and leaves behind a lightness and a crispness that baking powder cannot produce on its own. If you have tried to make these and wondered why they came out short and cakey, that is the missing ingredient.

Ingredients

Equipment: Stand mixer, or a bowl and a strong arm, Biscuit press, or a piping bag with a star nozzle, Baking sheets, Wire rack

  • 200 g (1 cup) granulated sugar
  • 125 ml (1/2 cup, 1 stick) unsalted butter, softened
  • 59 ml (1/4 cup) canola oil
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1.25 ml (1/4 tsp) salt
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) baking powder
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) baking soda
  • 1.25 ml (1/4 tsp) baker's ammonia (ammonium carbonate)
  • 1 envelope (8 g) vanillin sugar
  • 60 g (1/2 cup) fine desiccated coconut
  • 420 g (3 cups) all-purpose flour

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 193C (380F).
  2. Put the sugar, softened butter and oil into a mixer bowl.
  3. Add the eggs.
  4. Add the salt, the baking powder, the baking soda and the baker's ammonia.
  5. Add the vanillin sugar.
  6. Add the desiccated coconut and the flour.
  7. Mix well, until you have a stiff, smooth dough.
  8. Shape with a biscuit press onto baking sheets.
  9. Bake for 12 to 14 minutes, until pale gold at the edges.
  10. Cool on a wire rack — they crisp as they cool.

Chef Tips

  • Baker's ammonia is the secret and there is no real substitute. It is sold as ammonium carbonate or hartshorn, cheap, and it lasts for years. It smells sharply of ammonia in the raw dough and in the oven, and every trace of it gasses off — the baked biscuit has none of it.
  • Do not use baker's ammonia in a thick, soft bake. It only works in thin, dry biscuits where the gas can escape, which is exactly what these are.
  • Both butter and oil, not one or the other. The butter is the flavour and the oil keeps them from going hard.
  • The dough should be stiff enough to hold a piped shape. Too soft and it spreads into blobs; chill it for twenty minutes if that happens.
  • A piping bag with a star nozzle stands in for a press perfectly well. The classic shape is a short ridged length.
  • They crisp on cooling, so pull them when they are pale gold rather than browned.
  • Coconut powder means fine desiccated coconut, not coconut flour and not flakes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving out the baker's ammonia and expecting the same crispness.
  • Being alarmed by the ammonia smell in the oven — it is meant to do that and it leaves no taste.
  • Making the dough too soft, so the piped shapes spread flat.
  • Baking them until browned, after which they go hard rather than crisp.
  • Using coconut flour in place of desiccated coconut, which drinks all the moisture.

Health Benefits

  • Portion-controlled by nature — they are small and one is genuinely one.
  • Coconut supplies a little fibre and manganese.
  • Sugar and butter are the bulk of it, so treat them as biscuits.

Cultural Note

Brava, on the southern Somali coast, has its own language, Chimwiini, and its own baking, and these biscuits belong to it. The detail that carries the most is the biscuit press stamped Made in Somalia — a piece of ordinary household equipment from a manufacturing economy that has largely gone, still doing its job in a diaspora kitchen decades later.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight tin, 2 weeks. They stay crisp if kept dry.

Reheating: Refresh in a low oven for a few minutes if they soften.

Serve with: Qaxwo, Shaah Bigays, Shaah Cadays
