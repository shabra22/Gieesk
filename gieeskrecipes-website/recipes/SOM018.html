🍝 Isbaggeti Somali

Isbaggeti

Somalia's own take on spaghetti, a legacy of Italian colonial influence transformed into something distinctly Somali with a bold, spiced tomato and meat sauce, often eaten with the hands alongside bread.

Isbaggeti reflects a genuinely unusual culinary inheritance from Italy's colonial period in southern Somalia, and rather than remaining a foreign import, it has been fully absorbed into Somali home cooking, seasoned with xawaash and frequently served banquet-style alongside rice and bread rather than as its own separate course.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large saute pan

  • 400 g (14 oz) spaghetti
  • 400 g (14 oz) ground beef
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 2 tsp xawaash spice blend
  • 600 g (1.3 lb) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Fresh cilantro, chopped
  • Salt and chili flakes, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the oil in a large pan and saute the onion until softened.
  2. Add the garlic and ginger, cooking briefly until fragrant.
  3. Add the ground beef and cook, breaking it up, until browned.
  4. Stir in the xawaash spice blend and tomato paste, cooking for another minute.
  5. Add the crushed tomatoes and chili flakes, and simmer for 20-25 minutes until thickened.
  6. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the spaghetti until al dente.
  7. Drain the pasta and toss with the sauce, or serve the sauce spooned generously over the top.
  8. Finish with fresh cilantro and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Xawaash spice blend is what gives this dish its distinctly Somali character, setting it apart from an ordinary Italian-style ragu.
  • This dish is often served on a shared platter alongside other dishes rather than as its own standalone course.
  • Adjust the chili to taste - Somali cooking often includes a noticeable, though not overwhelming, heat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the xawaash spice blend, which is essential to the dish distinctly Somali flavor profile.
  • Overcooking the pasta, losing the pleasant bite that balances the rich sauce.
  • Under-seasoning the sauce, which needs confident spicing to stand out.

Cultural Note

Isbaggeti reflects Italy's colonial-era influence on southern Somalia, fully absorbed and transformed into a distinctly Somali dish through the use of xawaash spice and its place on the shared family table.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The sauce keeps refrigerated for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat the sauce gently on the stovetop; cook fresh pasta just before serving.

Serve with: Bariis Cad (plain rice), Bread, Basbaas
