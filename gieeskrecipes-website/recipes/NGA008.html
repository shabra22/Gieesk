🥬 Efo Riro

Efo riro — stirred leaf

Bell pepper and scotch bonnet fried fifteen to twenty minutes until the pepper dries out and reduces by almost half — and no tomatoes, which the author calls an absolute rule.

Efo riro means stirred leaf, and this is the version that defines itself by what it leaves out. The rule is stated flatly: using tomatoes for efo riro is an absolute no. What you get instead is a pepper base — tatashe and rodo blended coarse — fried hard for fifteen to twenty minutes until it has lost its water and reduced by half, so the finished soup tastes of pepper and palm oil rather than of stew. The greens go in raw at the very end and the pot comes off the heat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Colander

  • 2 bunches ugwu leaves (or spinach or soko)
  • 3 medium or 2 big bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 1 1/2 scotch bonnet (rodo)
  • 1/2 cup palm oil
  • 1 1/2 medium red onions
  • Assorted meats of your choice — shaki, kpomo, cow leg, smoked turkey or beef
  • Dried stockfish (panla)
  • Smoked catfish
  • 1 tbsp locust beans (iru)
  • 1/2 cup raw or cooked prawns (optional)
  • 1/4 cup smoked prawns (optional)
  • 2 bouillon cubes
  • 3 tbsp ground crayfish
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Season the meats with sliced onions and boil on medium heat, tougher cuts first, then the softer ones.
  2. Just before they are fully cooked, add the stockfish and boil until tender. Set aside and keep the stock.
  3. Wash the vegetables thoroughly with hot water to remove dirt, avoiding prolonged soaking so the nutrients stay in. Slice or chop.
  4. Blend the scotch bonnets, bell peppers and one onion coarsely — not smooth.
  5. Heat the palm oil on medium 2 minutes.
  6. Add the remaining onions and fry until fragrant.
  7. Add the locust beans and fry 1 more minute to release the flavour.
  8. Add the blended pepper and the two bouillon cubes. Fry 15 to 20 minutes, until the pepper dries out and reduces by almost half.
  9. Add one cup of the stock, or water, and boil 2 to 3 minutes.
  10. Add the assorted meats, prawns, crayfish, stockfish and catfish. Season, and cook 10 minutes further.
  11. Add the washed vegetables and combine.
  12. Take it off the heat and let it simmer 5 minutes on the residual heat.

Chef Tips

  • No tomatoes. The author's rule, as printed: 'A major rule is; using tomatoes for Efo Riro is an absolute No No!' For the opposing position from another named Nigerian authority, see Efo Riro with Shoko Leaf.
  • Fifteen to twenty minutes on the pepper is not optional and it is longer than feels right. You are waiting for it to dry out and halve in volume.
  • Blend the peppers coarse. Smooth gives you a sauce; coarse gives you the texture efo riro is supposed to have.
  • Wash the greens in hot water but do not soak them. She is explicit that prolonged soaking strips the nutrients.
  • The greens go in raw at the end and the pot comes off the stove. Five minutes of residual heat is all they get.
  • Do not salt if you are using seasoned meat stock. Taste first — between the stock, two cubes and the crayfish there is a lot of salt already in play.
  • Ugwu is the author's choice, with spinach or soko permitted. The strictest reading of the dish uses shoko or tete; Yewande Komolafe glosses efo riro as 'Stewed Amaranth Greens', which points at tete specifically.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding tomatoes.
  • Cutting the pepper fry short.
  • Blending the peppers smooth.
  • Soaking the greens.
  • Boiling the soup after the vegetables go in.

Health Benefits

  • Fluted pumpkin leaf is a strong source of iron, calcium and vitamins A and C.
  • Very low in carbohydrate for a main dish.
  • Red palm oil supplies carotenoids and vitamin E.
  • High in protein from the assorted meats, fish and prawns.

Cultural Note

Efo riro translates as stirred leaf, and the name describes the action rather than the ingredients — which is why the argument about tomatoes is possible at all. It is one of the most widely cooked soups in Nigeria and among the most contested in detail, because the thing that makes it efo riro is a technique, and every household fills the technique with slightly different things.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat. The greens are already at their limit, so warm rather than cook.

Serve with: Iyan, Amala, Eko
