🍠 Yam Porridge with Smoked Fish

Mpotompoto

Four hundred grams of yam to seven hundred millilitres of water with two smoked fish and five green chillies — spare, and its method is video-only.

The plainest documented mpotompoto: four hundred grams of cubed yam, seven hundred millilitres of water, two large smoked fish, one onion, five green chillies and salt. That is the whole list. Garnished with six whole green chillies and a sliced onion. It makes a useful counterpoint to the elaborate versions — no bell pepper, no tomato, no crayfish, no bouillon — just yam, water, smoked fish and heat. The author's one printed tip is about the oil: ensure your palm oil is sustainably produced, and it is best to use zomi for this recipe.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a lid, Knife, Wooden spoon

  • 400 g yam, peeled and cubed
  • 700 ml water
  • 2 large smoked fish
  • 1 large onion
  • 5 green chillies
  • Salt, to taste

Garnish

  • 6 whole green chillies
  • 1 onion, sliced

Noted in the author's tip but not in the ingredient list

  • Zomi palm oil

Method

    The published method for this recipe is video-only. No written steps exist in the source.

    1. Peel and cube the yam.
    2. Bring the water to the boil with the onion and chillies.
    3. Add the yam and simmer until tender.
    4. Flake in the smoked fish.
    5. Mash part of the yam to thicken the porridge, leaving some cubes whole.
    6. Season with salt and finish with zomi.
    7. Garnish with the whole green chillies and sliced onion.

    The order and timings above are inferred from the other documented mpotompoto, not from this source.

      Chef Tips

      • Be aware the method here is reconstructed. The source publishes a full ingredient list but its instructions are in a video, so the step order follows the other documented mpotompoto rather than this cook's own sequence.
      • Seven hundred millilitres of water to four hundred grams of yam is the only documented liquid ratio for this dish, and it is the reason this sparse entry is worth having.
      • Her tip as printed: ensure your palm oil is sustainably produced, and it is best to use zomi for this recipe. Note the oil is not in her ingredient list at all.
      • Eleven green chillies in total — five in the pot, six as garnish. That is a lot, and they are mild green chillies rather than scotch bonnet.
      • Whole chillies as a garnish is an Ewe habit and they are eaten alongside rather than stirred in.
      • No tomato, no bell pepper, no crayfish, no bouillon. This is yam, water, fish and heat, and it is a genuinely different dish from the elaborate version at Mpotompoto.
      • Two large smoked fish for four servings makes this very fish-forward. The smoke is the seasoning.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Treating the reconstructed step order as the author's own.
      • Leaving out the zomi because it is not in the ingredient list.
      • Mashing all the yam.
      • Using scotch bonnet in place of mild green chillies.
      • Adding tomato, which belongs to the other version.

      Cultural Note

      A list this short tells you the dish is everyday food rather than company food — yam, water, whatever fish is hanging in the kitchen, and chillies on the side for whoever wants them. The whole green chillies set beside the bowl rather than cooked in is a habit you see across the Volta Region.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

      Reheating: Low heat with water to loosen.

      Serve with: Fried plantain, Avocado, Boiled egg
