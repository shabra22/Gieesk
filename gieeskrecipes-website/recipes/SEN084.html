🥥 Sombi, Senegalese Coconut Rice Pudding

Sombi

Four cups of coconut milk with shredded coconut, brown sugar, salt and a vanilla bean reduced slightly, then two cups of cooked rice that break down and thicken it — ‘more like a creamy porridge than a pudding’.

15 minTotal time

The 2012 Saveur printing. Coconut milk, unsweetened shredded coconut, packed brown sugar, salt and a vanilla bean with its seeds are brought to a boil in a 4-quart saucepan over medium heat and cooked, stirring often, about 5 minutes until reduced slightly. Cooked white rice goes in and cooks, stirring, about 5 minutes until it breaks down and thickens the mixture slightly. Off the heat, fresh lime juice is stirred in, and it is served hot. The headnote calls it ‘more like a creamy porridge than a pudding’, eaten for breakfast or as an afternoon snack in Senegal.

Ingredients

Equipment: 4-quart saucepan

  • 4 cups coconut milk
  • 1 cup unsweetened shredded coconut, fresh or dried
  • 1/2 cup packed light or dark brown sugar
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 vanilla bean, seeds scraped and reserved
  • 2 cups cooked white rice
  • 2 tbsp fresh lime juice

Method

  1. Bring the coconut milk, coconut, sugar, salt and the vanilla bean with its seeds to a boil in a 4-quart saucepan over medium heat and cook, stirring often, until reduced slightly, about 5 minutes.
  2. Add the rice and cook, stirring, until the rice breaks down and thickens the mixture slightly, about 5 minutes.
  3. Remove from the heat, stir in the lime juice and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Two printed cues: ‘reduced slightly’ and ‘until rice breaks down and thickens’.
  • It is deliberately porridge-like, not set.
  • Lime is added off the heat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling hard, which can split the coconut milk.
  • Over-reducing before the rice goes in.
  • Adding lime while on the boil.

Cultural Note

SAVEUR’s headnote: ‘More like a creamy porridge than a pudding in consistency, this sweet dish is commonly eaten for breakfast or as an afternoon snack in Senegal.’ Published 30 May 2012.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Warm with a splash of coconut milk.

Serve with: Fresh mango, Sweet tea
