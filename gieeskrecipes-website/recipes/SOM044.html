🫓 Cambaabur

Cambaabur

Anjero's spiced cousin — a fermented batter ground with fennel, cumin and nigella seed, turmeric, garlic and onion, cooked into flatbreads then sugared, drizzled with ghee and eaten with yogurt on the morning of Eid.

Cambaabur is what the north makes for Eid. The batter starts like anjero and then goes somewhere else entirely: whole seeds ground fresh, turmeric for colour, garlic and onion blended straight in. The result is savoury, and then — this is the part that surprises people — it is sprinkled with sugar and served with yogurt, so one plate carries spice, sweetness and sourness at the same time. The spices change from region to region; this is the common set.

Ingredients

Equipment: Spice grinder, Blender, Non-stick pan with a lid

    The spices, ground fresh

    • 1/2 tsp fennel seeds
    • 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
    • 1/2 tsp nigella seeds (black seeds)
    • 1/8 tsp ground turmeric

    The batter

    • 95 g (1/2 cup) coarse semolina
    • 240 ml (1 cup) water, for blending
    • 1 clove garlic
    • 1/4 onion
    • 1 tsp instant dry yeast
    • 1/2 tsp salt
    • 1 tbsp granulated sugar
    • 280 g (2 cups) self-raising flour
    • 475 ml (2 cups) water

    To serve

    • Granulated sugar, for sprinkling
    • Melted ghee, for drizzling
    • Plain yogurt

    Method

    1. Grind the fennel, cumin and nigella seeds together to a powder.
    2. Put the semolina, 1 cup of water, the garlic clove and the quarter onion in a blender and blend well.
    3. Add the ground spices, the turmeric, the yeast, salt, sugar, self-raising flour and the remaining 2 cups of water.
    4. Blend again until completely smooth.
    5. Leave the batter to ferment for 2 hours, or longer if you have the time — longer is better.
    6. Heat a non-stick pan over medium heat. Pour in enough batter for the pan and stop short of the edges, so the rim does not dry out.
    7. Cover and cook without turning until the surface of the batter has dried right through and the holes have set. Lift out and repeat.
    8. Sprinkle each cambaabur with sugar, drizzle with melted ghee, and serve with a bowl of plain yogurt alongside.

    Chef Tips

    • Do not let the batter reach the sides of the pan. Batter touching the hot rim dries into a brittle edge.
    • Grind the seeds yourself, just before making the batter. This is a dish whose whole character is the spice, and pre-ground fennel and cumin have very little left.
    • Self-raising flour plus instant yeast is the shortcut that brings the fermentation down to two hours. A traditional overnight ferment with plain flour tastes better if you have the time.
    • Keep a little batter back. Stirred into the next batch a day or two later it gives the sourness a proper ferment would have.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Turning it. Cambaabur is cooked on one side only, like anjero — flip it and you lose the set holes that are the point of the texture.
    • Cooking it uncovered, so the top stays wet while the base browns.
    • Cutting the fermentation short, which leaves it bland and flat rather than sour and open.
    • Serving it savoury. The sugar and the yogurt are not optional extras — they are half the dish.

    Health Benefits

    • Fennel, cumin and nigella seeds are all traditionally used to settle digestion.
    • The fermentation makes the batter more digestible and develops B vitamins.
    • Served with yogurt, it supplies protein and live cultures alongside the bread.

    Cultural Note

    Cambaabur belongs to Eid mornings in northern Somalia, and is also strongly associated with Djibouti and with Awdal — Saylac and Borama in particular. The spice mix is not fixed: which seeds go in is one of the things that tells you whose kitchen you are eating in.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best the day it is made. Keeps a day wrapped; the batter keeps 2 days in the fridge.

    Reheating: Warm briefly in a dry pan. Sugar and ghee go on after reheating.

    Serve with: Plain yogurt, Shaah Cadays, Xalwo
