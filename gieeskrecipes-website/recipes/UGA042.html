🫘 Ugandan Brown Beans Stew

Bijanjalo

Two kilos of Roscoco beans boiled forty-five minutes before the bicarbonate goes in — not at the start — then salted, with a separately built tomato and curry sauce folded through at the end.

This is beans at scale: two kilos, five litres of water, twenty servings, which is the kind of pot a Ugandan household or a church function actually cooks. Two things in the method are worth holding on to. The bicarbonate of soda goes in after forty-five minutes, not at the beginning, and the salt goes in after that — both late, because salt and alkali added early affect how beans soften. And the tomato sauce is built in a separate pan and added at the end rather than cooked with the beans.

Ingredients

Equipment: Very large saucepan, Second pan, for the sauce, Wooden spoon

  • 2 kg dry Roscoco beans
  • 2 x 400 g tins chopped tomatoes, or fresh plum tomatoes
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) curry powder
  • 4 medium onions, chopped
  • 45 ml (3 tbsp) salt
  • 1.25 ml (1/4 tsp) sodium bicarbonate
  • 5 litres water

In the method but not the printed ingredient list

  • 30 to 45 ml (2 to 3 tbsp) cooking oil
  • Black pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Empty the beans into a large saucepan.
  2. Wash them thoroughly with cold water until the water runs clear.
  3. Add cold water to cover the beans completely.

Cooking the beans

  1. Put the pan on the stove and let it boil for 45 minutes.
  2. Add the sodium bicarbonate.
  3. Cook a further 15 minutes.
  4. Test for softness by pressing one bean with your thumb.
  5. Add the salt and cook a further 15 minutes.

The tomato sauce

  1. Heat the cooking oil in a separate pan.
  2. Add the onions and fry until golden brown.
  3. Add the curry powder and mix.
  4. Add the tomatoes immediately, so the curry powder does not burn.
  5. Season with black pepper.
  6. Cook 15 minutes, or until the sauce thickens.

Bringing it together

  1. Add the sauce to the beans, mix gently, and cook a further 15 minutes.
  2. Serve with rice, sweet potatoes or chapati.

Chef Tips

  • The bicarbonate goes in at forty-five minutes, not at the start. That is unusual enough to be worth noting and it is how the recipe is printed.
  • Salt goes in last of all, after the softness test. Salted early, beans can stay stubborn.
  • Add the tomatoes immediately after the curry powder. A quarter-minute too long and the spice burns, which you cannot undo.
  • Wash the beans until the water runs clear. Two kilos of unwashed beans carries a surprising amount of dust.
  • Build the sauce separately and fold it in. Cooked together from the start the tomatoes go dull.
  • The thumb-press test is the real measure of doneness. Times vary with how old your beans are.
  • The printed ingredient list omits the cooking oil and black pepper that its own method calls for — the amounts given here are a sensible minimum rather than the source's own.
  • Twenty servings. Quarter it for a household pot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the bicarbonate at the start.
  • Salting early.
  • Burning the curry powder before the tomatoes go in.
  • Cooking the tomatoes with the beans from the beginning.
  • Going by the clock rather than the thumb test.

Health Benefits

  • Beans are high in plant protein, fibre, folate, iron and potassium.
  • Very low in fat and entirely plant-based.
  • Tomatoes add lycopene and vitamin C.
  • One of the cheapest complete-protein meals available when eaten with a grain.

Cultural Note

Beans are the default Ugandan protein and the quantity here tells you how they are cooked in practice — once, in bulk, and eaten across several days with whatever starch is going. Roscoco is the speckled bean most common in the market.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days. Freezes well for 3 months.

Reheating: Warm with a splash of water. It thickens considerably when cold.

Serve with: Boiled Sweet Potatoes, Chapati, Posho
