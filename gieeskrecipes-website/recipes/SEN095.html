🐟 Domoda au Poisson, Sauce Béchamel Sénégalaise

Domoda au poisson

Fried fish soaked two minutes in a tomato sauce built from oil, tomato paste, cherry tomatoes and a litre of water, enriched with dried fish and yeet and thickened with flour diluted in half a glass of water — a Senegalese ‘béchamel’ made without milk.

The Slow Food and FAO booklet ‘Sénégal: de la terre à la table’ credits this recipe to chef Bineta Diallo Dioh and writes it for 10 people. Ten medium fish are fried; tomato paste and crushed cherry tomatoes are cooked a minute in 250 ml of oil; a litre of water goes in with vegetables of choice, dried fish and yeet; when the vegetables are cooked the fried fish go in to soak for two minutes; and the sauce is thickened with 30 g of wheat flour diluted in half a glass of water. It is served over white rice, which the booklet names niangkatang. Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Frying pan

  • 10 medium fish
  • 250 ml oil
  • 150 g cherry tomatoes
  • 100 g tomato paste (concentré de tomate)
  • 1 onion
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 1 spoonful vinegar or lemon juice, to your taste
  • 1 litre water
  • Vegetables of your choice — quantity not printed
  • 30 g wheat flour
  • 25 g dried fish
  • 25 g yeet (fermented dried sea snail, Yeet, Fermented Dried Sea Snail)
  • Salt, pepper and chilli (optional) — quantities not printed

Method

  1. Fry the fish. The method and time for the frying are not recorded in the research notes.
  2. Cook the tomato paste and the crushed cherry tomatoes in the oil for one minute. Where the onion and garlic are cooked in relation to this step is not recorded.
  3. Add the litre of water and the vegetables of your choice, with the dried fish and the yeet. The order and timing of these additions are not recorded.
  4. When the vegetables are cooked, put the fried fish in to soak in the sauce for 2 minutes.
  5. Thicken with the flour diluted in half a glass of water. No temperature is printed.
  6. Serve over white rice (the booklet names it niangkatang).

Chef Tips

  • The one quoted cue is that the fried fish go into the sauce only ‘when the vegetables are cooked’.
  • The booklet makes the sauce for 10 people; scale down with care because the flour and dried fish are small fixed quantities.
  • Dissolve the flour in the half glass of water before adding it, so it does not clump.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the fried fish in the sauce for longer than 2 minutes.
  • Adding the flour dry.
  • Skipping the vinegar or lemon.

Cultural Note

‘Béchamel sénégalaise’ is the booklet’s own name for the flour-thickened tomato sauce; it contains no milk or butter. Compare domoda with peanut butter, Domoda with Fried Fish.

Storage & Serving

Storage: No storage time is printed.

Reheating: Reheat gently; the page prints no instructions.

Serve with: White rice, Fried fish
