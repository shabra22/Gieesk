🍽️ Ethiopian Chicken Celebration Platter

ዶሮ ድስት — Doro Dist

Ethiopian Chicken Celebration Platter — Doro Wat (ETH061), Alicha Doro (ETH062), Doro Tibs (ETH063), and Doro Firfir (ETH069) assembled on a large communal injera. The complete chicken feast platter of the Ethiopian celebration — every chicken preparation in the tradition on one magnificent mesob.

The Ethiopian chicken celebration platter is the fullest expression of what the chicken chapter of Ethiopian cooking can produce — the red Doro Wat and the golden Alicha Doro providing heat and gentleness, the tibs providing immediacy, and the firfir providing morning-after comfort — all on one large injera.

Ingredients

Equipment: Multiple heavy pots, Large mesob for serving

    Assemble from individual recipes:

    • Doro Wat — the red centrepiece with eggs
    • Alicha Doro — the golden mild stew with eggs
    • Doro Tibs — the fast sauté
    • Doro Firfir — made from leftover Doro Wat
    • Ayib — mound alongside
    • Gomen — dark greens
    • Tikel Gomen — cabbage
    • Large injera as the base

    Method

    1. Day before: make Doro Wat (ETH061) and Alicha Doro (ETH062). These improve overnight.
    2. Morning of celebration: make Doro Tibs and prepare vegetables.
    3. 30 minutes before: warm the wats. Make Doro Firfir from leftover Doro Wat.
    4. Assemble the mesob: large injera as the base, Doro Wat on one side, Alicha Doro on the other, tibs in the centre, firfir at the edges, ayib mounded, greens between the wats.
    5. Rolled injera in a basket alongside.

    Chef Tips

    • The two great wats (Doro Wat and Alicha Doro) anchor the platter — everything else is in support.
    • The tibs is made last, served immediately hot.
    • The firfir is the surprise — made from leftovers of the wats, it is its own celebration.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not making the wats the day before.
    • Serving tibs before it is ready.

    Cultural Note

    The Ethiopian chicken celebration platter is Doro Wat in all its forms — the same chicken, the same spice tradition, expressed as red heat and golden mildness, as fast sauté and as injera scramble. Together on one injera, they are the complete expression of what Ethiopian chicken cooking can be.
