Sagne e Fagioli

A hearty peasant dish from Basilicata pairing hand-torn wide noodles with slow-cooked borlotti beans, tomato, and a soffritto base, warming and thick enough to eat with a fork.

Sagne are simple, rustic strips of pasta torn or cut by hand, embodying the resourceful cooking of the Basilicata and Calabria regions, where this dish traditionally stretched a small amount of flour into a filling meal for a large family using beans as the primary source of protein.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Large pot, Knife

  • 350 g (2⅔ cups) durum wheat flour
  • 160 ml (⅔ cup) warm water
  • Pinch of salt
  • 400 g (2 cups) dried borlotti beans, soaked overnight
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 carrot, finely chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, finely chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus more for serving
  • Chili flakes, to taste
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Drain the soaked beans and simmer in fresh water with the bay leaf until tender, about 1 hour; drain, reserving some cooking liquid.
  2. Meanwhile, make the dough by combining flour, water, and salt into a firm, smooth dough. Rest, covered, for 20 minutes.
  3. Roll the dough out thin and cut or tear it by hand into irregular wide strips, roughly 2cm across.
  4. In a large pot, warm the olive oil and sauté the onion, carrot, and celery until softened, then add the garlic and chili flakes and cook briefly.
  5. Add the crushed tomatoes and cooked beans along with a ladle of the bean cooking liquid, and simmer for 20 minutes to let the flavors meld.
  6. Bring a separate pot of salted water to a boil and cook the sagne for 3–4 minutes until tender.
  7. Drain the pasta and stir it directly into the bean sauce, adding more bean liquid if needed to loosen.
  8. Simmer everything together for a couple more minutes, season to taste, and serve with a drizzle of olive oil.

Chef Tips

  • Tearing the pasta by hand rather than cutting it evenly is traditional and gives the dish its characteristic rustic look.
  • Save plenty of bean cooking liquid — it thickens and flavors the final dish far better than plain water.
  • This dish should be thick and stew-like, closer to a hearty soup than a saucy pasta.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using canned beans without developing their own flavorful cooking liquid, which leaves the dish tasting flat.
  • Rolling the pasta too uniformly — the rustic, torn texture is part of the dish's character.
  • Undercooking the beans, which should be fully soft and starting to break down into the sauce.

Health Benefits

  • Borlotti beans provide plant-based protein and fiber.
  • A naturally vegan, dairy-free dish rich in complex carbohydrates.

Cultural Note

Sagne e fagioli is a classic of cucina povera from Basilicata and neighboring Calabria, historically eaten during Lent and other meat-free periods when beans provided essential protein.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; the dish thickens further and is excellent the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding a little water or stock to loosen as needed.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A drizzle of good olive oil at the table
