Tortellini in Brodo

Tortellini in Brodo are tiny, hand-folded pasta rings filled with a rich meat mixture, served simply in a clear, deeply savory capon or beef broth — a beloved Christmas and special-occasion dish from Bologna and Modena.

Legend has it the tortellini's navel-like shape was inspired by the goddess Venus, though its real origin lies simply in generations of Emilia-Romagna cooks perfecting the fold. The dish's soul is equally in the broth — traditionally simmered for hours with capon, beef, and aromatics.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small skillet, Food processor, Pasta machine or rolling pin, Small round cutter

  • 1 batch Fresh Egg Pasta Dough

Filling

  • 150 g (5 oz) pork loin, finely minced or ground
  • 100 g (3.5 oz) prosciutto, finely minced
  • 100 g (3.5 oz) mortadella, finely minced
  • 100 g (1¼ cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1 egg
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a pinch
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Broth

  • 2 L (8 cups) good-quality beef or capon stock, well seasoned
  • Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for serving

Method

  1. Cook the pork loin in a dry skillet until just cooked through, then let cool.
  2. Pulse the cooked pork, prosciutto, and mortadella in a food processor until finely and evenly ground (not pureed).
  3. Mix in the Parmigiano, egg, nutmeg, salt, and pepper until well combined. Chill for at least 1 hour, or overnight, for the best texture and easiest handling.
  4. Roll the pasta dough very thin. Cut into small squares, about 3–4cm.
  5. Place a small dab of filling in the center of each square.
  6. Fold each square into a triangle, pressing to seal tightly around the filling.
  7. Wrap the triangle around your finger, pinching the two bottom corners together to form the classic ring shape, folding the top edge back slightly.
  8. Repeat with all the pasta, keeping finished tortellini lightly floured on a tray so they don't stick.
  9. Bring the stock to a gentle simmer in a large pot.
  10. Cook the tortellini directly in the simmering broth for 3–4 minutes until they float and the pasta is tender.
  11. Ladle the tortellini and broth into bowls and serve immediately with grated Parmigiano.

Chef Tips

  • Chilling the filling makes it far easier to portion and roll neatly.
  • A good, deeply flavorful homemade stock makes all the difference in this simple dish — there is nowhere for a weak broth to hide.
  • Work in small batches to prevent the thin pasta squares from drying out before shaping.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a weak or store-bought stock, which undermines the whole dish since the broth is a central component, not just a cooking liquid.
  • Rolling the pasta too thick, resulting in a doughy rather than delicate bite.
  • Not sealing the tortellini tightly enough, causing them to burst open in the broth.

Cultural Note

Tortellini in Brodo is a treasured Christmas Eve and Christmas Day tradition throughout Emilia-Romagna, often made in large family gatherings where everyone helps with the painstaking folding.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Uncooked tortellini freeze well for up to 2 months; the broth keeps refrigerated for up to 4 days.

Reheating: Cook frozen tortellini directly in simmering broth, adding 1–2 extra minutes.

Serve with: A crisp Lambrusco, Nothing else — this dish is traditionally served unadorned
