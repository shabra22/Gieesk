🌰 Groundnut Soup with Kpakpo Shito

Nkatenkwan

The same cook's other printing — eight green kpakpo shito instead of two scotch bonnets, half a litre of ready-made chalé sauce, and up to 200 g of peanut butter.

The same author's groundnut soup appeared in a magazine profile in a noticeably different form, and the differences are worth having side by side. Two kilos of mixed bone-in chicken rather than two 850 g packs, five hundred millilitres of uncooked chalé sauce measured in rather than blended fresh, up to two hundred grams of peanut butter rather than a hundred and fifty, a full tablespoon of extra-hot chilli powder rather than half a teaspoon, and eight green kpakpo shito chillies where the other version uses one or two scotch bonnets. The method and the endpoint are identical.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Blender, Skimming spoon, Grater

  • 2 kg mixed bone-in chicken pieces
  • 500-750 ml water or good-quality chicken stock
  • 1 onion, finely diced
  • 5 cm fresh root ginger, grated
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 8 green kpakpo shito chillies, or 1-2 scotch bonnet
  • 1 tbsp extra-hot chilli powder
  • 1 tbsp curry powder
  • 2 tsp sea salt
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 500 ml uncooked chalé sauce
  • 100-200 g organic peanut butter
  • 1 red scotch bonnet chilli, pierced

To garnish

  • 3 tbsp crushed roasted peanuts
  • 2-3 spring onions, sliced julienne

Method

  1. Simmer the chicken in the stock with the onion, ginger, garlic, chillies, chilli powder, curry powder, salt and pepper for 25 minutes, until the juices run clear, skimming the froth.
  2. Stir in the 500 ml of uncooked chalé sauce.
  3. Add the peanut butter gradually, letting each addition dissolve.
  4. Drop in the pierced red scotch bonnet.
  5. Cook 45 minutes to 1 hour on low, stirring regularly, until the peanut oil separates on top.
  6. Check that the meat is falling from the bone and the consistency is soupy.
  7. Garnish with the crushed peanuts and julienned spring onions.
  8. Serve over fufu.

Chef Tips

  • Eight kpakpo shito is a lot of chilli by volume but they are aromatic rather than brutal — this is the more fragrant of the two printings, not simply the hotter one.
  • A full tablespoon of extra-hot chilli powder, against half a teaspoon to a teaspoon in the other version. That is a sixfold difference from one cook, so taste as you go rather than trusting either figure blindly.
  • Five hundred millilitres of chalé sauce measured in, rather than blended fresh into the pot. If you keep chalé sauce in the fridge, this is the quicker route — see Chalé Sauce.
  • Up to 200 g of peanut butter makes a distinctly thicker soup than the 150 g maximum of the other printing.
  • Oil separating on top, same as every other documented version. It is the one thing nobody disagrees about.
  • Note the garnish unit here is tablespoons of crushed peanuts, where the magazine printing says teaspoons. Tablespoons is the sensible reading.
  • Two kilos of bone-in chicken means real bones — the collagen is what gives this soup its body, so skinless thighs alone will leave it thin.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Treating this and the magazine version as the same recipe.
  • A tablespoon of extra-hot chilli powder without tasting first.
  • Boneless chicken.
  • Peanut butter all at once.
  • Serving before the oil breaks.

Health Benefits

  • Bone-in chicken yields collagen and minerals a boneless cut does not.
  • Peanuts provide monounsaturated fat, niacin and vitamin E.
  • Very high in protein.
  • Kpakpo shito and scotch bonnet both supply capsaicin and vitamin C.

Cultural Note

Having two printings of one cook's signature soup, differing in nearly every measurement, is a useful reminder that a recipe is a snapshot of a dish rather than its definition. The kpakpo shito in this version is the more Ghanaian choice — it is the chilli you actually find in Accra markets, and the scotch bonnet is the substitution offered to readers who cannot get it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Slowly, loosening with just-boiled water.

Serve with: Fufu, Omo Tuo, Rice
