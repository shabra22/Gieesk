🫙 Tanzanian Coastal Mezze

Meza ya Pwani ya Tanzania

Tanzanian Coastal Mezze — a sharing board of miniature coastal Tanzanian preparations. Zanzibar-spiced hummus, bhajia with chatni ya Nazi, mini mishkaki, avocado kachumbari, cassava chips with pili pili mayo, and warm chapati triangles. The Tanzanian coastal kitchen as a sharing board — the finest starter in modern Tanzanian restaurants.

The Tanzanian coastal mezze is the modern restaurant's most sophisticated interpretation of Zanzibar's street food tradition — each element drawn from the island's food culture, miniaturised and refined, and assembled on a beautiful wooden board. It is the Forodhani night market translated to fine dining.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wooden board or mezze plate, Multiple small bowls, Ramekins

    Zanzibar Hummus

    • 1 tin chickpeas
    • 3 tbsp tahini
    • 2 garlic cloves
    • 3 tbsp lemon juice
    • 1 tsp ground cumin
    • ½ tsp cardamom
    • 2 tbsp olive oil
    • ½ tsp smoked paprika for topping

    Mini Bhajia (TZA133) — 8 small pieces

      Mini Mishkaki (TZA172) — 4 small skewers

        Avocado Kachumbari (TZA108 + 1 diced avocado)

          Cassava Chips (TZA174) — 1 portion

            Chatni ya Nazi (TZA118)

              Pili Pili Mayo (4 tbsp mayo + 2 tbsp pili pili)

                2 warm chapatis (TZA004) — cut into triangles

                  Method

                  1. Zanzibar hummus: blend chickpeas, tahini, garlic, lemon, cumin, and cardamom until smooth. Drizzle olive oil. Dust paprika.
                  2. Prepare mini bhajia from TZA133.
                  3. Prepare mini mishkaki from TZA172.
                  4. Make avocado kachumbari: combine kachumbari with diced avocado.
                  5. Prepare cassava chips from TZA174.
                  6. Make all sauces.
                  7. Warm chapatis. Cut into triangles.
                  8. Board assembly: hummus in a bowl (centre). Bhajia, mishkaki, chips arranged around. Sauces in ramekins. Chapati triangles upright. Avocado kachumbari in a bowl.

                  Chef Tips

                  • The cardamom in the hummus is the Zanzibar signature — essential.
                  • Everything must be hot when assembled except the hummus and kachumbari.
                  • Build height on the board — different textures at different heights is more visually engaging.
                  • Serve with extra lime wedges — acidity lifts everything on the board.

                  Common Mistakes to Avoid

                  • Cold fried items.
                  • Flat board presentation — add height.
                  • Missing the lime wedges.

                  The Science

                  The Zanzibar hummus adds cardamom (1,8-cineole) to the classic chickpea preparation — this aromatic compound is fat-soluble and dissolves into the tahini's sesame oil, distributing evenly through the hummus. At the serving temperature (room temperature), the 1,8-cineole volatilises, producing the characteristic Zanzibar fragrance that differentiates this hummus immediately.

                  Cultural Note

                  The Tanzanian coastal mezze is the dish that Tanzania's best restaurants serve to international guests to demonstrate what the country's food culture is — diverse, proud, rooted in tradition, and executed with modern confidence. It is Tanzania saying 'sit down, let us show you who we are'.

                  Storage & Serving

                  Storage: Components separately. Assemble to order.

                  Reheating: Reheat fried items. Hummus and kachumbari served at room temperature.
