🥕 Thiou with Meat and Vegetables

Thiou à la viande

A tomato-red meat and vegetable stew, with turnips and carrots going in before the sweet potatoes, thickened with a splash of vinegar — served over separately boiled white rice.

1h 50mTotal time
30mPrep
1h 20mCook
6Servings
820Calories
MediumDifficulty

Thiou is the Senegalese stew you eat with rice, not a rice dish: the rice cooks separately and the sauce goes on top. Meat is browned in hot oil, onions are added and left until they turn pink, tomato concentrate and seeded fresh tomatoes cook five minutes, and a litre of water goes in for 25 minutes. Turnips and carrots go in next, and when they are almost cooked the potatoes and sweet potatoes follow with the remaining onions and garlic. A little pepper and vinegar are added and the sauce is left to thicken. The page’s tip is a small piece of sugar.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Separate pot for the rice

  • 1 kg rice
  • 1 kg meat — kind not specified in the list
  • 2 carrots
  • 2 potatoes
  • 2 turnips
  • 2 sweet potatoes
  • 1 fresh chile
  • Dried chile — quantity not printed
  • Pepper — quantity not printed
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 3 tbsp tomato concentrate
  • 5 fresh tomatoes
  • 250 g onions
  • 1/4 litre oil
  • 1 litre water, plus a little salt
  • A little vinegar — quantity not printed
  • A small piece of sugar (the page’s tip)

Method

  1. Cut the meat into pieces, plunge them into hot oil and let them brown.
  2. Add two chopped onions and let them go lightly pink.
  3. Add the tomato concentrate and the fresh tomatoes, seeded and cut. Let simmer five minutes.
  4. Pour in 1 litre of water and a little salt and simmer 25 minutes.
  5. Add the turnips and carrots (cut into cubes or rounds) and the chile.
  6. When these vegetables are almost cooked, add the potatoes and sweet potatoes cut into rounds, the remaining onions and the garlic.
  7. Check the seasoning, adding pepper and a little vinegar, and let the sauce thicken.
  8. Serve with white rice cooked separately.

Chef Tips

  • The structural cue is ‘when these vegetables are almost cooked’: turnips and carrots first, potatoes and sweet potatoes after, or the soft ones fall apart.
  • A small piece of sugar and a splash of vinegar are the page’s two unusual finishing touches.
  • Browned meat, pink onions and a thickened sauce are the visual cues; the page prints only two times.
  • Cook the rice separately — this is a stew, not a one-pot rice.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the vegetables at once.
  • Skipping the vinegar.
  • Cooking the rice in the sauce.

Health Benefits

  • Root vegetables give fibre and beta-carotene.
  • Tomatoes add lycopene.
  • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

Cultural Note

Aïssatou Mbaye calls thiou ‘moins célèbre que le yassa ou le mafé’, and says her own thiou sauces are light, close to the sauce of thiéré. It is the clean contrast with ceebu jen and ceebu yapp, where the rice finishes in the sauce.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Gentle heat, covered.

Serve with: Steamed white rice
