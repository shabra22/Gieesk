🍞 Agege Bread

Agege bread

Four cups of bread flour, four tablespoons of sugar and two of yeast, risen an hour and baked forty to forty-five minutes — the dense sweet loaf Lagos eats with everything.

Agege bread is named after a suburb of Lagos and it is unlike most bread: dense, close-crumbed, faintly sweet, and strong enough to be torn into a bowl of stew or steeped in hot tea without dissolving. The dough is mixed by hand, kneaded until smooth, risen once for about an hour, lightly knocked back and baked in a loaf tin. Note that the source's stated water quantity cannot be right — the method's instruction to add water gradually until a soft stretchy dough forms is the reliable guidance, and the number is not.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Loaf tin, Greaseproof paper, Damp napkin, Wire rack

  • 4 cups bread flour
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 4 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp dried yeast
  • Warm water — added gradually until a soft stretchy dough forms; see the note on quantity

Method

  1. Mix all the dry ingredients in a large bowl — flour, salt, sugar and yeast.
  2. Add warm water gradually while mixing by hand, until a soft, stretchy dough forms. It should not be sticky.
  3. Knead on a lightly floured surface several minutes, until smooth.
  4. Place in a bowl and cover with a damp napkin.
  5. Leave to rise about 1 hour.
  6. Knead lightly, then place in a loaf tin lined with greaseproof paper.
  7. Bake in a pre-heated oven at gas mark 4 for 40 to 45 minutes.
  8. Cool on a wire rack.

Chef Tips

  • Add the water gradually and judge by the dough, not by a measurement. The source's stated volume — roughly a third to half a glass against four cups of flour — cannot form a dough, and the method's own instruction is the one to follow.
  • Soft and stretchy but not sticky is the target. For four cups of bread flour expect to need somewhere around 300 ml, added in stages.
  • Two tablespoons of yeast is a lot by European standards and it is deliberate. Agege bread rises fast and hard.
  • Four tablespoons of sugar is what gives it that distinct sweetness and the dark crust.
  • A damp napkin rather than cling film. It keeps the surface from skinning during the rise.
  • The recipe gives only one rise and no second prove after shaping, which is unusual for a tinned loaf. A second 30-minute prove in the tin will give you a lighter crumb if you want one.
  • Gas mark 4 is about 180°C. Forty to forty-five minutes, and it should sound hollow underneath.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Trying to use the stated water volume literally.
  • Making the dough sticky, which gives a gummy crumb.
  • Under-kneading, so it never goes smooth.
  • Baking in an oven that has not been preheated.
  • Slicing it hot, which tears the crumb.

Health Benefits

  • Vegan and dairy-free as written.
  • Lower in fat than most enriched breads — there is no butter or egg in it.
  • Provides B vitamins and iron from fortified bread flour.
  • A dense crumb means a smaller slice satisfies.

Cultural Note

Agege bread is one of the few Nigerian foods named for a specific Lagos neighbourhood, and it is sold in a plastic wrapper from the top of a hawker's head in traffic all over the city. Its density is the point: it has to survive being torn, dunked in stew, filled with mashed beans and pepper sauce, or soaked in tea, and a lighter loaf would simply collapse.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days in a bread bag at room temperature, 3 months frozen sliced.

Reheating: Toast, or warm 5 minutes in a low oven.

Serve with: Ewa Agoyin, Akara, Hot tea
