🐟 Kenyan Tamarind Glazed Fish

Samaki wa Ukwaju wa Sherehe

Kenyan Tamarind Glazed Fish — whole fish marinated in tamarind, garlic, and spices, grilled over charcoal with repeated tamarind glaze applications until the skin is magnificently lacquered, sweet-sour, and fragrant. The showpiece fish dish of the Swahili coast celebration table.

Tamarind glazed fish is the coastal celebration fish dish — where everyday tamarind fish is a simple sauce preparation, this celebration version uses the tamarind as a glaze applied layer by layer during grilling, building a magnificent lacquered surface on a whole fish. The result is visually stunning and flavourfully extraordinary.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill, Basting brush, Sharp knife for scoring

  • 1 whole large fish (1—1.5 kg) — snapper, barracuda, or kingfish

Tamarind glaze

  • 5 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 2 tbsp brown sugar
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tsp ginger — grated
  • 1 tsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • ½ tsp chilli flakes — optional
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Score the fish deeply on both sides — 4—5 diagonal cuts through to the bone.
  2. Mix the tamarind glaze ingredients. Divide into two portions — one for marinating, one for basting.
  3. Rub the marinade portion all over and into the scored cuts. Marinate 1 hour.
  4. Prepare charcoal to medium-low coals.
  5. Grill fish for 8—10 minutes per side, basting with the second portion of glaze every 3 minutes.
  6. The fish is done when the skin is deeply lacquered and slightly charred at the edges.
  7. Final baste immediately off the grill for maximum glaze shine.
  8. Serve whole on a large platter.

Chef Tips

  • The multiple glaze applications are what create the lacquered effect — do not rush this.
  • Keep heat medium-low — the sugar in the tamarind burns quickly over high heat.
  • Score deeply to allow the marinade to penetrate the flesh.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • High heat — burns the glaze before the fish is cooked.
  • Single application of glaze — does not build the lacquered effect.

The Science

Repeated applications of the tamarind-sugar glaze build up successive Maillard-caramelised layers. Each layer dries and caramelises before the next is applied, creating a thick, complex, multi-layered surface with deeper flavour than a single application.

Cultural Note

Tamarind glazed whole fish is the showpiece of the Swahili coastal celebration table — its glistening, lacquered surface communicates the care and skill of the cook and the importance of the occasion.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh from the grill.

Reheating: Warm in oven — do not microwave.

Serve with: Coconut Celebration Rice, Kachumbari, Coconut Chutney, Lemon wedges
