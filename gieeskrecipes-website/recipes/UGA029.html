🍄 Curry Termite Mushroom Soup

Butiko

Foraged termite mushrooms simmered fifteen to twenty minutes in stock with curry powder, grated carrot and green pepper — a soup you make the night the mushrooms appear.

Termite mushrooms come up in the rainy season, randomly, around termite mounds, and they cannot be cultivated. Households get midnight calls to go and harvest them. They are expensive, they are treated as medicinal, and they can be eaten raw — sharp, part nutty, mostly earthy, slightly salty. They are mostly used for soup, and in Uganda that means curry powder, because Ugandans use curry powder in a great deal and this is one of the places it genuinely belongs.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan with a lid, Colander

  • 960 ml (4 cups) termite mushrooms
  • 240 ml (1 cup) carrot, grated
  • 3 large tomatoes, diced
  • 1 large green pepper, diced
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 1 clove garlic, crushed and chopped
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) ginger, grated
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) curry powder
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) oil
  • Vegetable stock, to cover
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Wash the mushrooms thoroughly but carefully — they are delicate. Drain and set aside.
  2. Put a saucepan on high heat and add the oil.
  3. Add the garlic and ginger and let them release a fragrant aroma.
  4. Add the onions and tomatoes and sauté until the onions are translucent and the tomatoes are soft and tender.
  5. Add the mushrooms, green pepper, carrots and curry powder.
  6. Cover the pan and let the mushrooms cook about 5 minutes.
  7. Add the vegetable stock and let it simmer 15 to 20 minutes.
  8. Season with salt and pepper, take it off the heat and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Wash them thoroughly but carefully. They grow in soil and they bruise, and those two facts pull in opposite directions.
  • Curry powder is not a compromise here. It is what Ugandans do with these mushrooms and it suits the earthy, nutty flavour better than anything subtler.
  • Five minutes covered before the stock goes in lets them release their own liquid, which is where a lot of the flavour is.
  • Fifteen to twenty minutes of simmering, no more. They collapse if you push it.
  • Any stock will do — vegetable is specified but the recipe is relaxed about it.
  • They can be eaten raw, which is worth trying once with a single mushroom before you cook the rest.
  • Ordinary mushrooms work with this method and taste like ordinary mushrooms. There is no substitute for butiko and it is honest to say so.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Washing them roughly and breaking them up.
  • Adding the stock before they have had their five minutes covered.
  • Over-simmering until they disintegrate.
  • Leaving out the curry powder.
  • Seasoning at the start rather than the end.

Health Benefits

  • Termite mushrooms are high in protein for a fungus, with B vitamins and selenium.
  • Carrot, tomato and pepper add carotenoids and vitamin C.
  • A tablespoon of oil for four servings, and no dairy.
  • Foraged, seasonal and entirely wild — nothing about this is cultivated.

Cultural Note

Butiko is a genuine delicacy in the way very few things are: it cannot be farmed, it appears unpredictably after rain, and finding it is luck. The midnight harvesting call is not a colourful detail — it is what you do, because by morning someone else will have them.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days. Mushroom soups do not keep well.

Reheating: Warm gently without boiling.

Serve with: Posho, Matooke, Kalo
