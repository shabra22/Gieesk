🍚 Ethiopian Style Plain Rice

ሩዝ — Ruz

Ethiopian Style Rice — long-grain rice cooked with onion, turmeric, and a little berbere in the Ethiopian manner. Used as an alternative to injera for everyday meals, particularly in urban households. Simple and distinctly Ethiopian from the first spoonful.

Rice was not historically part of Ethiopian cuisine but has become common in urban households. The Ethiopian approach — cooking rice with onion, turmeric, and berbere — immediately makes it their own. Used as the base for stews and wats when injera is not available.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan with tight lid

  • 2 cups long-grain rice — washed
  • 3.5 cups water
  • 1 onion — finely diced
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ¼ tsp berbere
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Heat oil. Cook onion until golden — 8 minutes.
  2. Add turmeric and berbere. Stir 30 seconds.
  3. Add washed rice. Stir to coat with the spiced oil.
  4. Add water and salt. Bring to a boil.
  5. Cover tightly. Lowest heat for 18 minutes.
  6. Rest covered 10 minutes. Fluff with a fork.

Chef Tips

  • Toast the rice in the spiced oil before adding water — adds depth.
  • The berbere must be very light — just a hint.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Lifting the lid during cooking.
  • Too much berbere — dominates.

The Science

Coating rice in spiced oil before adding water creates a thin fat layer on each grain — this partially waterproofs the grain surface, slowing water absorption and producing more separate, fluffy grains than plain boiled rice.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian rice reflects urban food culture's evolution — taking a non-traditional ingredient and making it completely Ethiopian through the spice tradition. It is the food of Addis Ababa's restaurants and urban homes.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days.

Reheating: Microwave covered with splash of water.

Serve with: Any Ethiopian stew, Particularly with Tibs or Doro Wat
