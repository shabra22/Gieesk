Pappa al Pomodoro

A thick, comforting Tuscan bread and tomato soup, stale bread simmered directly into ripe tomatoes and basil until it breaks down into a rustic, spoonable porridge-like texture.

Pappa al pomodoro reflects the same no-waste Tuscan philosophy behind ribollita and panzanella, transforming day-old bread and summer's ripest tomatoes into something greater than the sum of its humble parts.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 300 g (10.5 oz) stale rustic bread, torn into chunks
  • 1 kg (2.2 lb) ripe tomatoes, or 800g (28oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 3 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 1 L (4 1/4 cups) vegetable stock
  • Fresh basil, torn
  • 100 ml (scant 1/2 cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus more for serving
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. If using fresh tomatoes, blanch, peel, and roughly chop them.
  2. Warm the olive oil in a large pot and gently saute the garlic until fragrant, taking care not to brown it.
  3. Add the tomatoes and simmer for 15-20 minutes until they break down into a thick sauce.
  4. Add the stock and bring to a gentle simmer.
  5. Stir in the torn bread and cook, stirring frequently, for 10-15 minutes until it fully breaks down and thickens the soup to a porridge-like consistency.
  6. Stir in torn basil, season to taste, and let rest for a few minutes off the heat.
  7. Serve warm or at room temperature, with a generous drizzle of olive oil.

Chef Tips

  • Genuinely stale, sturdy bread breaks down the best into the right thick, cohesive texture.
  • The best pappa al pomodoro depends heavily on ripe, flavorful tomatoes when made fresh in summer.
  • This dish is traditionally served warm or even at room temperature, not piping hot, and is excellent the next day.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using fresh, soft bread, which does not break down properly into the traditional thick, cohesive texture.
  • Rushing the tomato cooking time, leaving them not fully broken down into a rich sauce.
  • Skimping on the olive oil, which is essential both during cooking and as a finishing touch.

Cultural Note

Pappa al pomodoro exemplifies Tuscany's resourceful cucina povera tradition, transforming stale bread and ripe summer tomatoes into one of the region's most beloved comfort dishes.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; flavor improves the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding a little water or stock if too thick.

Serve with: A drizzle of good olive oil at the table, A light Tuscan red wine
