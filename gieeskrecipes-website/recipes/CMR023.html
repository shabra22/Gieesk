🫘 Koki de niébé (CIFOR Northern Cameroon Recipe Book)

Koki de niébé

Cowpeas soaked about an hour, ground, beaten in a mortar in one direction for 30 minutes to lighten, mixed with slightly heated palm oil, salt and chilli, tied in flamed banana leaves and steamed 2 hours.

A page from the October 2023 recipe book of the ReSI-NoC project (CIFOR-ICRAF with IRAD and the University of Ngaoundéré), covering the northern part of Cameroon. Cowpeas are cleaned, crushed and soaked about an hour, rinsed several times and ground with optional chilli to a slightly fine paste. The paste is turned in a mortar in one direction for 30 minutes to make it light; slightly heated palm oil is added in portions, then salt. Water is added until the dough holds together as one phase. Portions go on flamed banana leaves tied with string and steam 2 hours. Served with boiled plantain, cassava, sweet potato or macabo. The printed salt quantity (200 g for 1 cup of cowpeas) looks wrong and is reported as printed, not corrected.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle, Steamer

  • 1 cup cowpeas (niébé; cup size not printed)
  • 200 g salt (as printed — a suspected misprint)
  • 4 chillies (the method calls chilli optional)
  • 2/3 litre red palm oil
  • Water as needed — quantity not printed
  • Banana leaves
  • String

Method

  1. Clean and crush the cowpeas and soak them about an hour. Rinse several times.
  2. Grind with the optional chilli to a slightly fine paste.
  3. Turn the paste in a mortar, always in the same direction, for 30 minutes to make it light.
  4. Add the slightly heated palm oil in portions, then the salt. Add water until the dough holds together as one phase.
  5. Place portions on flamed banana leaves, tie with string and steam for 2 hours.
  6. Serve hot with boiled plantain, cassava, sweet potato or macabo.

Chef Tips

  • Printed cues are that the paste becomes light after 30 minutes of turning and the dough ‘holds together as one phase’.
  • Check the salt against the printed figure before you cook — it is almost certainly a misprint.
  • Flame the banana leaves so they are pliable.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Salting at the printed 200 g without checking.
  • Turning the paste in different directions.
  • Skipping the 30-minute beating.

Cultural Note

The book attributes its recipes to the northern part of Cameroon and names no community for any recipe. The same book gives ‘cuisson (2 heure)’ with a singular plural — reported as printed.

Storage & Serving

Storage: No storage time is printed.

Reheating: No reheating instructions are printed.

Serve with: Boiled plantain, Cassava, Sweet potato, Macabo
