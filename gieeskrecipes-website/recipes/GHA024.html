🍃 Palaver Sauce

Palava sauce — kontomire abom

Two documented versions from one kitchen — and in the classic one the ground melon seed is added and then left to set for five minutes without stirring.

Palaver sauce takes its name, by its own author's account, from the Palaver at Elmina, where the dish served took on the name. It is greens, fish, meat and ground melon seed in palm oil, and the published recipe gives two routes. The time-saving version simmers the greens fifteen minutes then cooks the agushi ten minutes covered without stirring. The classic version boils the greens about five minutes and then, once the agushi is in, simply allows it to set for five minutes. Not stirring is the technique in both. Beaten egg or mashed beans substitute for the melon seed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a lid, Knife, Wooden spoon

  • 1/2 lb stewing beef
  • 1/2 lb fish fillets
  • 8 oz smoked fish
  • 2 x 10 oz packages frozen greens
  • 2 onions, about 2 cups chopped
  • 1/2 cup palm oil
  • 1 cup grated tomatoes, or a 16 oz tin
  • 1/4 cup tomato paste
  • 1/2-1 cup ground agushi (melon seeds)
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 cup water
  • Salt, begin with 1/2 tsp
  • Fresh or dried red pepper, to taste

Optional

  • 1 tbsp dried ground shrimp
  • About a square inch of salted cod

Method

  1. Steam the stewing beef about 10 minutes in the cup of water.
  2. Soften the onions and garlic in the palm oil.
  3. Add the grated tomatoes and tomato paste and cook down.
  4. Add the beef, the fish fillets, the smoked fish and the optional shrimp or salted cod.

Classic version

  1. Boil the greens about 5 minutes, then add them to the pot.
  2. Add the ground agushi and allow it to set for 5 minutes — do not stir.

Time-saving version

  1. Simmer the greens in the pot 15 minutes.
  2. Add the ground agushi, cover, and cook 10 minutes without stirring.
  3. Season and serve with ampesi or rice.

Chef Tips

  • Do not stir once the agushi is in. Both versions say so in different words — allow it to set, or cover and cook without stirring — and it is the only way to get the melon seed to hold together.
  • The classic version boils the greens separately for about five minutes; the time-saving one simmers them in the pot for fifteen. The difference is colour: separate and brief keeps them greener.
  • Half a cup of palm oil to half a pound each of beef and fish is a moderate amount for this dish, less than the cup some kontomire stews use.
  • Beaten egg, or refried or mashed beans, will stand in for the melon seed — her own substitution, and useful if agushi is hard to find.
  • Three protein sources at once: stewing beef, fresh fish fillets and smoked fish. That layering is the point, not excess.
  • The quantity range on the agushi is wide — half a cup to a full cup — and it decides whether this is a sauce with clumps in it or a thick mass. Start at half.
  • Whether palaver sauce and kontomire stew are one dish is not settled by any source. This recipe uses frozen greens generically; the kontomire versions specify cocoyam leaves.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring after the agushi goes in.
  • Boiling the greens long in the classic version.
  • A full cup of agushi on a first attempt.
  • Leaving out either the fresh or the smoked fish.
  • Substituting another oil for palm oil.

Health Benefits

  • Melon seeds supply plant protein, magnesium, zinc and healthy fats.
  • Leafy greens contribute vitamin A, vitamin K, folate and iron.
  • Three protein sources give a very complete amino acid profile.
  • Red palm oil provides provitamin-A carotenoids.

Cultural Note

Elmina is where the Portuguese built their castle in 1482, and a dish named after a palaver held there carries that history in its name — palaver meaning a dispute or a fuss, from the Portuguese palavra. Whether the sauce was served at a negotiation or simply became associated with one, two independent sources put its naming at the same coastal town.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat without stirring, to keep the agushi intact.

Serve with: Ampesi, Rice, Boiled yam
