🌭 Kenyan Smokie Pasua

Smokie Pasua

Smokie Pasua is Nairobi's most iconic street food — a smoked sausage (smokie) split open (pasua means 'to split' in Swahili), filled with kachumbari, pili pili sauce, and sometimes bhajia or chips, then eaten on the go. A Nairobi institution.

'Pasua' — split it open. That is the entire concept and the genius of smokie pasua. A smoked sausage, grilled until hot and slightly charred, is split lengthwise and filled with raw kachumbari — tomatoes, onions, coriander, lemon — and pili pili sauce. The contrast of the hot, smoky sausage with the fresh, acidic kachumbari stuffed inside it is one of the most satisfying flavour combinations in Kenyan street food. At Nairobi bus stops and street corners in the evening, smokie vendors are as much a part of the landscape as the matatus.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill or pan, Sharp knife

  • 4 smoked beef sausages (smokies)
  • 2 medium tomatoes — finely diced
  • 1 medium onion — finely diced
  • 1 green chilli — finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • ½ tsp salt
  • Pili pili sauce — to taste
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Make the kachumbari filling: combine diced tomatoes, onion, chilli, coriander, lemon juice, and salt. Toss and set aside.
  2. Heat a grill or pan over high heat. Add oil.
  3. Grill the smokies for 4—5 minutes, turning, until heated through and slightly charred on the outside.
  4. Remove from heat. Using a sharp knife, cut the smokie lengthwise — split it almost all the way through without cutting completely in half.
  5. Open the smokie like a book.
  6. Spoon kachumbari generously into the split. Add pili pili sauce.
  7. Serve immediately — eat on the go or on a plate.

Chef Tips

  • Cut the smokie almost all the way through but keep it hinged — this creates a pocket that holds the filling.
  • The kachumbari should be fresh and sharp — the acid of the lemon against the smoky sausage is the whole point.
  • Add bhajia or chips inside for the ultimate Nairobi street food experience.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cutting the smokie completely in half — it should open like a book, not split into two pieces.
  • Serving without fresh kachumbari — this is not optional.

The Science

Smokies are cured and smoked during production — the smoke deposits phenolic compounds on the surface that act as natural preservatives and contribute the distinctive smoky flavour. The final grilling reheats the sausage and creates additional Maillard browning on the casing.

Cultural Note

Smokie Pasua is as Nairobi as the matatu — a street food that has become inseparable from the rhythm of the city's evening commute. It is the snack that fuels workers heading home, students leaving campus, and anyone who needs something hot, satisfying, and portable before reaching their destination.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Grill from refrigerated.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Pili Pili Sauce, Bhajia, Chips, Cold Soda
