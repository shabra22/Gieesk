🍚 Thiébou Dieun

Thiébou dieun — riz au poisson

A kilo of thiof, a kilo of broken rice, and both fermented seasonings — a small piece of yet and a small piece of guedj — in a two-hour cook.

The most completely specified of the documented thiébou dieun recipes where the fermented seasonings are concerned: it names both a small piece of yet and a small piece of guedj by quantity, which most printings leave out. A kilo of thiof goes with a kilo of rice broken once or twice, a quarter of a white cabbage, two carrots, a yam or sweet potato, a turnip, an African eggplant, two or three okra, and a hundred and forty grams of concentrated tomato. The rof stuffing is curly parsley, a chilli, two cloves of garlic, a bouillon cube and black pepper. Total cooking time as printed is two hours.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Mortar and pestle, Slotted spoon

  • 1 kg thiof — mérou, grouper
  • 1 kg rice broken once or twice — brisures de riz
  • 1/4 white cabbage
  • 2 carrots
  • 1 yam or 1 sweet potato
  • 1 turnip
  • 1 African eggplant — diakhato
  • 2 to 3 okra
  • 1 fresh tomato
  • 1 large onion
  • A small piece of yet
  • A small piece of guedj
  • 1 Antillean chilli
  • 2 to 3 bouillon cubes, plus 1 adja cube (optional)
  • 140 g concentrated tomato — one small tin
  • 250 ml oil
  • Salt, and pepper — very little pepper

For the rof, the fish stuffing

  • 1 bunch curly parsley
  • 1 chilli
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 1 bouillon cube
  • Black pepper

Method

  1. Pound the rof: curly parsley, chilli, garlic, bouillon cube and black pepper.
  2. Slit the fish and stuff it with the rof.
  3. Heat the oil and brown the fish in it — y faire dorer les poissons.
  4. Add the onion and the concentrated tomato and let it simmer about 10 minutes, stirring.
  5. Add the water, the yet and the guedj, the chilli and the bouillon.
  6. Add the vegetables and let it cook 45 minutes.
  7. Take the fish out after 20 minutes if you find it overcooked.
  8. Remove the vegetables as soon as they are done.
  9. Add the washed rice to the broth and finish the cooking.

Chef Tips

  • This is the printing to follow if you want the fermented seasonings specified — a small piece of yet AND a small piece of guedj, both named, which most versions omit or leave to taste.
  • Pull the fish at twenty minutes if it looks done. Her instruction is explicit and it is the right call — the vegetables need longer than the fish.
  • Remove each vegetable as it finishes rather than all together. They cook at very different rates.
  • Two hundred and fifty millilitres of oil is a lot and it is correct for the dish — the oil separating out is what the rice later absorbs.
  • Very little pepper, by her own note, despite the chilli. The heat comes from the Antillean chilli, not from black pepper.
  • A kilo of rice to a kilo of fish is a one-to-one ratio, which is the generous end. Scale the rice down before the fish if you want it lighter.
  • Rice broken once or twice, not three times — the degree of brokenness changes how much broth it drinks.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the fish in for the full forty-five minutes.
  • Lifting all the vegetables out at once.
  • Adding black pepper generously.
  • Using whole-grain rice.
  • Cutting the oil, which starves the rice of the fat it needs.

Health Benefits

  • Grouper supplies protein, selenium and vitamin B12.
  • Seven vegetables plus okra in one pot.
  • Fermented fish seasonings contribute glutamates that cut the need for added salt.
  • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

Cultural Note

Published on a pan-African news and culture site by a named Senegalese contributor, this is the version of ceebu jen that a Dakar household would recognise — including the bouillon cubes that purists leave out of printed recipes and everybody uses at home.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam the rice; warm the fish and vegetables in broth.

Serve with: Lime wedges, Kaani, Attaya
