🪲 Gogo Roasted Termites

Kumbikumbi za Kuchoma — Wagogo

Gogo Roasted Termites — flying termites (kumbikumbi) collected at the beginning of the rainy season, dry-roasted with salt until crunchy and nutty. The annual protein harvest of central Tanzania — sustainable, extraordinarily nutritious, and deeply embedded in Gogo and many other Tanzanian traditions.

Termite harvesting at the beginning of the rainy season is one of the most widespread food traditions across Tanzania — practised by the Gogo, Sukuma, Hehe, and many other groups. The flying termites (alates) emerge from their mounds after the first heavy rain, drawn to lights, and are collected in enormous quantities. Roasted with salt, they are crunchy, nutty, and high in protein and fat. This is not an exotic food — it is a completely normal, beloved seasonal harvest food for millions of Tanzanians.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy dry pan

  • 200g fresh flying termites (kumbikumbi) — wings removed
  • ½ tsp coarse salt

Method

  1. Remove wings from termites (they detach easily — roll between palms).
  2. Heat a heavy dry pan over medium heat.
  3. Add termites. Do not add oil — they contain their own fat.
  4. Dry roast, stirring constantly, for 8—10 minutes until crunchy and golden.
  5. Add salt. Toss to coat.
  6. Cool before eating — they crisp further as they cool.

Chef Tips

  • Wings must be removed — they make eating unpleasant.
  • No oil is needed — termites contain 30—40% fat by dry weight.
  • They should be completely crunchy — soft termites are under-roasted.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not removing wings.
  • Under-roasting.
  • Adding oil — not needed, produces greasy result.

The Science

Flying termites contain 35—40% protein and 30—40% fat by dry weight — comparable to meat in protein density. They also contain all essential amino acids, making them a complete protein source. Their fat is primarily unsaturated (oleic acid and linoleic acid). Gram for gram, roasted termites are one of the most nutritionally dense foods available.

Cultural Note

Roasted termites are the seasonal gift that Tanzania's entire central plateau celebrates — when the first rains bring the flying termites, communities gather around lights and mounds to collect them. It is a social occasion as much as a food gathering — the termite harvest brings communities together in the same way that fishing or planting does.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Consume same day when fresh. Dried roasted termites keep 1 week in an airtight container.

Reheating: Briefly in a dry pan.

Serve with: As a standalone snack, Mixed into ugali, With drinks
