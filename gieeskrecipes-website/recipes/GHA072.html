🟠 Kuli-Kuli

Kuli kuli — kulikuli, groundnut cakes

A cup of roasted peanuts ground to a paste, the oil pressed out between two cutting boards through cloth, then deep-fried until crisp and brown.

Kuli-kuli is a peanut snack and the reason it matters beyond itself is that pressing the oil out is what makes tankora powder possible. A cup of dry roasted unsalted peanuts is ground, the powder is wrapped in cloth, and the oil is pressed out between two cutting boards. A little hot water is added to bring it together, and the shapes are deep-fried in hot peanut oil for a few minutes until crisp and brown on the outside. The result is both a snack in its own right and the de-oiled peanut that chichinga's coating depends on.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grinder or mortar, Clean cloth, Two cutting boards, Deep pot or fryer

  • 1 cup dry roasted unsalted peanuts
  • A little hot water
  • Peanut oil, for deep frying

Optional

  • Salt, ground chilli or ginger, to taste

Method

  1. Grind the dry roasted peanuts to a powder or paste.
  2. Wrap the powder in cloth.
  3. Press the oil out between two cutting boards.
  4. Add a little hot water to bring the de-oiled paste together.
  5. Shape into small sticks, rings or balls.
  6. Deep-fry in hot peanut oil a few minutes, until crisp and brown on the outside.
  7. Drain and cool.

For tankora powder: grind the finished kuli-kuli to a powder. See Tankora Powder.

    Chef Tips

    • Pressing the oil out is the whole technique and it is harder work than it sounds. Cloth, two boards, and real pressure.
    • Save the pressed oil. It is fragrant roasted groundnut oil and it is good for frying the kuli-kuli itself.
    • A little hot water, not cold. The warmth helps the de-oiled paste bind rather than crumble.
    • Crisp and brown on the outside is the frying cue, within a few minutes. The inside stays denser.
    • Dry roasted and UNSALTED. Salted peanuts will make the finished kuli-kuli and any tankora from it too salty to control.
    • This is the prerequisite for tankora powder — the de-oiled peanut is what keeps the spice blend a dry powder instead of turning to butter. See Tankora Powder.
    • Kuli-kuli is eaten on its own across West Africa, crumbled over salads and porridges, and ground into spice blends. It is more useful than it looks.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not pressing out enough oil, so the paste will not hold or grind.
    • Using salted peanuts.
    • Cold water instead of hot.
    • Frying too long, which makes them hard rather than crisp.
    • Discarding the pressed oil.

    Health Benefits

    • Very high in protein once the oil is pressed out.
    • Pressing removes a large share of the fat.
    • Peanuts supply niacin, folate, magnesium and vitamin E.
    • Vegan and gluten-free.

    Cultural Note

    Kuli-kuli is sold in little stacks at markets from Senegal to Cameroon and it is one of the oldest documented processed foods in the region — a way of making peanuts keep, travel and turn into a dry spice powder, all from one press between two boards.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 weeks in an airtight tin once fully cool.

    Reheating: A low oven briefly to re-crisp.

    Serve with: Eaten on its own, Crumbled over Koko, Ground into Tankora
