🍳 Ethiopian Spiced Kita Firfir

ጨጨብሳ — Chechebsa

Ethiopian Chechebsa — freshly baked kita flatbread torn into pieces and cooked with kibbeh (spiced clarified butter), berbere, and mitmita into a rich, spiced breakfast scramble. Ethiopia's most beloved morning dish — eaten with yogurt or as a standalone. The breakfast that Ethiopians worldwide dream about.

Chechebsa (also called Kita Firfir) is the Ethiopian breakfast that requires no further context — a piece of fresh kita torn apart and tossed in a pan with kibbeh and berbere until every piece is coated in spiced, fragrant butter. Eaten hot from the pan, sometimes with a spoonful of Ethiopian yogurt (ayib) on top, it is simple, immediate, and completely addictive.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide frying pan

  • 2 kita flatbreads — freshly baked and warm
  • 3 tbsp kibbeh (Ethiopian spiced clarified butter — ETH092)
  • 1.5 tsp berbere spice blend
  • ½ tsp mitmita (optional — for extra heat)
  • Salt to taste

Serve with: 2 tbsp ayib (Ethiopian fresh cheese — ETH096)

    Method

    1. Tear the warm kita into small irregular pieces — 3—4cm.
    2. Heat kibbeh in a wide pan over medium heat.
    3. Add berbere. Stir in the hot kibbeh for 30 seconds until fragrant.
    4. Add torn kita pieces. Toss to coat every piece thoroughly.
    5. Cook 3—4 minutes, tossing regularly, until every piece is coated in spiced butter and slightly toasted.
    6. Season with salt if needed.
    7. Serve hot in the pan or on a plate.
    8. Top with a spoonful of ayib.

    Chef Tips

    • Use warm kita — fresh from the pan. Cold kita absorbs less of the kibbeh.
    • The kibbeh must be hot before adding berbere — it blooms the spices.
    • Toss constantly — you want every single piece coated.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using cold kita.
    • Not blooming the berbere in the kibbeh first.
    • Under-tossing — some pieces uncoated.

    The Science

    Blooming berbere in hot kibbeh extracts the fat-soluble carotenoids and capsaicinoids from the dried chillies into the butter — making the spice colour and heat more bioavailable and uniformly distributed. This is the same principle used in Indian tadka (tempering spices in hot fat).

    Health Benefits

    • Kibbeh's spice infusion adds significant anti-inflammatory compounds.
    • Berbere contains capsaicin, which is thermogenic.
    • Satisfying and warming breakfast.

    Cultural Note

    Chechebsa is the Ethiopian breakfast that every Ethiopian living abroad reproduces first when they want to feel at home — it requires nothing more than kita, kibbeh, and berbere, all of which can be made from scratch wherever you are. It is the breakfast of homesickness and the breakfast of comfort.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately.

    Reheating: Briefly in kibbeh.

    Serve with: Ayib (Ethiopian fresh cheese), Ethiopian yogurt, Chai
