🥥 Tanzanian Coconut Chutney

Chatni ya Nazi

Tanzanian Chatni ya Nazi — fresh grated coconut blended with green chilli, coriander, garlic, and lemon into a fragrant, cooling condiment. The coastal Tanzanian condiment that accompanies biriani, pilau, and grilled fish — the cooling counterpoint to spiced food.

Coconut chutney is the cooling coastal condiment — where pili pili brings heat, chatni ya Nazi brings cooling freshness. Made from fresh grated coconut, it is one of the great accompaniments of the Tanzanian coast.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or food processor

  • 1 cup fresh grated coconut (or desiccated, rehydrated)
  • 2 green chillies
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger
  • ½ cup fresh coriander
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 2—3 tbsp water

Method

  1. Combine all ingredients in a blender.
  2. Blend until you reach a coarse, slightly chunky paste.
  3. Add water a little at a time to reach the right consistency.
  4. Taste and adjust lemon, sugar, and salt.
  5. Serve immediately or refrigerate.

Chef Tips

  • Fresh coconut makes a far superior chutney — use it if available.
  • A slightly coarse texture is more authentic.
  • The sugar balances the chilli — do not omit.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-blending — loses texture.
  • No sugar — too sharp.

The Science

Fresh coconut contains lauric acid (a medium-chain fatty acid) which acts as a mild natural preservative — allowing the chutney to last 2—3 days in the refrigerator.

Cultural Note

Chatni ya Nazi is the Zanzibar table's cooling balance — the condiment that makes the heavily spiced biriani and pilau approachable for all heat levels.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 2—3 days.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Pilau (TZA003), Biriani, Grilled Fish, Samaki wa Kupaka (TZA061)
