🐦 Tagine of Cornish Hens with Almonds

Tagine de pigeon aux amandes — Cornish hens version

A formal-occasion tagine, traditionally made with pigeon, adapted by Kitty Morse with Cornish hens browned in olive oil, then baked with saffron, turmeric, onions, parsley and whole blanched almonds.

The page credits the recipe to Kitty Morse, excerpted from her memoir cookbook Mint Tea and Minarets. The hens are browned on all sides in a tagine dish or Dutch oven, the onions, spices, parsley and almonds go in, and the dish is covered and baked at 375°F for 50 to 55 minutes, or until the juices run clear.

Ingredients

Equipment: Tagine dish or Dutch oven, Oven

  • ¼ c. olive oil
  • ⅛ t. Spanish saffron, crushed
  • 1 t. turmeric
  • 2 large Cornish hens, rinsed and patted dry
  • 2 medium onions, thinly sliced
  • ½ c. chopped parsley
  • 1 c. whole blanched almonds
  • Salt to taste
  • 1 t. freshly ground pepper
  • Chopped parsley, for garnish

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 375 degrees F.
  2. In the tagine dish or Dutch oven, brown the hens on all sides over medium heat.
  3. Add the onions, saffron, turmeric, pepper, parsley and almonds; season with salt.
  4. Cover and bake until tender, 50 to 55 minutes, or until the juices run clear.
  5. Serve with parsley scattered on top.

Chef Tips

  • Doneness cue quoted: tender and juices run clear.
  • Traditionally pigeon; Cornish hens are the widely available stand-in.
  • The page reserves this tagine for formal occasions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overbaking, which dries small birds.
  • Skipping the browning.
  • Salting before tasting the almonds and stock.

Cultural Note

The page says the tagine is reserved for formal occasions in Morocco and was traditionally made with pigeon.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 2 days in the fridge.

Reheating: Warm covered in a low oven.

Serve with: Khobz, Couscous
