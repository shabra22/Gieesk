🦟 Roasted Crickets

Amayanje — jjenje, mayenje

Thumb-sized crickets dug from their holes, wings and lower leg joints plucked off, the head pulled forward to draw out the digestive tract, dunked in water and roasted on the fire.

Amayenje are large crickets about the size of a thumb, with a broad flat forehead, black wing covers and brown wings, and they live in holes they dig in the reddish earth beside footpaths in mixed gardens. The preparation has one step worth knowing precisely: pulling the head forward removes the digestive tract with it, which is the whole cleaning. The eating is unusually well described — thick thighs with a crisp shell and a concentrated chicken flavour, and a head full of fat and umami with a silky texture.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pail of water, Open fire or charcoal grill

  • Large crickets (amayanje) — no quantity is given in the source
  • Salt

For cleaning

  • A pail of water

Method

  1. Pluck off the wings and the lower joint of each leg.
  2. Pull the head forward to remove the digestive tract.
  3. Dunk them in a pail of water.
  4. Roast them on the fire.
  5. Salt and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Pull the head forward, do not twist it off. The digestive tract comes with it in one motion, and that is the entire cleaning process.
  • The lower leg joint has to go. It is hard and it will not soften.
  • The dunk in water is a rinse, not a soak. Straight in and out.
  • No timings or quantities are given in the source for the roasting — it says only that they are roasted on the fire. Go by colour and the shell going crisp.
  • The thighs are the best part: thick, crisp-shelled, and with a concentrated flavour of chicken.
  • The head is worth eating and is the part people are squeamish about. It is full of fat and umami juices with a silky texture, closer to lamb's brains than to an insect.
  • They are dug out of holes in the reddish earth at the side of footpaths, which is why they turn up in gardens rather than at market.
  • This is prose in its source rather than a numbered recipe, so the quantities stay open.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Twisting the head off rather than pulling it forward, leaving the tract inside.
  • Leaving the lower leg joints on.
  • Soaking them rather than dunking.
  • Roasting them over flames rather than embers.
  • Discarding the heads.

Health Benefits

  • High in protein, iron, zinc and B vitamins.
  • Roasted with no added fat.
  • The fat in the head is largely unsaturated.
  • Wild-harvested and seasonal.

Cultural Note

Crickets are the least commercial of Uganda's edible insects — dug from garden paths rather than harvested in swarms or sold in bags — which is why they are the least documented of the three. The precision of the tasting notes in the one good source suggests somebody took them seriously enough to sit down and pay attention.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh from the fire. 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Briefly over coals.

Serve with: Matooke, Posho, A cold drink
