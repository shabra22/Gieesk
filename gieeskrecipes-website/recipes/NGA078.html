🌶️ Nigerian Pepper Soup

Pepper soup — with urheri, erhe, gbafilo and umilo

A kilo and a quarter of goat or chicken in two and a half litres of water, with a spice mix dry-toasted five to six minutes until fragrant and then ground.

This is pepper soup with a properly specified spice blend rather than a commercial sachet, and that is the reason to make it. Eight skins of urheri, four seeds of erhe, two of gbafilo and two of umilo, plus half a teaspoon each of alligator pepper and uziza seeds, are dry-toasted on low to medium heat until fragrant — five or six minutes — cooled completely, then ground. The meat starts in only about 500 ml of water with a tablespoon of the mix, and the remaining two litres and the rest of the spice go in once it boils.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Dry frying pan or skillet, Spice mill or grinder, String, for the lemongrass bundles

  • 1.25 kg soup chicken or goat meat, in bite-sized pieces
  • 2.5 litres water, added in two stages
  • 3 tbsp pepper soup spice mix — see below
  • 1 stalk lemongrass, bruised, cut into three and tied into bundles
  • 1-2 bouillon cubes
  • Ground red chilies or cayenne pepper, to taste
  • 2 tsp ground crayfish
  • Salt to taste

Pepper soup spice mix

  • 8 skins or barks urheri (grains of Selim), bitter seeds discarded
  • 4 seeds erhe (calabash nutmeg), shelled
  • 2 seeds gbafilo, shelled and roughly chopped
  • 2 seeds umilo, shelled and roughly chopped
  • 1/2 tsp alligator pepper (grains of paradise)
  • 1/2 tsp uziza seeds

Method

  1. For the spice mix, put all six spices in a dry frying pan or skillet on low to medium heat, stirring frequently until fragrant — about 5 to 6 minutes.
  2. Remove from the heat and let it cool down completely.
  3. Once cool, blend in a mill or spice grinder until combined.
  4. Put the chopped meat in a large pot and cover with some water, about 500 ml.
  5. Add a tablespoon of the spice mix, some salt, the bouillon cubes, some red chilli powder, the crayfish if using, and the lemongrass bundles.
  6. Stir and let it simmer on low heat until the liquid comes to a boil.
  7. When it boils, add the remaining 2 litres of water and the rest of the pepper soup spice mix.
  8. Cook on medium-low heat. Adjust the salt and pepper to taste.
  9. Simmer until the meat is soft but isn't falling off the bone.
  10. Serve with boiled plantains and yams, and some palm oil if you like.

Chef Tips

  • Toast the spices dry, cool them completely, then grind. Grinding them warm makes a paste rather than a powder.
  • Fragrant is the cue for the toasting, at five to six minutes on low to medium. Spices this aromatic burn quickly.
  • Discard the bitter seeds from inside the urheri pods. The pod skin is what you want; the seeds are harsh.
  • The two-stage water is deliberate. A tablespoon of spice in 500 ml concentrates the flavour into the meat before the pot is let down with the remaining two litres.
  • Soft but not falling off the bone is the doneness cue. Pepper soup meat should still have some structure.
  • Bruise and tie the lemongrass so you can fish it out. Three pieces tied in bundles is the instruction.
  • The proportions here — 8 urheri to 4 erhe to 2 gbafilo to 2 umilo — are the most precise pepper soup spice specification found in any source. Make a jar of it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Grinding the spices while they are still warm.
  • Toasting on high heat and scorching them.
  • Leaving the bitter seeds in the urheri.
  • Adding all the water at the start.
  • Cooking the meat until it collapses off the bone.

Health Benefits

  • Very low in carbohydrate and relatively low in fat.
  • Grains of Selim, calabash nutmeg and alligator pepper are all traditionally used as digestives and decongestants.
  • High in protein.
  • No added oil unless you choose to finish it with palm oil.

Cultural Note

Pepper soup is described on its source page as chicken soup to the rest of the world — the thing people long for when it is cold, and the thing served at parties and celebrations of every sort. It is also genuinely medicinal in Nigerian practice: given after childbirth, during illness, and late at night. The spice names here are Urhobo, and the fact that every region has its own names for the same four spices is a fair measure of how widely the dish travels.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. The spice mix keeps 6 months in a jar.

Reheating: Simmer gently until hot through.

Serve with: Boiled yam, Boiled plantain, Agidi or eko
