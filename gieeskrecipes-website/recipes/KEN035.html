🫚 Kenyan Boiled Cassava

Muhogo wa Kuchemsha

Muhogo wa Kuchemsha is Kenya's most satisfying root vegetable snack — cassava peeled and boiled until tender, then served simply with salt, chilli, and lemon. A beloved street food and home staple across the coast, Western Kenya, and Eastern regions.

Cassava (muhogo) is one of the most widely consumed starchy crops in Kenya, valued for its affordability, versatility, and ability to grow in difficult soils. Boiled simply in salted water and served with a squeeze of lemon and a pinch of chilli, it becomes a deeply satisfying snack or light meal. Along the Kenyan coast, boiled cassava is often dipped in coconut sauce — one of the great simple pleasures of coastal cooking.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Sharp knife, Chopping board

  • 600g fresh cassava — peeled and cut into chunks
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 lemon — for serving
  • 1 tsp chilli powder — optional

Method

  1. Peel the cassava carefully — cut off both ends, score along the length, and pull the tough brown skin away. Remove any pink inner skin as well.
  2. Cut into 5—8 cm chunks. Remove and discard the woody central fibre if present.
  3. Place in a pot, cover with cold salted water, and bring to a boil.
  4. Simmer for 25—35 minutes until completely tender and starting to split slightly at the edges.
  5. Drain well and serve with lemon and chilli salt.

Chef Tips

  • Remove both the outer brown skin AND the inner pinkish layer for the best texture.
  • Cassava is ready when the chunks begin to split slightly at the edges.
  • Never eat raw cassava — it must always be cooked thoroughly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Undercooking — raw cassava is unpleasant and can be harmful.
  • Not removing the woody central fibre.
  • Leaving the inner pink skin on — it becomes stringy when cooked.

The Science

Cassava contains cyanogenic glycosides (primarily linamarin) that release hydrogen cyanide when raw tissue is damaged. Peeling removes the skin where concentration is highest, and thorough boiling breaks down the remaining compounds completely, making cooked cassava entirely safe.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in complex carbohydrates — one of the most energy-dense root vegetables.
  • Good source of vitamin C and manganese.
  • Naturally gluten-free and fat-free.

Cultural Note

Cassava is a staple crop across much of Kenya, particularly prized in coastal regions, Western Kenya, and parts of Eastern Kenya. Both boiled and fried preparations are popular, and along the coast, muhogo wa nazi (cassava in coconut sauce) is a celebrated dish.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a pan with a little water or microwave briefly.

Serve with: Lemon and Chilli Salt, Coconut Sauce, Kenyan Chai, Omena Stew
