🫕 Oromo Marqa Porridge

ማርቃ — Marqa

Oromo Marqa — thick barley (or oat) porridge cooked smooth and served with a well of melted kibbeh and honey in the centre. The traditional Oromo breakfast — richer than Genfo, eaten across Oromia from Jimma to Harrar.

Marqa is the Oromo porridge tradition — similar to Genfo but made with barley or oats (not teff) and always served with both kibbeh and honey rather than just kibbeh. The combination of the warm porridge, the melted butter, and the honey is the Oromo morning.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Oromo people — spread across the largest territory of any Ethiopian ethnic group

The Oromo are Ethiopia's largest ethnic group, spread across the central, western, and southern regions of the country. Oromo cuisine reflects this geographic diversity — but the traditional staple food of the highland Oromo is Marqa, a thick barley or oat porridge cooked with butter and honey. The Oromo also have a long tradition of dairy foods (including ayib and fermented milk) and cattle herding. Marqa is the food that Oromo families eat at breakfast and at celebrations — it is richer than Genfo because of the butter and honey addition.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan

  • 1 cup barley flour or rolled oats (ground)
  • 3 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • 2 tbsp Ethiopian honey

Method

  1. Bring water to a boil. Add salt.
  2. Gradually whisk in barley flour.
  3. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, 20—25 minutes until very thick.
  4. Turn out onto a serving plate or into a bowl.
  5. Make a well in the centre.
  6. Fill the well with kibbeh and honey.

Chef Tips

  • The kibbeh and honey must be in the well — guests mix them in themselves as they eat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not stirring — sticks badly.

Cultural Note

Oromo Marqa is the food of Oromia's morning — eaten from Jimma coffee country to the Harrar region, from the western lowlands to the highland Shewa, it is the porridge that connects the diverse Oromo people across their vast territory.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten warm and fresh.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: As the main breakfast, At Oromo celebrations
