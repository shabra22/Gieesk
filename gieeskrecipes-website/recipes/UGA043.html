🍠 Boiled Ugandan Sweet Potatoes

Lumonde

Peeled straight into cold water so they do not darken, then boiled in water that covers them only halfway — the top half finishes by steam.

Ugandan sweet potatoes are white-fleshed, dry and starchy rather than the orange sweet kind, and they are the standard partner for malakwang, groundnut sauce and beans. Two details make the difference. The peeled potatoes go straight into cold water, which stops them discolouring while you work through the pile. And the cooking water covers them only halfway, so the bottom boils and the top steams — which keeps them from going waterlogged.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan with a lid, Peeler or knife, Skewer or knife, for testing

  • Fresh Ugandan sweet potatoes — as many as you need
  • 1 litre water

Method

  1. Fill the saucepan halfway with cold water.
  2. Peel the sweet potatoes and put them straight into the water as you go.
  3. Scrape or cut out any dark spots and wash them thoroughly.
  4. Cut them to whatever size you want.

Boiling

  1. Add hot or cold water to the pan so it covers the potatoes halfway — no more.
  2. Cover the pan with the lid and boil for 25 minutes.
  3. Test the softness by poking one or two with a skewer or the point of a knife.
  4. Serve with your sauce.

Chef Tips

  • Peeled potatoes go straight into the water. Left on the board they darken within minutes, which does not affect the taste but looks wrong.
  • Water halfway up, not covering. The top half steams under the lid and the potatoes stay dry and floury rather than sodden.
  • Keep the lid on. Without it the steam escapes and the top never cooks.
  • No weight is given in the source — this is cooked to however many you need, which is how it works in practice.
  • Twenty-five minutes is the guide and the skewer is the measure.
  • These are the dry white-fleshed Ugandan variety. Orange sweet potatoes cook faster, go wetter and are a different thing on the plate.
  • Cut them large. Small pieces break up and absorb water.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving peeled potatoes out to darken.
  • Covering them completely with water.
  • Boiling without a lid.
  • Cutting them too small.
  • Going by the clock rather than the skewer.

Health Benefits

  • High in fibre, potassium and vitamin C.
  • A slow-release carbohydrate with a lower glycaemic impact than white potato.
  • Boiled with nothing added at all.
  • The white-fleshed Ugandan varieties are starchier and less sugary than orange types.

Cultural Note

Lumonde is the plain partner to Uganda's sauces, and it is what malakwang is served on in the north as a matter of course. It is also the most forgiving staple in the country — it grows in poor soil, keeps in the ground, and needs nothing but water and a pan.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Steam for a few minutes, or fry the leftovers in a little oil.

Serve with: Malakwang, Binyebwa, Ugandan Brown Beans Stew
