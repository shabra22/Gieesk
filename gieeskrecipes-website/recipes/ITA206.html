🍚 Risotto al Radicchio di Treviso

Risotto al Radicchio

A striking magenta-tinted risotto built on Treviso's prized bitter red chicory, its pleasant bitterness balanced by butter, Parmigiano, and a splash of red wine.

Radicchio di Treviso is a specifically prized variety of chicory from the Veneto, its elegant elongated leaves and pleasantly bitter edge softening as it cooks into the risotto, staining the rice a beautiful deep red.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide, heavy-bottomed pan, Ladle

  • 320 g (1½ cups) Carnaroli or Vialone Nano rice
  • 2 heads Treviso radicchio, sliced
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1.2 L (5 cups) hot vegetable or chicken stock
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry red wine
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) unsalted butter, divided
  • 60 g (¾ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Keep the stock warm in a saucepan over low heat throughout cooking.
  2. Melt half the butter with the olive oil in a wide pan and sauté the onion until soft.
  3. Add most of the sliced radicchio, reserving a little for garnish, and cook for a few minutes until wilted.
  4. Add the rice and toast, stirring, for 2 minutes.
  5. Pour in the wine and stir until absorbed.
  6. Begin adding the hot stock one ladle at a time, stirring frequently, for about 18 minutes until the rice is creamy with a slight bite.
  7. Remove from the heat and beat in the remaining butter and Parmigiano vigorously.
  8. Fold in the reserved raw radicchio for a fresh, slightly crunchy finish, and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Reserving a little raw radicchio to fold in at the end adds welcome texture and fresh bitterness against the creamy rice.
  • The wine's acidity helps balance radicchio's natural bitterness -- don't skip it.
  • The final vigorous beating in of butter and cheese (mantecatura) is what gives good risotto its glossy, wave-like consistency.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the radicchio at the start, losing the fresh, slightly crunchy contrast a final raw addition provides.
  • Adding the stock all at once instead of gradually, preventing the rice from releasing its starch properly.
  • Overcooking the rice until it loses all bite.

Cultural Note

Radicchio di Treviso is a prized, protected vegetable of the Veneto, and this risotto is one of the most celebrated ways the region showcases its distinctive bitter flavor.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately; risotto does not hold or reheat particularly well.

Reheating: Reheat gently with a splash of stock, stirring constantly.

Serve with: A dry red wine from Veneto
