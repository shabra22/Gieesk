🐠 Nzikinziki

Nzikinziki

Fish boned, mashed to a paste and cooked into groundnut sauce — a Madi dish especially known in Adjumani, where the fish loses its shape entirely.

Nzikinziki is a Madi dish in which the fish is boned, mashed and cooked into groundnut paste, so that nothing recognisable as a fillet survives. It is especially known in Adjumani. This matters beyond the recipe: Madi and Alur are the two West Nile communities for which no dedicated food source exists at all, and this dish is one of the very few Madi preparations documented anywhere. The groundnut sauce follows the universal rule — paste dissolved in cold water first.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Bowl, Masher or fork

  • Fish, bones removed
  • Groundnut paste
  • Cold water, to dissolve the paste
  • Salt

No quantities are documented

    Method

    1. Remove the bones from the fish.
    2. Mash the fish to a paste.
    3. Dissolve the groundnut paste in cold water to a smooth sauce.
    4. Bring the sauce to the boil and cook until ready.
    5. Stir the mashed fish through it, add salt, and simmer to bring the two together.
    6. Serve with a starch.

    Chef Tips

    • Bone it thoroughly before mashing. Once the fish is a paste you will not find a bone until you bite it.
    • Mash it properly. This dish is defined by the fish losing its shape — pieces of fish in groundnut sauce is Mputa Stew, a different thing.
    • Dissolve the groundnut paste in cold water first, as with every Ugandan groundnut sauce.
    • It is a Madi dish rather than a Lugbara one, and it is especially known in Adjumani.
    • No quantities are documented. Nothing here is estimated.
    • Worth stating plainly: Madi and Alur are the two West Nile communities with no dedicated food source anywhere I could find, and this is one of the few Madi dishes that is written down at all. Anything more would need field or oral sourcing, or a Ugandan collaborator.
    • Smoked or dried fish works as well as fresh and is what a household away from the river would have.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Leaving bones in before mashing.
    • Leaving the fish in recognisable pieces.
    • Dissolving the groundnut paste in hot water.
    • Boiling it hard after the fish is in.
    • Over-salting a sauce that reduces.

    Health Benefits

    • Fish supplies complete protein, omega-3 and vitamin B12.
    • Groundnut paste adds plant protein, niacin and vitamin E.
    • Mashed and boned, so it suits children and anyone who struggles with bones.
    • Contains fish and peanuts.

    Cultural Note

    West Nile sits where Uganda meets South Sudan and the Congo, and it is the least documented food region in the country. That one Madi dish survives in writing, and only inside an article about a neighbouring people's cuisine, is a fair measure of how much of Uganda's food has never been written down at all.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

    Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Enya, Mutere, Karo
