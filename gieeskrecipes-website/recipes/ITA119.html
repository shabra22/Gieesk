Piadina Romagnola

A thin, unleavened flatbread from Romagna, griddled quickly and folded around fillings like prosciutto, squacquerone cheese, and arugula — the region's beloved answer to a sandwich wrap.

Piadina's simple dough, just flour, water, fat, and salt, needs no rising time, making it a genuinely quick bread to prepare, and its griddled, slightly charred surface is what gives it the flexible, chewy texture perfect for folding around fillings.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Large flat griddle or heavy skillet

  • 400 g (3 cups) 00 flour
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) lard or olive oil
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) warm water
  • 8 g (1½ tsp) salt
  • 5 g (1 tsp) baking soda (optional, for a softer texture)
  • Prosciutto crudo, squacquerone or stracchino cheese, and arugula, for filling

Method

  1. Combine the flour, lard or oil, salt, and baking soda if using.
  2. Gradually add the warm water, bringing the mixture together into a smooth, soft dough.
  3. Knead briefly until elastic, then rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
  4. Divide the dough into 4 portions and roll each out into a thin round, about 25cm across.
  5. Prick the surface lightly with a fork to prevent excessive puffing.
  6. Cook on a hot, dry griddle or heavy skillet for 2–3 minutes per side, until lightly charred in spots and cooked through.
  7. Keep warm, wrapped in a clean cloth, while cooking the remaining piadine.
  8. Fill each warm piadina with prosciutto, cheese, and arugula, fold in half, and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Roll the dough thin and even so it cooks through quickly without burning on the outside.
  • A dry, very hot pan or griddle is essential for achieving the characteristic light char without drying the bread out.
  • Keep finished piadine wrapped in a cloth to stay warm and pliable while you cook the rest.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rolling the dough too thick, resulting in a doughy rather than thin, flexible flatbread.
  • Cooking over heat that's too low, leaving the piadina pale and tough rather than lightly charred and tender.
  • Overfilling, making the piadina difficult to fold and eat neatly.

Health Benefits

  • A versatile bread that can be made vegan by using olive oil instead of lard.

Cultural Note

Piadina is so central to Romagna's identity that roadside piadina stands (piadinerie) are a common sight throughout the region, each with its own devoted local following.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store cooked, unfilled piadine wrapped for up to 2 days; reheat briefly before filling.

Reheating: Warm briefly on a hot dry pan or griddle before filling and serving.

Serve with: Cold cuts, Fresh cheeses, A simple side salad
