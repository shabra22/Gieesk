🥟 Fataya à la Viande Hachée

Fataya — minced beef turnovers

A flour-and-egg dough enriched with butter, filled with minced beef cooked with onion, spring onion, garlic, green chilli and parsley, sealed and fried until the dough goes golden — printed with several internal inconsistencies, all recorded.

Amina Savoura’s printing of the minced-beef fataya, published by Afrik in 2017. The dough combines 500 g of flour, a teaspoon of baking powder, a tablespoon of peanut oil, salt, water and three eggs, with 250 g of butter named in the method; the filling is 500 g of minced beef cooked in a little oil, cooled and mixed with the seasonings, chilli paste, vinegar and diced onion. Balls of dough are flattened on a tray, a spoonful of filling is placed on each and sealed, and the pastries are fried until the dough turns golden. The list contradicts the method in five places and every one of them is recorded rather than corrected.

Ingredients

Equipment: Frying pan, Large bowl, Deep pan for frying, Tray

    Dough

    • 500 g flour
    • 1 tsp baking powder
    • 1 tbsp peanut oil
    • Salt, water — quantities not printed
    • 3 eggs
    • 250 g butter (named in the list after the filling items — see the note)

    Filling

    • 500 g minced beef
    • 3 onions
    • 2 stalks green onion
    • 2 cloves garlic
    • 1 green chilli
    • 1 fish-flavour seasoning cube
    • Parsley, salt, pepper, coriander — quantities not printed
    • Chilli paste — quantity not printed
    • Vinegar — used in the method, not in the list; quantity not printed

    Frying

    • 1 litre peanut oil

    Method

      Filling

      1. Cook the minced meat in a little oil, then let it cool. Mix in the pepper, salt, seasoning cube, chilli paste, garlic, vinegar and diced onion, and let the mixture rest.

      Dough

      1. Mix the eggs, butter, flour and salt, knead until smooth, form balls and flatten them on a tray.

      Shaping and frying

      1. Place a spoonful of filling on each flattened round and seal carefully.
      2. Heat the litre of oil and fry the pastries, letting the dough go golden. Drain well.

      Chef Tips

      • The only printed cue is ‘laisser dorer la pâte’ — let the dough go golden. No temperature or frying time is given beyond the page’s overall 45 minutes of cooking.
      • Seal the edges firmly, as in every other pastry entry in this set.
      • Decide the garlic yourself: the page prints it twice (2 cloves, then 1 clove) and does not say whether that is three or a duplicate.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Using the ‘sachet de levure’ in the filling — it is listed under the filling but makes no sense in a cooked meat mixture (see the note).
      • Frying with no oil temperature check — the page gives none.
      • Adding the vinegar without a measure — none is printed.

      Health Benefits

      • Minced beef supplies iron and protein.
      • Parsley and coriander add vitamin K and fresh flavour.

      Cultural Note

      Savoura writes for a pan-African outlet and is not identified as Senegalese. Mbaye’s site notes that the fataya, like the chawarma, is Lebanese in origin and so embedded in Senegalese cooking that the origin is forgotten.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Eat the day they are fried.

      Reheating: Recrisp in a hot oven.

      Serve with: Pili-pili / chilli paste, Hot sauce
