Culurgiones Sardi

Culurgiones are Sardinia's ornately hand-pleated dumplings, filled with potato, pecorino, and fresh mint, their distinctive wheat-ear sealing pattern considered a genuine folk art.

Each culurgione is sealed by hand with a series of tight overlapping pleats resembling an ear of wheat — a technique passed down through generations of Sardinian women, taking real skill and patience to master.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin or pasta machine, Round cutter, Large pot

    Dough

    • 400 g (3⅓ cups) semolina flour, plus more for dusting
    • 200 ml (¾ cup) warm water
    • 1 tbsp olive oil
    • Pinch of salt

    Filling

    • 600 g (1.3 lb) starchy potatoes, boiled and mashed
    • 200 g (2½ cups) grated Pecorino Sardo (or Pecorino Romano)
    • 2 garlic cloves, minced
    • 2 tbsp fresh mint, finely chopped
    • 60 ml (¼ cup) olive oil
    • Salt and pepper, to taste

    To serve

    • Melted butter or simple tomato sauce
    • Extra grated Pecorino

    Method

    1. Combine the semolina flour, warm water, olive oil, and salt into a smooth, firm dough. Knead for 8–10 minutes. Rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
    2. For the filling, combine the mashed potato, Pecorino, garlic, mint, and olive oil in a bowl. Season with salt and pepper, mixing until smooth and well combined.
    3. Roll the dough thin and cut into rounds about 8cm in diameter.
    4. Place a spoonful of filling in the center of each round.
    5. Fold the edges up around the filling and pinch to seal, working around the circle to create the traditional overlapping wheat-ear pleats — pinch each new fold slightly over the last, working toward the top, then twist to seal the point.
    6. Bring a large pot of salted water to a gentle boil. Cook the culurgiones in batches for 5–8 minutes until they float and the pasta is tender.
    7. Lift out gently and toss with melted butter or a simple tomato sauce.
    8. Serve with extra grated Pecorino.

    Chef Tips

    • The pleating technique takes real practice — start with the potato-mint filling forgiving enough that even imperfect seals still hold reasonably well.
    • Semolina dough is sturdier than egg pasta and better suited to holding the intricate pleated shape.
    • Fresh mint is essential to the traditional flavor profile — dried mint doesn't give the same bright, distinct note.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not sealing the pleats tightly enough, causing the culurgiones to open during cooking.
    • Overfilling, which makes the pleating process difficult and can cause bursting.
    • Substituting basil for mint, which changes the dish's distinctive Sardinian character.

    Health Benefits

    • A good source of calcium and protein from the Pecorino.
    • Mint aids digestion and adds a fresh, herbal note without extra fat.

    Cultural Note

    Culurgiones are recognized as a traditional agri-food product of Sardinia, and their intricate wheat-ear pleating is considered such a distinctive skill that competitions are held to celebrate the finest hand-shaping techniques.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Uncooked culurgiones freeze well in a single layer for up to 2 months.

    Reheating: Cook frozen directly from frozen in boiling water, adding 1–2 extra minutes.

    Serve with: Simple melted butter or fresh tomato sauce, A crisp Sardinian white wine such as Vermentino
