🥜 Egyptian Peanut Dukkah

Dukkah — دقة

Sesame, coriander and cumin seeds and peanuts dry-roasted separately, cooled and pounded coarsely with salt for dipping bread in olive oil.

55mTotal time
5mPrep
20mCook
12Servings
90Calories
EasyDifficulty

Each ingredient is dry-roasted on medium heat, shaking the pan, until fragrant but not burning or smoking, then cooled to room temperature. The seeds and peanuts are pounded or processed until coarsely ground, not a fine powder, and salted. It makes about 1.3 cups and is served with olive oil, labneh and fresh bread.

Ingredients

Equipment: Dry frying pan, Mortar and pestle or food processor

  • ½ cup raw white sesame seeds
  • ¼ cup coriander seeds
  • ¼ cup cumin seeds
  • ½ cup raw skinless unsalted peanuts
  • 1 tsp fine salt
  • extra-virgin olive oil and/ or labneh and fresh Lebanese bread, to serve

Method

  1. Dry-roast the sesame seeds in a pan on medium heat, shaking, until fragrant but not burning or smoking. Tip out and cool to room temperature.
  2. Repeat with the coriander and cumin seeds, then with the peanuts.
  3. Pound or process the cooled ingredients until coarsely ground; you do not want a fine powder.
  4. Stir in the salt. Serve with olive oil, labneh and bread.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: fragrant, not burning or smoking; coarsely ground. The 30-minute rest on the page is the cooling time.
  • The yield of 1.3 cups was read once only.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Grinding too fine, which turns the nuts to paste.

Cultural Note

The SBS standfirst states that dukkah originates in Egypt, where peanuts are widely used; this is unattributed editorial text.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight at room temperature for a few weeks (our estimate; the page gives none).

Reheating: Not needed.

Serve with: Aish baladi
