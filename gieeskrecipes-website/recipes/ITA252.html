🐟 Brodetto all Anconetana

Brodetto

The Marche's own version of Adriatic fish stew, traditionally built from at least thirteen varieties of fish and shellfish, lighter and less tomato-forward than its Tuscan cacciucco cousin.

Every fishing town along the Adriatic seems to have its own brodetto, but Ancona's version is particularly prized for restraint, using a lighter hand with tomato and vinegar to let the natural sweetness of the fresh catch shine through.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wide pot

  • 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) mixed Adriatic fish and shellfish (such as monkfish, mullet, cuttlefish, prawns, mussels)
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp white wine vinegar
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 800 ml (3 1/4 cups) fish stock or water
  • 100 ml (scant 1/2 cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Toasted bread, for serving
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Clean and prepare all the seafood, cutting larger pieces into manageable portions.
  2. Warm the olive oil in a large wide pot and saute the onion and garlic until softened.
  3. Add the vinegar and let it evaporate briefly.
  4. Stir in the crushed tomatoes and fish stock, and bring to a simmer.
  5. Add the seafood in stages according to cooking time, starting with firmer, longer-cooking varieties like cuttlefish and monkfish.
  6. Add quicker-cooking shellfish like mussels and prawns in the final 5-8 minutes.
  7. Season to taste and finish with fresh parsley.
  8. Serve hot with toasted bread alongside.

Chef Tips

  • Add the seafood in stages based on cooking time so nothing ends up over- or under-cooked.
  • A genuinely wide variety of fish and shellfish, traditionally many types, gives the broth its full character.
  • Keep the tomato relatively light here, distinguishing this from heavier, more tomato-forward fish stews further south.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the seafood at once, resulting in uneven cooking.
  • Using too much tomato, which can overwhelm the delicate balance this specific regional version is known for.
  • Skipping the toasted bread, which is an essential traditional accompaniment.

Cultural Note

Brodetto all'Anconetana reflects Ancona's Adriatic fishing tradition, one of many regional brodetto variations found along Italy's eastern coastline.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; seafood stew does not keep or reheat well.

Reheating: Not recommended; the seafood toughens significantly.

Serve with: Toasted bread, A dry white wine from the Marche
