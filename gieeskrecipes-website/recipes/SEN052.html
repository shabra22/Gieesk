🐟 Ceebu Jen, Rice with Fish and Vegetables

Ceebu jen — cheh-boo jen

Stuffed whole fish browned and simmered with carrot, cabbage, pumpkin and eggplant; the broth is strained and measured to four cups, in which two cups of rice simmer for 20 minutes.

A compact Wolof-glossed rendering. A two-pound fish is slashed three times each side and stuffed with the roff — parsley, chile and garlic; it browns in oil, then onions cook 5 to 7 minutes and tomato paste 2 to 3. Stock and the vegetables simmer 35 to 45 minutes; the fish returns for 15 minutes more. Fish, vegetables and about a cup of broth are lifted out and kept warm. The rest of the broth is strained, topped up with water to 4 cups, brought to a boil, and 2 cups of rice cook covered 20 minutes. The platter gets the xooñ — crispy bottom bits — and is traditionally eaten by hand.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large deep pot, Strainer, Platter

  • About 2 pounds whole fish (or fillets), cleaned
  • 1/4 cup parsley, finely chopped
  • 2 or 3 hot chile peppers, finely chopped
  • 2 or 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • Salt and pepper, to season
  • 1/4 cup peanut, red palm, or vegetable oil
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 1/4 cup tomato paste
  • About 1/4 cup water, for the paste
  • 5 cups stock or water
  • 3 carrots, cut into rounds
  • 1/2 head cabbage, cut into wedges
  • 1/2 pound pumpkin or winter squash, peeled and cubed
  • 1 eggplant, cubed
  • 2 cups rice
  • Salt and pepper, to season (listed a second time on the page)
  • 3 lemons, cut into wedges

Method

  1. Rinse the fish inside and out with cool water and pat dry. Make three diagonal slashes about 1/2 inch deep on each side. Mix the parsley, chile and garlic (the roff), season, and stuff it into the slashes.
  2. Heat the oil over medium-high and brown the fish on both sides. No time is printed. Set aside.
  3. Sauté the onions 5 to 7 minutes, until cooked through and just beginning to brown. Stir in the tomato paste and about 1/4 cup water and cook 2 to 3 minutes.
  4. Add the stock and the carrots, cabbage, pumpkin and eggplant. Simmer over medium 35 to 45 minutes, until the vegetables are cooked through and tender.
  5. Return the browned fish and simmer about 15 minutes more.
  6. Remove the fish, vegetables and about 1 cup of the broth to a platter, cover and keep warm (the page gives no oven temperature).
  7. Strain the remaining broth, discard the solids, and add water to make 4 cups. Bring to a boil, stir in the rice, season, reduce to medium-low, cover, and simmer 20 minutes, until the rice is cooked through and tender.
  8. Spread the rice on a platter including the xooñ from the bottom of the pan. Top with the vegetables and fish, pour the reserved broth over and serve with lemon wedges.

Chef Tips

  • Brown the fish, but the page gives no time — go by colour.
  • Measuring the strained broth to exactly 4 cups for 2 cups of rice is the printed ratio.
  • Xooñ — the crispy rice at the bottom — is scraped onto the platter, not discarded.
  • Eaten by hand from a common dish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Discarding the xooñ.
  • Not straining the broth.
  • Adding too much liquid to the rice.

Cultural Note

The page describes ceebu jen as one of the most popular dishes in Senegal, especially along the coast, and says it ‘resembles Spanish paella and Creole jambalaya’. It names smoked dried fish (guedge) and fermented snails (yete) as additions most Senegalese cooks include; its own recipe uses neither.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam gently.

Serve with: Lemon wedges
