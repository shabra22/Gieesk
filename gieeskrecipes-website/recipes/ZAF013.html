🍗 Izingingila Namasi

Izingingila namasi

Chicken gizzards simmered two hours in curry spices, then crumbed and deep-fried, and served with amasi hung until thick and spiced, plus a blistered tomato relish.

Gizzards are cheap, dense and completely unforgiving of shortcuts — they need two hours of wet cooking before frying will do anything but toughen them. The pay-off is a crisp shell over something with real bite, and the hung amasi alongside is what makes the dish modern: strained of its whey for four hours until it is thick enough to scoop, then spiced like a raita. It is a restaurant plate built on the cheapest thing in the butchery.

Ingredients

Equipment: Sieve and cheesecloth or muslin, Heavy saucepan with a lid, Deep pan or fryer, Three wide dishes, for the crumbing

    The gizzards

    • 500 g chicken gizzards
    • 1 onion, roughly chopped
    • 1 tsp mild curry powder
    • 1 tsp garam masala
    • 950 ml (4 cups) water
    • Salt, to taste
    • Oil, for deep-frying

    To crumb

    • 200 g cake flour
    • 2 free-range eggs, beaten
    • 150 g dry breadcrumbs

    The spiced hung amasi

    • 300 g amasi, or sour cream
    • 1/4 tsp garam masala
    • 1/4 tsp mild curry powder
    • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
    • 1/4 tsp ground cumin
    • 1/4 onion, finely chopped
    • 8 sprigs fresh coriander, chopped with the stems
    • Salt, to taste

    The tomato relish

    • 1 medium onion, chopped
    • 1 tsp cayenne pepper
    • 3 large tomatoes, roughly chopped
    • Salt, to taste

    Method

    1. Start the amasi first. Line a sieve with cheesecloth, set it over a jug, pour in the amasi and leave it to drain for 4 hours or overnight.
    2. Rinse the gizzards and pull off the yellow inner skin — it stays tough however long you cook it.
    3. Sauté the chopped onion in a little oil until soft and lightly browned, then add the curry powder and garam masala and cook for 2 minutes.
    4. Add the gizzards and turn them to coat, cover with the water, and simmer for 2 hours. When they are tender and the liquid has reduced, season with salt.
    5. Let the gizzards cool, then roll them through the flour, then the beaten egg, then the breadcrumbs.
    6. Deep-fry until golden and drain on paper towels.
    7. Mix the drained amasi with the chopped onion, coriander, garam masala, curry powder, pepper and cumin, and season.
    8. For the relish, cook the onion in a little oil until lightly browned, add the cayenne and cook for 2 minutes, then add the tomatoes and cook until blistered and saucy. Season off the heat.
    9. Serve the fried gizzards hot with the spiced amasi and the relish alongside.

    Chef Tips

    • Start the hung amasi before anything else. Four hours of draining is the longest single step in the recipe and it cannot be hurried.
    • Pull off the yellow inner skin. It is the one part of a gizzard that two hours of simmering will not soften.
    • Cool the gizzards before crumbing. Warm gizzards make the coating slide off in the oil.
    • Two hours is not negotiable. Gizzards fried from raw are close to inedible.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Frying the gizzards without the long simmer first, which gives something with the texture of a rubber band.
    • Leaving the yellow skin on.
    • Crumbing them warm, so the coating detaches while frying.
    • Using amasi straight from the tub instead of hanging it — it is too loose to hold the spices or sit on a plate.

    Health Benefits

    • Gizzards are very high in protein and iron and low in fat before frying.
    • Amasi contributes calcium and live cultures.
    • The relish and the coriander add vitamin C to what is otherwise a rich plate.

    Cultural Note

    Offal became township and rural street trade because formal markets refused these cuts and apartheid barred Black women from formal employment, so they built businesses on what was left. Calling gizzards, tripe or heads humble food misses the point: the cooking is skilled, the trade was women's, and the dishes outlived the system that produced them.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate the fried gizzards up to 2 days; the amasi and relish keep 3 days.

    Reheating: Re-crisp in a hot oven for 8 minutes. A microwave ruins the coating.

    Serve with: Uphuthu, Isonka Samanzi, A cold beer
