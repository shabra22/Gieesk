Biryaani

Chicken marinated overnight in a green paste of ginger, garlic, mint, cilantro and freshly ground spice, grilled half-done, then layered with fried onions, part-cooked rice and saffron and steamed sealed in the oven.

The Somali approach to biryani solves the problem every biryani cook knows — rice and meat that want different amounts of time. Here the chicken is grilled to halfway, the rice boiled to six minutes, the vegetables cooked separately, and everything finishes together under foil. Nothing is left to guesswork, which is why Somali cooks pass this method around rather than the all-in-one version.

Ingredients

Equipment: Spice grinder, Food processor or blender, Deep pan, for frying onions, Large pot, for boiling rice, Deep baking dish and aluminium foil

    For the marinade

    • 1 tbsp coriander seeds
    • 2 tsp cumin seeds
    • 10 cardamom pods
    • 1 tsp black peppercorns
    • 1/2 tsp whole cloves
    • 4 bay leaves
    • 50 g (2 inches) fresh ginger
    • 4 cloves garlic
    • 80 g (1/2 cup) fresh cilantro, chopped
    • 40 g (1/4 cup) fresh mint
    • 2 chilli peppers
    • 1 tsp chicken bouillon powder
    • 1 tsp ground turmeric
    • 120 ml (1/2 cup) canola oil

    The chicken

    • 1.4 kg (3 lb) bone-in chicken pieces

    For the rice

    • 414 g (2 cups) basmati rice
    • 1.5 L (6 cups) water, for soaking
    • 3 L (12 cups) water, for boiling
    • 3 tbsp salt
    • 2 bay leaves
    • 1 tsp cumin seeds
    • 2 tbsp canola oil

    For the vegetable stew

    • 1 large onion, sliced
    • Canola oil, 5 cm deep, for frying the onions
    • 2 tbsp canola oil
    • 1 large tomato, peeled and diced
    • 1 large potato, peeled and diced
    • 120 ml (1/2 cup) plain yogurt
    • 1 tsp chicken bouillon powder
    • 1 tbsp lemon juice

    To layer and finish

    • 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
    • 2 tbsp fresh mint, chopped
    • 1 tsp saffron threads, steeped in 80 ml hot water
    • 3 tbsp ghee or browned butter
    • 1/2 tsp rose water or kewra water (optional)
    • 30 g (1/4 cup) toasted almonds (optional)

    Method

    1. Grind the coriander, cumin, cardamom, peppercorns, cloves and bay leaves to a fine powder.
    2. Blend the ginger, garlic, cilantro, mint and chillies to a paste in a food processor.
    3. Mix the paste with the ground spices, turmeric, bouillon powder and the 120 ml oil, rub it all over the chicken, cover and refrigerate for at least 2 hours and preferably overnight.
    4. Soak the rice in the 1.5 L of water for 1 hour.
    5. Slice the onions and fry them in batches over medium heat until deep golden. Drain and reserve.
    6. Shake the excess marinade off the chicken, keeping it, and grill or broil the chicken at 204C / 400F for 15 minutes — it should be only half cooked.
    7. Heat the 2 tbsp oil in a pan, add the reserved marinade and fry for a minute. Add the tomato and potato and stir, then the yogurt and bouillon powder. Cover and cook on low for 30 minutes, until the potato yields to a fork.
    8. Steep the saffron in the hot water.
    9. Bring the 3 L of water to a hard boil with the salt, bay leaves, cumin seeds and 2 tbsp oil. Drain the soaked rice, add it and boil for exactly 6 minutes. Lift it out with a slotted spoon while it is still hot and very moist — do not drain it dry.
    10. In a deep baking dish, lay the chicken, scatter a handful of fried onions over it and spread half the rice evenly on top.
    11. Spoon on the vegetable stew, then more fried onions, the chopped cilantro and mint, and cover with the remaining rice.
    12. Spoon over the saffron water, the rose water if using, and the ghee. Cover the dish very tightly with foil and bake at 204C / 400F for 30 minutes. Scatter with almonds to serve.

    Chef Tips

    • The whole method is built on part-cooking. Chicken to halfway, rice to six minutes, potatoes to just tender — the final thirty minutes of sealed steam finishes all three at once.
    • Lift the rice out wet. Rice drained until dry has nothing left to steam with and the top layer bakes hard.
    • Seal the foil properly, right around the rim. Escaped steam is the difference between this working and not.
    • Marinate overnight if you possibly can. Two hours is the minimum that gets flavour past the skin.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Boiling the rice to tender, so it is already overcooked before the steaming stage begins.
    • Draining the rice thoroughly, which is the single commonest cause of a dry biryaani.
    • Covering the dish loosely, letting the steam escape and leaving the top layer of rice crunchy.
    • Cooking the chicken through before layering, after which the extra half hour dries it out.

    Health Benefits

    • Chicken provides lean protein, niacin and selenium.
    • Fresh mint, cilantro, ginger and garlic go in raw quantities rather than as a token.
    • Yogurt contributes calcium and gives the sauce body without cream.

    Cultural Note

    Biryani reached the Somali coast along the same Indian Ocean routes as the samosa and the spice trade, and Somali kitchens made it their own by breaking it into stages. It is party food: a dish made in a wide tray for a table of people rather than a weekday dinner.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Cover and warm in a low oven with a splash of water so the rice steams.

    Serve with: Basbaas, Plain yogurt, Green salad
