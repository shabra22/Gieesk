🥣 Breakfast Couscous Thiakry

Thiakry / thiakiri / degue

Couscous stirred into boiling spiced milk, covered off the heat for 7 minutes, fluffed, mixed with Greek yoghurt and topped with banana and mango — a quick, regular-couscous take on the millet dessert.

Abi Olayiwola’s breakfast version of thiakry — published 12 February 2021 and modified 3 July 2022 — pours 1 1/2 cups of milk into a pan over medium heat with sugar, nutmeg, cinnamon and vanilla; as soon as the milk starts to roll-boil the couscous goes in, the pan is covered and taken off the heat, and after 7 minutes the couscous is fluffed with a fork. Two cups of Greek yoghurt are mixed in; more milk is added if it is too thick; it is served topped with banana and mango or other toppings. She says openly that her version swaps regular couscous for millet because millet is unavailable where she lives, and that it is less runny, rich and sweet than the traditional dessert. The dish thickens the longer it sits, so extra yoghurt or milk may be needed before serving.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan with lid, Fork

  • 1 1/2 cups fresh milk
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 cups Greek yoghurt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla essence
  • 1 cup couscous
  • 1 banana, sliced
  • 1 mango, cut into cubes

Method

  1. Pour the milk into a pan over medium heat. Add the sugar, nutmeg, cinnamon and vanilla essence.
  2. Allow the milk to come to a boil and pour in the couscous. Cover the pan and remove from the heat.
  3. Leave for 7 minutes, then fluff the couscous with a fork.
  4. Pour in the yoghurt and mix well. Add more milk if it is too thick.
  5. Serve and top with banana and mango, or your favourite toppings.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Watch the pan and pour in the couscous as soon as the milk starts to roll boil.’
  • The dish thickens as it sits; add extra yoghurt or milk before serving if needed.
  • No temperatures beyond ‘medium heat’.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the pan on the heat after adding the couscous.
  • Letting the milk boil over.
  • Expecting the richness of the traditional millet-and-lait-caillé dessert.

Cultural Note

The author describes thiakry as a dairy-based dish eaten across West Africa and especially popular in Senegal and Gambia, where street vendors often sell it, traditionally made with millet, evaporated milk, sweetened condensed milk, yoghurt, sugar and spices. This is an honest adaptation, not the traditional method.
