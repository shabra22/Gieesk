🥬 Sauteed Garlic Dodo

Dodo — Ebbuga — Bbuga

Four handfuls of amaranth leaves thrown into browning garlic and stirred hard until they turn bright green — off the heat within minutes, while they still have a bite.

Dodo is amaranth, and it grows everywhere the moment it rains — the green one is dodo, the red one bbuga. It is measured in handfuls because it is genuinely hard to weigh, and the entire technique is not overcooking it: bright green is the cue, and anything greyer than that means the vitamins are gone. Slightly browned garlic in a little oil is what carries it, and the fast stir-fry locks in the moisture instead of stewing it out.

Ingredients

Equipment: Frying pan, Colander

  • Four handfuls of dodo (amaranth leaves), washed and drained
  • 4 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) cooking oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Put a pan on high heat, add the oil and let it get hot.
  2. Add the garlic and stir vigorously so it does not burn.
  3. Once it starts browning and releasing an aroma, add the dodo and stir continuously.
  4. Keep stirring until the dodo turns bright green, is fully coated in the oil and the garlic is evenly distributed through it.
  5. Take it off the heat and let it cool a little.
  6. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Bright green is the doneness cue and it arrives in a couple of minutes. Grey means overcooked, and overcooked amaranth has lost the point of eating it.
  • Brown the garlic slightly before the greens go in — the aroma that comes off it is what flavours the whole dish.
  • Stir continuously. Amaranth releases water fast and stirring keeps it a stir-fry rather than a braise.
  • Handfuls, not grams. The published recipe says outright that it is quite hard to measure, and four handfuls cooks down to very little.
  • Dry the leaves after washing. Wet leaves steam instead of frying.
  • It is a good breakfast vegetable and gets eaten as one — it is an energiser rather than a heavy side.
  • The red variety, bbuga, is better suited to a longer stew thickened with groundnut paste than to this quick fry.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking it until it goes grey.
  • Burning the garlic before the greens go in.
  • Adding wet leaves.
  • Walking away from the pan.
  • Adding water — it does not need any.

Health Benefits

  • Amaranth leaves are exceptionally high in vitamins A and C, iron, calcium and folate.
  • Barely cooked, so very little is lost to heat.
  • One tablespoon of oil for four servings.
  • Grows readily and cheaply, which makes it one of the most accessible nutrient-dense foods in the region.

Cultural Note

Dodo springs up unbidden as soon as the rains come, which makes it the definition of a free vegetable — and one of the quiet arguments about Ugandan eating is that it is precisely these cheap indigenous greens that are the good part of the diet, while the expensive processed foods are the ones doing harm. The same leaf is terere in Kenya and mchicha in Tanzania.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten straight away. Refrigerate up to 1 day.

Reheating: Warm very briefly in a hot pan, or eat it cold.

Serve with: Posho, Matooke, Katogo
