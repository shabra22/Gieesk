🍬 Tanzanian Coconut Candy

Kashata

Tanzanian Kashata — grated coconut and sugar cooked together into a firm, intensely sweet, slightly chewy confection, flavoured with cardamom and cut into squares. The most beloved Swahili coastal sweet — sold at every market, made for every celebration.

Kashata is the sweet that every Zanzibari grows up eating — at the market, at school, at home. The combination of fresh coconut and caramelised sugar produces a candy of extraordinary flavour — sweet, slightly chewy, fragrant with cardamom. The version made with peanuts alongside the coconut is equally popular.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Zanzibar and the Swahili coast — one of the oldest Swahili confections

Kashata is one of the oldest sweets of the Swahili coast — made from grated coconut and sugar cooked together until the mixture caramelises and sets into firm, sweet squares. It has been made in Zanzibar's homes and sold at its markets for centuries. The version with peanuts is equally beloved; the pink-tinted version using rose water or hibiscus is particularly festive.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Greased plate or baking tin, Wooden spoon

  • 2 cups freshly grated coconut
  • 1 cup sugar
  • ½ cup water
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • Pinch of salt

Optional peanut version: add ½ cup roasted peanuts

    Method

    1. Combine sugar and water in a heavy saucepan.
    2. Cook over medium heat without stirring until the syrup reaches soft-ball stage (115°C) — about 8 minutes.
    3. Add grated coconut, cardamom, and salt.
    4. Stir vigorously and continuously over medium heat for 5—7 minutes until the mixture thickens, pulls away from the sides, and begins to look dry.
    5. Immediately pour onto a greased plate or tin.
    6. Spread to 1cm thickness.
    7. Cut into squares while still warm.
    8. Allow to cool and set completely — 30 minutes.

    Chef Tips

    • The mixture is ready when it pulls completely away from the sides and starts to look crystalline.
    • Work quickly once off the heat — it sets fast.
    • Cut while warm — once set it is too hard to cut cleanly.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not working fast enough — sets before spreading.
    • Under-cooking — stays soft and sticky.

    The Science

    Kashata sets through sugar crystallisation. When the sugar-coconut mixture is cooked past the soft-ball stage and the remaining water evaporates, the sugar recrystallises as the mixture cools — forming the characteristic firm, slightly grainy texture of kashata.

    Cultural Note

    Kashata is Zanzibar's most universal sweet — made in every home for Eid, sold at every market, given as a gift, and eaten by every Zanzibari from childhood to old age. Its simple ingredients and profound result represent the genius of Swahili coastal cooking.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Airtight container for up to 2 weeks at room temperature.

    Reheating: Not applicable.

    Serve with: Chai ya Tangawizi, As a standalone sweet
