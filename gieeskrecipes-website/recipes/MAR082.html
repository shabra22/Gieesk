🍵 Moroccan Mint Tea (Atay bil Naanaa)

Atay bil naanaa — gunpowder tea with spearmint (أتاي بالنعناع)

Chinese gunpowder green tea rinsed, boiled with plenty of sugar and a handful of fresh spearmint in a berrad teapot, then poured from a height to raise foam.

The tea leaves are washed once with boiling water, which is discarded, then the pot is filled about two-thirds with boiling water and set on medium-low to medium heat with the lid open. Once bubbles form on the surface, a handful of fresh spearmint goes in, and the tea is kept on the fire until it reaches a nearly violent boil and the leaves have started to bloom. The first glass is poured back into the pot, and the tea is poured from at least an arm's length above the glass.

Ingredients

Equipment: Moroccan teapot (berrad) of about one liter, Tea glasses (cas), Serving tray (siniya)

  • 2 rounded tablespoons Chinese gunpowder (or Chun Mee) green tea (atay) - for a one-liter capacity pot
  • Boiling water - enough to fill the berrad about two-thirds full, plus 1 cup for rinsing
  • Fresh spearmint (naânaâ) - a handful
  • Sugar (sukkar) - the author suggests 3 "bricks", or 7 tablespoons granulated sugar, for a first berrad
  • Lemon verbena (louiza) and wormwood (sheeba) - optional

Method

  1. Rinse: pour a cup of boiling water over the tea in the berrad, let it sit a minute, and pour it into a glass without swirling. Discard it.
  2. Add the sugar. Fill the berrad about two-thirds with boiling water and set it on a burner at medium-low to medium heat with the lid open.
  3. Watch for bubbles forming on the surface, then add a handful of fresh spearmint (and the lemon verbena or wormwood, if using).
  4. Keep the tea on the fire until it reaches a nearly violent boil, and check that the leaves have absorbed water and started to bloom.
  5. Pour a glass and return it to the pot to mix, then lift the berrad at least an arm's length above the glass and pour the tea in a steady stream.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: bubbles form on the surface; a nearly violent boil; the leaves start to bloom.
  • The author says washing tea leaves in boiling water is common in the Maghreb and thought to prevent bitterness.
  • For a first berrad she suggests the lower sugar quantity.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the mint before the bubbles form.
  • Stirring or swirling the rinse.
  • Pouring from too low, which gives no foam.

Health Benefits

  • Mint is soothing; the tea is traditionally very sweet and contains caffeine.

Cultural Note

The Peace Corps page gives the Darija names for the berrad, siniya, cas and the tea ingredients, and the author notes washing the leaves is common in the Maghreb.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best drunk fresh.

Reheating: Make fresh.

Serve with: Chebakia, Ghriba
