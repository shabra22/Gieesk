🌶️ Peri-Peri Sauce

Peri-peri — piri-piri

Red onion and red pepper roasted until caramelised, blended with a serious quantity of bird's-eye chilli, smoked paprika, red wine vinegar and lime, then cooked down for twenty minutes into a baste and a sauce at once.

Peri-peri is the Portuguese rendering of a Bantu word for chilli, and the sauce is African-Portuguese rather than European — the chilli itself is American, carried to Africa on Portuguese ships. South Africa got it through Mozambique, through Madeiran and mainland Portuguese immigration to Johannesburg's southern suburbs, and through five centuries of Indian Ocean trade. The roasting is what separates a good one from a hot one: caramelised vegetables give it body, so the heat sits on something.

Ingredients

Equipment: Roasting tray, Blender, Saucepan

  • 2 red onions, quartered
  • 2 red peppers, quartered and seeded
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 8 to 10 bird's-eye chillies, chopped
  • 2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 3 to 4 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 limes, zested and juiced
  • Sea salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

Optional, to finish

  • A knob or two of butter

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 180C. Drizzle the onion and pepper quarters with 1 tbsp of the olive oil and roast for 30 minutes, until softened and caramelised at the edges.
  2. Blend the roasted vegetables with the chopped chillies, smoked paprika, the remaining olive oil, the vinegar and the lime zest and juice until completely smooth.
  3. Transfer to a saucepan and cook over medium heat for 20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until it has darkened and thickened.
  4. Season with salt and pepper to taste.
  5. For a glossier finish, whisk in a knob or two of butter off the heat.

Chef Tips

  • Blend it genuinely smooth. A smooth sauce works as a baste and as a table sauce; a chunky one only works as a relish.
  • Eight to ten bird's-eye chillies is a lot of heat and the source does not say whether to seed them. Seeded, it is assertive; with the seeds in, it is fierce. Start seeded.
  • The roasting is not optional. Raw onion and pepper blended into chilli gives a thin, sharp sauce with nothing underneath it.
  • Keep half back if you are basting raw meat, so the portion that reaches the table has not touched it.
  • It keeps for two weeks in a sterilised jar in the fridge and the heat mellows slightly over the first few days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the twenty minutes in the pan, which leaves it tasting raw and separating on the plate.
  • Basting with the whole batch and having nothing safe to serve.
  • Using ordinary paprika in place of smoked, which removes the depth the sauce is built on.
  • Adding the butter over heat, where it splits rather than emulsifying.

Health Benefits

  • Chillies are high in capsaicin and vitamin C.
  • Red peppers add more vitamin C and carotenoids.
  • Entirely plant-based and very low in calories for the flavour it carries.

Cultural Note

South Africa was never a Portuguese colony — the influence arrived through trade with Mozambique, through Madeiran and Portuguese immigration to Gauteng, and through large Mozambican migration, particularly after 1975. The name often attached to the grilled chicken version, frango a cafreal, derives from cafre, which is a slur; it is worth knowing and worth not using.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Sterilised jar in the fridge, up to 2 weeks. Freezes in an ice-cube tray for 3 months.

Reheating: Warm gently, or use it cold as a table sauce.

Serve with: Braaied Peri-Peri Chicken, Grilled prawns, Prego Roll
