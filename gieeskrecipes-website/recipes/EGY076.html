🍯 Sharbat / Attar (Egyptian Simple Syrup)

Sharbat — شربات

Equal parts sugar and water with lemon juice, orange rind and optional spices, boiled 5 to 8 minutes into a syrup for basbousa, atayef and fritters.

Sugar, water and lemon juice are boiled with optional cinnamon, orange rind, cloves and cardamom for 5 minutes, or 8 if you want a thick syrup, then cooled and used on basbousa, atayef and fried sweets. The author's notes say to simmer gently, avoid stirring once the sugar dissolves, and to cool before using.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan

  • 1½ cups granulated white sugar
  • 1 ½ cups water
  • 1 tablespoon lemon or lime juice
  • 1 stick cinnamon, optional
  • 1 rind of orange, 1 inch X 2 inches
  • 3 whole cloves, optional
  • 3 whole cardamom, optional
  • (Orange blossom or rose water is stirred in per the method but is not listed)

Method

  1. Combine the sugar, water, lemon juice and any spices and rind in a saucepan and stir until the sugar dissolves.
  2. Boil for 5 minutes, or 8 minutes for a thicker syrup; simmer gently and stop stirring once the sugar has dissolved.
  3. Stir in orange blossom or rose water and cool before using.

Chef Tips

  • No colour or spoon-coat cue is printed; the card says 5 to 8 minutes. The author's notes (single reading) say a thick syrup for basbousa and atayef boils up to 10 to 13 minutes and a thin one no more than 5 to 7.
  • Her blog method says 1 teaspoon of lemon and a 10-minute simmer, which conflicts with the card.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling too long until the syrup turns candy-like.

Cultural Note

Nermine Mansour says the syrup is 'sharbat' in Egyptian dialect and 'attar' in Levantine dialect.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate in a clean jar for weeks (our estimate).

Reheating: Warm gently to loosen.

Serve with: Basbousa, Atayef, Balah el sham
