🍢 Kefta Kebab (Grilled Ground Meat Skewers)

Kefta (كفتة مشوية)

Finely ground beef or lamb with suet, grated onion, parsley, cilantro, paprika and cumin, moulded into logs and grilled over coals until browned but juicy.

30mTotal time
20mPrep

Ground meat and suet are kneaded by hand with onion, herbs and spices, shaped into small sausage-like logs on skewers or in a grill basket, and grilled over medium-hot coals several minutes per side until evenly browned and cooked through but still juicy. The page notes that Moroccans prefer little to no pink, and offers a 350°F (180°C) oven alternative of about 15 to 20 minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill or grill basket, Metal skewers, Large bowl

  • 1 lb. finely ground beef or lamb or a mix of the two
  • 4 to 5 oz. beef or lamb suet (recommended for classic flavor and texture)
  • 1 medium onion finely chopped or grated
  • 1/4 cup chopped fresh parsley
  • 1/4 cup chopped fresh cilantro
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh mint leaves (optional; great with lamb)
  • 2 tsp sweet paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon (optional)
  • 1 tsp ground ginger (optional)
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric (optional)
  • 1/2 tsp ras el hanout (optional)
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper (optional)

Method

  1. Combine the meat with the suet, onion, herbs and spices in a large bowl.
  2. Knead by hand for a minute or two so the seasoning is evenly distributed.
  3. Shape into small sausage-like logs. Arrange them in a grill basket, or thread and mould them onto skewers.
  4. Grill over medium-hot coals several minutes per side, until evenly browned and cooked through but still juicy.
  5. Alternatively, bake on a rack set over a pan at 350°F (180°C) for approximately 15 to 20 minutes, until browned and cooked through. Wrap in foil to keep warm until serving.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: evenly browned, cooked through but still juicy; the page says Moroccans prefer little to no pink, so watch carefully to avoid drying the meat.
  • Oven time depends on how thick the kefta is shaped.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-grilling until dry.
  • Turning up the heat so the outside burns before the inside is cooked.

Cultural Note

One reading of the Splendid Table version describes kefta as a staple of street stalls and roadside stops; the Taste of Maroc page itself gives the recipe without cultural remarks.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten hot; refrigerate leftovers.

Reheating: Reheat in a hot pan or oven until steaming.

Serve with: Salads, such as Zaalouk or Taktouka, Bread
