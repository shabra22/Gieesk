🐟 Fish Yassa with Butter Rice

Yassa — poisson yassa, adapted

A declared British-kitchen adaptation — cod with grains of paradise, butter rice, and a blackened pepper and olive salad.

Published as a composed plate rather than a traditional poisson yassa, and better for being honest about it. Peppers are blistened until all sides are blackened and steamed five minutes. The butter rice is thirty grams of butter with half a teaspoon of grains of paradise, two teaspoons of ginger, two garlic cloves and two hundred and forty grams of basmati in four hundred millilitres of stock, simmered ten minutes. The yassa is four onions in half moons cooked ten minutes until softened and translucent with smoked paprika, two lemons zested and juiced, Dijon and five hundred millilitres of fish stock, reduced ten minutes. Six hundred grams of cod goes in covered for ten minutes, lifted at five and finished in the oven to crisp.

Ingredients

Equipment: Griddle or grill, for the peppers, Covered bowl, to steam them, Saucepan with a lid, for the rice, Large wide pan with a lid, Oven, Pestle and mortar or spice grinder

    Roasted peppers

    • 1 red pepper
    • 1 yellow pepper
    • 1 handful of black olives, pitted and halved

    Butter rice

    • 30 g butter
    • 1/2 tsp grains of paradise
    • 2 tsp ginger, grated
    • 2 garlic cloves, grated
    • 240 g easy cook basmati rice
    • 400 ml vegetable stock
    • Sea salt

    Yassa

    • 2 tbsp rapeseed oil
    • 4 onions, sliced into thin half moons
    • 4 garlic cloves, crushed
    • 1 tbsp ginger, grated
    • 2 tsp smoked paprika
    • 2 lemons, zested and juiced
    • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
    • 500 ml fish stock
    • 1 scotch bonnet chilli, finely chopped, or kept whole and pierced if you prefer less heat
    • 600 g cod fillet, cut into 4 pieces
    • Lemon thyme
    • 2 tsp sea salt
    • 1/2 tsp grains of paradise, ground in a pestle and mortar or spice grinder

    Method

    1. Char the peppers about a minute on each side, until all sides are blackened, then cover and continue to steam for 5 minutes. Peel, slice, and toss with the olives.
    2. Melt the butter over a low heat with the grains of paradise, ginger and garlic.
    3. Cook for 2 minutes.
    4. Add the rice and stock, bring the rice to the boil, then turn down the heat, cover and simmer for 10 minutes.
    5. Heat the oil over a low-medium heat and cook the onions for 10 minutes, until softened and translucent.
    6. Add the garlic, ginger and smoked paprika and cook for two minutes.
    7. Add the lemon zest and juice, the Dijon, the fish stock and the scotch bonnet. Cook uncovered for 10 minutes, to slightly thicken the sauce.
    8. Season the cod with the sea salt and the ground grains of paradise and add it to the sauce with the lemon thyme.
    9. Cook covered on medium heat for 10 minutes — but take out the fish after 5 minutes and finish it in a preheated oven for the final 5 minutes, to crisp it up.
    10. Serve the cod on the butter rice with the sauce, and the pepper and olive salad alongside.

    Chef Tips

    • Until all sides are blackened, then steam five minutes covered. That is how the pepper skins come off cleanly, and it is worth doing properly.
    • Grains of paradise twice — half a teaspoon in the rice butter and half a teaspoon ground over the fish. It is the West African peppercorn and it is what ties this plate to Senegal.
    • The fish comes out at five minutes and finishes in the oven for five. That is the step that keeps cod from collapsing into the sauce.
    • Be aware no oven temperature is printed for that final five minutes. The source does not give one and none is invented here — a hot oven, around 200C, is a reasonable reading and it is offered as a reading only.
    • Ten minutes uncovered to slightly thicken the sauce, before the fish goes anywhere near it.
    • Scotch bonnet whole and pierced rather than chopped, if you want the aroma without the full heat. Her own option.
    • This is a declared adaptation, not a traditional poisson yassa — cod, butter rice and a pepper salad. Enjoy it as what it is. For something closer to the traditional fish treatment, a fried fish with onion sauce is the documented shape.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Under-charring the peppers, so the skins cling.
    • Leaving the cod in the sauce for the full ten minutes.
    • Skipping the second dose of grains of paradise.
    • Rushing the ten-minute onion softening.
    • Adding the fish before the sauce has thickened.

    Health Benefits

    • Cod is very lean and high in protein, iodine and vitamin B12.
    • Two lemons zested and juiced supply vitamin C and limonene.
    • Charred peppers are high in vitamin C even after cooking.
    • Grains of paradise and ginger both carry documented anti-inflammatory compounds.

    Cultural Note

    Grains of paradise is the West African peppercorn that Europe traded for centuries and then forgot, and a British-Nigerian cook putting it twice into a Senegalese dish published by a British magazine is the whole history of that trade folded back on itself. The dish is an adaptation and says so, which is the right way to do it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 days refrigerated. Cod does not keep well.

    Reheating: Warm the sauce and rice; the fish is best eaten fresh.

    Serve with: Butter rice, from the recipe, Roasted pepper and olive salad, from the recipe, Bissap
