🥫 Chalé Sauce

Chalé sauce

The tomato base half this cook's book is built on — blended raw to a paste, then simmered thirty-five to forty minutes until the tartness goes.

Chalé sauce is the foundation: a blended tomato, onion, ginger and scotch bonnet paste that goes into the groundnut soups, the jollof and the peanut sauces. It exists in two states and both are useful. Blended and unblended it is the uncooked chalé sauce, which can be measured straight into a pot. Cooked, it means softening a diced onion in oil, adding curry powder and extra-hot chilli powder, then the blended mixture, and simmering gently thirty-five to forty minutes until the tomato tartness diminishes. It keeps three days refrigerated and freezes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Saucepan, Airtight container, for storing

  • 400 g tin tomatoes, or 250 g fresh tomatoes
  • 30 g (2 tbsp) tomato purée
  • 1 onion, roughly chopped
  • 5 cm piece fresh root ginger, grated — unpeeled if organic
  • 1 red scotch bonnet chilli, deseeded
  • 1 tbsp dried chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • 3 garlic cloves (optional)

To cook it

  • 1 tbsp sunflower oil
  • 1 onion, finely diced
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp extra-hot chilli powder

Method

  1. Blend the tomatoes, tomato purée, roughly chopped onion, ginger, scotch bonnet, chilli flakes, salt and garlic until you have a fairly smooth paste.

This is your uncooked chalé sauce. Stop here if a recipe calls for it uncooked.

  1. To cook it: heat the sunflower oil and sauté the finely diced onion a few minutes until softened.
  2. Add the curry powder and extra-hot chilli powder.
  3. Add the blended mixture.
  4. Simmer gently 35 to 40 minutes, until the tomato tartness diminishes.
  5. Use straight away, or cool and store.

Chef Tips

  • Two states, two uses. Uncooked chalé sauce is a measured ingredient — several of this cook's recipes call for 500 ml of it. Cooked chalé sauce is a sauce in its own right.
  • Until the tomato tartness diminishes is the cue, at thirty-five to forty minutes. It is a taste cue rather than a visual one, so taste it.
  • Fairly smooth paste, not a purée. A little texture survives into whatever you use it in.
  • Deseed the scotch bonnet. With a tablespoon of chilli flakes and a teaspoon of extra-hot chilli powder also going in, the seeds would push it past useful.
  • Ginger unpeeled if organic, which is her own note — the skin carries flavour and there is no reason to lose it.
  • Three days refrigerated or frozen for later, per the author. Making a double batch is the sensible move given how many dishes start here.
  • A later printing of this sauce adds two roasted red peppers and more than doubles the fresh tomato. That version is at Chalé Sauce with Roasted Peppers and it is a noticeably sweeter, rounder sauce.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using it cooked where a recipe asks for uncooked, or the reverse.
  • Cutting the thirty-five minutes short, leaving it sharp and raw-tasting.
  • Leaving the scotch bonnet seeds in.
  • Blending it to a completely smooth purée.
  • Skipping the separate diced onion for the cooked version.

Health Benefits

  • Cooked tomato is high in lycopene, which becomes more bioavailable with cooking and fat.
  • Ginger and garlic both carry documented anti-inflammatory compounds.
  • Vegan and very low in calories per serving.
  • Capsaicin from the scotch bonnet and chilli flakes.

Cultural Note

Chalé is Ghanaian Pidgin for friend or mate — the most common word you will hear on an Accra street — and naming a foundation sauce after it is a good joke about how often you reach for it. That a diaspora cookbook formalised an everyday tomato base into a measured product is exactly how home cooking becomes transmissible.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated in an airtight container, or frozen, per the author.

Reheating: Gentle heat. It thickens further on reheating.

Serve with: Groundnut Soup, Jollof Rice, Peanut Sauce
