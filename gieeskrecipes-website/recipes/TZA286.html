🐟 Tanzanian Grilled Fish Tamarind Glaze

Samaki wa Kuoka na Ukwaju

Tanzanian Tamarind Glazed Fish — whole red snapper or sea bream roasted with a Zanzibar spice rub, finished with a tamarind-honey-ginger glaze that caramelises magnificently. Served with coconut jasmine rice and sautéed local greens. The hotel restaurant signature — elevated traditional flavours presented with fine dining refinement.

Tamarind glazed fish is Tanzania's finest hotel dining signature — the technique (roasting then glazing) is classical, but the flavours (Zanzibar spice rub, tamarind-honey glaze, coconut rice) are entirely Tanzanian. This is the dish that demonstrates Tanzanian fine dining's coming of age.

Ingredients

Equipment: Oven 200°C, Roasting pan, Small saucepan for glaze, Pastry brush

  • 2 whole red snapper (600g each) — scored deeply

Zanzibar Spice Rub

  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp olive oil

Tamarind-Honey-Ginger Glaze

  • 3 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 3 tbsp honey
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp lime juice
  • 2 tbsp water

Serve with: coconut jasmine rice, sautéed greens

    Method

    1. Mix spice rub ingredients. Rub inside cavities and into score marks of each fish.
    2. Rest 30 minutes at room temperature.
    3. Make glaze: combine all glaze ingredients in a small saucepan. Simmer 3 minutes until slightly thickened. Cool.
    4. Roast fish in a hot oven at 200°C for 14—16 minutes.
    5. Remove from oven. Brush generously with glaze.
    6. Return to oven (grill setting) for 3—4 minutes until glaze caramelises.
    7. Rest 3 minutes. Serve with coconut rice and greens.

    Chef Tips

    • Score the fish deeply — the spice rub penetrates into the score marks.
    • The glaze goes on at the end — earlier application burns before the fish is cooked.
    • The grill finish caramelises the glaze — do not skip this step.
    • Rest the fish — the residual heat continues cooking the centre.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Glazing too early — burns.
    • Not scoring deeply.
    • Skipping the rest.

    The Science

    The tamarind's tartaric acid reacts with the honey's natural sugars during glazing to produce a faster, deeper caramelisation than honey alone — the Maillard reaction is accelerated by the pH-lowering effect of the acid. This produces the characteristic mahogany glaze with complex caramelised notes.

    Storage: Best eaten immediately.

    Reheating: Very gently in oven.
