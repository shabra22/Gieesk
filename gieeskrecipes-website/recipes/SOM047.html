🫛 Maraq Baamiye iyo Digir

Maraq Baamiye iyo Digir

Okra fried with onion first to take the slime out of it, then stewed in tomato with allspice and turmeric and green beans added whole — a meatless stew that eats like a main course.

Okra stew belongs to a family of dishes eaten right across the Middle East and the Horn, each country tilting it its own way. The Somali version leans on allspice, which is not an obvious choice and is exactly what makes it work. The technique that matters comes first: five minutes of okra and onion in hot oil before any liquid, which is what deals with the texture people object to.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a lid

  • 60 ml (1/4 cup) vegetable oil
  • 200 g (1 medium) onion, sliced
  • 225 g (1/2 lb) okra, stem ends removed
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tsp ground allspice, or 1/2 tsp cinnamon plus a pinch of ground cloves
  • 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
  • 600 g (3 large) tomatoes, diced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3/4 tsp xawaash spice blend plus 1 1/2 tsp salt
  • 240 ml (1 cup) hot water
  • 150 g (1/3 lb) green beans, ends removed, cut into 3 cm lengths
  • 40 g (1/4 cup) fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 1/2 tsp ground black pepper

Method

  1. Wash the okra and cut the tops off, leaving the pods whole.
  2. Wash and trim the green beans and cut them into 3 cm lengths.
  3. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat and fry the onion and the whole okra together for 5 minutes. This is the step that takes the sliminess out.
  4. Add the garlic, then the allspice, then the turmeric, stirring well after each.
  5. Add the diced tomatoes and stir, then the tomato paste and stir again.
  6. Add the xawaash and salt, the hot water and the green beans. Stir, cover and cook on low heat for 25 minutes.
  7. Stir through the cilantro and the black pepper, then take it off the heat and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Leave the okra pods whole. Cutting them smaller reduces the sliminess further but they disintegrate over twenty-five minutes in the pot — the five-minute fry achieves the same thing without that cost.
  • Allspice is worth buying for this. The cinnamon-and-clove substitute is close but allspice is the flavour the dish is built on.
  • Trim the okra stems without cutting into the pod itself; a nick in the side lets the interior out.
  • It is good as a main with vermicelli rice, and equally good as one of several dishes on a shared plate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the five-minute fry and putting raw okra straight into liquid, which is where the texture complaints come from.
  • Dicing the okra, which trades slime for pods that fall apart.
  • Adding the green beans at the end to keep them crisp — here they are meant to cook down with everything else.
  • Adding the cilantro early, where twenty-five minutes destroys it.

Health Benefits

  • Okra is high in soluble fibre, folate and vitamin K.
  • Green beans add vitamin C and more fibre.
  • Entirely plant-based, and substantial enough to be the main dish.

Cultural Note

Okra stews travel the Indian Ocean and the Red Sea, and versions of this dish turn up from Egypt to the Gulf. What arrives in a Somali pot has allspice and xawaash in it, and gets eaten with rice, anjero or muufo rather than with bread.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the hob with a splash of water.

Serve with: Bariis Shaciiriyo Leh, Canjeero, Muufo
