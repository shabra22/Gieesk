☕ Café Touba, Filtered with Boiling Water

Café Touba / café « saf »

50 g of ready-made café Touba powder wet with a splash of water, set in a cloth or paper filter over a sieve, and filtered with a litre of boiling water; reheated if it cools, sweetened at the cup.

The one full weighed recipe for café Touba, published on Au Sénégal under the title ‘Le café Touba, café « saf »’ (published 9 December 2014, modified 28 January 2023) and credited to Slow Food’s ‘Sénégal de la terre à la table’. It begins from ready-made café Touba powder — a coffee-and-djar blend — so it contains no roasting, grinding or djar ratio. The water is boiled; the powder is placed on a firm cloth tied over a container, or in a coffee filter set in a sieve, and the boiling water poured through to filter a liquid coffee. If it cools during filtering it is reheated; it is sweetened at the moment of drinking. THE PAGE PRINTS ‘1,2 litre d’eau (1 litre d’eau pour le café et 20 ml pour mouiller la poudre)’: 1 litre plus 20 millilitres is not 1.2 litres. Both figures are reported as printed and neither is corrected.

Ingredients

Equipment: Firm cloth tied over a container, or a coffee filter in a sieve, Pot or kettle

  • 50 g café Touba powder (ready-made blend of coffee and djar)
  • 1.2 litres water (printed ‘1,2 litre’) — the parenthetical reads ‘1 litre for the coffee and 20 ml to wet the powder’, which does not add up
  • Sugar — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Boil the water.
  2. Place the coffee powder on a firm cloth tied over a container, or tip it into a coffee filter set in a sieve. Wet the powder with the small amount of water the page specifies (20 ml as printed in the parenthesis).
  3. Pour the boiling water through and let it filter to obtain a liquid coffee.
  4. Reheat the coffee if it cools during filtering. Sweeten to taste when drinking.

Chef Tips

  • The page’s only cue is that the coffee should be a ‘café liquide’ once filtered; no timing is printed.
  • Reheat rather than leave it to cool during filtering.
  • Sweeten at the cup, not in the pot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Reading the water as a clean 1.2 litres.
  • Pouring all the water at once through a fine cloth.
  • Sweetening the whole pot.

Health Benefits

  • Contains caffeine; djar adds a peppery warmth. No intake figures are printed in this source.

Cultural Note

Café Touba is tied to the Mouride brotherhood and to the city of Touba; the recipe’s origin is attributed to the founder, Cheikh Amadou Bamba, who brought coffee back from Gabon after his exile (1902 in the sources reached). Several sources record a devotional frame around its preparation (a blessing recited over the powder is recorded on one page of uncertain authorship); this entry cites none of that detail.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Not applicable.

Reheating: The page says to reheat if it cools during filtering.

Serve with: Sweet beignets, Morning bread
