🍖 Slow-Roasted Dibi-Style Leg of Lamb

Dibi au four

A leg of lamb rubbed with thyme, Dijon, cumin, salt, pepper and oil, sealed with sliced onions and a quarter cup of water in kraft paper and roasted 8 hours at 275 °F until the meat pulls away from the bone and is tender.

A slow-roasted take on dibi, the grilled lamb sold in Dakar’s dibiteries, published on Shepherd Song Farm on 25 August 2018 (page metadata last modified 3 March 2025) and credited ‘Used with permission from Chef Pierre Thiam’. Ingredients

Equipment: Baking sheet, Kraft paper (or parchment, or a paper grocery bag)

  • 1 leg of lamb — weight not printed
  • 3 sprigs thyme, leaves chopped
  • 2 tablespoons Dijon mustard, plus more for serving
  • 2 tablespoons ground cumin
  • 2 tablespoons salt
  • 2 tablespoons freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 2 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 1/4 cup water
  • Baguettes, for serving — quantity not printed
  • Tamarind kani sauce, for serving — quantity not printed
  • 1 sheet of kraft paper, parchment or a paper grocery bag

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 275 °F.
  2. Mix the thyme, mustard, cumin, salt, pepper and oil and rub over the leg of lamb.
  3. Line a baking sheet with kraft paper large enough to enclose the lamb. Spread the sliced onions in the centre, set the lamb on top and carefully pour the water around the leg.
  4. Fold the paper tightly around the lamb and onions to make a fully sealed package. A clean paper grocery bag tied shut also works.
  5. Roast for 8 hours.
  6. Cut the meat into bite-sized pieces and serve with the caramelised onions, or arrange on a platter with baguette chunks, extra mustard and kani sauce.

Chef Tips

  • The one doneness cue is that the meat pulls away from the bone and is tender.
  • Seal the paper fully so the steam stays in.
  • 275 °F is the only numeric oven temperature printed for any recipe in this collection.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving gaps in the paper package.
  • Guessing the leg size without checking the weight — the page gives none.
  • Cutting before the meat falls from the bone.

Cultural Note

Small eateries called dibiteries ‘serve slow roasted dibi lamb and goat meat as a street food’ in Senegal and elsewhere in Africa — the only dibiterie documentation this research could source. This oven version is not the charcoal-grilled street dish (Dibi, Grilled Lamb and Onions with Mustard, Dibi Hausa, Senegalese Grilled Beef Kebabs).
