🦶 Kouraine (Lamb Trotters with Chickpeas)

Kouraine — pieds d'agneau aux pois chiches (الكوراع)

A traditional Eid dish of lamb feet slowly cooked with chickpeas, saffron, cumin and ginger until the sauce is thick, served with hot peppers and bread.

Kouraine is described by Morocco World News as a Moroccan dish par excellence, made with the legs of cow, lamb or goat and chickpeas. The feet are charred over coals to remove the hair and cleaned well; then they are pressure-cooked 1 hour 15 minutes with oil, spices, garlic and butter, the soaked chickpeas are added, and the pot cooks another 45 minutes or more. The pot is opened and left over low heat until the sauce thickens.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pressure cooker, Open flame or coals for charring

  • 6 lamb legs (trotters)
  • 250g chickpeas soaked
  • 1 tbsp butter or ghee
  • ¼ cup olive oil
  • Few saffron stamens
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 1 tbsp cumin
  • 6 chopped cloves of garlic
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 2 liters water
  • Hot peppers and bread to serve

Method

  1. Char the legs over coals to remove the fur, then clean them well.
  2. Put the legs, oil, spices, garlic and butter in the pressure cooker and mix; add the water and close the cooker.
  3. Cook for 1 hour and 15 minutes, then open and add the soaked chickpeas.
  4. Close again and cook 45 minutes or more, until the legs and chickpeas are well cooked.
  5. Open the cooker and leave over low heat until the sauce is thick.
  6. Serve on a plate with hot peppers and bread.

Chef Tips

  • Doneness cues from the source are only "well cooked" and "until the sauce is thick".
  • Ask the butcher to burn off and clean the feet if you cannot char them over coals.
  • The source also allows the legs of cow or goat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the thorough cleaning after charring.
  • Adding the chickpeas at the start; they would overcook.
  • Serving with a loose sauce; thicken it uncovered.

Health Benefits

  • Collagen-rich and high in protein; chickpeas add fibre.

Cultural Note

The source calls kouraine a Moroccan dish par excellence and writes that nearly all parts of the lamb are used in Eid dishes.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 3 days in the fridge; it sets to a gel when cold.

Reheating: Warm gently until the sauce loosens.

Serve with: Khobz
