🍩 Kenyan Sweet Mandazi

Mandazi Tamu

Kenyan Sweet Mandazi — a sweeter, more indulgent version of the beloved fried bread, dusted with icing sugar and fragrant with vanilla and cardamom. Closer to a doughnut in sweetness — a treat version of the Kenyan everyday classic.

Sweet mandazi pushes the classic in the direction of a treat — more sugar in the dough, a touch of vanilla, and a generous dusting of icing sugar after frying that makes it feel celebratory and indulgent. Children particularly love this version, and it is a popular choice for school parties, celebrations, and any occasion where mandazi is called for but something a little more special is wanted.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Deep frying pan, Sifter for icing sugar

  • 4 cups (500g) all-purpose flour
  • ¾ cup sugar — more than classic mandazi
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 cup coconut milk or whole milk
  • ½ cup warm water
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp butter — melted
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Oil for deep frying
  • Icing sugar for dusting

Method

  1. Combine flour, sugar, baking powder, cardamom, and salt.
  2. Mix milk, warm water, egg, melted butter, and vanilla.
  3. Combine wet and dry ingredients into a soft dough.
  4. Knead for 6—8 minutes until smooth.
  5. Cover and rest for 1 hour.
  6. Roll to 1cm thick. Cut into triangles or squares.
  7. Fry at 170°C for 2—3 minutes per side until deep golden.
  8. Drain on paper towels.
  9. Dust generously with icing sugar while still warm. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Dust with icing sugar while the mandazi is still warm — it melts slightly and adheres better.
  • The extra sugar means they brown more quickly — watch the oil temperature carefully.
  • A pinch of cinnamon mixed with the icing sugar adds another layer of warmth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too high oil temperature — the extra sugar burns the outside.
  • Too much icing sugar — becomes cloyingly sweet.

Cultural Note

Sweet mandazi is the treat version of Kenya's favourite fried bread — prepared for children's celebrations, school parties, and any occasion that calls for something a little more indulgent than the everyday classic.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten the day they are made. Store in airtight container for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm in oven at 160°C for 5 minutes. Re-dust with icing sugar before serving.

Serve with: Hot Chocolate, Kenyan Masala Chai, Fresh Juice, Jam
