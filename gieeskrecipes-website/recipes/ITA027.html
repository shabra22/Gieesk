🍝 Penne alla Vodka

Penne alla Vodka

Penne alla Vodka combines tomato and cream into a blush-pink sauce, with a splash of vodka that heightens the tomato's flavor without leaving any boozy taste behind.

A relatively modern addition to the Italian pasta canon, rising to popularity in the 1970s-80s, this dish relies on vodka to help release flavor compounds in the tomato that are only soluble in alcohol, resulting in a rounder, more complex sauce than tomato and cream alone.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large skillet

  • 400 g (14 oz) penne
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 30 g (2 tbsp) butter
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • ¼ tsp red pepper flakes
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) vodka
  • 120 ml (½ cup) heavy cream
  • 50 g (⅔ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the penne until al dente.
  2. Meanwhile, heat the olive oil and butter in a skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and cook until soft, about 5 minutes.
  3. Add the garlic and red pepper flakes, cooking for 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. Pour in the vodka and simmer for 2–3 minutes to cook off most of the alcohol, scraping up any browned bits.
  5. Add the crushed tomatoes and a pinch of salt, simmering for 12–15 minutes until thickened.
  6. Stir in the cream and simmer for another 3–4 minutes until the sauce turns a pale pink and silky.
  7. Drain the pasta and toss directly into the sauce, adding the Parmigiano and tossing to combine.
  8. Serve immediately with extra Parmigiano.

Chef Tips

  • Simmering the vodka for a few minutes cooks off most of the harsh alcohol taste while retaining its flavor-enhancing effect.
  • Add the cream at the end and avoid boiling it hard, to prevent the sauce from splitting.
  • A touch of tomato paste can deepen the color and flavor if your tomatoes are on the milder side.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the vodka simmer time, leaving a harsh, boozy taste.
  • Boiling the sauce hard after adding cream, which can cause it to separate.
  • Using too little tomato, resulting in a sauce that's more cream than tomato.

  • Best enjoyed in moderation given the cream and butter content.

Cultural Note

Despite feeling like a timeless classic, Penne alla Vodka is a 20th-century invention, and its exact origin is disputed between Italy and Italian-American kitchens in the United States.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate the sauce for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently over low heat, stirring, to avoid splitting the cream.

Serve with: Garlic bread, A simple green salad, Crusty bread
