🥛 Jus de Bouye, Baobab Fruit Juice

Jus de bouye / pain de singe

Baobab fruit soaked in a litre of boiling water, a litre of cold water added, worked by hand until it dissolves, picked of black seeds, sieved, then beaten with sugar and concentrated milk until the juice is ‘onctueux et homogène’.

Amina Savoura’s jus de bouye, published on Afrik on 17 June 2017, uses 1/2 kg of bouye (also known as pain de singe or baobab fruit), 2 litres of water, 300 g of sugar, 3 packets of vanilla sugar and one can of concentrated milk — ‘sweetened or not’. The bouye is soaked in 1 litre of boiling water and 1 litre of cold water added; a few minutes later the baobab pulp dissolves as it is worked with the hands; the black seeds are removed; the pulp juice is sieved to remove impurities; the sugar is beaten in with a spatula, and the concentrated milk poured in in the same rhythm and well stirred. The page notes that a ‘royal’ version adds small pieces of pineapple, finely grated coconut and banana nectar. TWO GAPS: the page does not give the volume of the can of milk and does not choose between sweetened and unsweetened — and the choice materially changes the result. The vanilla sugar is listed in the ingredients but not placed in the method.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, Fine sieve, Spatula

  • 1/2 kg bouye (pain de singe, baobab fruit)
  • 1 can concentrated liquid milk, sweetened or not — volume not printed and the choice is not made
  • 300 g sugar
  • 2 litres water (1 L boiling plus 1 L cold, as in the method)
  • 3 packets vanilla sugar

Method

  1. Soak the baobab fruit in 1 litre of boiling water and add 1 litre of cold water.
  2. A few minutes later the baobab will dissolve as you work it with your hands. Remove the black seeds, which carry no pulp.
  3. Sieve the pulp juice through a fine sieve to remove impurities.
  4. With a spatula, beat the juice rhythmically while adding the sugar and stirring. Keeping the same rhythm, pour in the concentrated milk and stir well. The page does not say where the vanilla sugar goes.
  5. You should get a juice that is smooth, homogeneous and without imperfection.

Chef Tips

  • Two printed cues: the pulp dissolves as you work it by hand; the finished juice should be ‘onctueux et homogène sans imperfection’.
  • The only time printed is ‘quelques minutes’ after soaking.
  • Chilling is not mentioned on the page.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Pouring the milk in without keeping up the stirring.
  • Leaving black seeds or fibre in.
  • Choosing sweetened condensed milk without cutting the 300 g of sugar — the page does not decide for you.

Health Benefits

  • Baobab is rich in vitamin C and fibre.

Cultural Note

‘Jus de Bouye royal’ is the variant with pineapple, grated coconut and banana nectar; no quantities are printed for it. A second page by Aïssatou Mbaye (4 September 2024) gives a lighter bouye juice for children but prints no quantities at all.

Storage & Serving

Storage: No storage time is printed.

Reheating: Served cold.

Serve with: Sweet tea, Ngalakh
