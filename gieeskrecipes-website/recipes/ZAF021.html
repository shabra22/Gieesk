🥣 Tripe and Trotter Curry

Pens en pootjies

Tripe and sheep's trotters boiled in separate pots for four hours with bay and lemon, then brought together in a masala gravy with butter beans — a celebration dish, and asked for by name at birthdays.

Offal curry is a Cape Muslim delicacy and treated as one. The two pots at the start are not fussiness: tripe and trotters cook at different rates and one would be falling apart while the other was still tough. The lemon juice and bay in each pot are doing the work of taking the edge off, and the butter beans at the end are there to soak up a gravy that would otherwise be too rich to finish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Two large saucepans, Large pan for the curry, Pressure cooker (optional, saves three hours)

    To boil, in two separate pots

    • 2 kg white beef tripe
    • 1 kg sheep's trotters, cleaned and split
    • 4 bay leaves — 2 per pot
    • 2 lemons, juiced — half the juice per pot

    The curry

    • 2 tbsp oil
    • 2 large onions, finely chopped
    • 2 cloves
    • 2 to 3 tbsp garlic-and-ginger paste
    • 1 tsp salt
    • 1 tsp ground turmeric
    • 4 tbsp masala
    • 2 x 420 g tins butter beans, drained
    • 360 ml (1 1/2 cups) water, added in stages
    • Fresh coriander, to garnish

    To serve

    • Rice

    Method

    1. Put the tripe in one saucepan and the trotters in another. Cover each with water and add 1 bay leaf and half the lemon juice to each pot.
    2. Cook both over medium-high heat for 3 to 4 hours. Two pots, not one — they do not cook at the same rate.
    3. Lift each into its own bowl. Once the tripe has cooled enough to handle, cut it into bite-sized pieces. Cover both and set aside.
    4. Heat the oil in a large pan and fry the onions with the remaining bay leaves and the cloves until golden brown.
    5. Add the garlic-and-ginger paste, salt, turmeric and masala and cook for 1 minute.
    6. Add 1/2 cup of water and cook a further 5 to 8 minutes, until a proper gravy forms rather than a paste.
    7. Add the cooked tripe, the trotters, the drained butter beans and 1 cup of water. Stir well and simmer for 20 to 30 minutes.
    8. Garnish with coriander and serve with rice.

    Chef Tips

    • Separate pots for the initial boil. This is the instruction people skip and then wonder why the tripe has gone to rags.
    • A pressure cooker takes each of them down to about an hour, which turns a four-hour dish into a two-hour one.
    • Cut the tripe once it has cooled. Hot tripe is slippery and hard to cut evenly.
    • Let the masala fry for a full minute and then cook out with water for five to eight. Masala that has not cooked out tastes chalky through the whole pot.
    • Buy the trotters cleaned and split. Preparing them from scratch is a job in itself.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Boiling the tripe and trotters together, which overcooks one and undercooks the other.
    • Skipping the lemon and bay in the boiling water, which leaves the offal smelling strongly of itself.
    • Cutting the initial boil short. Three hours is the floor, not the target.
    • Adding the butter beans early, where thirty minutes of simmering breaks them down into the gravy.

    Health Benefits

    • Tripe is very high in protein and low in fat, and rich in selenium and B12.
    • Trotters contribute collagen and gelatine.
    • Butter beans add fibre and plant protein to balance the offal.

    Cultural Note

    Offal is celebration food in Cape Muslim households, not a fallback — it is the dish people request for their birthdays. The same combination, pens en pootjies, appears in Afrikaans farm cooking and in Durban Indian kitchens as trotter curry, which is a fair illustration of how much three separate traditions in this country have quietly shared.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Freezes well for 3 months.

    Reheating: Reheat gently on the hob with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Plain white rice, Roti, Blatjang
