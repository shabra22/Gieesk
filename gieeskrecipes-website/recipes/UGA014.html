Rolex — rolled eggs

Two eggs beaten with cabbage, tomato, onion and chilli, fried flat, laid on a warm chapati and rolled tight — the national street food, cooked on charcoal at the roadside in about four minutes.

The rolex is a chapati rolled around an omelette, and the name is nothing to do with watches — it is rolled eggs, worn down in the mouth. Two eggs per rolex is the consensus across every source. Where the vegetables go is the one real choice: beaten into the egg, or laid on raw after the omelette is on the chapati, which is the version vendors call nyanya mbissi. It became Uganda's best-known food through the stalls around Makerere University, and there are Guinness records for the biggest and the fastest.

Ingredients

Equipment: Frying pan or flat griddle, Spatula, Bowl

  • 2 chapatis
  • 4 large eggs
  • 55 g (1/4 cup) green cabbage, thinly sliced
  • 50 g (1/4 cup) tomato, chopped
  • 40 g (1/4 cup) onion, finely chopped
  • 1 green chilli, seeded and minced
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) neutral oil

Optional additions

  • Avocado, sliced
  • Fresh tomato slices, for nyanya mbissi

Method

  1. Beat the eggs with the cabbage, tomato, onion, chilli, salt and pepper. The cabbage goes in raw.
  2. Heat a pan over medium heat with the oil.
  3. Pour in half the egg mixture and spread it to the width of the pan.
  4. Cook 1 to 2 minutes a side, until set and lightly golden.
  5. Warm a chapati in the pan alongside, or on the griddle.
  6. Lay the omelette on the warm chapati.
  7. Roll the chapati over the eggs tightly into a log, starting from the edge nearest you.
  8. Repeat for the second one. Cut in half or wrap in paper and eat hot.

Chef Tips

  • Two eggs per rolex. Every source agrees, and it is the right amount for one chapati.
  • The cabbage goes in raw and cooks in the egg. Nobody pre-cooks it.
  • There is a second, equally authentic assembly: lay the chapati onto the egg while the top is still a little wet, flip the two together so they fuse, then roll. Both are street practice.
  • Nyanya mbissi is the version where fresh raw tomato goes on after the omelette is cooked, rather than being beaten into it. The one where the tomato is fried into the egg has its own name too — bisiikiremu.
  • Warm the chapati before you roll. A cold one cracks along the fold.
  • Roll it tight from the near edge. A loose rolex falls apart in the hand, which is the only thing that matters in a food eaten standing up.
  • Double face is two eggs and two chapatis; titanic is four eggs and two chapatis. Those are vendors' names and they are worth knowing.
  • Avocado, sausage, minced beef and chilli are all standard additions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using one egg.
  • Pre-cooking the cabbage.
  • Rolling a cold chapati.
  • Rolling it loosely.
  • Overcooking the omelette until it is rubbery — a minute or two a side is all it needs.

Health Benefits

  • Two eggs give a complete protein with choline, selenium and vitamin B12.
  • Cabbage, tomato and onion put raw vegetables into a street breakfast.
  • Substantial enough to carry a morning's work, which is why it costs what it does.
  • The chapati is oily, so this is filling rather than light.

Cultural Note

The rolex is the one Ugandan dish that crosses every regional line, and the woman who founded the Rolex Festival makes exactly that argument: not everyone wants embooli, and a Banyankore will want eshabwe, but everybody eats rolex. Its neutrality is its strength — you load it with whatever your region eats. The story that a particular vendor invented it around 2006 circulates everywhere and is supported by no source that shows its working; the academic account leaves the date and the place open.

Storage & Serving

Storage: None — it is eaten immediately, standing up.

Reheating: Not worth it. Make a fresh one.

Serve with: Ugandan tea, Chilli sauce, A cold soda
