🍮 Aish el Saraya (Toasted Bread and Cream Pudding)

Aish el-saraya — عيش السرايا

Toasted bread soaked in syrup, covered with a mastic-scented cream pudding and chilled, finished with ground pistachios and rose petals.

3h 5mTotal time
20mPrep
25mCook
8Servings
420Calories
MediumDifficulty

White bread slices toast at 350°F (175°C) until golden brown and soak at least 10 minutes in a hot lemon syrup that simmered about 10 minutes until slightly thickened. A pudding of milk, cream, sugar and cornstarch with crushed mastic is stirred until it thickens to a pudding-like consistency, poured over the bread, cooled to room temperature and refrigerated at least 2 hours.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepans, Baking tray, Serving dish, Plastic wrap

    Syrup

    • 1 cup sugar
    • 1 cup water
    • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
    • 1 teaspoon orange blossom water (optional)

    Pudding

    • 6-8 slices white bread, crusts removed
    • 2 cups milk
    • 1 cup heavy cream
    • 1/2 cup sugar
    • 3 tablespoons cornstarch
    • 1 teaspoon rose water (optional)
    • 5 frozen mastic resin + 1 teaspoon of white granulated sugar, crushed together

    Garnish

    • 1/2 cup ground pistachios
    • 1/4 dehydrated roses

    Method

    1. Syrup: simmer the sugar, water and lemon juice about 10 minutes until slightly thickened, then set aside.
    2. Pudding: dissolve the cornstarch in a little of the milk, then cook all the pudding ingredients, stirring, until thickened to a pudding-like consistency. Cover the surface with plastic film.
    3. Toast the bread at 350°F (175°C) until golden brown. Pour hot syrup over the toast and soak at least 10 minutes.
    4. Pour the pudding over the bread, cool to room temperature, and refrigerate at least 2 hours.
    5. Garnish with ground pistachios and dried rose petals.

    Chef Tips

    • Cues: syrup slightly thickened; pudding thick; bread golden. The soak and the chill are NO CUE, TIME ONLY.
    • The page tells you to cool the syrup and the pudding and later to pour both hot; follow the hot-syrup-on-toast order above. The garnish line is missing its unit ('1/4 dehydrated roses').

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using soft bread without toasting.

    Cultural Note

    Nermine Mansour links the dessert to the Cairo bakery Koueidar and says its origins are somewhat vague, placing it in Levantine cuisine.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate covered up to 2 days (our estimate).

    Reheating: Serve cold.

    Serve with: Tea or coffee
