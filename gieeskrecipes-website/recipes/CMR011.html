🍛 Achu and Yellow Soup (Keng’s Kitchen)

Achu and sauce jaune

Cow skin and smoked meat cooked with roasted achu spices, kanwa dissolved in water and poured slowly into warmed palm oil until the stock turns yellow, with pounded cocoyam and steamed greens, mushrooms and garden eggs on the side.

Sylvia Keng Dasi’s page says achu and yellow soup comes from Bamenda (Abakwa), capital of the Northwest Region. The achu spices — pebbe, alligator pepper, sweet alligator pepper, Ethiopian pepper, black pepper, country onion, African oil bean seed, bambala groundnut, sickle bush, sweet root, country carrot and four-corner spice — are roasted in a pan on low heat for 10 minutes. Cow skin and meat are cooked until 70% done, then the spice mix is added. The kanwa is dissolved in half a cup of water; the palm oil is heated on low heat 5 minutes and turned off, and the kanwa water poured in slowly. The stock is blended in until it changes to yellow. The page prints a safety note: watch the palm oil while heating, and use only the dissolved kanwa, leaving out the residue.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, Frying pan, Blender, Mortar

    Achu

    • 1 tuber mami cocoyam
    • 2 lbs Igbo cocoyams

    Yellow soup

    • 2 cups palm oil
    • 1 lb cow skin
    • 1 lb smoked meat
    • 2 habanero peppers
    • Kanwa/limestone (thumb size)
    • Salt and bouillon — quantities not printed

    Achu spices

    • 3 pebbe or calabash nutmeg
    • 2 alligator pepper
    • 1 sweet alligator pepper
    • 6 Ethiopian peppers
    • 1 teaspoon black pepper
    • 3 country onion
    • 3 African oil bean seed
    • 2 bambala groundnut
    • 2 pieces of sickle bush
    • 2 pieces of sweet root
    • Country carrot (size of an index finger)
    • 2 thumb-size pieces of four-corner spice

    Sides

    • 1 lb steamed spinach or njama njama (African nightshade)
    • 1/2 lb steamed mushrooms
    • 1/2 lb steamed garden eggs (njakatu)

    Method

    1. Roast the achu spices in a frying pan on low heat for 10 minutes. Grind them.
    2. Cook the cow skin and meat until they are about 70% cooked, then add the spice mix.
    3. Dissolve the kanwa in 1/2 cup of water. Use only the dissolved liquid and leave out the residue.
    4. Heat the palm oil on low heat for 5 minutes, then turn off the stove. Pour the kanwa water in slowly.
    5. Blend in the stock until the colour of the stock changes to yellow.
    6. Cook the cocoyam and pound it into achu. Serve with the yellow soup and the steamed sides.

    Chef Tips

    • Printed cues: meat ‘70% cooked’ and the stock ‘changes to yellow’.
    • Safety (printed): watch the palm oil while it heats, and use only the dissolved kanwa, leaving the residue out.
    • Pass-one-only note: if the soup is thin, thicken with a little pounded cocoyam (not confirmed on the second pass).

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Pouring the kanwa residue in with the water.
    • Leaving the hot palm oil unattended.
    • Adding all the stock at once instead of blending in gradually.
