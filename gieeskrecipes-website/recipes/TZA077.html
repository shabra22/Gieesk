🦪 Tanzanian Mussels in Coconut

Chaza wa Nazi

Tanzanian Chaza wa Nazi — fresh Zanzibar mussels steamed open in a fragrant coconut milk broth with garlic, ginger, and lemon grass. Simple, fast, and extraordinarily good — the broth is as important as the mussels.

Zanzibar mussels collected from the tidal rocks by coastal women — incredibly fresh, they need almost nothing beyond coconut milk, garlic, and heat. The steaming broth mopped up with chapati until the bowl is empty.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid

  • 1 kg fresh mussels — scrubbed and debearded
  • 300ml coconut milk
  • ½ cup water
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 1 stalk lemon grass — bruised
  • 1 green chilli
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup coriander
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Discard any mussels already open that do not close when tapped.
  2. Combine coconut milk, water, garlic, ginger, lemon grass, chilli, and turmeric. Bring to simmer.
  3. Add mussels. Cover tightly.
  4. Steam 4—5 minutes until all mussels opened.
  5. Discard any that did not open.
  6. Add lemon juice and coriander. Serve in the cooking broth.

Chef Tips

  • Discard unopened mussels before and after cooking.
  • Do not overcook — mussels toughen rapidly.
  • Provide chapati to mop up the broth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not discarding unopened mussels.
  • Overcooking.

The Science

Mussels open when heat denatures the adductor muscle protein holding shells closed. Continuing to cook after opening causes over-contraction of proteins — rubbery result.

Cultural Note

Mussels in coconut broth — shellfish collected an hour ago, cooked in coconut from the trees above the beach, eaten at the water's edge. Nothing more is needed.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Must be eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: Chapati (TZA004), Crusty bread, Cold Drinks
