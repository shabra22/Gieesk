🥚 Beid Meza'lil (Crispy Fried Hard-Boiled Eggs)

Beid mez'alil — بيض مزعلل

Hard-boiled eggs peeled and fried in ghee until the skin turns crispy and golden, seasoned with paprika, sumac or zaatar.

25mTotal time
5mPrep
20mCook
4Servings
170Calories
EasyDifficulty

Eggs are boiled 10 to 12 minutes at the most, cooled in cold water and peeled, then fried in ghee until the skin becomes crispy and a nice golden hue. A holiday dish used to break the Coptic fast at Christmas and Easter.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Skillet

  • 4 small or large eggs, preferrably free range and organic
  • Enough tap water, to cover the eggs
  • 2-3 tablespoons ghee, or more if necessary
  • salt and pepper to taste

Optional

  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika or sumac, optional
  • 1 teaspoon zaatar, optional
  • 1 teaspoon Aleppo Pepper or Egyptian Shatta, optional
  • 3 tablespoons freshly grated Parmesan, optional

Method

  1. Bring the water to a rolling boil and cook the eggs 10 to 12 minutes at most.
  2. Cool in cold water for 2 to 3 minutes (the card) or 3 to 5 minutes (the body), then peel.
  3. Fry the eggs in ghee until the skin becomes crispy and a nice golden hue; the body says to turn off the heat once the skin turns golden.
  4. Season and serve with baladi bread.

Chef Tips

  • Cue: skin crispy and golden. The cooling time is NO CUE, TIME ONLY and differs between card and body. The card has a typo ('Keep writing the eggs').

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Frying wet eggs, which spatters; dry them first (our caution).

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Nermine Mansour was raised in a Coptic family that kept fasts of up to seven weeks without animal products; her mother served this to break the fast at Christmas and Easter.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best fresh.

Reheating: Re-crisp in a pan.

Serve with: Aish baladi
