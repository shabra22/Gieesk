🥗 Puntarelle alla Romana

Puntarelle alla Romana

A distinctly Roman winter salad of curly, crunchy chicory shoots, soaked in ice water until they curl dramatically, dressed with a pungent anchovy and garlic vinaigrette.

Puntarelle refers specifically to the tender inner shoots of Catalonian chicory, painstakingly cut into thin strips by hand before their signature ice-water soak, a technique that both mellows their bitterness and makes them curl into their characteristic springy shape.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl of ice water, Sharp knife, Mortar and pestle

  • 1 large head Catalonian chicory (puntarelle)
  • 4 anchovy fillets
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 3 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 100 ml (scant ½ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Trim the outer leaves of the chicory, separating the tender inner shoots.
  2. Cut the shoots lengthwise into very thin strips using a sharp knife.
  3. Place the cut strips into a large bowl of ice water and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes, until they curl dramatically.
  4. Meanwhile, mash the anchovy fillets and garlic together in a mortar and pestle until a rough paste forms.
  5. Whisk in the vinegar, then gradually whisk in the olive oil to form an emulsified dressing.
  6. Drain the curled puntarelle thoroughly and pat dry.
  7. Toss the puntarelle with the anchovy dressing just before serving, coating well.
  8. Season with salt and pepper to taste and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The ice water soak is essential — it's what causes the sliced shoots to curl and also mellows their natural bitterness.
  • A traditional Roman mortar and pestle gives the best texture for the anchovy dressing, though a food processor works in a pinch.
  • Dress the salad only just before serving so it stays crisp rather than wilting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping or rushing the ice water soak, missing the dish's signature curled texture.
  • Making the dressing too mild, when a confident amount of garlic and anchovy is traditional and expected.
  • Dressing the salad too far ahead, causing it to wilt and lose its crunch.

Health Benefits

  • Chicory is rich in fiber and has traditionally been valued for its digestive properties.

Cultural Note

Puntarelle alla Romana is a distinctly seasonal, wintertime specialty found almost exclusively in and around Rome, rarely appearing on menus elsewhere in Italy.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Cut, un-dressed puntarelle keep in ice water in the refrigerator for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Not applicable; this is a cold salad.

Serve with: As part of a Roman antipasto spread, Grilled meats
