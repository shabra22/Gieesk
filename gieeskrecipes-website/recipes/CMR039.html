🍌 Kwem / Kpwem (Cassava Leaves with Ripe Banana and Palm-Nut Juice)

Kwem / kpwem

A packet of cassava leaves cooked 20 minutes with four finely blended ripe bananas, then a can of palm-nut juice stirred in and cooked 20 more minutes on low heat.

1h 10mTotal time

The page describes kwem as a cassava-leaf paste much prized among the peoples of the centre of the country (Beti, Bulu, Eton). The bananas are peeled and blended finely. The cassava leaves go in a pot with a glass of water; the banana is added and mixed, and the pot cooks 20 minutes, stirring constantly. The can of palm-nut juice (sauce graine) is poured in and stirred to a uniform mixture, then it cooks 20 more minutes on low heat. Served with cassava or macabo. SAFETY, STATED PLAINLY: the page prints no step for preparing the cassava leaves and no warning about them; elsewhere in this collection the Immaculate Bites cassava leaf page warns that fresh, raw cassava leaves are toxic when eaten raw. The packet’s preparation state is not printed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, Blender

  • 1 packet cassava leaves (pack size not printed)
  • 1 can palm-nut juice (sauce graine; can size not printed)
  • 4 ripe bananas
  • A glass of water (in the method, not the list)

Method

  1. Peel the bananas and blend them finely.
  2. Put the cassava leaves in a pot with a glass of water. Add the banana and mix.
  3. Cook 20 minutes, stirring constantly.
  4. Add the palm-nut juice and stir to a uniform mixture.
  5. Cook another 20 minutes on low heat. Serve with cassava or macabo.

Chef Tips

  • Printed cues: ‘en remuant constamment’ and ‘une préparation homogène’.
  • The page lists 30 minutes of preparation and 1 hour of cooking but its steps add up to 40 minutes of cooking.
  • Use leaves already prepared as the pack states; the page gives no preparation or warning.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Eating cassava leaves that have not been properly cooked.
  • Not stirring constantly so the banana catches.
  • Using an unspecified can without tasting for salt.
