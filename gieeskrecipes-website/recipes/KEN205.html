✝️ Kenyan Easter Coconut Fish

Samaki wa Nazi ya Pasaka

Kenyan Easter Coconut Fish — the traditional Easter Friday and Saturday fish dish. Rich, fragrant, and celebratory — fresh fish in a coconut milk and spice sauce, prepared with extra care for one of Kenya's most important religious holidays.

Easter is one of the most significant occasions in Kenya's predominantly Christian calendar. The tradition of eating fish on Good Friday is widely observed, and Kenyan families prepare fish with particular care and ceremony during Easter weekend. The coconut fish preparation — rich, fragrant, and celebratory — is a favourite choice, bridging religious observance with culinary celebration.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wide pan with lid, Wooden spoon

  • 1 kg firm fish fillets — snapper, kingfish, or tilapia
  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 2 large onions — finely sliced
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 2 green chillies
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • ½ cup fresh coconut — grated, for garnish

Method

  1. Season fish with salt, turmeric, and paprika. Set aside 15 minutes.
  2. Heat oil. Cook onions until deeply golden — 8 minutes.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, and chillies. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes, curry powder. Cook 8 minutes until very thick.
  5. Pour in coconut milk. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  6. Add fish carefully. Spoon sauce over each piece.
  7. Cover and simmer 10—14 minutes until fish is cooked through.
  8. Add lemon juice. Garnish with coriander and fresh coconut.

Chef Tips

  • For Easter, use the finest, freshest fish available — the quality of the fish is the statement.
  • Fresh grated coconut as a garnish adds texture and fragrance that dried coconut cannot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling coconut milk hard.
  • Moving fish too early — it breaks apart.

The Science

Fish proteins denature at 60—65°C. The coconut milk sauce maintains a gentle simmering temperature well below vigorous boiling, keeping the fish proteins from over-tightening and producing perfectly moist, flaky fish.

Cultural Note

Easter fish dishes represent the intersection of Christian tradition and Kenyan culinary culture — fish is the natural Easter food for a country with access to Lake Victoria, the Indian Ocean, and numerous rivers and lakes. Kenyan Easter fish is both religious observance and culinary celebration.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh.

Reheating: Very gently over low heat.

Serve with: Coconut Rice, Chapati, Kachumbari, Mango Slices
