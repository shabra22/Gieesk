🍚 Kenyan Plain Steamed Rice

Wali wa Kawaida

Wali wa Kawaida — perfectly cooked plain steamed rice, Kenyan style. Fluffy, separate grains that serve as the ideal base for stews, curries, and grilled meats across every Kenyan kitchen.

Plain steamed rice might seem too simple to document, but getting it right — genuinely fluffy, separate grains with no sticking or mushiness — is a skill that transforms every meal it accompanies. The Kenyan method is simple: rinse thoroughly, cook in the right amount of water, never stir, and always rest. Follow these steps and your rice will be perfect every time.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan with tight-fitting lid, Measuring cups

  • 2 cups long-grain or basmati rice
  • 3 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp vegetable oil — optional

Method

  1. Rinse the rice 3—4 times under cold running water until completely clear.
  2. Drain well.
  3. Combine water, salt, and oil (if using) in a saucepan. Bring to a boil.
  4. Add the rinsed rice. Stir once gently.
  5. Reduce heat to the very lowest setting. Cover tightly.
  6. Cook undisturbed for 18—20 minutes.
  7. Remove from heat. Leave covered for 10 minutes.
  8. Fluff gently with a fork. Serve.

Chef Tips

  • Rinsing is the most important step — it removes surface starch that causes sticking.
  • The oil prevents grains from sticking together.
  • Never lift the lid during cooking.
  • The rest period allows the steam to redistribute evenly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not rinsing — produces sticky, clumped rice.
  • Cooking on high heat — burns the bottom before the top is done.
  • Stirring during cooking — breaks the grains and creates starch paste.
  • Skipping the rest — unevenly cooked rice.

The Science

Rinsing removes amylose (surface starch) that dissolves in cooking water and acts as glue between grains. Cooking at low heat allows the rice to steam in its own moisture rather than boiling, which keeps grains intact and separate.

Health Benefits

  • Good source of complex carbohydrates for energy.
  • Naturally gluten-free and fat-free when cooked without oil.
  • Easily digestible — gentle on the stomach.

Cultural Note

Plain steamed rice has become one of the most widely eaten starches across Kenya, alongside ugali. It is the neutral, versatile base that carries every Kenyan stew, curry, and grilled meat, and appears on tables from the coast to the highlands daily.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days. Freeze for up to 1 month.

Reheating: Sprinkle with 1—2 tbsp water and microwave covered, or reheat in a pot over low heat.

Serve with: Kenyan Beef Stew, Kuku Kienyeji, Ndengu Stew, Suqaar, Kachumbari, Sukuma Wiki
