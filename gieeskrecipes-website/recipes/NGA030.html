🫘 Ewa Agoyin

Ewa agoyin — ewa aganyin

Beans boiled without salt so they cook faster, then salted and boiled on until they collapse — mashed until almost no whole bean is left in sight.

The beans half of ewa agoyin is almost absurdly simple and the technique is in the order of operations. Salt goes in only once the beans are tender, because salting early slows them down; then they keep boiling until they are very soft, and a wooden spoon takes them the rest of the way. The target is explicit — mash until there is almost no whole bean in sight. What you want is a loose, grey-brown, structureless mass, which is exactly the right backdrop for the very dark pepper sauce that goes on top.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Wooden spoon

  • 3 cups black-eyed beans
  • Water, to cover generously
  • Salt, added only once the beans are tender

Method

  1. Pick over the beans and wash them.
  2. Cover generously with water and cook until tender, without adding any salt — the beans cook faster this way.
  3. When they are tender, add salt.
  4. Continue boiling until very soft.
  5. Use a wooden spoon to mash the beans until there is almost no whole bean in sight.
  6. Serve hot under the agoyin sauce, with agege bread.

Chef Tips

  • No salt until tender. The reason given is simply that the beans cook faster — and it is the opposite of what another named source does, boiling everything together from the start.
  • Go past tender. Ewa agoyin beans are not meant to hold their shape at all.
  • Mash in the pot with a wooden spoon, over low heat. Off the heat it stiffens and resists.
  • Keep it loose. It will tighten as it sits, and the sauce needs something to sink into.
  • Black-eyed beans here, though Nigerian honey beans — ewa oloyin — are the sweeter traditional choice and are widely preferred.
  • Make much more sauce than you think you need. The beans are plain by design and the whole dish is the ratio of sauce to beans.
  • Agege bread is not optional in Lagos. The beans go into a split loaf and that is the street version.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Salting at the start.
  • Stopping at tender rather than collapsed.
  • Mashing off the heat.
  • Draining the cooking water away.
  • Serving it without enough sauce.

Cultural Note

Ewa agoyin is Lagos street breakfast and the name carries its history: the Agoyin people of Benin and Togo brought it, and Lagosians adopted it so completely that it now reads as a Lagos dish. It is sold from a wheelbarrow or a basin with a loaf of agege bread beside it, and the sauce — black, oily and fierce — is what people queue for.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat with water to loosen. It sets solid when cold.

Serve with: Agoyin Sauce, Agege Bread, Dodo (fried plantain)
