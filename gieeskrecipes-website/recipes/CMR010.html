🟡 Achu Soup (Yellow Soup) with Achu Coco

Achu and sauce jaune

Beef, tripe and cow skin simmered in stock, which is cooled, then blended with melted red palm oil, ground achu spice and ground limestone until it turns bright yellow and emulsified — poured over mashed steamed cocoyam.

80 minTotal time
20 minPrep

Adamu’s achu soup has two parts. The soup: assorted meats are boiled in stock with salt and red pepper about 30 to 60 minutes (a pressure cooker roughly halves it), the stock is cooled to room temperature and about 6 cups reserved, and the red palm oil is melted until liquid. The stock, oil, achu spice, ground limestone, salt and bouillon are blended until smooth and emulsified; the page says to look for a bright yellow colour. The achu coco is cocoyam steamed unpeeled until tender, peeled, pureed in a food processor or mortar, and salted. If the soup separates, the page says to add a little limestone and pulse again.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, Blender, Steamer, Food processor or mortar and pestle

    For the soup

    • 1–2 pounds (450–900 g) assorted meat (beef, tripe, cow skin, smoked fish, dried crawfish, etc.)
    • Salt, 1/2–3/4 teaspoon (3–4 g) per pound of meat
    • Ground red pepper to taste (or a whole Scotch bonnet)
    • 6–7 cups (1 1/2 L) stock (or 6 cups water with 2 tablespoons beef bouillon)
    • 1/2 cup (118 ml) red palm oil
    • 2 tablespoons (15 g) ground achu spice
    • 1 tablespoon (10–11 g) bouillon powder or Maggi
    • 1 tablespoon (10 g) ground limestone

    For the achu coco

    • 4–5 pounds (1.8–2.25 kg) cocoyams (taro)
    • 2 green bananas or plantains (optional)
    • Salt to taste

    Method

      Soup

      1. Put the meat in a saucepan with salt, red pepper and the stock. Boil until tender, about 30–60 minutes or longer. A pressure cooker roughly halves it.
      2. Remove the meat. Cool the stock to room temperature and reserve about 6 cups.
      3. Heat the red palm oil until liquid (microwave or stove).
      4. Blend the stock, warmed oil, achu spice, limestone, salt and bouillon until smooth and emulsified. The soup should turn bright yellow. Adjust the seasoning.

      Achu coco

      1. Steam the cocoyams (and green bananas, if using) unpeeled until tender. Rinse in cold water and peel.
      2. Puree in a food processor or pound in a mortar. Salt it. Serve the meat with the yellow soup poured over, beside the achu.

      Chef Tips

      • Printed cues: ‘bright yellow color’ and emulsified soup; cocoyam ‘until tender’.
      • If the soup separates, the author adds a little limestone and pulses again.
      • The steaming time for the cocoyam is not printed — test for tenderness.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Blending hot stock with the oil instead of cooled stock.
      • Not checking the colour and emulsion before serving.
      • Over-handling the limestone: the page gives no safety guidance for it, so use a food-grade product.

      Health Benefits

      Cultural Note

      Adamu writes that the dish is fairly common in the Western and North West provinces. Other pages tie achu to the Bafut or the Ngemba; those pages were weaker sources and are not used here. The page gives no safety guidance for limestone or for hot palm oil.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Leftovers keep 3–4 days refrigerated or 4–5 months frozen (page note).

      Reheating: No reheating instructions are printed.

      Serve with: Achu coco (mashed cocoyam), Njama njama, Taro pilé
