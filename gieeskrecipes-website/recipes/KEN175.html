🍲 Pokot Goat Soup

Supu ya Mbuzi ya Pokot

Pokot Goat Soup — bone-in goat simmered for hours with wild highland herbs, onions, and simple spices into a deeply nourishing, clear, restorative broth. The ceremonial and healing soup of the Pokot people.

Pokot goat soup is both sustenance and medicine — prepared for those who are unwell, for mothers after childbirth, for elders, and for ceremonies. The combination of slow-simmered goat bones and wild highland herbs creates a broth of extraordinary depth.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: West Pokot and Baringo Counties — Rift Valley

The Pokot people inhabit the mountainous region of West Pokot and Baringo. Their goat soup is prepared for celebrations, healing, and communal gatherings. Wild herbs collected from the highlands are incorporated for both flavour and medicinal benefit.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Ladle, Strainer

  • 1.5 kg bone-in goat — including neck and knuckle bones
  • 2 large onions — roughly chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp fresh ginger — sliced
  • 2 litres water
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • Fresh coriander — to garnish

Method

  1. Blanch goat bones: cover with cold water, bring to a boil for 5 minutes, discard water.
  2. In fresh water, combine all ingredients.
  3. Bring to a gentle boil. Reduce to lowest simmer.
  4. Cook for 90—120 minutes until meat falls off bones and broth is deeply flavoured.
  5. Skim foam regularly for a clear broth.
  6. Season to taste.
  7. Serve in bowls with meat and broth, garnished with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • The blanching step produces a clearer broth — do not skip it.
  • Wild herbs add the authentic Pokot character.
  • This soup should be clear golden, not murky.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling vigorously.
  • Skipping the blanching step.

The Science

Long gentle simmering of bones extracts collagen, converting it to gelatin that gives the broth its silky body.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in collagen and gelatin.

Cultural Note

Pokot goat soup is more than food — it is medicine and ceremony, representing the deep botanical knowledge of the Pokot people.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze for 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently.

Serve with: Ugali, Wild greens, As a standalone healing broth
