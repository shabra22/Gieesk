Pizza Marinara

Older than the Margherita and entirely dairy-free, this Neapolitan classic dresses the same slow-fermented dough with just tomato, garlic, oregano, and olive oil.

Despite the name, marinara pizza has no seafood at all — the name is thought to reference sailors' wives, who supposedly made this simple, long-keeping pizza for their husbands' voyages, since it contains no dairy that would spoil at sea.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Pizza stone or steel, Pizza peel

  • 500 g (4 cups) 00 flour or bread flour
  • 325 ml (1⅓ cups) water, room temperature
  • 10 g (1¾ tsp) fine sea salt
  • 2 g (½ tsp) active dry yeast
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in the water, then gradually mix in the flour and salt to form a shaggy dough.
  2. Knead for 10 minutes until smooth and elastic, then place in an oiled bowl, cover, and let rise at room temperature for 2 hours.
  3. Divide into 4 balls, shape each into a tight round, and refrigerate covered for at least 8 hours, ideally 24.
  4. Remove the dough 1–2 hours before baking to come to room temperature.
  5. Preheat the oven with a pizza stone or steel to its highest setting for a full hour.
  6. Stretch one dough ball by hand into a round, leaving a slightly thicker border.
  7. Spread crushed tomatoes over the base, scatter with sliced garlic and oregano, season with salt, and drizzle with olive oil.
  8. Slide onto the hot stone and bake for 6–8 minutes until the crust is puffed and charred in spots.
  9. Finish with an extra drizzle of good olive oil and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Slice the garlic thinly and evenly so it cooks through without burning in the hot oven.
  • Because there's no cheese to add moisture and richness, generous, good-quality olive oil matters even more here.
  • The same long-fermented dough used for Margherita works perfectly for marinara.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using pre-minced garlic, which burns far more easily than freshly sliced cloves in a hot oven.
  • Skimping on olive oil, leaving the pizza tasting flat without cheese to carry richness.
  • Overloading with oregano, which can turn bitter and overpowering in large amounts.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Marinara predates the Margherita and remains a point of pride among traditional Neapolitan pizzaiuoli, valued for showing off good tomatoes and dough with nothing to hide behind.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; keeps refrigerated for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat in a hot skillet or oven; avoid the microwave, which leaves the crust soggy.

Serve with: A simple arugula salad, A light Italian red or white wine
