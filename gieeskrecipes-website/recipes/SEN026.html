🥬 Soupe Kandia

Soupou kandja — soupe kandia

A puréed-okra stew scented with palm oil, cooked until the red oil rises back to the surface — meat, fish, crab, shrimp, dried fish and yet over white rice.

The Afrik version is the full-dress soupou kandia: oil, yet and meat first, then onion until it turns pink, tomato concentrate, water, fresh fish, dried fish, crab, shrimp and fresh chile, and finally the okra, washed, cut and puréed. A nokoss of pounded dried chile, nététou, garlic and green onion goes in. Palm oil is added last and the stew cooks gently ‘until the red oil rises to the surface’, which is the dish’s true finish line. The page prints almost no times and no quantities for half the pot, so the stew is made by cue, not by the clock.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Mortar for the nokoss, Blender or mortar for the okra

  • 1 kg rice
  • 1/4 litre palm oil
  • 1 kg okra
  • 1 good slice of fish
  • 250 g meat
  • A little shrimp, 4 crabs, 1 slice of smoked fish, 1 piece of yet, 1 piece of dried fish — quantities not printed for shrimp and smoked fish
  • 1 fresh chile, dried chile (quantity not printed), nététou (quantity not printed), 3 garlic cloves, green onion (quantity not printed), onion (quantity not printed)
  • 1 tbsp tomato concentrate, 2 tbsp oil
  • Salt — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Heat the 2 tablespoons of oil. Add the yet and the meat, and after a few moments the onion in pieces; let it turn pink.
  2. Add the tomato concentrate and cook 3 to 4 minutes.
  3. Add water to the pot with salt and bring to a boil to cook the meat.
  4. Add the fresh fish, dried fish, cleaned crabs (opened), shrimp and fresh chile, then let simmer.
  5. Wash the okra, cut and reduce it to a purée.
  6. Make the nokoss: pound the dried chile, nététou, garlic and green onion, and add it to the pot.
  7. Add the okra and let simmer, stirring from time to time so the sauce does not stick to the bottom.
  8. Add the palm oil and let cook; check seasoning and cook gently until the red oil rises to the surface.
  9. Cook the rice separately and serve the soup over white rice.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Until the red oil rises to the surface’ is the best finish cue anywhere in the soupou kandia sources — wait for it.
  • The nokoss of pounded chile, nététou, garlic and green onion is the same technique used in the mbakhal and in Nokoss.
  • Stir from time to time; puréed okra and palm oil catch on the bottom.
  • Almost no times are printed. Cook by sight: onions pink, meat cooked, okra puréed, oil red on top.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the palm oil early.
  • Leaving the pot unstirred.
  • Skipping the nokoss.
  • Removing the dish from the heat before the oil has risen.

Cultural Note

Soupou kandia is described in the Afrik introduction as a Senegalese staple from the Casamance that now turns up from Dakar to The Gambia. The names ‘soupe kandia’ and ‘soupou kandja’ are used interchangeably. Saveur calls the dish ‘undoubtedly a predecessor of Louisiana-style gumbo’ — a claim with no citation, recorded here as a claim.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated; 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat, covered, with a little water.

Serve with: Steamed white rice, Nokoss
