☕ Kenyan Coffee Cake

Keki ya Kahawa

Kenyan Coffee Cake — a deeply flavoured coffee sponge made with Kenya AA or Nyeri single-origin coffee, layered with coffee buttercream and dusted with cocoa. A celebration of Kenya's world-class coffee in cake form — the café dessert that makes visitors understand why Kenyan coffee is legendary.

Kenya produces some of the world's finest coffee — the volcanic soils of Nyeri, Kirinyaga, and the slopes of Mount Kenya yield beans with extraordinary brightness, fruit complexity, and depth. Using strong brewed Kenyan coffee in a cake amplifies these qualities — the result is a coffee cake with more character than any other.

Ingredients

Equipment: Two 20cm cake tins, Electric beater, Piping bag optional

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1½ tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 200g butter — softened
  • 1½ cups sugar
  • 4 large eggs
  • 4 tbsp strong brewed Kenyan coffee — cooled
  • 2 tsp coffee extract or additional 2 tbsp espresso
  • ½ cup milk

Coffee Buttercream

  • 200g butter — softened
  • 3 cups icing sugar
  • 3 tbsp strong Kenyan coffee
  • 1 tsp vanilla
  • Cocoa powder — for dusting

Method

  1. Preheat oven to 180°C. Grease and line tins.
  2. Beat butter and sugar until pale and fluffy — 5 minutes.
  3. Add eggs one at a time. Beat well.
  4. Combine coffee and milk.
  5. Alternate adding flour and coffee-milk mixture in three additions.
  6. Divide between tins. Bake 30—35 minutes.
  7. Cool completely.
  8. Make buttercream: beat butter until pale. Add icing sugar, coffee, and vanilla. Beat until fluffy.
  9. Fill and frost with coffee buttercream.
  10. Dust with cocoa powder.

Chef Tips

  • Use the strongest, most flavourful Kenyan coffee you can find — Nyeri AA or Kirinyaga are ideal.
  • Cool the coffee completely before adding to the batter — warm coffee melts the butter.
  • The coffee buttercream is the star — be generous.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Warm coffee in the batter — curdled mixture.
  • Under-beating the buttercream — not fluffy enough.

The Science

Coffee's aromatic compounds (furans, pyrazines, aldehydes) are released during brewing and partially survive baking. The fat in the butter carries these aromatic compounds into the cake crumb — the same reason coffee flavour is more intense in coffee ice cream (fat medium) than in coffee jelly.

Cultural Note

Kenyan coffee cake is a statement of pride — Kenya produces some of the world's most sought-after coffee, and using it in a cake is the café's way of saying: we know what we have, and we celebrate it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Room temperature 3 days. Refrigerate up to 5 days.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Iced Kenyan Coffee (KEN249), Kenyan Mocha (KEN250), Fresh Cream
