🧃 Sobolo

Sobolo — spiced hibiscus drink

The Ghanaian version of bissap, and traditionally on the spicy side — grains of selim, African nutmeg, cloves, peppercorn and grains of paradise.

Sobolo is hibiscus steeped with a serious load of spice, and its author puts the difference plainly: this is the Ghanaian version of bissap, and in Ghana we pack in the spices. Fifteen grains of selim, seven African nutmegs, two tablespoons of cloves, two dried chillies, a tablespoon of black peppercorn and a teaspoon of grains of paradise are blended with a whole pineapple, a third of a cup of ginger and a cup of pitted dates. The pineapple skin and leaves go into the pot too. It steeps at a rolling boil for at least thirty minutes, uncovered so it does not splash, and makes fifteen cups.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Fine mesh strainer or nut-milk bag, Bottles

  • 2 cups dried hibiscus — sorrel
  • 1 pineapple, with its skin and leaves reserved
  • 2 large thumbs, about 1/3 cup, ginger, peeled
  • 1 cup dates, pitted
  • 15 grains of selim — hwentia
  • 7 African nutmeg
  • 2 tbsp cloves
  • 2 dried chillies
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorn
  • 1 tsp grains of paradise
  • 16 cups water — see the note on this quantity
  • Sugar to taste, about 3/4 cup brown sugar

The spices are all optional. See the author's substitution note.

    Method

    1. Soak the pineapple in water and vinegar 2 to 3 minutes, then cut it, reserving the skin and leaves.
    2. Soak and drain the dates.
    3. Blend the pineapple flesh, ginger, spices and dates until smooth.
    4. Put the pineapple leaves, the skin, the blended mixture and the spices into a pot with 10 cups of water and bring to the boil. Do not cover the pot, otherwise it will splash.
    5. Add the sorrel and bring the heat down to allow the mixture to steep at a rolling boil.
    6. Let it steep for at least 30 minutes, then turn off the stove.
    7. Add the sugar a quarter cup at a time, to taste — about 3/4 cup of brown sugar in all.
    8. Let it cool completely, to room temperature.
    9. Strain through a fine mesh strainer or a nut-milk bag.
    10. Bottle and refrigerate.

    Chef Tips

    • Do not cover the pot. Her instruction and her reason — it will splash, and hibiscus stains everything it touches.
    • The pineapple SKIN and LEAVES go in the pot, not in the bin. That is where a lot of the flavour is and it is the detail most recipes omit.
    • Steep at a rolling boil for at least thirty minutes, not a simmer. Thirty is a floor, not a target.
    • Sugar a quarter cup at a time after the heat is off. Three quarters of a cup is where she landed, but hibiscus sourness varies with the batch.
    • Cool completely before straining. Hot hibiscus through a nut-milk bag is a burn waiting to happen and it strains cloudier.
    • All the spices are optional, by her own note: you can remove them entirely and replace them with an extra half tablespoon of cloves and two cinnamon sticks, or not replace them at all.
    • Be aware of a gap in the printed recipe — sixteen cups of water is listed but only ten go into the pot. The other six are never placed.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Covering the pot.
    • Discarding the pineapple skin and leaves.
    • Simmering rather than holding a rolling boil.
    • All the sugar at once, while it is still hot.
    • Straining it hot.

    Health Benefits

    • Hibiscus calyces are very high in anthocyanins and vitamin C, and trials associate them with modest blood-pressure reduction.
    • Ginger, cloves and grains of paradise all carry documented anti-inflammatory compounds.
    • Vegan, caffeine-free and far lower in sugar than a commercial soft drink.
    • Dates sweeten while adding potassium and fibre.

    Cultural Note

    Sobolo is poured at naming ceremonies, weddings and funerals, and it is the drink a Ghanaian household makes in a stockpot rather than a jug. The spicing is the point — fifteen grains of selim and seven African nutmegs is not a cautious recipe, and the author says as much.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Up to a week refrigerated in sealed bottles.

    Reheating: Served cold. Do not reheat.

    Serve with: Kelewele, Spicy Coated Groundnuts, Chichinga
