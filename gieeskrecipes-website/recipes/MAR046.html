🍆 Zaalouk (Eggplant and Tomato Salad)

Zaalouk (زعلوك)

Charred eggplant mashed into a garlicky tomato sauce spiced with paprika and cumin, eaten as a cooked salad or dip with bread.

1hTotal time
15mPrep
45mCook
4Servings
190Calories
EasyDifficulty

The eggplants are broiled close to the element for 20 to 25 minutes until the skins are charred and puckered and crumble like burnt paper, leaving very soft flesh. Meanwhile tomatoes cook down with garlic, herbs, oil and spices into a rich sauce in 10 to 20 minutes; the scooped flesh goes in and is mashed and cooked 5 to 10 more minutes, or longer for a drier, pastier salad.

Ingredients

Equipment: Broiler and baking sheet, Skillet

  • 2 large eggplants (or 3 medium)
  • 2 medium or large tomatoes (or more to taste)
  • 6 cloves garlic, pressed or finely chopped (or to taste)
  • 2 tablespoons chopped fresh parsley (approx.)
  • 2 tablespoons chopped fresh cilantro (approx.)
  • 1 teaspoon salt (or to taste)
  • 2 teaspoons paprika (or to taste)
  • 2 teaspoons cumin (or to taste)
  • 4 tablespoons olive oil (approx.; no need to measure)
  • Cayenne pepper or whole chili peppers (optional; to taste)
  • Lemon juice or small wedge of lemon (optional; to taste)
  • Chopped parsley or cilantro (optional; for garnish)
  • Olive oil (optional; to drizzle on top)

Method

  1. Trim the stems and cut the eggplants in half lengthwise. Arrange skin side up on a foil-lined baking sheet and broil as close to the element as possible.
  2. Roast about 20 to 25 minutes, until the skins are charred and puckered, with sections that crumble or crack like burnt paper when pressed. The flesh will be very soft.
  3. Meanwhile peel, seed and chop the tomatoes. Put them in a skillet with the oil, garlic, herbs and spices (and the optional cayenne or chilies). Cook over medium heat, stirring frequently, until the tomatoes break down and a rich sauce forms, usually 10 to 20 minutes.
  4. When the eggplant is cool enough to handle, scoop the flesh from the skin and add it to the tomato mixture. Mash any firm bits with a spoon or fork.
  5. Cook 5 to 10 minutes to blend flavours, or longer for a drier, pastier salad. Taste, adjust and serve with bread.

Chef Tips

  • Cues quoted: skins charred and puckered; flesh very soft; tomatoes broken down into a rich sauce.
  • The page offers a one-pot alternative where chopped eggplant and tomato cook covered with 1/4 to 1/3 cup water until soft enough to mash.
  • Drizzle olive oil on top to serve.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-charring the eggplant.
  • Leaving it watery.
  • Skipping the herbs.

Cultural Note

The author describes zaalouk as a Moroccan cooked salad of eggplants and tomatoes usually eaten as a dip with Moroccan bread, and says she learned it from her mother-in-law.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 4 days in the fridge.

Reheating: Serve cold or warm gently.

Serve with: Khobz, Chermoula
