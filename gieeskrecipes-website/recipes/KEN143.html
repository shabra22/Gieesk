🍚 Kenyan Coconut Rice Pudding

Uji wa Nazi na Wali

Kenyan Coconut Rice Pudding — short-grain rice slowly simmered in coconut milk with cardamom, cinnamon, and vanilla until thick, creamy, and fragrant. A comforting Swahili coastal dessert that is simultaneously simple and luxurious.

Coconut rice pudding is the coastal answer to a cold evening or the desire for something sweet, warm, and deeply comforting after a meal. The rice breaks down gently in the coconut milk, releasing its starch to create a naturally thick, creamy pudding fragrant with cardamom and cinnamon. Topped with toasted coconut and a dusting of cinnamon, it is a dessert that feels both homely and festive.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium heavy saucepan, Wooden spoon

  • 1 cup short-grain rice or pudding rice
  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 2 cups whole milk
  • 4—5 tbsp sugar
  • 4 cardamom pods — crushed
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt
  • 2 tbsp desiccated coconut — toasted, for garnish
  • Ground cinnamon — for dusting

Method

  1. Rinse the rice until water runs clear. Drain.
  2. Combine rice, whole milk, cardamom, and cinnamon stick in a heavy saucepan.
  3. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat, stirring frequently.
  4. Cook for 20—25 minutes, stirring regularly, until the rice is very soft and the mixture has thickened.
  5. Add coconut milk, sugar, vanilla, and salt. Stir well.
  6. Continue to simmer for 8—10 minutes, stirring constantly, until the pudding is thick and creamy.
  7. Remove the cinnamon stick and cardamom pods.
  8. Serve warm or cold, garnished with toasted coconut and a dusting of cinnamon.

Chef Tips

  • Short-grain rice releases more starch than basmati, creating a creamier pudding.
  • Stir frequently to prevent the rice from sticking to the bottom.
  • The pudding thickens further as it cools — serve slightly looser than desired consistency.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not stirring enough — burns on the bottom.
  • Overcooking after adding coconut milk — becomes too thick.

The Science

Short-grain rice has a higher amylopectin content than long-grain varieties. Amylopectin gelatinises readily and creates the thick, sticky, creamy texture characteristic of rice pudding. Long-grain rice has more amylose, which produces a firmer, less sticky result.

Cultural Note

Coconut rice pudding is one of the Swahili coast's most comforting desserts — the intersection of the Indian Ocean rice tradition and the coastal abundance of coconut milk.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days. Add milk when reheating to restore creaminess.

Reheating: Warm gently on stovetop with a splash of milk, stirring continuously.

Serve with: Fresh mango slices, Toasted coconut, Passion fruit coulis, Kenyan Masala Chai
