🌱 Mboume (Névadaye), Moringa-Leaf Sauce

Mboume / Névadaye

Moringa leaves steeped two minutes in boiling water to take out the bitterness, then cooked 40 minutes with raw ground peanut flour, meat, dried fish, cherry tomatoes and tomato paste until the sauce thickens and the peanut oil floats.

A thick leaf sauce from the Slow Food Foundation and FAO’s Senegal booklet of traditional products and dishes (‘de la terre à la table’), with recipes credited to chef Bineta Diallo Dioh. The moringa leaves, dried or fresh, are steeped in boiling water for two minutes to remove bitterness and drained; they go into a pot with 500 g of raw peanut flour, 500 g of meat, 150 g of dried fish, 100 g of cherry tomatoes, 50 g of tomato paste and an onion, and cook 40 minutes. The printed cue is that at the end of cooking the sauce becomes thick and the peanut oil floats to the surface. It is served with couscous, millet or maize.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Strainer

  • Dried or fresh moringa leaves — quantity not printed
  • 500 g raw peanut flour
  • 500 g meat
  • 150 g dried fish
  • 100 g cherry tomatoes
  • 50 g tomato paste
  • 1 onion
  • Salt, pepper and chilli (optional) — quantities not printed

Method

  1. Steep the moringa leaves in boiling water for 2 minutes to remove bitterness, then drain.
  2. Put the meat, the leaves and all the other ingredients in a pot and cook for 40 minutes, then add salt.
  3. Lower the heat and adjust the seasoning. At the end of cooking the sauce should be thick and the peanut oil should float on the surface.
  4. Serve with couscous, millet or maize.

Chef Tips

  • The printed doneness cue: the sauce thickens and the peanut oil rises to the surface.
  • The two-minute steep removes the bitterness of the leaves.
  • The page prints no pot size or amount of water; add water as needed to keep the sauce from catching.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the bitterness steep.
  • Adding salt before the end of the 40 minutes — the page salts after cooking.
  • Stopping before the oil rises.

Health Benefits

  • Moringa leaves are rich in iron, vitamin A and calcium.
  • Contains peanuts.

Cultural Note

The booklet notes moringa originated near the Himalayas and has been present in Africa since the end of ancient Egypt. No region or ethnic group is stated for this recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Warm gently, adding a little water.

Serve with: Couscous, Millet, Maize
