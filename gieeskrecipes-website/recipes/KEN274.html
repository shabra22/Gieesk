🦞 Kenyan Garlic Butter Lobster

Kambamtupa wa Siagi na Vitunguu Saumu

Kenyan Garlic Butter Lobster — whole Indian Ocean lobster halved and grilled with a generous garlic butter, fresh herbs, and lemon. The pinnacle of coastal Kenyan luxury dining — when the lobster is this fresh, simplicity is the finest approach.

Indian Ocean spiny lobster (rock lobster) from the Kenyan coast is one of the finest lobsters in the world. Halved and grilled with good garlic butter and the best lemon, it is served at Mombasa's finest hotels as the ultimate expression of coastal luxury.

Ingredients

Equipment: Sharp heavy knife for splitting lobster, Charcoal grill or broiler, Basting brush

  • 2 whole spiny lobsters (600—800g each)

Garlic Butter

  • 100g unsalted butter — softened
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced to paste
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • Lemon halves
  • Fresh herb garnish

Method

  1. Make garlic butter: beat softened butter with garlic, herbs, lemon, salt, and pepper until combined.
  2. Split lobsters: place on a board shell-side up. Insert a sharp knife at the head. Push down firmly to split in half. Remove the stomach sack.
  3. Crack the claws slightly to ensure even cooking.
  4. Place cut-side up on the grill.
  5. Spread garlic butter generously over the exposed flesh.
  6. Grill for 8—12 minutes depending on size, basting every 3 minutes.
  7. The flesh should be just opaque throughout.
  8. Serve with extra garlic butter and lemon.

Chef Tips

  • The garlic butter made in advance and refrigerated can be sliced over the lobster for a restaurant presentation.
  • Do not overcook — overcooked lobster is rubbery and expensive regret.
  • Rest 2 minutes before serving.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking — rubbery, dry flesh.
  • Under-basting — dry exterior.

The Science

Lobster muscle proteins are particularly sensitive to heat — they denature and coagulate fully between 60—70°C, becoming tough and rubbery if held at high temperatures for too long. The shell's opacity change from translucent to white is the visible indicator of protein coagulation — white throughout means cooked.

Cultural Note

Garlic butter lobster at a Mombasa beachside hotel is one of Kenya's greatest dining experiences — the Indian Ocean's finest crustacean, simply prepared, eaten with the sound of waves and the smell of the sea.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Must be eaten immediately — lobster deteriorates extremely rapidly.

Reheating: Not recommended.
