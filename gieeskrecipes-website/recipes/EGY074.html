🍋 Lemon-Mint Juice (Limoon bel Na’na’)

Lamoon bil-na'na' — ليمون بالنعناع

Peeled whole lemons blended with mint, ice and sugar until frothy and smooth into a bright green, tangy frozen-style lemonade.

20mTotal time
20mPrep
4Servings
70Calories
EasyDifficulty

Only the yellow zest is peeled from the lemons, the pith and seeds are removed, and the fruit is quartered. It is blended with water, ice, mint and sugar at high speed for a few minutes until frothy and smooth, poured into a pitcher, thinned with water if too tangy, and served immediately before the ice melts and the bright green turns a dull brown.

Ingredients

Equipment: High-speed blender, Pitcher

  • 4 whole lemons, unwaxed (unsprayed)
  • 10-15 mint leaves
  • 2 cups cold water
  • ¼ cup milk, full fat or skimmed, optional
  • 1/4-1/2 cup white sugar or monk fruit sugar
  • 1 cup ice cubes

Method

  1. Peel only the yellow zest from the lemons, remove the pith and seeds, and quarter the lemons.
  2. Blend the lemon, mint, water, ice and sugar at high speed for a few minutes until frothy and smooth.
  3. Pour into a pitcher; add more water if too tangy.
  4. Serve immediately, before the ice melts and the bright green colour turns into a dull brown shade.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: frothy and smooth; bright green. The card's blending step omits the mint, though mint is listed; the body blends the mint and strains.
  • Do not substitute lime, says the author.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the pith on, which makes it bitter.
  • Waiting to serve, since the colour fades quickly.

Cultural Note

Nermine Mansour says Egyptian lemons and mint grow widely in Egypt and that her recipe was inspired by a lemon tree in her in-laws' California garden.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best served at once.

Reheating: Serve cold.

Serve with: Ice
