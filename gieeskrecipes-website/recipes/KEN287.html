🍋 Kenyan Lemon Tart

Tart ya Ndimu

Kenyan Lemon Tart — a buttery sweet pastry shell filled with a silky, tart lemon curd baked until just set, dusted with icing sugar and blowtorched for a brulée finish. The classic tart that showcases Kenya's abundant, sharp-flavoured lemons.

A properly made lemon tart — the passionfruit version's citrus sibling — is one of the greatest pastry achievements. Kenya's lemons are sharp, fragrant, and perfectly suited to this preparation. The brulée top (or simple icing sugar dusting) adds a textural contrast to the silky filling.

Ingredients

Equipment: 23cm tart tin, Small saucepan, Oven (160°C), Blowtorch optional

    Sweet Pastry (same as KEN283)

      Lemon Curd Filling

      • 4 eggs
      • 2 egg yolks
      • 150g sugar
      • 120ml fresh lemon juice (3—4 large lemons)
      • Zest of 2 lemons
      • 100g butter — cubed

      Finish

      • Icing sugar for dusting

      Method

      1. Make and blind bake sweet pastry shell as per KEN283.
      2. Make lemon filling: whisk eggs, yolks, sugar, lemon juice, and zest in a saucepan over medium heat.
      3. Stir constantly until mixture thickens enough to coat a spoon — 8—10 minutes.
      4. Remove from heat. Whisk in cold butter piece by piece.
      5. Strain into the baked shell.
      6. Bake at 160°C for 15—18 minutes until just set with a slight wobble.
      7. Cool completely. Refrigerate.
      8. Dust with icing sugar before serving, or blowtorch for a brulée effect.

      Chef Tips

      • Cook the curd over medium-low heat — high heat scrambles the eggs.
      • A slight wobble is right — it sets fully during refrigeration.
      • The brulée finish requires a blowtorch — icing sugar is the home alternative.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Scrambled egg curd — too high heat.
      • Over-baking in the shell — too firm.

      The Science

      Lemon curd sets through the same protein coagulation as crème pâtissière — egg proteins set between 60—85°C. The high acid content of lemon juice speeds coagulation (acid destabilises egg proteins at lower temperatures) and prevents curdling by creating a more acidic environment that promotes gradual protein network formation.

      Cultural Note

      The Kenyan lemon tart demonstrates the depth of pastry skill in Nairobi's finest bakeries — a technically demanding classic executed with precision, using Kenya's abundant, sharp lemons to produce a dessert of international standard.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

      Reheating: Serve cold or room temperature.

      Serve with: Whipped cream, Fresh berries, Kenyan Iced Coffee (KEN249)
