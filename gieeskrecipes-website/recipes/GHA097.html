🍚 Ghana Jollof Rice

Jollof rice — party-perfect jollof

Long grain white rice, not parboiled, baked covered in a 350-degree oven — and the colour comes from tomatoes, not palm oil.

Ghanaian jollof differs from Nigerian in a way its author states plainly: the distinctive red comes from tomatoes and tomato paste, not red palm oil, and it is somewhat reminiscent of Spanish rice. Two cups of long grain white rice, not parboiled, go into a roasting pan with browned chicken and four cups of hot stock, and bake covered for fifteen to twenty minutes, then another ten to fifteen. Her own solution to the mushiness problem is the oven itself, where the heat is evenly distributed rather than only on the bottom. Stir from the outside in, because the edges brown faster.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large roaster or casserole with a lid, Heavy skillet, Oven, Wooden spoon

  • 2 cups long grain white rice, not parboiled
  • 4 cups water, broth or a combination — including up to a cup of drained tomato juice
  • 2 lb boneless skinless chicken, cubed — or 3 lb with bones, cut small
  • 2 cups fresh grated tomatoes, seeded — or 2 cups well-drained chopped, or 2 cups pureed
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped (a good cup)
  • 3 good-sized cloves garlic, about 1 tbsp, minced or crushed
  • 1 tbsp freshly peeled, grated ginger
  • 5 tbsp (1/3 cup) tomato paste
  • 1/3-1/2 cup (5-8 tbsp) peanut or other vegetable oil
  • 2 tsp salt or to taste — plain, adobo or other seasoning salt
  • 1 tsp curry powder, or about 1/2 tsp thyme
  • About 1/2 tsp dried ground cayenne, or chopped fresh chilli
  • 1 bay leaf (optional)
  • 1/2 tsp black or white pepper (optional)

Vegetables, about 2-3 cups

  • 1 carrot, peeled and diced
  • Frozen green peas
  • Bell peppers or green beans, or frozen mixed vegetables

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken: mix about 1/4 cup of the chopped onion with a little of the garlic and ginger, about 1/2 tsp salt, 1/8 tsp black or white pepper and 1/8 tsp red pepper.
  2. Heat 3 or 4 tablespoons of oil in a heavy skillet. Brown the chicken on medium-high in 2 batches, adding another 1/4 cup chopped onion and another tablespoon of oil if necessary.
  3. Move the chicken to a large roaster or casserole.
  4. Bring the 4 cups of broth, water or tomato juice to the boil with the bay leaf and keep warm.
  5. Preheat the oven to 350 degrees Fahrenheit, with the rack low enough for the covered pan.
  6. Add another 3 to 4 tablespoons of oil to the skillet with the remaining onion, garlic, ginger and the curry powder or thyme. Stir-fry a few minutes.
  7. Stir in the tomatoes, tomato paste, pepper and salt.
  8. Add the 2 cups of rice to the roasting pan with the chicken.
  9. Pour the hot liquid into the skillet and stir to loosen the onion, then pour it all into the roasting pan, stir well, cover and put in the oven.
  10. Cook covered 15 to 20 minutes. Check once for doneness and liquid, adding a little water or broth if needed. Stir the rice from the outside in, as the outside edges will brown faster than the inside.
  11. Return for another 10 to 15 minutes.
  12. Cook the vegetables separately and stir them in.

Chef Tips

  • The oven is her answer to mushy jollof, and she says so directly: cooking it in the oven distributes the heat evenly rather than only on the bottom.
  • Stir from the outside in. The edges of a roasting pan brown faster, and that one instruction prevents the usual unevenness.
  • Long grain white, NOT parboiled. That is the opposite of the Nigerian party-jollof convention, where parboiling is the whole technique.
  • What makes it Ghanaian rather than Nigerian, in her words: the red colour comes from the tomatoes and tomato paste, not from red palm oil, and it is somewhat reminiscent of Spanish rice.
  • Cook the vegetables separately and stir them in at the end, or add them during the final minutes. Boiled into the rice they go to mush.
  • It tastes even better the day after it is made, and it freezes nicely — her own note.
  • If you cook it on the stovetop instead, beware of burning the rice on the bottom and over-stirring it into mush. That is her explicit warning.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using parboiled rice.
  • Stirring from the middle out, or not stirring at all.
  • Adding the vegetables to the raw rice.
  • Cold liquid into the pan.
  • Stovetop cooking without watching the base.

Health Benefits

  • Tomato-led, so high in lycopene, which cooking makes more available.
  • A generous separate vegetable load keeps its vitamin C.
  • High in protein with the chicken.
  • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

Cultural Note

The jollof argument between Ghana and Nigeria is the most enjoyable food rivalry in the world and this recipe states the Ghanaian case clearly: tomato-red rather than palm-oil-red, long grain rather than parboiled. Its author's own aside — that it is always amusing to read this called the national dish of any one country, since it belongs to the whole region — is the wisest line in the literature.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. Better the next day, per the author.

Reheating: Steam, or microwave with a splash of water under a damp cover.

Serve with: Shito, Braised cabbage, Kelewele
