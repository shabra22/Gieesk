🥜 Ninac Loos

Ninac Loos — Lows

Roasted peanuts folded into golden caramel in a two-and-a-half to one ratio, then rolled into balls or pressed flat and cut — the peanut half of the pair Somalis serve as lows iyo sisin.

Same technique as sisin, different seed and a different balance: more nut to less sugar again, so what you taste is roasted peanut held together rather than candy with nuts in it. It sets fast and it sets hard, which means every decision about shape has to be made in the first minute after the pan comes off the heat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pan, Oiled board or parchment, Sharp knife

  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 1/2 cups roasted peanuts, skins rubbed off
  • 1 tsp oil, for the board and your hands

Method

  1. Oil the board, and have the peanuts weighed out and beside the hob before you start.
  2. Melt the sugar in a heavy pan over medium heat, swirling rather than stirring, until it is a golden caramel.
  3. Tip in the roasted peanuts and mix quickly until every nut is coated.
  4. Take the pan off the heat immediately.
  5. Either roll the mixture into balls with lightly oiled hands, or spread it onto the oiled board and press it flat.
  6. Cut the flat version into squares as soon as it has cooled just enough to hold a line, and let everything harden completely before lifting it.

Chef Tips

  • Work fast once the peanuts go in. It stiffens as it cools and within a minute it will not shape at all.
  • Use peanuts that are already roasted. Raw peanuts will not cook through in caramel, and they taste green.
  • Rub the papery skins off first — they burn in the hot sugar and leave a bitter note.
  • Oil your hands, not just the board, if you are rolling balls. Hot caramel sticks to everything else.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring the sugar as it melts, which crystallises it.
  • Leaving the pan on the heat after the nuts go in, which burns the caramel around them.
  • Waiting too long to shape or cut, after which it can only be broken.
  • Using salted roasted peanuts without accounting for it — the salt is not unwelcome, but it is a different sweet.

Health Benefits

  • Peanuts supply plant protein, niacin and monounsaturated fat.
  • The nut-heavy ratio means less sugar per piece than most caramel sweets.
  • Plant-based and naturally gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Lows and sisin are the pair — peanut and sesame, made the same way, served on the same plate, and named together. In southern Somalia they are among the favourite sweets, and both keep long enough in a tin to be there whenever someone calls.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight tin at room temperature, 3 weeks.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: Sisin, Shaah Cadays, Somali coffee
