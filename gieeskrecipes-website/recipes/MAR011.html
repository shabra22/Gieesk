🍗 Chicken Tagine with Prunes and Almonds, Rif-Style

Tajine de poulet aux pruneaux et amandes à la façon du Rif

A whole chicken rubbed with cumin and slowly cooked with onions, turmeric, ginger and cinnamon-spiced prunes, finished with fried blanched almonds. Wolfert's tribute to a Tangier legend.

This is Wolfert's recreation of a dish described to her by members of Tangier's literary set, who said Mohammed Mrabet cooked it; she could not reconstruct it from their descriptions, and Paul Bowles recalled the measurements from memory. The chicken is rubbed with salt, pepper and cumin and left for an hour, then steamed with the onions and spices, the prunes are simmered with cinnamon, and the almonds are browned in 4 or 5 tablespoons of oil.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy casserole with a tight lid, Parchment paper, Skillet for the almonds, Small pot for the prunes

  • One 3 1/4-pound chicken, preferably organic and air-chilled
  • Coarse salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 teaspoons ground cumin, preferably Moroccan, or more to taste
  • 12 ounces moist prunes, pitted
  • 2 to 3 teaspoons ground Ceylon cinnamon
  • 2 large yellow onions, halved and sliced lengthwise
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1 cup blanched whole almonds
  • Vegetable oil for frying

Method

  1. Rub the chicken inside and out with salt, pepper and the cumin. Let stand for 1 hour.
  2. Put the prunes in a pot with water to cover and the cinnamon; bring to a boil, reduce the heat and simmer for 10 minutes. Set aside.
  3. Layer the onions in the casserole with the turmeric, ginger and a little water, set the chicken on top, cover and steam for 15 minutes.
  4. Meanwhile brown the almonds in 4 or 5 tablespoons oil in a large skillet, then drain.
  5. Cover the chicken with a sheet of parchment paper and cook over the lowest possible heat for about 1 1/4 hours.
  6. Add the cooked prunes to the casserole, bring to a boil, and serve topped with the almonds.

Chef Tips

  • Wolfert says rubbing cumin into the chicken is not found elsewhere in Morocco; this is what makes it distinctive.
  • The source gives only elapsed time as a doneness guide, so check the thigh joint is loose and juices run clear.
  • Keep the heat as low as it will go for the long cook.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the 1 1/4 hours on higher heat, which dries the breast.
  • Cooking the prunes too long; they are only added back at the end.
  • Skipping the rest after rubbing.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Wolfert's headnote says the dish was described to her by members of Tangier's literary set, who said Mohammed Mrabet cooked it, and that Paul Bowles recalled the measurements from memory. She writes that rubbing cumin into the chicken is not found elsewhere in Morocco.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 3 days in the fridge; add almonds fresh.

Reheating: Warm gently covered; crisp almonds in a dry pan.

Serve with: Khobz
