🍲 Egusi Soup, Caking Method

Obe egusi

Six hundred grams of ground melon seed stirred into the stock and left to cake, broken and watered again and again for twenty-five minutes — until the clear egusi oil rises on its own.

There are four ways to make egusi soup and this is the one with the best doneness cue. The ground seed goes into the meat stock with no oil at all, cooks until it cakes into a solid mass, gets broken up and watered, and cakes again — repeatedly, for about twenty-five minutes. At that point the seed's own clear oil floats to the surface, and that is your signal. Only then does the palm oil go in, and seven minutes later the two have blended to a light yellow. More egusi and less oil than the frying method, and the site that publishes both calls this one the healthier of the two.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Wooden spatula, Dry mill or blender

  • 4 1/2 cups (600 g) egusi (melon) seeds, ground
  • 2 cooking spoons red palm oil
  • 1 litre water, for the first boil
  • Beef: best cut and shaki (cow tripe)
  • Fish: dry fish and stockfish
  • 3 tbsp ground crayfish
  • Pepper and salt, to taste
  • Vegetable: Nigerian pumpkin leaves, spinach or bitter leaves
  • 3 small stock cubes
  • 1 ogiri okpei (optional)

Method

  1. Boil the shaki, stockfish and dry fish in 1 litre of water with the stock cubes until done. The first sign of completion is that the cuts will start curling on themselves.
  2. Add the beef and cook on medium until done.
  3. Add the crayfish and pepper, stir, then remove all the meat from the stock and set it aside.
  4. Add the ground egusi to the stock and stir. If the stock is not enough to give you a medium consistency, add some water.
  5. Cover and cook until the egusi cakes. Stir, and add water gradually.
  6. Repeat for about 25 minutes. After about 25 minutes you will notice the clear egusi oil coming to the surface of the soup.
  7. Now add the red palm oil and the bitter leaves, with pepper and salt.
  8. Cook 7 minutes, until the palm oil and egusi form a good blend of light yellow colour.
  9. Return the meat and fish. Add the softer vegetables now.
  10. Season, and simmer a maximum of 2 minutes.
  11. Take it off the heat and let it stand 5 minutes before serving.

Chef Tips

  • The clear oil rising at twenty-five minutes is the cue, and it is the best one in any egusi method. It is the seed's own oil, not the palm oil, which has not gone in yet.
  • No palm oil at the start. That is the entire difference from the frying method, and it is why this version takes more egusi and less oil.
  • Caking is supposed to happen. Let it set into a mass, break it, add water, let it cake again. The repetition is the technique.
  • Light yellow is the second cue, seven minutes after the palm oil goes in. If it still looks orange and separate, it is not blended.
  • Curling cuts tell you the shaki and stockfish are done — before the beef goes anywhere near the pot.
  • Take the meat out before the egusi goes in. It gets in the way of the caking and breaking.
  • Two minutes maximum on the soft vegetables, then five minutes standing off the heat. Egusi soup keeps cooking in its own mass.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the palm oil at the start, which makes this the frying method.
  • Stirring continuously to stop it caking.
  • Giving up before the clear oil surfaces.
  • Leaving the meat in during the caking stage.
  • Cooking the soft greens more than two minutes.

Health Benefits

  • Melon seed is high in protein, magnesium and zinc.
  • No added frying fat — the seed's own oil does most of the work.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.
  • Bitter leaf and pumpkin leaf add iron, calcium and vitamins A and C.

Cultural Note

Egusi is the most widely eaten soup in Nigeria and belongs to no single group, which is why it appears here as national rather than Yoruba or Igbo. The disagreement over technique maps loosely onto region — one named source calls the watery style Igbo while a Yoruba author claims the same style as Ijebu — but the argument runs through families as much as through peoples, and people are genuinely partisan about it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 5 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. It thickens considerably when cold.

Reheating: Low heat with a little water, stirring to bring the oil back into the blend.

Serve with: Iyan, Amala, Eba or garri
