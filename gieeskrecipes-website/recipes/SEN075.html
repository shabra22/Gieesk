🍌 Alloco, Fried Plantain

Alloco — banane plantain frite

Plantain peeled and cut into rounds 1/2 to 1 cm thick, fried in a litre of oil, stirred now and then until a good brown — and tasted: if it is tender inside, it is done.

Two printed cues make this the most testable recipe in the street set: ‘the plantain must take on a good brown colour’ and ‘taste it: if it is tender inside, it is cooked’. 1.5 kg of plantain is peeled and cut into fairly thick rounds, 1/2 to 1 cm; a litre of palm, peanut or sunflower oil is heated in a deep fryer or pot; the rounds are fried, stirred from time to time so the slices colour evenly. No temperature is printed. The page does not frame the dish as specifically Senegalese — alloco is a pan-West-African street food.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep fryer or heavy pot, Slotted spoon

  • 1.5 kg plantains
  • 1 litre palm, peanut or sunflower oil

Method

  1. In a fryer or heavy pot, heat the litre of oil.
  2. Meanwhile, peel the plantains and cut them into fairly thick rounds, 1/2 to 1 cm.
  3. Fry, stirring from time to time so the slices colour evenly, until the plantain takes on a good brown colour.
  4. Taste one: if it is tender inside, it is done. Drain and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Two printed cues: the brown colour and the tenderness when tasted.
  • No temperature is printed; keep the heat moderate so the inside cooks before the outside burns.
  • Ripeness is not stated — the recipe does not say yellow or black plantain.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cutting slices of uneven thickness.
  • Frying cold, which soaks up oil.
  • Pulling them when brown but still firm inside — taste, as the page says.

Cultural Note

Savoura frames the dish simply as ‘Alloco’, a fried plantain; the research notes that Afrik does not present it as specifically Senegalese. It is street food across West Africa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten the same hour.

Reheating: Recrisp in a hot oven.

Serve with: Grilled fish, Pepper sauce, Dibi
