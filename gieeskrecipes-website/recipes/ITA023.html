Cacio e Pepe

Cacio e Pepe — 'cheese and pepper' — is deceptively simple in ingredients yet notoriously tricky in execution, transforming just Pecorino Romano, black pepper, and pasta water into a glossy, clinging sauce.

This Roman classic has just three ingredients beyond the pasta itself, which means there's nowhere for technique to hide — the emulsion of starchy pasta water and finely grated Pecorino is what separates a silky sauce from a clumped, broken mess.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large skillet, Mortar and pestle or spice grinder, Tongs

  • 400 g (14 oz) tonnarelli or spaghetti
  • 200 g (2½ cups) finely grated Pecorino Romano
  • 2 tsp whole black peppercorns, plus more to taste
  • Fine sea salt, for the pasta water

Method

  1. Toast the whole peppercorns in a dry skillet over medium heat for 1–2 minutes until fragrant, then coarsely crush with a mortar and pestle or the flat of a knife.
  2. Bring a large pot of lightly salted water to a boil — use less salt than usual, since Pecorino is already very salty.
  3. Add the crushed pepper to the dry skillet and toast for another minute to bloom its aroma, then add a ladleful of pasta water and simmer gently.
  4. Cook the pasta until just shy of al dente, reserving at least 2 cups of pasta water before draining.
  5. In a separate bowl, mix the grated Pecorino with a few tablespoons of pasta water, whisking to form a smooth, loose paste — this pre-emulsification prevents clumping later.
  6. Add the drained pasta to the skillet with the pepper, tossing with a splash more pasta water.
  7. Remove from heat, then add the cheese paste gradually, tossing continuously and vigorously, adding pasta water as needed until glossy and creamy.
  8. Serve immediately, off the heat — the sauce can seize if it gets too hot or sits too long.

Chef Tips

  • Combining the cheese with pasta water into a paste BEFORE adding it to the hot pasta is the most reliable way to avoid clumping.
  • Work off direct heat when adding the cheese — too much heat causes the proteins to seize into stringy clumps.
  • Freshly cracked pepper, toasted briefly, makes a noticeably more fragrant dish than pre-ground pepper.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding cheese directly to very hot pasta without pre-mixing with water first — this is the number one cause of a clumpy, broken sauce.
  • Over-salting the pasta water, since Pecorino already brings significant salt.
  • Letting the dish sit too long before serving, as the sauce thickens and can seize.

Cultural Note

Cacio e Pepe is considered one of Rome's 'four great pastas,' alongside Carbonara, Amatriciana, and Gricia — all built on the same foundational trio of guanciale, Pecorino Romano, and black pepper in various combinations.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately — the emulsified sauce does not hold or reheat well.

Reheating: Not recommended; the sauce breaks upon reheating. Make fresh each time.

Serve with: A simple arugula salad, Crisp white wine, Crusty bread
