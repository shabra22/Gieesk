🥞 Masa

Masa — waina, rice cake

A rice batter left eight to twelve hours in a warm place, then fried two and a half minutes on the first side and two on the second.

Masa is the north's fermented rice cake, eaten at breakfast with soup or with suya. Raw rice soaks overnight, is drained completely and blended with a yeasted sugar-and-akaun mixture, and a tablespoon and a half of cooked rice goes in as a starter. The batter then sits eight to twelve hours in a warm place to rise and ferment — a figure two independent named sources agree on exactly, which makes it the most reliable number in the northern record. Then three or four tablespoons per cake, flipped at two and a half minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, Blender, Frying pan, Ladle or large spoon

  • 1 1/2 cups raw rice
  • 1 1/2 tbsp cooked rice
  • 1 1/2 tsp active dry yeast
  • 4 tbsp sugar
  • 2 cups water at room temperature — see the note on the three water figures
  • 1/4 tsp ground akaun (cooking potash), optional
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • Oil, for wiping the pan

Method

  1. Soak the raw rice in water overnight.
  2. Dissolve the sugar and the akaun in water, add the yeast, and set aside 10 to 15 minutes until frothy.
  3. Drain the rice completely and blend it with the yeast mixture to a smooth batter. Add the cooked rice and blend again.
  4. Cover the batter in a bowl and leave it in a warm place 8 to 12 hours, to rise and ferment.
  5. Add the salt, and extra sugar if it needs it. Adjust the water to a pap-like consistency.
  6. Heat a pan over medium heat and wipe it with oil.
  7. Stir the batter and pour 3 to 4 tablespoons per cake.
  8. Flip after 2.5 minutes and cook the other side 2 minutes.
  9. Repeat until the batter is used up.

Chef Tips

  • Eight to twelve hours in a warm place. Two independent named sources give exactly this window, which makes it the most dependable figure in the northern repertoire.
  • The cooked rice is the starter — the backslop that carries the fermentation. Do not leave it out.
  • Drain the soaked rice completely before blending. Residual soaking water throws the batter consistency off.
  • Pap-like consistency after the ferment. If it has thickened too much overnight, let it down with water before you fry.
  • Two and a half minutes, then two. The second side always takes less.
  • Wipe the pan with oil rather than pouring it in. Masa is not fried, it is griddled.
  • Both documented sources use commercial yeast. The traditional wild or spontaneous leaven for masa is not documented anywhere consulted, and that gap is worth knowing about.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the cooked-rice starter.
  • Fermenting somewhere cold, which stalls it.
  • Blending with the soaking water still in.
  • Frying a batter that has thickened too far overnight.
  • Pouring oil into the pan rather than wiping it.

Health Benefits

  • Fermentation improves the bioavailability of minerals in the rice.
  • Griddled with barely any oil.
  • Gluten-free.
  • The fermentation makes it easier to digest than plain rice.

Cultural Note

Masa is breakfast across the north, sold from a griddle at the roadside and eaten with soup, with suya, or with honey. Daily Trust's reporting from Kebbi records it at marriage events, male circumcision, Quranic graduation, thanksgiving, turbaning, child naming ceremonies and festivals — which is to say it is both the most ordinary food imaginable and a thing that marks every occasion.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days at room temperature, 4 refrigerated.

Reheating: Dry pan, a minute a side.

Serve with: Miyan Taushe, Suya, Honey or sugar
