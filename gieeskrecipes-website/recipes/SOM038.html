🍝 Suugo Suqaar

Suugo Suqaar

The Somali pasta sauce — beef cut into tiny cubes rather than minced, browned with xawaash and simmered down in tomato for three quarters of an hour until the oil comes to the surface.

Italy left Somalia its pasta and Somalia returned the sauce with the spice blend in it. Two things make suugo Somali rather than Italian: the xawaash, and the beef in very small cubes instead of mince — partly because meat grinders were scarce, and partly because a sauce with real pieces of meat in it is the meat course as well as the sauce, which is a considerable economy in a large household.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor or blender, Heavy pot with a lid

  • 900 g (2 lb) beef, inside round, cut into very small cubes
  • 1 onion
  • 1/2 green pepper
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 120 ml (1/2 cup) olive oil
  • 1 tbsp xawaash spice blend
  • 2 x 800 g tins diced tomatoes, or 8 medium fresh tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp vegetable seasoning, or 4 bouillon cubes
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper, or to taste

Method

  1. Blend the onion, green pepper and garlic together to a rough purée.
  2. Heat the olive oil over medium-high heat, add the blended vegetables and cook for 2 minutes.
  3. Add the cubed beef, cover and cook for 10 minutes, until the meat has released its moisture and taken it back up.
  4. Once the pan is dry again, add the xawaash and brown the meat for 2 minutes.
  5. Add the tinned tomatoes and the tomato paste.
  6. Stir well, then add the vegetable seasoning and the sugar — the sugar is there to take the edge off the tomatoes, not to sweeten the sauce.
  7. Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 45 minutes.
  8. When the sauce has reduced and the oil starts to come up to the surface, stir in the cilantro and the black pepper. That surfacing oil is the sign it is done.

Chef Tips

  • Cut the beef very small — 5 mm cubes, not mince and not stewing chunks. The whole character of suugo is in that cut.
  • Wait for the meat to dry out again before the xawaash goes in. Spice added to a wet pan steams instead of frying and tastes raw in the finished sauce.
  • The oil rising to the surface is the doneness test. Until it does, the sauce has not reduced enough.
  • Eight fresh tomatoes replace the two tins when tomatoes are in season, which is what Somali cooks do at home.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using mince. It works, and Somali cooks abroad often do it, but the texture that makes suugo recognisable is gone.
  • Cutting the 45 minutes short, which leaves a thin, acidic sauce that has not come together.
  • Skipping the sugar, and then wondering why the tomatoes taste sharp.
  • Adding the cilantro at the start, where three quarters of an hour of simmering destroys it.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of protein, iron and zinc from the beef.
  • Cooked tomato is rich in lycopene, and the olive oil helps absorb it.
  • Naturally gluten-free — it is the pasta, not the sauce, that is not.

Cultural Note

Somali pasta is eaten with a heavier stew rather than an Italian-style sauce, and almost always with a whole banana on the side. Suugo suqaar is the everyday version of that stew, and the Somali restaurant staple known as federation — rice and spaghetti divided across one oval plate — is built on top of it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days. Freezes well for 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the hob with a splash of water.

Serve with: Isbaggeti Somali, Bariis Shaciiriyo Leh, Fresh banana
