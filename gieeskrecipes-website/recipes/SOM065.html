🍰 Doolshe Buuro

Doolshe Buuro

The Somali teashop pound cake — butter and oil together rather than butter alone, so it stays moist for days, flavoured the traditional way with cardamom or the modern way with vanilla and orange zest.

Pound cake was one of only a handful of cakes widely made in Somalia, second in popularity to the sponge. It was sold in teashops and baked at home by women whose recipes were closely guarded, and the flavouring was always cardamom — not because anyone preferred it to vanilla, but because cardamom was cheaper. The butter-and-oil combination is the refinement that keeps it from drying out by the second day.

Ingredients

Equipment: Stand mixer or electric beaters, 25 x 11 cm (10 x 4 inch) tube tin, Zester

  • 200 g (1 cup) granulated sugar
  • 125 ml (1/2 cup) unsalted butter, softened
  • 120 ml (1/2 cup) canola or vegetable oil
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 tsp salt (optional)
  • 1 tbsp vanilla extract, or 1 1/2 tsp ground cardamom for the traditional flavour
  • Zest of 1 orange (omit if using cardamom)
  • 280 g (2 cups) plain flour
  • 60 ml (1/4 cup) milk
  • 1 tbsp butter, for greasing the tin

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 177C / 350F and grease the tube tin with the tablespoon of butter.
  2. Zest the orange, if you are making the vanilla version.
  3. Put the sugar in the mixer bowl with the softened butter and the oil, and beat on high for 5 minutes, scraping the sides down once.
  4. Add the eggs and beat them in.
  5. Add the baking powder, the salt, the vanilla or cardamom, and the orange zest, and mix on high for 2 minutes.
  6. Add the flour and mix for 1 minute.
  7. Add the milk and mix for 1 minute more.
  8. Pour the batter into the greased tin and bake for 45 minutes, until a skewer comes out clean.
  9. Let it cool in the tin for 10 minutes before turning it out.

Chef Tips

  • Half butter and half oil is the point of this version. Butter alone gives a better flavour on the day and a dry cake on the second; the oil keeps it moist all week.
  • Cardamom is the traditional Somali flavouring, and a teaspoon and a half in place of the vanilla and zest gives you the teashop cake rather than the modern one.
  • Five minutes of beating the sugar, butter and oil is not an approximation. That is where the crumb comes from.
  • Baking margarine works in place of the butter, though a soft spread does not. Shortening can be swapped in at the same weight.
  • It keeps two days on the counter, a week refrigerated and six months frozen.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using butter straight from the fridge, which will not cream and leaves lumps through the batter.
  • Cutting the initial five-minute beat short, giving a dense, heavy cake.
  • Overmixing after the flour goes in, which develops the gluten and toughens it.
  • Turning it out of the tin immediately, when it is still fragile enough to break.

Health Benefits

  • Eggs and milk contribute protein and calcium.
  • Made with oil as well as butter, so it is lower in saturated fat than a classic pound cake.
  • Cut into twelve, which is the portion the tin is built for.

Cultural Note

Somali home bakers guarded their cake recipes closely, and for good reason — in a country where most kitchens had no oven, a woman who could reliably produce a cake in a pot on a charcoal stove had a genuinely scarce skill. Teashops sold them; households made them for guests.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days at room temperature, up to a week refrigerated, 6 months frozen.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature. A few seconds of warmth suits a refrigerated slice.

Serve with: Shaah Cadays, Somali coffee, Xalwo
