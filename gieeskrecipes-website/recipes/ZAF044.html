🌈 Seven Colours

Seven colours — sewe kleure

The Sunday plate — a roast or a stew at the centre and seven distinctly coloured things arranged around it, each cooked separately and none of them touching until they reach the plate.

Seven colours is not one recipe and it does not have a fixed seven. It is a way of building a Sunday plate: a hero meat and then as many separately coloured components as the household runs to — and it is usually more than seven. The point is abundance made visible, and it is read that way deliberately: this is the meal of families who spent generations apart under migrant labour, cooked on the one day everyone was home. Every family has its own set, and arguing about which seven is part of it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Every pot you own, A very large serving platter or individual dinner plates, Oven

    The centre — choose one

    • A roast leg of lamb, a whole roast chicken, or a beef stew

    The seven, or as many as you like

    • Beetroot, boiled and dressed with vinegar and sugar
    • Pumpkin or butternut, mashed with butter and cinnamon
    • Creamed spinach, or morogo greens
    • Coleslaw, dressed with mayonnaise and a little vinegar
    • Yellow rice with raisins, or plain white rice
    • Iimbotyi, or a three-bean salad
    • Chakalaka

    The starch and the gravy

    • Uphuthu or stiff pap
    • Thick gravy from the roasting pan or the stew

    Often on the plate as well

    • Potato salad
    • Caramelised onions
    • A green salad

    Method

    1. Decide the centre first and work backwards from its timing — a leg of lamb or a roast chicken sets the clock for everything else.
    2. Start the longest items: the beans if they need soaking and boiling, and the beetroot, which takes an hour and stains everything it touches.
    3. Get the meat into the oven or the stew onto the hob.
    4. Cook the pumpkin and mash it with butter and a little cinnamon. Keep it warm.
    5. Make the chakalaka, or take it out of the fridge if you made it yesterday, which is better.
    6. Cook the rice, and the pap if you are serving both. Both is common.
    7. Dress the coleslaw and the beetroot cold, and keep them cold. They are the two components that are meant to be.
    8. Wilt or cream the spinach at the last minute so it keeps its colour.
    9. Make the gravy from the roasting pan while the meat rests.
    10. Plate it: the meat at the centre, the components arranged around it in distinct blocks so no two of the same colour touch, and the gravy over the starch. Serve at once.

    Chef Tips

    • Keep the components separate on the plate. A seven colours plate where everything has run together has missed the entire point.
    • Make the chakalaka and the beetroot the day before. Both are better cold and both are better for the wait, and it takes two pots off Sunday morning.
    • Alternate warm and cold, and alternate colours. The contrast is the dish.
    • Count the pots before you start. This is a genuinely large cook and the failure mode is running out of hobs, not running out of skill.
    • Seven is a floor, not a target. Households routinely put nine or ten things on the plate and still call it seven colours.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cooking everything into a single tray, which turns it into a roast dinner.
    • Serving all of it hot. The beetroot and the coleslaw are cold components.
    • Cooking the spinach early, so it greys.
    • Starting everything at once instead of working back from the meat's timing.

    Health Benefits

    • Genuinely vegetable-heavy — six or seven different vegetables in one meal.
    • The cold components go on raw or barely cooked, so their vitamin C survives.
    • Very large by design. It is a once-a-week meal, and that is how it is eaten.

    Cultural Note

    Seven colours is a post-migrant-labour Sunday ritual and should be read as one. For generations the migrant labour system split families between the cities and the reserves, and Sunday was the day a household ate together. A plate loaded with as many separate, visible, distinctly coloured dishes as the family could manage is a statement about abundance, made by people who had every reason to make it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate the components separately up to 3 days. Assembled, it does not keep.

    Reheating: Reheat the hot components and plate fresh. The cold ones go straight from the fridge.

    Serve with: Umqombothi, at a celebration, Ginger beer, Malvapoeding, afterwards
