🐐 Chichinga Suya Goat Kebabs

Chichinga

Goat rather than beef, with a wet rub of a hundred and twenty-five grams of ground roasted peanuts — turned every two minutes for medium-rare.

MediumDifficulty

The goat version, and the only documented chichinga that uses a wet rub rather than a dry powder: a hundred and twenty-five grams of roasted peanuts ground with three garlic cloves, five centimetres of ginger, cayenne or chilli flakes, smoked paprika and two tablespoons of oil. Four hundred and fifty grams of diced boneless leg of kid goat marinates one to two hours minimum, preferably overnight. The grilling instruction is the most precise of the three: turn every two minutes for medium-rare, or every three for well done, then rest two minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or food processor, Skewers, Grill or barbecue, Tongs

  • 450 g boneless leg of kid goat, diced
  • 1 large red onion
  • 1-2 red, green or yellow peppers
  • Groundnut or vegetable oil
  • A few sprigs of fresh coriander

Spice rub

  • 125 g roasted peanuts
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 5 cm piece ginger
  • 1 tsp cayenne pepper, or red chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 tbsp groundnut or vegetable oil
  • Sea salt and coarse black pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Grind the roasted peanuts with the garlic, ginger, cayenne, smoked paprika and 2 tablespoons of oil to a thick rub.
  2. Season with sea salt and coarse black pepper.
  3. Coat the diced goat in the rub.
  4. Marinate 1 to 2 hours minimum, preferably overnight.
  5. Thread onto skewers with the onion and peppers.
  6. Grill, turning every 2 minutes for medium-rare, or every 3 minutes for well done.
  7. Rest 2 minutes.
  8. Scatter with fresh coriander and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Turn every two minutes for medium-rare, every three for well done. That is the most precise grilling instruction in the chichinga record and it works because a wet peanut rub needs constant movement to keep from burning.
  • A wet rub, not a dry powder — this is what distinguishes this version. Ground roasted peanuts with oil cling differently and char differently from tankora.
  • Overnight is better than the one-to-two-hour minimum, by her own framing. Goat benefits from the longer marinade.
  • Rest two minutes after grilling. Short, but it matters on small pieces of goat.
  • Kid goat, boneless leg, diced. Goat is the more common street meat in Ghana than the sirloin or beef the other documented versions use.
  • Smoked paprika is her addition and is not traditional. It reinforces the charcoal note if you are grilling indoors.
  • The seasoning quantities are genuinely ambiguous in the source — both 'to taste' and a specific pinch-and-teaspoon are printed and it is unclear which belongs where. Season by taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the skewers to sit between turns.
  • Using a dry powder rub, which belongs to the other versions.
  • A short marinade on goat.
  • Skipping the rest.
  • Grinding the peanuts without the oil, which gives a powder rather than a rub.

Health Benefits

  • Goat is leaner than beef or lamb and high in iron and vitamin B12.
  • Roasted peanuts supply monounsaturated fat, niacin and vitamin E.
  • Peppers are very high in vitamin C.
  • Grilled with minimal added oil.

Cultural Note

Goat is the street meat of West Africa and a wet peanut rub is closer to the Nigerian tsire suya tradition than to the dry tankora dusting of Ghanaian chichinga — which, given this author's Ghanaian-Irish background and London kitchen, is exactly the kind of crossing you would expect. All three documented versions are honestly chichinga.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best at once. 2 days refrigerated cooked.

Reheating: Briefly on a hot grill.

Serve with: Shito, Jollof Rice, Kelewele
