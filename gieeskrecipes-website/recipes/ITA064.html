🥟 Anolini in Brodo

Anolini di Parma in Brodo

Anolini in Brodo are Parma's small, round filled pasta parcels, traditionally stuffed with a rich stracotto (slow-braised beef) filling and served in a deeply savory capon broth.

Where Bologna has tortellini, Parma has anolini — round rather than ring-shaped, and traditionally filled with the reduction from a very long, slow beef braise rather than fresh meat, giving the filling remarkable depth.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot or Dutch oven, Food processor, Round cutter, Large pot for broth

  • 1 batch Fresh Egg Pasta Dough

Filling (stracotto)

  • 600 g (1.3 lb) beef chuck or shin, in one piece
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery stalk, roughly chopped
  • 250 ml (1 cup) dry red wine
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 100 g (1¼ cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1 egg
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a pinch
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Broth

  • 2 L (8 cups) good-quality beef or capon stock

Method

  1. Season the beef and brown it well on all sides in a heavy pot.
  2. Add the onion, carrot, and celery, cooking until softened.
  3. Pour in the red wine, scraping up browned bits, and simmer until reduced by half.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes, cover, and braise on very low heat for 3–4 hours, until the meat is completely fall-apart tender and the sauce is thick and deeply concentrated.
  5. Shred or finely chop the beef, discarding any large fat pieces, and mix with some of the concentrated braising sauce, the Parmigiano, egg, and nutmeg until you have a firm, cohesive filling. Season with salt and pepper. Chill until firm.
  6. Roll the pasta dough thin and cut into small rounds, about 4cm.
  7. Place a small dab of filling in the center of each round, fold in half to form a half-moon, and press to seal, then bring the two corners together and pinch to close, similar to tortellini but with a fuller, rounder shape.
  8. Bring the stock to a gentle simmer.
  9. Cook the anolini directly in the simmering broth for 3–4 minutes until they float and the pasta is tender.
  10. Serve immediately in bowls with the broth, with grated Parmigiano on the side.

Chef Tips

  • The long braise for the filling is what gives anolini its signature depth — there's no substitute for the time invested here.
  • Chilling the filling before shaping makes it much easier to portion neatly.
  • A rich, well-seasoned stock is just as important as the filling itself, since the broth is a central part of the dish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the beef braise, resulting in a filling that lacks the traditional deep, concentrated flavor.
  • Using a weak stock for the broth, undermining the whole dish.
  • Overfilling the anolini, making them difficult to seal properly.

Cultural Note

Anolini in Brodo is a treasured Christmas tradition in Parma, much as tortellini is in Bologna, with families often guarding closely held recipes for the stracotto filling passed down for generations.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Uncooked anolini freeze well for up to 2 months; the broth keeps refrigerated for up to 4 days.

Reheating: Cook frozen anolini directly in simmering broth, adding 1–2 extra minutes.

Serve with: A crisp Malvasia or Lambrusco wine, Nothing more — served simply in its broth
