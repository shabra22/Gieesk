Sisin — Zinoorho za Mashtaayta

Sesame brittle in the Bravanese proportion — two parts toasted seed to one part caramelised sugar, rolled out and cut into diamonds while still warm enough to give.

Two cups of sesame to one of sugar is a seed-heavy ratio, and it is what distinguishes sisin from the sweeter sesame candies made elsewhere: the seeds are the dish and the caramel only holds them together. In Brava it is called zinoorho za mashtaayta, and grandmothers kept it in large tins against the arrival of visitors. The technique turns on one habit — swirl the melting sugar, never stir it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pan, Oiled board or marble slab, or parchment, Rolling pin, Sharp knife

  • 2 cups sesame seeds
  • 1 cup granulated sugar — reduce to 3/4 cup if you want to roll it into balls
  • 1 tsp oil, for the board and the rolling pin

Method

  1. Toast the sesame seeds in a dry pan over medium heat for 4 to 5 minutes, stirring frequently, until they smell nutty and a few begin to pop. Tip them out and keep them nearby.
  2. Oil the board and the rolling pin now. Once the caramel is made there is no time to prepare anything.
  3. Put the sugar in the same pan over medium heat and let it melt gradually, 4 to 5 minutes. Swirl the pan to move it — do not stir, which makes the sugar crystallise and go grainy.
  4. When the sugar is a clear amber, tip in the toasted seeds and stir hard for about a minute, until every seed is coated.
  5. Turn the mixture straight onto the oiled board and roll it out to the thickness you want.
  6. Cut into diamonds or squares while it is still warm and pliable. For balls, use the smaller quantity of sugar and shape them by hand while very warm.
  7. Let them cool completely and harden before lifting them off.

Chef Tips

  • Swirl, never stir, while the sugar is melting. Stirring dry sugar drags crystals up the side of the pan and seeds the whole batch.
  • Have the board oiled and the seeds within arm's reach before the sugar goes on. From amber to burnt is about twenty seconds.
  • Cut while warm. Brittle left to set hard shatters along its own lines rather than yours.
  • Three quarters of a cup of sugar instead of a full cup gives a mixture soft enough to roll into balls, which is how it is often served in Brava.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring the melting sugar, which turns the caramel cloudy and grainy.
  • Toasting the seeds too dark before the sugar even starts — they carry on cooking in the hot caramel.
  • Trying to cut it once cold, when it breaks into shards.
  • Walking away from the pan. Sugar at this stage needs watching continuously.

Health Benefits

  • Sesame seeds are high in calcium, magnesium, copper and healthy fats.
  • Two parts seed to one part sugar makes this far less sugar-dominated than most brittles.
  • Plant-based and naturally gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Sisin is often named in the same breath as lows, the peanut version, and the two are served together — which is where the phrase lows iyo sisin comes from. They are two separate sweets made by the same method, not one bar.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight tin at room temperature, 3 to 4 weeks. Humidity makes it sticky.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: Shaah Cadays, Bun iyo Toor, Somali coffee
