🍛 Thiou Carry Guinar, Chicken Curry

Thiou carry guinar — curry de poulet à la sénégalaise

A Senegalese-style chicken curry built on a vegetable thiou: three tablespoons of Indian curry powder, carrots, potatoes, cabbage and sweet potato in a litre and a half of water.

The Aistou Cuisine post is a thiou in disguise — vegetables stewed in tomato and onion — remixed with Indian curry powder. Carrots, potatoes, cabbage, sweet potato, aubergine and a fresh tomato go in a pot with garlic and three generous tablespoons of curry, cook about five minutes, then 1.5 litres of water. Onions go in at the boil. After about 20 minutes the heat is lowered and the pot simmers another 30; halfway through, the chicken is added, and the sweet potato and aubergine are taken out. Rice goes alongside.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Pot for the rice

  • 1 fresh tomato
  • Cabbage — quantity not printed
  • 1 sweet potato
  • 1 aubergine
  • 2 carrots
  • 300 g potatoes
  • 3 onions
  • Chile — quantity not printed
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 3 tbsp Indian curry powder (‘3 belles cuillères à soupe’ in the method)
  • Salt and pepper — quantities not printed
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 4 tbsp oil
  • 1.5 litres water
  • Chicken — quantity not printed
  • White rice — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Peel and wash the vegetables. Cut the carrots into rounds, the potatoes into quarters, the onions into dice and the tomato roughly.
  2. Heat the oil, add the tomato pieces, the curry, salt, pepper and garlic, and cook about 5 minutes, stirring from time to time.
  3. Add the water. When it boils, add the cut onions and the bay leaf.
  4. Stir in the vegetables and cook everything about 20 minutes.
  5. Lower the heat and simmer another 30 minutes.
  6. Halfway through the cooking, add the chicken and remove the sweet potato and the aubergine.
  7. Serve with white rice.

Chef Tips

  • The post prints no doneness cue at all: this is a time-driven recipe. Check the chicken is cooked through before serving.
  • The page’s step says ‘3 belles cuillères à soupe’ of curry while the list says ‘3 c. à soupe’. Start with level tablespoons and add to taste.
  • Remove the sweet potato and aubergine when the page says; they are done and would otherwise dissolve into the sauce.
  • Chicken quantity is not printed. Allow roughly 150–200 g per person.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the chicken at the start so it overcooks.
  • Leaving the sweet potato and aubergine in until they fall apart.
  • Under-seasoning — the page prints no salt quantity.

Cultural Note

Mbaye’s post says that ‘in Senegal we like to remix dishes and put them in our own sauce’, and that Senegalese paella has become a fixture at Dakar parties. She adds that the curry reminds her of the spice markets of India.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Gentle heat with a splash of water.

Serve with: Steamed white rice
