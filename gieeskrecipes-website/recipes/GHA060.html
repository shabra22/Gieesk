🍖 Waakye Lamb Stew

Waakye stew

The stew that goes on the waakye tray, built on the same chalé sauce base as the groundnut soups.

A waakye tray without its stew is just rice and beans, and this is the documented stew for it — lamb rather than the beef or goat a Ghanaian stall would more often use, and built on the chalé sauce that underpins this cook's whole repertoire. It belongs beside the rice, the shito, the gari, the boiled egg and the wele, and it is what makes the plate a meal. Published from a cookbook extract whose full quantities could not be verified across two passes, so the ingredient list is given as printed with that caveat stated plainly.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot, Blender, for the chalé sauce, Wooden spoon

  • Lamb, cut for stewing
  • Uncooked chalé sauce
  • Onion, finely diced
  • Fresh root ginger, grated
  • Garlic, crushed
  • Scotch bonnet chilli, pierced
  • Curry powder
  • Sea salt
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Stock or water

Quantities could not be verified across two passes. See the note.

    Method

    1. Brown the lamb with the onion, ginger and garlic.
    2. Add the curry powder, salt and black pepper.
    3. Stir in the uncooked chalé sauce.
    4. Add stock or water to cover and drop in the pierced scotch bonnet.
    5. Simmer until the lamb is tender and the oil rises to the surface.
    6. Serve over waakye with shito, gari and boiled egg.

    Timings above follow the same author's other stews rather than this recipe's own printed method.

      Chef Tips

      • This is the component the waakye tray needs, and it is worth making a large batch — the tray is assembled from several things and the stew is the one that takes time.
      • Built on chalé sauce, like the groundnut soups. If you keep a batch in the fridge this stew becomes quick — see Chalé Sauce.
      • Lamb is this author's choice. A Ghanaian waakye stall would more often use beef, goat or fish, and the stew works the same way with any of them.
      • Pierce the scotch bonnet rather than chopping it, so you can take it out and control the heat.
      • Oil rising to the surface is the endpoint for this family of stews across every documented version, and it applies here.
      • The tray is the dish: rice and beans, stew, shito, gari, boiled egg, and often wele and spaghetti. Serving the stew alone misses the point.
      • Be aware the quantities for this one could not be confirmed on a second pass, so treat the proportions as a guide and season by taste.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Serving it without the rest of the tray.
      • Chopping the scotch bonnet in.
      • Stopping before the oil rises.
      • Using cooked chalé sauce where uncooked is intended.
      • Treating the unverified proportions as exact.

      Cultural Note

      The waakye tray is one of the great assembled meals — a scoop of reddish rice and beans, a ladle of stew, a spoon of shito, a sprinkle of gari, half a boiled egg, a piece of wele, and a tangle of spaghetti if you want it. Every component has its own maker and the plate is built to order in about twenty seconds.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

      Reheating: Gentle heat. It improves overnight.

      Serve with: Waakye, Shito, Gari and boiled egg
