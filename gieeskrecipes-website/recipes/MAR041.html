🍲 Harira, Royal Mansour Style

Harira (حريرة) — chef's version

A hotel-kitchen harira with lamb or beef, chickpeas, lentils, celery and large amounts of fresh herbs, thickened with cornstarch and finished with vermicelli.

1h 45mTotal time
40mPrep
1h 5mCook
6Servings
360Calories
MediumDifficulty

This version, published by Marrakech's Royal Mansour, uses weights throughout: 900 g of meat to 4 litres of water, plenty of celery and fresh herbs, a stock cube, and a cornstarch thickener instead of the flour tedouira. After one hour on low heat the thickener is stirred in, vermicelli cooks 3 more minutes, and the soup is finished with chopped herbs.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 200 g dry chickpeas (printed "cheakpeas")
  • 100 g dry lentils
  • 900 g lamb or beef
  • 800 g tomatoes (400 g of crushed tomato pulp)
  • 50 g tomato paste
  • 300 g celery branch thinly sliced
  • 200 g fresh chopped cilantro
  • 200 g fresh chopped parsley
  • 400 g onion
  • 1 g saffron
  • 2 g pepper
  • 20 g salt
  • 2 g turmeric
  • 4 l water
  • 1 lamb or beef stock cube
  • 7 cl organic olive oil
  • 25 g vermicelli or angel hair
  • 50 g cornstarch
  • 5 g rancid butter (traditional smen)

Method

  1. The night before, soak the chickpeas and lentils separately for 24 hours in the fridge. Remove the skin from the chickpeas and drain the lentils.
  2. Cut the meat into 1 cm dice. Peel, seed and dice the tomatoes. Peel the celery stalks, split lengthwise and slice thinly.
  3. In a large pot heat a drizzle of olive oil and brown the meat. Add the onions, celery, cilantro and parsley and brown 2 to 3 minutes.
  4. Add the diced and crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, saffron, pepper, chickpeas, lentils and stock cube. Stir in the water, salt, bring to a boil and cook over low heat for 1 hour.
  5. Keep the harira on the heat and add the thickener of cornstarch mixed with 1 cup of water, stirring constantly and keeping the soup simmering.
  6. Add the vermicelli and cook another 3 minutes. Adjust the seasoning, sprinkle with chopped cilantro and parsley, and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • The page gives time only as a cue ("cook over low heat for 1 hour").
  • The page does not say when to use the turmeric or smen; add them with the spices.
  • Serve hot; it is not limited to Ramadan according to the page.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the cornstarch off the heat, which lumps.
  • Overcooking the vermicelli.
  • Skipping the soak for the pulses.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in herbs, pulses and lean meat.

Cultural Note

The page says harira is traditionally eaten during the holy month of Ramadan after a day of fasting, served with dates and chebakia, and is not limited to Ramadan.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 3 days in the fridge.

Reheating: Loosen with water and reheat gently.

Serve with: Dates and chebakia
