🥣 Tagoula (Amazigh Barley Porridge)

Tagoula / tagoulla — Amazigh barley porridge (تاكولا)

A thick Amazigh porridge of coarse barley grits simmered for an hour and a half, served with argan oil, honey and almonds or walnuts.

1h 35mTotal time

Barley grits are rinsed, then boiled with salt and water, lowered to a simmer for about an hour and a half and stirred often with a wooden spoon until tender. It is served topped with argan oil and almonds or walnuts. Morocco World News ties it to the Souss region and the Amazigh New Year (Yennayer).

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot, Wooden spoon

  • 250 grams of barley grits, very roughly milled
  • 2 liters of water
  • 1 teaspoon of salt
  • Optional: argan oil, honey, walnuts or almonds (no quantities printed)

Method

  1. Rinse the grits and drain.
  2. Combine the water, salt and grits in a pot.
  3. Heat on high until boiling, then lower the heat.
  4. Simmer about an hour and a half, stirring often with a wooden spoon, until the grits are tender.
  5. Serve topped with argan oil and almonds or walnuts, and honey if you like.

Chef Tips

  • Doneness cue: the grits are tender.
  • Yabiladi writes it is also eaten warm or chilled with whey (lben).
  • Taste of Maroc distinguishes tagoulla (scooped by hand) from assida (eaten with a spoon).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving it unstirred.
  • Using fine flour instead of rough grits.
  • Adding toppings before it is served.

Health Benefits

  • Barley supplies fibre and slow energy; the source calls it a great source of energy.
  • Argan oil and nuts add healthy fats.

Cultural Note

Morocco World News writes that tagoula is traditionally made in the Souss region for Yennayer, the Amazigh New Year, and is a postnatal food.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 2 days in the fridge; it sets firm.

Reheating: Warm with a splash of water.

Serve with: Argan oil and honey (optional toppings)
