🧀 Semolina Gnocchi (Gnocchi alla Romana)

Gnocchi alla Romana

Gnocchi alla Romana are entirely different from their potato cousins — semolina cooked into a thick porridge, cooled and cut into discs, then baked under butter and Parmigiano until golden and gratinéed.

A specialty of Rome, this dish has no boiling step at all — the semolina mixture is poured out to set like a firm polenta, stamped into rounds (traditionally with a glass), and layered like scales in a baking dish before a final trip under the broiler.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Whisk, Baking sheet, Round cutter or glass, Baking dish

  • 1 L (4 cups) whole milk
  • 200 g (1⅓ cups) semolina flour
  • 2 egg yolks
  • 100 g (1¼ cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, divided
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) butter, divided
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a pinch

Method

  1. Bring the milk to a gentle simmer with the salt and nutmeg.
  2. Whisking constantly, pour in the semolina in a steady stream to avoid lumps.
  3. Cook over low heat, stirring constantly, for 10–15 minutes until very thick and the spoon can stand up in it.
  4. Remove from heat and quickly beat in the egg yolks, half the Parmigiano, and half the butter until fully incorporated.
  5. Spread the mixture about 1cm thick onto a lightly oiled baking sheet or tray. Smooth the top and let cool completely, at least 2 hours, until firm.
  6. Preheat the oven to 220°C (425°F). Cut the set semolina into rounds using a glass or cutter.
  7. Arrange the rounds overlapping like roof tiles in a buttered baking dish. Dot with the remaining butter and scatter with the remaining Parmigiano.
  8. Bake for 20–25 minutes until golden and bubbling on top.
  9. Rest for a few minutes before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Cook the semolina mixture until genuinely thick — under-cooking makes it hard to slice cleanly once set.
  • Work quickly when beating in the egg yolks so they don't scramble from the heat.
  • Spreading the mixture evenly to a consistent thickness ensures the rounds bake evenly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not letting the mixture cool and firm completely before cutting, resulting in messy, misshapen rounds.
  • Under-baking, leaving the top pale instead of golden and gratinéed.
  • Pouring semolina in too quickly at the start, causing lumps.

Cultural Note

Unlike potato or ricotta gnocchi, Gnocchi alla Romana is a distinctly Roman specialty with no boiling at all, more closely related in technique to polenta than to typical Italian dumplings.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The baked dish keeps refrigerated for up to 2 days; the uncooked semolina disc can be made 1 day ahead.

Reheating: Reheat in a 180°C (350°F) oven until warmed through and re-crisped on top.

Serve with: A simple green salad, Roasted vegetables, As a starter before a meat main course
