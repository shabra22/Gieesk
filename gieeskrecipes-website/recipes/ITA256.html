Agnello alla Pignata

Lamb slow-braised in a traditional terracotta pot with tomato, onion, and pecorino, the earthenware vessel said to lend the finished stew a uniquely earthy, mellow depth.

Pignata refers to the rounded terracotta cooking pot itself, historically set directly into the embers of a wood fire, and Basilicata cooks maintain that this specific vessel genuinely changes the character of the finished stew compared to cooking in metal.

Ingredients

Equipment: Terracotta pot (pignata) or heavy Dutch oven with lid

  • 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) lamb shoulder, cut into chunks
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 2 potatoes, cubed
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 100 g (1 cup) grated pecorino
  • 150 ml (2/3 cup) dry white wine
  • 80 ml (1/3 cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh rosemary
  • Salt and chili flakes, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the olive oil in the pignata or a heavy pot and brown the lamb pieces on all sides.
  2. Add the onions and garlic, and saute until softened.
  3. Pour in the wine and let it reduce.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes, potatoes, rosemary, and chili flakes, and enough water to nearly cover the ingredients.
  5. Cover and cook over very low heat for 2-2.5 hours, until the lamb is completely tender.
  6. Stir in the grated pecorino in the final 10 minutes, letting it melt into the sauce.
  7. Season to taste and serve hot, directly from the pot if using a traditional pignata.

Chef Tips

  • If using a genuine terracotta pignata, cook over the lowest possible heat, as these vessels can crack under high or uneven heat.
  • The low, slow cooking is essential for both the lamb tenderness and the sauce depth of flavor.
  • Stirring in the pecorino at the end thickens the sauce naturally without needing any flour or cream.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking a terracotta pot over high heat, which risks cracking it.
  • Rushing the braise, leaving the lamb tough rather than properly tender.
  • Adding the pecorino too early, which can cause it to separate rather than melt smoothly into the sauce.

Cultural Note

Agnello alla pignata is closely tied to the traditional terracotta cooking vessel it takes its name from, a method still practiced in parts of rural Basilicata.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; flavor improves the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, covered.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A robust red wine from Basilicata
