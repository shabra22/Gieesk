🍰 Cassata Siciliana

Cassata Siciliana

A showstopping Sicilian layered cake wrapping sweetened ricotta between sponge cake, finished with a shell of marzipan and vividly colored icing, traditionally reserved for Easter.

Cassata's flavors reflect Sicily's layered history — Arab-introduced sugar and almonds, Spanish-influenced marzipan, and Norman dairy traditions — all combined into a cake so visually striking it has become one of the island's defining culinary symbols.

Ingredients

Equipment: Cake pan (same size as the sponge), Rolling pin, Offset spatula

  • 1 sponge cake (pan di Spagna), about 23cm, sliced into layers
  • 500 g (2 cups) ricotta, well drained
  • 150 g (¾ cup) granulated sugar
  • 80 g (½ cup) mini chocolate chips
  • 60 g (⅓ cup) candied fruit, finely chopped
  • 3 tbsp Marsala wine or maraschino liqueur
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) marzipan, rolled thin, tinted green
  • 300 g (2½ cups) powdered sugar, for the icing
  • 3 tbsp water or lemon juice, for the icing
  • Whole candied fruit, for decoration

Method

  1. Beat the drained ricotta with the sugar until smooth, then fold in the chocolate chips and chopped candied fruit.
  2. Line a cake pan with plastic wrap and line the sides with strips of marzipan.
  3. Brush the sponge cake slices lightly with Marsala or maraschino liqueur.
  4. Layer sponge cake in the bottom of the pan, followed by half the ricotta filling, another layer of sponge, the remaining filling, and a final layer of sponge on top.
  5. Press down gently, cover, and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight, to firm up.
  6. Invert the chilled cassata onto a serving plate and remove the plastic wrap.
  7. Whisk the powdered sugar with water or lemon juice into a smooth, pourable icing and spread over the top and sides.
  8. Decorate elaborately with whole candied fruit before the icing fully sets.
  9. Chill again briefly to set the icing before slicing and serving.

Chef Tips

  • Draining the ricotta thoroughly prevents a watery, unstable filling that won't hold its shape when sliced.
  • Building the cake inside a lined pan and inverting it makes for much cleaner, more even layers.
  • The decoration is traditionally elaborate — don't hold back with the candied fruit on top.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the chilling time, which is essential for the layers to firm up enough to slice cleanly.
  • Using ricotta that hasn't been drained, resulting in a filling that's too loose to hold its shape.
  • Making the icing too thin, causing it to run off rather than coat the cake evenly.

Cultural Note

Cassata is closely tied to Sicilian Easter celebrations, its intricate layers and decoration reflecting centuries of Arab, Norman, and Spanish culinary influence on the island.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, covered, for up to 4 days.

Reheating: Not applicable; served chilled or at room temperature.

Serve with: Espresso, A glass of Marsala wine
