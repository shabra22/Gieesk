🍯 Ebwanga

Ebwanga

Ten very ripe sweet bananas mashed syrupy with crushed roasted groundnuts and cassava flour, wrapped in young banana leaves and steamed on a bed of leaf spines for twenty-five minutes.

Ebwanga is a treat rather than a regular food — made once in a while, and in the household this comes from the children had to be on their best behaviour to get a piece. It is also, incidentally, the best written record anywhere of how Ugandan leaf steaming actually works: the hard spines stripped from the leaves are cut an inch shorter than the pan, packed in to fill it halfway, and the water sits below them so it never touches the parcels. That rig is the same one used for matooke and luwombo, and almost nobody has written it down.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Deep cooking pan with a tight lid, Steamer, if you have one

  • 10 super-ripe sweet bananas
  • 240 ml (1 cup) cassava flour
  • A handful of roasted groundnuts, crushed
  • Clean young banana leaves

From the leaves themselves

  • The hard spines, reserved for the steaming rig

Method

  1. Peel the bananas and put them in a bowl. Mash them until syrupy.
  2. Add the crushed groundnuts and mix.
  3. Add the cassava flour and keep mixing to a thick mixture.

The parcels

  1. Take clean tender young banana leaves, remove the hard spine from each, and cut the leaves into rectangles about 10 by 5 inches. Keep the spines.
  2. Spoon the banana mixture into the centre of a leaf rectangle, leaving an inch border all round.
  3. Fold the top edge down over the mixture, bring the bottom edge up over that, then fold in both sides to make a rectangular parcel.
  4. Repeat until the mixture is used up, taking care that the leaves do not tear.

The steaming rig

  1. If you have a steamer, use it. Otherwise steam them the way matooke is cooked.
  2. Take the reserved hard spines and cut them an inch shorter than the diameter of your pan.
  3. Pack the spines into the pan so they fill it halfway.
  4. Pour water into the pan — it must stay below the spines so it never touches the parcels.
  5. Lay the parcels on top of the spines.
  6. Cover the pan and put it on the heat. Cook 20 to 25 minutes.
  7. Take it off the heat, let the parcels cool, and serve as a snack.

Chef Tips

  • The spine rig is the technique to learn here, and it is the same one used for matooke and luwombo: spines cut an inch short of the pan, packed to fill it halfway, water below them, food on top. It steams without ever wetting the parcel.
  • Super-ripe bananas. Not just ripe — the sugar is the whole point and underripe fruit makes a bland, starchy parcel.
  • Mash until syrupy before anything else goes in.
  • Young tender leaves fold without splitting. Older leaves crack along the fold and the mixture leaks out.
  • Leave the inch border. Fill to the edge and you cannot make a seal.
  • Watch the water level. If it rises to touch the parcels you are boiling them.
  • Let them cool before serving — hot from the pan the mixture is loose.
  • Twenty to twenty-five minutes is the whole cook, which is short for a leaf parcel because the mixture is already soft going in.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using bananas that are merely ripe.
  • Using mature leaves that tear.
  • Overfilling the parcels.
  • Water high enough to touch the food.
  • Lifting the lid during the steam.

Health Benefits

  • Very ripe bananas provide potassium, vitamin B6 and natural sugars with no added sweetener.
  • Roasted groundnuts add protein, healthy fats and vitamin E.
  • Steamed rather than fried, and no dairy or egg.
  • Contains peanuts.

Cultural Note

That children had to earn a piece tells you exactly what ebwanga is: not everyday food, made rarely, and remembered. It also preserves, almost by accident, the banana-leaf steaming method that underpins the most important dishes in Ugandan cooking — knowledge normally passed hand to hand in a kitchen and very rarely written.

Storage & Serving

Storage: In their leaves, 2 days at room temperature or 4 refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam the parcels again for a few minutes.

Serve with: Ugandan tea, Mandazi, Roasted groundnuts
