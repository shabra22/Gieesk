🐛 Mashonzha

Mashonzha — masonja, mashotja, matomani

Dried mopane worms rehydrated, parboiled and then simmered in a curried tomato sauce — one of the highest-protein foods in the country, and a Limpopo delicacy rather than a novelty.

Mopane worms are the caterpillar of an emperor moth that feeds on mopane leaves, harvested and dried across the warmer parts of southern Africa. Dried they keep for months; rehydrated and stewed they are earthy and faintly nutty, and in Limpopo they are eaten as a matter of course with vhuswa. The rehydration is the technique: two hours of soaking, then a parboil, then a rinse, and only then do they go near a sauce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium saucepan, Frying pan, Colander

  • 1 cup dried mopane worms
  • 475 ml (2 cups) hot water, for soaking
  • 240 ml (1 cup) boiling water, for parboiling
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 clove garlic, crushed
  • 1 tbsp mild curry powder
  • 2 medium tomatoes, peeled and diced
  • 1 tbsp brown onion soup powder
  • 120 ml (1/2 cup) water
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Soak the dried mopane worms in the hot water for about 2 hours.
  2. Drain them, put them in a medium saucepan, cover with the boiling water and cook gently for 20 minutes.
  3. Rinse them and keep them warm.
  4. Heat the oil and fry the onion, garlic and curry powder until the onion is soft.
  5. Add the diced tomatoes and cook for 5 minutes.
  6. Mix the onion soup powder with the 1/2 cup of water and stir it into the pan.
  7. Add the worms, stir, and simmer for about 10 minutes. Season with salt and serve over vhuswa or soft pap.

Chef Tips

  • Rinse after the parboil and before the sauce. It washes off the grit that comes with sun-dried anything.
  • For the peanut version, dofhi, simmer 25 ml ground peanuts with 250 ml water for 15 minutes until creamy and use that instead of the tomato sauce, seasoning with salt and cayenne.
  • Dried mopane worms are also eaten as they are, as a snack, with no cooking at all.
  • The brown onion soup powder is a South African pantry shortcut for thickening and seasoning at once. A teaspoon of flour and a stock cube does the same job.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the two-hour soak, which leaves them leathery.
  • Going straight from the soak into the sauce without the 20-minute parboil.
  • Not rinsing after parboiling, so grit ends up in the finished dish.
  • Boiling them hard, which breaks them up.

Health Benefits

  • Exceptionally high in protein — dried mopane worms are roughly half protein by weight.
  • A good source of iron, zinc and calcium.
  • A far lower-impact protein than beef by land and water use, which is why they are studied as a food of the future.

Cultural Note

Mopane worms are harvested across Limpopo, Zimbabwe, Botswana and Mozambique, so this is a shared regional food rather than a South African exclusive. In South Africa they belong to Venda, Pedi and Tsonga kitchens, where the harvest is seasonal, largely done by women, and a genuine part of the rural economy.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate the cooked stew up to 3 days. Dried worms keep for months in an airtight jar.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the hob with a splash of water.

Serve with: Vhuswa or stiff pap, Morogo greens, Umqa
