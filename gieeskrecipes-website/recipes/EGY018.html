🥬 Kolkas (Egyptian Taro and Swiss Chard Stew)

Qolqas — قلقاس

Diced taro soaked to remove sliminess, simmered in spiced beef broth with chard and cilantro, finished with a garlic-coriander taklia.

8h 2mTotal time
52mPrep
2h 10mCook
6Servings
360Calories
HardDifficulty

A spiced beef broth of cinnamon, bay, mastic, cardamom, peppercorns, onion and allspice simmers 90 minutes to fork-tender. Taro, soaked in the fridge at least 5 hours, goes in with citrus juice until it is fork-tender. Chard and cilantro are blended with broth into the soup, and a taklia of ghee, coriander and garlic is fried a minute until crispy and orange-hued.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Gloves for peeling taro, Small frying pan

  • 2 medium taro, diced into 1/2 inch x 1/2 inch
  • 4 sprigs chard and/or spinach
  • 2 sprigs fresh cilantro, only the leaves
  • 1 1/2 liters broth, vegetable or beef broth, (recipe below)
  • Juice of 1 lime or lemon
  • Taklia, recipe below
  • Salt and pepper to taste

Beef broth

  • 500 grams of cubed eye round or top round
  • 2 tablespoons ghee or 1 tablespoon ghee + 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 4 bay leaves
  • 2 mastic resin
  • 5 cardamom pods
  • 10 whole peppercorn
  • 1 quartered medium onion
  • 3-5 garlic cloves
  • 5 allspice berries
  • 2 liters boiling water

Taklia

  • 2 tablespoons ghee
  • 2 tablespoons ground coriander
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Method

  1. Peel the taro with gloves, slice and dice it and soak it in the fridge overnight or no less than 5 hours to remove sliminess; rinse.
  2. Broth: toast the spices in ghee for 30 seconds or until fragrant, brown the meat, add the 2 litres of boiling water, boil vigorously for 5 minutes, then simmer about 90 minutes until fork-tender.
  3. Add the taro and citrus juice and cook on medium-low until the taro is fork-tender.
  4. Blend the chard and cilantro with ½ cup of broth and stir into the soup.
  5. Taklia: fry the ghee, coriander and garlic for one minute, until crispy and orange-hued, and stir in.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: spices fragrant; beef fork-tender; taro fork-tender; taklia crispy and orange-hued. The taro soak is NO CUE, TIME ONLY.
  • The card says 1-inch taro slices and ½ cup of broth to blend; the body says ½-inch slices and 1 cup. We use the card figures except for the ½-inch dice as printed in the ingredient list.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-soaking the taro; the slime makes the stew gloopy.
  • Burning the taklia garlic.

Health Benefits

  • Taro provides resistant starch; chard adds vitamins A and K.

Cultural Note

Nermine Mansour writes that kolkas is central to the Coptic Epiphany feast, and shares a folk rhyme warning against skipping it at night.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days (our estimate).

Reheating: Reheat gently.

Serve with: Aish baladi
