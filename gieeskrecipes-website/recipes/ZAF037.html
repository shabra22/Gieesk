🫘 Dhal Curry

Dhal — dholl

Lentils simmered with garlic and turmeric, folded into a dry-fried vegetable curry and finished with cumin and mustard seeds popped in butter and poured over at the last second.

Dhal is the dish that comes most directly off the indenture ration list — dried pulses were part of what labourers were issued, and what the community built from them became a cornerstone of the cuisine. The technique worth learning here is the finish: whole cumin and mustard seeds fried in butter until they pop, then tipped into the pot off the heat. That last step takes ninety seconds and it is the difference between dhal and lentil stew.

Ingredients

Equipment: Two saucepans, Small frying pan, for the tempering

    The lentils

    • 250 g brown lentils, rinsed
    • 3 cloves garlic, crushed
    • 1 tbsp ground turmeric
    • 1 chilli, chopped
    • Salt, to taste

    The curry

    • 2 tbsp oil
    • 10 g breyani spice mix
    • 2 onions, chopped
    • 1 tbsp curry powder
    • 1 tsp garam masala
    • 1 tsp ground coriander
    • 1 tbsp garlic-and-ginger paste
    • 1 x 400 g tin chopped tomatoes

    The vegetables

    • 2 carrots, peeled and diced
    • 375 ml (1 1/2 cups) cabbage, shredded
    • 125 g green beans, cut into pieces
    • 250 g cauliflower and broccoli florets
    • 125 g baby corn, cut into pieces
    • 6 to 8 baby brinjals, quartered
    • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

    The tempering, added last

    • 1 tbsp butter
    • 2 tsp cumin seeds
    • 2 tsp mustard seeds
    • Fresh coriander, to garnish

    Method

    1. Put the lentils, crushed garlic, turmeric and chopped chilli in a saucepan and cover with water. Simmer over medium heat until the lentils are cooked and soft, adding more water as needed, and season with salt towards the end.
    2. In a second pan, heat the oil and fry the breyani spice mix until fragrant.
    3. Add the onions and fry until golden.
    4. Add the curry powder, garam masala, ground coriander, garlic-and-ginger paste and the tinned tomatoes, and simmer until nearly all the moisture has evaporated. This is the step that concentrates the base.
    5. Add all the vegetables and simmer for about 15 minutes, until done but stopping well short of mushy.
    6. Fold the cooked lentils through the vegetable curry and season with salt and pepper.
    7. For the tempering, heat the butter in a small pan and fry the cumin and mustard seeds until they pop and smell fragrant.
    8. Pour the tempering over the curry, stir through, garnish with coriander and serve with rice.

    Chef Tips

    • Reduce the tomato base until it is nearly dry before the vegetables go in. Skip this and you get vegetables poached in tomato water.
    • The tempering goes on at the very end, off the heat. Seeds fried in at the start have nothing left to give an hour later.
    • Cook the lentils separately. Lentils and vegetables want different times, and together one of them loses.
    • Stop the vegetables short. They carry on softening in the hot pot after you fold the lentils in.
    • Use ghee in place of the butter to make it richer, or oil to make it vegan.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding the vegetables before the tomato has reduced.
    • Cooking the vegetables to soft, so the finished dish is uniform mush.
    • Frying the cumin and mustard seeds with everything else at the start.
    • Letting the lentils cook dry, which scorches them and taints the whole pot.

    Health Benefits

    • Lentils are high in plant protein, fibre, folate and iron.
    • Six different vegetables in one pot.
    • Naturally vegetarian, and vegan if the butter is swapped for oil.

    Cultural Note

    Dhal, mealie rice and tinned pilchard curry are the three dishes that show most clearly how this cuisine was built from rationing and substitution. Naming that is not diminishing the food — it is the reason the food is as inventive as it is.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days. Better on the second day.

    Reheating: Reheat gently with a splash of water. Make a fresh tempering if you want the aroma back.

    Serve with: Basmati rice, Rotis, Mango atchar
