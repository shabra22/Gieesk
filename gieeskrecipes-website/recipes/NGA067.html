🤍 Afia Efere

Afia efere — Efik white soup

The ceremonial Efik white soup, made without palm oil at all — a whole chicken, four cups of water, and pounded yam dissolved into it as the thickener.

Afia efere is the soup Efik ceremonies are not complete without, and the author's own occasion for learning it was her father's coronation. It is made without palm oil, which is what distinguishes the ceremonial version from the everyday one and what keeps it white. A whole chicken is marinated thirty minutes, steamed until the water evaporates and then twenty minutes more, and four cups of water go in with the crayfish, pepper, iwun and Aidan fruit. The thickener is a medium slice of yam, boiled and pounded very firm with no water, flattened and dissolved into the soup.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Mortar and pestle, Separate pot for the yam, Spice grinder

  • 1 whole broiler chicken, cut into pieces
  • A handful of smoked fish, shredded
  • 6 fingers okro (optional)
  • 2 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 1 tbsp dried pepper
  • 2 pieces blended African nutmeg (iwun)
  • 2 pieces Aidan fruit, the size of a little finger
  • 1 crayfish seasoning tablet
  • 1 beef seasoning cube
  • 4 cups water
  • Salt to taste

The thickener, which is not in the original ingredient list

  • 1 medium slice of yam, boiled and pounded

Method

  1. Season the chicken and marinate 30 minutes.
  2. Boil the yam separately and pound it until very firm, adding no water at all.
  3. Steam the chicken until the water evaporates, then cook 20 minutes more, until half cooked.
  4. Add the 4 cups of water, the ground crayfish, the shredded smoked fish, the dried pepper, the blended iwun, the Aidan fruit and the seasoning, and bring to the boil.
  5. Flatten the pounded yam into 3 or 4 pieces and add them to the soup.
  6. Boil until the yam has dissolved completely.
  7. Add the okro if using and cook 5 minutes.
  8. Taste, adjust the salt, and serve.

Chef Tips

  • No palm oil. That is what makes it the ceremonial version and what keeps the soup pale.
  • Pound the yam with no water added. Firm and dry is what you want — wet pounded yam will not dissolve cleanly, it will go slimy.
  • Flatten it into three or four pieces before it goes in. Flat pieces dissolve from both sides.
  • Steam the chicken until the water evaporates first, then twenty minutes more. The dry-steaming concentrates the flavour before any water goes in.
  • Iwun is African nutmeg, two pieces blended, and the Aidan fruit pieces should be the size of a little finger. Those two are the aromatics that make afia efere taste of itself.
  • Okro is optional and goes in last for five minutes. With it the soup draws slightly; without, it stays clean.
  • The yam is not in the printed ingredient list — it comes from a clarification in the comments. It is the thickener of a thickened soup, so it is listed above under its own heading.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding palm oil.
  • Adding water while pounding the yam.
  • Dropping the pounded yam in as a single ball.
  • Adding the four cups of water before the chicken has dry-steamed.
  • Over-cooking the okro until it goes stringy.

Health Benefits

  • No palm oil, so much lower in fat than most Nigerian soups.
  • Chicken and smoked fish give lean protein.
  • African nutmeg and Aidan fruit are traditionally used as digestives.
  • Low in carbohydrate for a thickened soup.

Cultural Note

Afia efere is ceremonial food and the absence of palm oil is the marker of that — the everyday version is made with it, the version for a coronation or a traditional rite is not. It is the Efik member of a family that includes Igbo ofe nsala, and across the whole family the pattern holds: no palm oil, yam as the thickener, and a trio of aromatic dried spices doing the work that oil and pepper do elsewhere.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated. The yam thickening tightens when cold.

Reheating: Low heat with a little water.

Serve with: Iyan, Eba or garri, Akpu or fufu
