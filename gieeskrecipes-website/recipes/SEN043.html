🥣 Gourmbane, Millet and Peanut Soup

Gourmbane

Millet grits and raw peanut flour stirred into three litres of meat, cowpea, dried fish and yet broth, soured with tamarind or lemon.

MediumDifficulty

A thick millet-and-peanut soup from the Slow Food/FAO Senegal booklet. Meat, crushed cherry tomatoes, tomato paste, cowpeas, dried fish, yet, garlic, onion and four small toufas boil in three litres of water; millet grits mixed with raw peanut flour are added gradually to avoid lumps, stirring often with a wooden spatula over medium heat, and tamarind juice or lemon is stirred in. The finish is described, not timed: the soup must be neither too compact nor too fluid, and the millet must be well cooked.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep pot, Wooden spatula, Mortar or blender

  • 250 g meat
  • 150 g millet grits
  • 100 g raw peanut flour
  • 2 tbsp tomato concentrate
  • 200 g cherry tomatoes
  • 100 g cowpeas
  • 1 onion
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 50 g dried fermented fish, in pieces
  • 50 g yet, in pieces
  • 4 small toufas (as printed; not defined in the source)
  • 3 tbsp tamarind juice or lemon juice
  • 3 litres water
  • Salt and pepper — quantities not printed

Method

  1. Wash the meat, the dried fish and the yet. Mix the millet grits with the peanut flour.
  2. Bring the 3 litres of water to a boil in a deep pot. Add the meat, crushed cherry tomatoes, tomato paste, cowpeas, dried fish, yet, garlic and onion.
  3. Pound or blend as needed, then pepper and salt.
  4. Once boiling, add the millet-and-peanut mixture gradually to avoid lumps. Stir often with a wooden spatula so it does not stick; cook over medium heat.
  5. Add the tamarind or lemon juice. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • The printed finish is ‘neither too compact nor too fluid’ with ‘the millet grits well cooked’.
  • Add the millet-peanut mix a little at a time, stirring, or it clumps.
  • No cooking time is printed. Cook until the millet is soft; 60 minutes is an estimate.
  • Yet and dried fish are bought, already fermented.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Dumping the millet in all at once.
  • Not stirring.

Health Benefits

  • Millet and cowpeas give fibre and plant protein.
  • Gluten-free.

Cultural Note

The toufa and yet content points to the Saloum and Petite Côte shellfish economy, but the booklet does not say so; that reading is inferential.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with extra water.

Serve with: Served hot, as a meal in itself
