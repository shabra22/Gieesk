🍲 Tuo Zaafi with Ayoyo

Tuo zaafi — TZ

The fully quantified version — one cup of corn flour to two thirds of cassava, ten cups of loosely packed ayoyo, and a marinade with African nutmeg and selim.

This is the most completely measured tuo zaafi in print, and it gives all three components. The swallow is a cup of corn flour and two thirds of a cup of cassava flour in four and a half cups of water, stirred the way you would stir amala or banku, simmered ten minutes and then mixed again until smooth. The ayoyo is ten cups of fresh leaves blended semi-smooth with two cups of water, two tablespoons of dawadawa and shrimp powder. The stew carries the other half of a marinade built on anise, cloves, star anise, African nutmeg, selim and bay.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a handle, Strong wooden spoon or paddle, Blender, Two further pots

    Tuo

    • 1 cup corn flour
    • 2/3 cup kokonte or cassava flour
    • 4 1/2 cups water
    • 1/2 tsp salt

    Meat and marinade

    • 600 g beef or goat chunks — choose cuts with a bit of fat
    • 1 large onion
    • Pepper, as much as you need
    • 2 tbsp ginger
    • 2 tbsp garlic
    • 1/2 tsp anise
    • 1/4 tsp cloves
    • 1 star anise
    • 1/2 tsp dried rosemary
    • 1 African nutmeg
    • 2 grains of selim
    • 1 bay leaf

    Ayoyo

    • 10 cups fresh ayoyo, loosely packed — discard thick stems
    • 2 cups water
    • 2 tbsp dawadawa
    • 1 small onion, roughly chopped
    • 2 tbsp shrimp powder
    • 5 small pieces dried herring (optional)

    Stew

    • 1 large onion, sliced
    • 2 smoked mackerel, each cut into 3 pieces
    • 1/2 cup palm or vegetable oil
    • 1 1/3 cups blended fresh tomatoes
    • 1/4 cup tomato paste
    • 1 tsp curry powder
    • 1 tbsp shrimp powder

    Method

    1. Blend the marinade ingredients. Cook the beef with half of it over medium heat, covered, until soft — check every 15 minutes or so.
    2. For the ayoyo: blend the leaves with the 2 cups of water until semi-smooth.
    3. Cook the blended ayoyo with the dawadawa, onion, shrimp powder and dried herring over medium heat 5 minutes.
    4. For the stew: fry the sliced onion until translucent, then add the rest of the marinade.
    5. Cook 3 minutes, then add the tomato paste.
    6. Cook 3 minutes, then add the blended tomatoes and the cooked beef with its stock.
    7. Simmer about 30 minutes, or until thickened to the consistency you want.
    8. For the tuo: combine the corn flour with the water over medium-low heat and add half a teaspoon of salt.
    9. Keep stirring as you would for amala, banku or a choux pastry.
    10. Simmer 10 minutes, then start the mixing process again until smooth and lump-free, working in the cassava flour.
    11. Stir a further 3 minutes, then scoop into a bowl.
    12. Serve the tuo first, then the ayoyo, then the stew.

    Chef Tips

    • Her stirring comparison is the most useful line here: keep stirring as you would for amala, banku or a choux pastry. If you have made any of those, you know the resistance you are after.
    • Ten cups of ayoyo loosely packed is a lot of leaf and it collapses to very little. Discard the thick stems first.
    • Blend the ayoyo semi-smooth, not smooth. Some leaf structure survives and that is what gives the slipperiness its body.
    • Check the meat every fifteen minutes or so rather than trusting a total time. Goat and beef vary enormously.
    • Simmer the tuo ten minutes BETWEEN two rounds of mixing. That rest-and-remix is what gets rid of the lumps.
    • Her marinade is unusually aromatic for a northern dish — anise, cloves, star anise, African nutmeg, selim and rosemary. The rosemary is not traditional; the rest are.
    • Half the marinade goes into the meat and half into the stew. Keep the split.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Stirring the tuo gently.
    • Blending the ayoyo completely smooth.
    • Leaving the thick ayoyo stems in.
    • All the marinade into the meat.
    • Skipping the ten-minute simmer between mixings.

    Health Benefits

    • Ayoyo (jute leaf) is very high in vitamins A, C and E, calcium and iron.
    • Dawadawa is a fermented food with documented B-vitamin content.
    • Beef or goat supplies protein, iron and vitamin B12.
    • The mucilage in ayoyo is a soluble fibre.

    Cultural Note

    Tuo zaafi with ayoyo is the northern Ghanaian meal, and the slipperiness of the ayoyo is the whole point — it is what carries the tuo down. That the fullest written version of it is by a diaspora food writer rather than a northern Ghanaian cook is a real gap in the record, and it is stated rather than papered over.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: The tuo is same-day. The stew keeps 4 days refrigerated; the ayoyo 2 days.

    Reheating: Steam the tuo. Reheat the stew gently; make the ayoyo fresh.

    Serve with: Ayoyo Soup, Bito Soup, Benne Soup
