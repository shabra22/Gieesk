🍚 Ceebu Yapp

Ceebu yapp — riz à la viande sénégalais

An easy ceebu yapp published under a site byline — the simplest documented route into the meat thieb.

1h 30mTotal time
20mPrep
1h 10mCook

The most approachable of the documented ceebu yapp recipes, published under a site name rather than a personal byline and flagged as such. It follows the same architecture as every Senegalese rice-and-meat dish — brown the meat, caramelise the onions hard for colour, build a broth, finish the rice in it — with fewer components than the household versions and no separate onion sauce to make alongside.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Knife, Wooden spoon

    The source's quantities are recorded in the note; several are absent

    • Meat, cut into pieces — beef or lamb
    • Rice, washed
    • Onions
    • Garlic
    • Bell pepper
    • Tomato paste
    • Oil
    • Bay leaf
    • Bird's eye chilli
    • Carrot
    • Bouillon cube
    • Salt and pepper
    • Water

    Method

    1. Brown the meat in hot oil until well coloured.
    2. Add the sliced onions and diced bell pepper and caramelise them well.
    3. Add the garlic and the tomato paste and cook it down.
    4. Add the water, the bay leaf, the chilli and the bouillon, and bring to the boil.
    5. Simmer until the meat is tender.
    6. Add the carrot and cook until done, then remove the vegetables and meat and keep them warm.
    7. Add the washed rice to the broth, with the liquid just covering the grain.
    8. Cover and cook until the rice is tender and the broth absorbed.
    9. Serve the rice with the meat and vegetables on top.

    Chef Tips

    • This is the one to start with if you have not made a Senegalese rice dish before — same technique, fewer moving parts than the household versions.
    • Caramelise the onions properly even here. It is the step that makes a thieb taste Senegalese rather than merely like rice with meat.
    • Broth just covering the rice, as in every version of this dish.
    • Remove the meat and vegetables before the rice goes in. They will overcook otherwise and you want them intact on the platter.
    • Published under a site byline rather than a person's name, which this collection flags. For a named-author version of the same dish, Thiebou Yaap and Thiébou Yap with Senegal River Valley Basmati are both by a Senegalese cook.
    • Tomato paste here, unlike the browned-onion-only household versions. It is a legitimate variation and it gives colour more easily.
    • Once you can make this, the step up to a full ceebu jen is mostly about the fish and the fermented seasonings.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Skipping the caramelising because the tomato paste provides colour.
    • Leaving the meat in through the rice stage.
    • Too much broth.
    • Not washing the rice.
    • Boiling rather than simmering the meat.

    Cultural Note

    Teranga is the Wolof word for hospitality and it is the word Senegal uses about itself, so a site called Cuisine Teranga publishing the easy version of the meat thieb is about right — the dish you make for whoever turns up.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

    Reheating: Steam the rice; warm the meat gently.

    Serve with: Kaani, Green olives, Bissap
