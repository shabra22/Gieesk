🍗 Maraq Digaag

Maraq Digaag

Chicken browned in bloomed xawaash, then cooked down with potato, carrot and a blended purée of tomato, cilantro, pepper and onion in only a cup of water — a thick stew, finished with a spoonful of ghee.

Maraq means soup and stew both, which is why this dish exists in two quite different forms and both are correct. This is the stew: a whole chicken, skinned and cut small, browned in xawaash that has just hit hot oil, then simmered in a blended vegetable purée with barely a cup of water, so what you get is thick and clinging rather than brothy. The detail worth learning is why the chicken goes in immediately after the spice — the cold meat quenches the oil and stops the xawaash burning in the two seconds it would otherwise take.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Large pot with a lid, Sharp knife

    The chicken

    • 1 whole chicken, 2 kg (4.5 lb), skinned and cut into small pieces

    To blend

    • 3 tomatoes, roughly chopped
    • 1 cup fresh cilantro
    • 1/2 green pepper, roughly chopped
    • 1/2 onion, roughly chopped
    • 1 jalapeño, seeded
    • 4 cloves garlic

    The pot

    • 45 ml (3 tbsp) vegetable or canola oil
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) xawaash
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) vegetable seasoning
    • 10 ml (2 tsp) tomato paste
    • 1 potato, diced
    • 1 carrot, diced
    • 240 ml (1 cup) water

    To finish

    • 30 ml (2 tbsp) ghee or subag, optional

    Method

    1. Roughly chop the tomatoes, cilantro, green pepper and onion, then blend them together with the jalapeño and garlic into a purée.
    2. Dice the potato and carrot.
    3. Add the oil to a pot over medium heat and add the xawaash. Immediately add the chicken pieces — the cold meat stops the spice burning.
    4. Brown the chicken for about 2 minutes, then add the vegetable seasoning.
    5. Add the diced potato and carrot, then the blended vegetables, the tomato paste and the water.
    6. Cover and cook for 30 minutes, until the chicken is tender and the potato is soft.
    7. Stir in the ghee or subag if you are using it, and serve.

    Chef Tips

    • Xawaash into the oil, chicken in straight after. That sequence is deliberate — you want the spice to bloom in the hot fat and then be stopped before it scorches, and the cold chicken is what stops it.
    • One cup of water for a 2 kg chicken. This is a thick stew, and adding more turns it into something else.
    • Skin the chicken. The skin will not crisp in a covered pot and just makes the sauce greasy.
    • Blend the vegetables rather than chopping them. Same principle as maraq digir — the purée gives the sauce its body.
    • Cut the chicken small. Small pieces cook through in the thirty minutes and large ones do not.
    • The ghee at the end is optional and it is what takes it from good to rich. Add it off the heat.
    • There is a genuinely different dish under the same name: a clear broth, with the chicken covered in water and skimmed, whole spices instead of ground xawaash, cabbage in it, simmered 45 minutes or so. Both are maraq, and if someone tells you yours is wrong, they may mean theirs is the soup.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding the xawaash to the oil and then pausing, which burns it.
    • Adding more water and ending up with a thin broth by accident.
    • Leaving the skin on.
    • Cutting the chicken into large pieces.
    • Stirring the ghee in over heat, where it just disappears into the sauce.

    Health Benefits

    • Skinned chicken is a lean, high-protein choice with niacin and selenium.
    • Potato, carrot, tomato and pepper make it close to a complete meal in one pot.
    • Cumin, coriander, turmeric and cinnamon all arrive with the xawaash.
    • Naturally free of gluten.

    Cultural Note

    That maraq means both soup and stew is not a translation problem so much as a description of how the dish works: the same pot, thinned or thickened according to how many people turned up and what there was. It is the everyday centre of Somali cooking, and the version someone grew up with is the version they will defend.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Better on the second day.

    Reheating: Warm gently on the hob with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Bariis Iskukaris, Canjeero, Insalaaddo
