🦀 Soupou Kanja, Seafood and Okra Stew

Soupou kanja

Stock reduced by a quarter, fish sauce standing in for fermented conch, then mussels, shrimp, crab, palm oil and eight cups of sliced okra.

75 minTotal time
25 minPrep
50 minCook
7Servings
270Calories
MediumDifficulty

Jessica Harris’s version for the Culinary Institute of America’s companion to ‘High on the Hog’ is a faster, cleaner take: three quarts of stock with bay and onion cook about twenty minutes until reduced by a quarter, fish sauce and a Scotch bonnet go in for ten, and mussels and shrimp cook covered for about four minutes until the mussels open. Lump crab and palm oil follow for two minutes, then eight cups of thinly sliced okra simmer ten more. The page says the fish sauce takes the place of a fermented conch called yeett.

Ingredients

Equipment: 6-quart saucepan, Slotted spoon

  • 24 mussels (fresh, or frozen on the half-shell)
  • 3 quarts fish or vegetable stock
  • 3 bay leaves
  • 2 medium yellow onions, roughly chopped
  • 3 or 4 tbsp fish sauce (substitute for fermented conch)
  • 1 Scotch bonnet
  • 16 medium shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 8 oz lump crabmeat, picked free of shells
  • 6 tbsp palm oil
  • 8 cups thinly sliced okra
  • Freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • Cooked fonio or rice, to serve

Method

  1. If using fresh mussels, rinse and scrub under running water, discard any that will not close, and remove the beards.
  2. Bring the stock to a boil in a 6-quart saucepan over high heat. Add the bay leaves and onions, reduce to medium-low, and cook about 20 minutes, until reduced by one-quarter.
  3. Add the fish sauce and the chile and cook 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  4. Add the mussels and shrimp, cover, and cook about 4 minutes, until the mussels open and the shrimp are cooked through. Discard any mussels that have not opened.
  5. Add the crabmeat and palm oil and cook about 2 minutes more.
  6. Add the okra, stir once, and simmer 10 minutes.
  7. Ladle into bowls and serve over fonio or rice.

Chef Tips

  • Mussels that do not close before cooking and mussels that do not open after cooking both go in the bin — the two printed safety cues.
  • Stir the okra once and leave it; too much stirring turns it slimy.
  • Taste before the fish sauce: 3 tablespoons is the low end, 4 the high end, and brands vary widely in salt.
  • The page lists black pepper and never uses it in the method — add it at the end.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the mussels and shrimp.
  • Salting before tasting the fish sauce.
  • Stirring the okra constantly.

Health Benefits

  • Crab, mussels and shrimp supply lean protein, B12 and zinc.
  • Okra supplies soluble fibre.
  • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

Cultural Note

Harris is the historian behind the ‘High on the Hog’ series, and she writes about West Africa as the source of much of the American South’s cooking. The page attributes the dish to her by a courtesy line rather than a byline.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Gentle heat, just to hot.

Serve with: Fonio or steamed white rice
