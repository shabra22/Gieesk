🥜 Lamb Shank Mafé

Mafé — peanut stew of lamb shanks

Six lamb shanks braised in a sauce of tomato paste and a full cup of peanut butter, finished with a spoonful of raw parsley gremolata.

185 minTotal time
20 minPrep
165 minCook
6Servings
640Calories
MediumDifficulty

Thiam calls lamb shanks slowly simmered in peanut sauce ‘the image of comfort food in Senegal’. The shanks are browned in batches, the onions are taken down with garlic and a diluted tomato paste until the paste is deep dark red, and the peanut butter is whisked into simmering stock a spoonful or two at a time so it dissolves rather than clumps. The shanks go back in under the sauce with a whole Scotch bonnet and fish sauce and cook covered for about an hour and a half, then uncovered until the sauce coats the back of a spoon. A pinch of rof gremolata on each shank at the table cuts the richness.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy saucepan or Dutch oven, Wooden spoon, Slotted spoon

  • 2 tbsp peanut or vegetable oil, plus more if needed
  • 6 lamb shanks, about 1 1/4 lb each
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper for the shanks — quantity not printed in the list
  • 2 cups chopped yellow onion
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste, mixed with a few tablespoons of water — amount of water not printed
  • 2 quarts chicken stock or water
  • 2 dried bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp finely chopped fresh thyme
  • 2 tsp salt and 1 tsp black pepper, for the stock — printed in the method
  • 1 cup unsweetened smooth peanut butter
  • 1 Scotch bonnet pepper, whole
  • 2 tbsp Vietnamese or Thai fish sauce

To serve

  • Rof gremolata — quantity not printed
  • Spring vegetable fonio pilaf — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a large saucepan or Dutch oven over medium-high. Season the shanks with salt and pepper and brown them in batches without crowding the pan, turning to colour all sides — about 8 minutes. Set aside, adding more oil if the pan dries.
  2. In the same pan brown the onions. Lower the heat to low, add the garlic, then the diluted tomato paste. Stir with a wooden spoon for 7 to 10 minutes, until the paste is deep, dark red. Add 1 to 2 tablespoons of water if it starts to scorch.
  3. Pour in the stock, raise the heat to bring it to a boil, then reduce to a simmer. Add the bay leaves, thyme, 2 teaspoons salt and 1 teaspoon pepper.
  4. Add the peanut butter 1 to 2 tablespoons at a time, stirring constantly until each addition is dissolved.
  5. Return the shanks, pressing them down so they are submerged. Add the Scotch bonnet and fish sauce, bring to a boil, reduce to a simmer, cover, and cook about 1 hour 30 minutes, until the shanks are tender.
  6. Uncover and keep cooking until the sauce is thick and coats the back of a spoon. No time is printed for this stage.
  7. Remove the bay leaves, skim the fat, adjust the seasoning.
  8. Serve hot on a platter. Top each shank with a generous pinch of rof gremolata and serve with the fonio pilaf.

Chef Tips

  • Add the peanut butter in small spoonfuls and wait for each to dissolve — dumping it in a block gives a grainy, split sauce.
  • Cook the tomato paste until it is deep dark red before any liquid goes in; it is the colour and the depth of the sauce.
  • The two printings of this recipe disagree about time: the recipe text says about 1 1/2 hours covered, while the article around it reports the shanks needing about two and a half hours. Judge by the shank — tender, and the meat easy to move on the bone — not by the clock.
  • The sauce is done when it is thick enough to coat the back of a spoon, not before.
  • Skim the fat at the end; the peanut butter and the lamb both release plenty.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the peanut butter at once.
  • Skipping the browning of the shanks.
  • Trusting the 90-minute figure on a larger shank without testing for tenderness.
  • Serving without the gremolata — it is half the point.

Cultural Note

Mafé is the groundnut stew that most of West Africa claims a version of. In Senegal it is a Sunday and celebration dish, thick enough to need rice or couscous to carry it. Thiam’s version is a restaurant-cook’s mafé, built on a whole shank per guest.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. The sauce thickens as it cools.

Reheating: Low heat, covered, with a splash of water or stock.

Serve with: Rof gremolata, Spring vegetable fonio pilaf, Steamed rice or millet couscous
