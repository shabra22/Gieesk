🥩 Oromo Muqmad (Dried Beef)

ሙቅማድ — Muqmad

Oromo Muqmad — beef cut into strips, slow-cooked with berbere and spices, then preserved in melted kibbeh fat in clay pots. The Oromo confit — the traditional preserved meat that pastoralist communities prepare for long journeys and lean seasons.

Muqmad is the Oromo answer to food preservation — beef cooked in its own fat and spices until completely tender, then submerged in kibbeh to create an airtight seal. Kept in clay pots, it lasts months without refrigeration.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot, Clay pot or jar for storage

  • 1kg beef — cut into thin strips
  • 3 tbsp berbere
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • ½ tsp mitmita

Preservation: additional 3—4 tbsp kibbeh for sealing

    Method

    1. Toss beef strips with berbere, salt, and mitmita.
    2. Place in a heavy pot. Cook over medium-low heat in their own fat and kibbeh 45—60 minutes.
    3. Beef should be completely tender and fat-rendered.
    4. Transfer to a clean clay pot or jar.
    5. Pour hot kibbeh over to completely submerge the beef.
    6. Seal with a cloth. Cool. Store in a cool, dark place.

    Chef Tips

    • Complete submersion in fat is the preservation technique.
    • The kibbeh seal must have no air pockets.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Insufficient fat coverage — spoilage.

    The Science

    Muqmad is a confit preparation — immersion in fat (kibbeh) removes oxygen, which is required for most bacterial and fungal growth. The high salt and berbere content further inhibit microbial activity. This is one of the world's oldest and most effective meat preservation methods.

    Cultural Note

    Muqmad is the food of the Oromo pastoralist's long journey — prepared before cattle drives, before long seasonal migrations, and before any period of uncertain food supply. It represents the Oromo people's mastery of their environment.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: In fat-sealed clay pot: months without refrigeration.

    Reheating: Remove from fat. Cook in a pan.

    Serve with: Reheated and served with injera, On long journeys, At celebrations after long storage
