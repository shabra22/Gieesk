🥣 Taro pilé et sauce jaune (Pounded Taro with Yellow Sauce)

Taro pilé et sauce jaune

Taro boiled in its skin about 2 hours and pounded hot, served with a palm-oil sauce emulsified with rock salt and lukewarm water, seasoned with cubes and a flamed whole pepper, with beef tripe cooked about 1 hour.

A French-language printing from the Cameroonian news site camer.be, credited to Maman Tonta of Bazou. The taro is boiled in its skin about 2 hours. Tripe is cooked with salt and onion about 1 hour. The palm oil is warmed gently to liquefy, the rock salt added, and the lukewarm water poured in while whisking; once the emulsion holds, the rock salt is removed. Cubes, tripe and a flamed whole pepper go in. The taro is peeled and pounded in a mortar while still hot (the page says cold it hardens) until smooth with no lumps, with a little water if it is too stiff.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Mortar and pestle, Whisk or bowl

  • 2 kg taro
  • 190 grams palm oil
  • 16 grams rock salt (sel gemme)
  • 50 grams condiments to dilute (the achu spice mix — contents not printed)
  • 750 ml lukewarm water
  • 1 onion
  • Salt — quantity not printed
  • 4 stock cubes
  • 1 whole chilli (flamed) — quantity not printed
  • 1 kg beef tripe

Method

  1. Boil the taro in its skin, ‘en robe des champs’, about 2 hours.
  2. Cook the tripe with salt and the onion for about 1 hour. Set aside.
  3. Dissolve the condiments in enough water.
  4. Warm the palm oil gently so it liquefies. Add the rock salt, then pour in the lukewarm water, whisking, until the emulsion is stable. Remove the rock salt.
  5. Crumble in the cubes, add the condiments and salt, then add the tripe and the flamed whole chilli. Taste.
  6. Peel the hot taro and pound it in a mortar until the paste is smooth. Moisten with a little water if too stiff. Serve with the sauce.

Chef Tips

  • Printed cue: ‘une fois que l’émulsion est stabilisée, ôter le sel gemme’.
  • Pound the taro while it is hot — cold, it hardens (printed).
  • The page does not name the condiments or give a final sauce cooking time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Pounding cold taro.
  • Leaving the rock salt in the sauce after the emulsion forms.
  • Adding all the water at once to the oil.
