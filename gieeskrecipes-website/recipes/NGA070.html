🐟 Ukang

Ukang — smoked fish in scent leaf stew

Three whole smoked fish and three cups of finely sliced scent leaf, with the palm oil poured over the fish rather than stirred in, so nothing breaks up.

Ukang is an Oron dish of Akwa Ibom — smoked fish in a scent leaf stew — and it is the simplest soup in this collection. Water, dry pepper, crayfish and seasoning boil for three minutes, the washed fish goes in with some of the scent leaf and the pot is shaken rather than stirred, and it cooks until the water is almost dry. The palm oil is then poured over the fish, which both coats them and avoids breaking them, and the rest of the scent leaf goes in for two or three minutes until wilted. Note the palm oil quantity is printed ambiguously and is flagged rather than resolved.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pot with a lid, Sharp knife, Ladle

  • 3 whole medium-size smoked fish — silver catfish or shiny nose
  • 1 tsp dry pepper
  • 1/2 - 1 cooking spoon scotch bonnet pepper, coarsely ground
  • 3 cups finely sliced scent leaf (ntong, efinrin, nchawun), used in two additions
  • 2 tbsp ground crayfish
  • Palm oil — see the note on quantity
  • Seasoning cube, to taste
  • 2-3 cups water

Method

  1. Add 2 to 3 cups of water to the pot with the dry pepper, the crayfish and the seasoning, and boil 3 minutes.
  2. Wash the fish and add it to the pot to cook and soften.
  3. Add some of the scent leaf and shake the pot rather than stirring.
  4. Cook until the water is almost dry.
  5. Add the palm oil, pouring it over the fish to prevent them breaking, and cook 1 minute.
  6. Add the remaining scent leaf and cook 2 to 3 minutes, until wilted.
  7. Serve with boiled yam or plantain.

Chef Tips

  • Shake the pot, do not stir. Smoked fish falls apart the moment a spoon touches it.
  • Pour the palm oil over the fish rather than into the liquid. It coats them and it is also the gentlest way to add it.
  • Until the water is almost dry is the cue before the oil goes in. This is a stew, not a soup.
  • Scent leaf in two additions — some early with the fish, the rest at the end. Early for depth, late for aroma.
  • Finely sliced scent leaf, three cups in total. The page does not say how to divide it between the two additions, so split it roughly in half.
  • ON THE PALM OIL: the source prints '21/2 cups', almost certainly 2 1/2 with a lost space, and adds 'you can use more or less'. Two and a half cups is a very large amount even for a palm-oil-forward Oron stew, and half a cup is also a plausible reading of the same characters. Start with half a cup and add more to taste — the ambiguity is in the source and is not resolved here.
  • Silver catfish or shiny nose are the fish named. Any firm smoked fish will do.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring the pot and breaking the fish.
  • Adding the oil before the water has reduced.
  • Putting all the scent leaf in at once.
  • Taking the printed palm oil quantity at face value without tasting as you go.
  • Over-cooking the final scent leaf past wilted.

Health Benefits

  • Smoked fish is a concentrated source of protein, calcium and omega-3 fats.
  • Scent leaf carries aromatic oils with documented antimicrobial activity.
  • Red palm oil supplies carotenoids and vitamin E.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.

Cultural Note

Ukang belongs to the Oron people of Akwa Ibom and is made for significant occasions including funeral rites, which is a reminder that the simplest dishes are not always the most casual. Three fish, three cups of a single herb and some palm oil — it is a dish that depends entirely on the quality of the smoked fish, which is exactly the kind of cooking a fishing community produces.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat, no stirring.

Serve with: Boiled yam, Boiled plantain, Eba or garri
