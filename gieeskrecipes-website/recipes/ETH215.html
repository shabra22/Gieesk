🍯 Ethiopian Honey Wine

ጠጅ — Tej

Ethiopian Tej — the ancient honey wine of Ethiopia. Raw honey dissolved in water with gesho twigs (or hops) and fermented for 2 weeks. See ETH095 for the complete recipe and heritage. This entry covers the tej house (tej bet) tradition and serving culture.

Tej has been Ethiopia's primary alcoholic drink since before recorded history — mentioned in ancient Ethiopian chronicles, served at imperial courts, and still served today in tej houses (tej bets) throughout the country. The tej bet is the Ethiopian equivalent of a wine bar — the place where tej is served in berele (flask-shaped glasses), alongside grilled meats and conversation.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large glass or clay fermentation vessel, Straining cloth, Berele glasses for serving

    • 1 kg raw honey
    • 3 litres water
    • 50g gesho twigs (Rhamnus prinoides)

    Gesho is available at Ethiopian specialty stores

      Chef Tips

      • Gesho is the defining ingredient — without it, it is honey wine, not tej.
      • The berele glass is the cultural vessel — drinking tej from a regular glass loses something.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Substituting hops for gesho changes the character completely.

      Cultural Note

      The Ethiopian tej house (tej bet) is one of Africa's most distinctive social institutions — low tables, small berele glasses, the smell of honey wine and charcoal-grilled meat, and conversations that run for hours. Tej is not consumed quickly — it is sipped slowly over the course of an evening, and the quality of the tej tells you the quality of the establishment. Emperor Haile Selassie served tej at his court; today it is served in the same berele glasses in tej bets from Addis Ababa to Gondar.
