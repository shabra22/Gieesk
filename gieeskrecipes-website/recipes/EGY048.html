🍞 Feteer Al Malak (Archangel Michael Buns)

Feteer al-malak — فطير الملاك

Soft yeasted buns marked with a thin-dough cross, glazed with cinnamon-sugar oil and baked puffed and golden; baked in honour of Archangel Michael.

3h 35mTotal time
30mPrep
25mCook
12Servings
290Calories
MediumDifficulty

Yeast is proofed with honey until frothy and foamy, then mixed into flour, baking powder, oil, sugar and vinegar to a soft, sticky dough, risen until doubled (or overnight in the fridge). It is divided into 100 g portions, shaped into balls with a thin cross, glazed with oil, brown sugar and cinnamon, risen again for 20 minutes and baked 25 minutes at 180°C until puffed and golden.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Baking tray, Pastry brush

    Dough

    • 500 grams all-purpose flour or whole wheat flour, You can mix 250 whole wheat with 250 grams all purpose flour
    • ½ teaspoon fine salt
    • 1 teaspoon baking powder
    • ½ cup canola oil
    • ½ cup white sugar or brown sugar + 1 tablespoon for the surface of the buns, For a savory version, don't add the sugar
    • 1 ½ teaspoon white distilled vinegar
    • ½ cup raisins or dry cranberry, optional
    • ½ cup warm water, to need the dough

    Proof yeast

    • 1 tablespoon honey
    • 1 tablespoon instant yeast
    • 1 cup warm water

    Glaze

    • 1 tablespoon canola oil
    • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
    • 1 tablespoon brown sugar

    Method

    1. Proof the yeast with honey and warm water in a warm place for 5 minutes, or until it is frothy and foamy.
    2. Mix the flour, baking powder and salt, rub in the oil, and add the sugar.
    3. Add the yeast mixture and vinegar to a well and mix to a soft, sticky dough, using the extra warm water as needed.
    4. Let the dough rise in an oiled, covered bowl in a dark place. When it doubles you can rest it in the fridge overnight or continue.
    5. Heat the oven to 180°C (360°F). Divide the dough into 100 g portions and shape balls decorated with thin crosses of dough.
    6. Brush with the glaze, cover and let rise again for 20 minutes. Bake 25 minutes, until puffed with a golden crust.

    Chef Tips

    • Cues: yeast frothy and foamy; dough doubled; buns puffed with a golden crust. The overnight fridge rest and the 20-minute second rise are NO CUE, TIME ONLY.
    • The page's tips say rising takes at least two hours while the card says 1 hour; the body says 100 to 110°F water where the card says only warm.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Water that is too hot, which kills the yeast.

    Cultural Note

    Nermine Mansour bakes it at the start of the school year and during the St. Mary fast; her grandmother baked it when a family member was ill or facing an exam and shared it with Muslim and Christian neighbours. 'It is said' that if the buns rise overnight, the Archangel marks them with his sword.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best the day it is baked; keeps in an airtight tin 2 days (our estimate).

    Reheating: Warm briefly in the oven.

    Serve with: Jam, cheese or zaatar (page suggestion)
