♨️ Kilishi, Oven Method

Kilishi — kilichi

The domestic version with no sun at all — beef sliced as thin as 3 mm, baked at 250°C for fifteen minutes flipping every five, then ten to fifteen more.

This is kilishi for a kitchen without a roof to dry on. Ten pieces of wafer-thin beef, sliced as thin as three millimetres, are seasoned with suya spice, garlic, cloves, ginger, a stock cube, salt and cayenne, and baked at 250°C — fifteen minutes with a flip every five, then another ten to fifteen. The target is stated well: dry and chewy, not crunchy, and the caution that follows matters — it will become drier once it has cooled completely, so stopping while it still seems slightly soft is correct.

Ingredients

Equipment: Very sharp knife, Oven, Baking tray and rack, Spice grinder

  • 10 pieces wafer-thin beef, as thin as 3 mm
  • 1 tbsp suya spice (suya pepper)
  • 3 cloves of garlic
  • 1 tsp cloves (kanafuru)
  • A small piece of ginger
  • 1 small stock cube
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp dry cayenne pepper seeds

Method

  1. Slice the beef as thin as 3 mm — wafer thin.
  2. Grind the garlic, cloves, ginger, cayenne seeds and stock cube together with the suya spice and salt.
  3. Coat the beef pieces thoroughly in the spice mixture.
  4. Preheat the oven to 250°C (475°F), gas mark 9.
  5. Bake 15 minutes, flipping every 5 minutes.
  6. Bake a second time, 10 to 15 minutes.
  7. Take it out while it is dry and chewy, not crunchy.
  8. Cool completely — it will become drier as it cools.

Chef Tips

  • Dry and chewy, not crunchy. That is the stated target and it is easy to overshoot at 250°C.
  • It becomes drier when it has cooled down completely. Pull it while it still has a little give.
  • Flip every five minutes during the first bake. Thin meat at that temperature cooks unevenly if you leave it.
  • Three millimetres means partially freezing the beef first, which makes slicing that thin possible with a domestic knife.
  • Two hundred and fifty degrees is very hot for drying, and it is doing the work the sun does in the traditional method. Watch it rather than trusting the clock.
  • Cloves are in this recipe, which is worth noting — they are canonical in the academic description of yaji and absent from most published blends.
  • This is not the traditional method and makes no claim to be. There is no sun-drying at all, and no labu marinade — the spice goes on dry. For the four-stage traditional process see Kilishi.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Baking until crunchy.
  • Judging the texture while it is still hot.
  • Not flipping during the first bake.
  • Slicing too thick because the beef was not chilled.
  • Expecting the shelf life of properly dried kilishi.

Health Benefits

  • Very high in protein and very low in carbohydrate.
  • No added oil.
  • Lean beef supplies iron, zinc and B12.
  • Cloves and ginger both have documented anti-inflammatory activity.

Cultural Note

The oven kilishi is a diaspora and urban solution, and it is honest about what it is: no sun, no labu, no year on the shelf. What it keeps is the thing people actually want from kilishi — thin, intensely spiced, chewy beef — and it gets there in under two hours instead of a day and a half.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 weeks in an airtight container. It does not keep like properly dried kilishi.

Reheating: Eat as is.

Serve with: Yaji, Cold beer or stout, Hot tea
