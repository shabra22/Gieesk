🌶️ Harissa (Dried Chili Paste)

Harissa (هريسة)

A thick paste of soaked dried red chilies ground with garlic, salt and olive oil, optionally sautéed and flavoured with cumin, caraway and paprika.

1h 10mTotal time
1hPrep
10mCook
32Servings
30Calories
EasyDifficulty

Dried chilies are slit, seeded and soaked in hot water 30 to 60 minutes, drained and ground with garlic, salt, oil and any optional spices. Optionally the mix is sautéed a few minutes until it looks like a thick dark paste, a step the author warns will make you sneeze. The paste is jarred under oil and kept in the fridge for 2 to 4 weeks, or frozen. It is not fermented.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor or meat grinder, Frying pan, Jars, Plastic or latex gloves

  • 10 oz dried red chili peppers
  • 2 heads of garlic cloves, peeled
  • 1 tbsp salt
  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • 1 tbsp cumin seeds (optional)
  • 1 tbsp caraway seeds (optional)
  • 1 tbsp paprika (optional)

Method

  1. Wear plastic or latex gloves to handle the chilies.
  2. Run the knife through the top end of each dried chili, shake them head down and scrape out the seeds. Discard the seeds and soak the peppers in hot water for 30 to 60 minutes.
  3. Drain and grind the chilies in a food processor or meat grinder (or mortar and pestle). Add the garlic, salt, oil and any other spice.
  4. Optional: sauté the mix in a frying pan for a few minutes over medium heat, stirring constantly, until it looks like a thick dark paste.
  5. Transfer to jars and top with oil. Store in a dark place; the fridge is better, where it keeps 2 to 4 weeks, or the freezer for longer.

Chef Tips

  • Cue for the sauté: a thick dark paste. It is optional.
  • The page notes harissa is often flavoured with caraway, coriander seeds or cumin.
  • Work in a ventilated kitchen; the fumes sting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Handling chilies bare-handed.
  • Skipping the oil layer in the jar.
  • Sautéing over high heat.

Health Benefits

  • Chilies add capsaicin; a little goes a long way.

Cultural Note

The page describes harissa as a North African condiment made from chili peppers.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 to 4 weeks in the fridge under oil, longer frozen (per the page).

Reheating: Not heated.

Serve with: Soups, tagines and bessara
