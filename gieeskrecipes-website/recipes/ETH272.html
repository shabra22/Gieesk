Ethiopian New Year Grand Feast — the complete Ethiopian New Year celebration: Doro Wat, Key Wat, Tibs, Gomen, Beyaynetu, Enkutatash Cookies, Honey Cake, and Tej. The most joyful feast in the Ethiopian calendar.

Enkutatash (September 11 by the Gregorian calendar) is the Ethiopian New Year — celebrated when the rainy season ends, the yellow daisies bloom, and children carry flowers from house to house. The feast combines the best of Ethiopian meat cooking with the season's first harvest.

Ingredients

Equipment: Everything — multiple large pots

  • Doro Wat
  • Key Wat
  • Tibs
  • Gomen
  • Beyaynetu
  • Enkutatash Cookies
  • Honey Cake
  • Tej

Method

  1. 2 days before: Doro Wat, Key Wat, start Tej.
  2. 1 day before: bake cookies and cake.
  3. New Year morning: assemble grand mesob. Open Tej.
  4. Share with family and neighbours.

Chef Tips

  • The yellow cookies are the visual centrepiece — yellow is the New Year colour.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • No yellow cookies — they are the seasonal signature.

Cultural Note

Enkutatash is Ethiopia's most joyful day — the end of the rains, the bloom of the yellow daisies, the children singing, and the families gathering to eat together. The Ethiopian New Year feast is the celebration of life, of harvest, and of community.
