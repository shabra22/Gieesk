🍮 Bonet Piemontese

Bonet

A dense, chocolate-amaretti custard baked over a layer of caramel, one of Piedmont's oldest and most beloved desserts, its name coming from the small hat-shaped mold it was traditionally baked in.

Bonet combines cocoa, crushed amaretti cookies, and a splash of rum into a custard base baked gently in a water bath, unmolded to reveal a glossy caramel coating — a dessert so tied to the region that no Piedmontese holiday table is considered complete without it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small saucepan for caramel, Baking pan or individual molds, Larger roasting pan for water bath

  • 150 g (¾ cup) granulated sugar, for the caramel
  • 3 tbsp water, for the caramel
  • 500 ml (2 cups) whole milk
  • 4 eggs
  • 100 g (½ cup) granulated sugar
  • 80 g (⅔ cup) cocoa powder
  • 100 g (3.5 oz) amaretti cookies, crushed
  • 3 tbsp rum

Method

  1. Make the caramel by heating the sugar and water in a small saucepan until it turns a deep amber color, then immediately pour into the bottom of a baking pan or individual molds, tilting to coat.
  2. Warm the milk in a saucepan until just below a simmer.
  3. Whisk the eggs and sugar together, then whisk in the cocoa powder.
  4. Gradually whisk the warm milk into the egg mixture, then stir in the crushed amaretti and rum.
  5. Pour the mixture over the caramel in the pan or molds.
  6. Place the pan in a larger roasting pan and add hot water to come halfway up the sides, creating a water bath.
  7. Bake at 160°C (325°F) for 45–50 minutes until just set with a slight wobble in the center.
  8. Cool completely, then refrigerate for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight.
  9. Run a knife around the edge and invert onto a serving plate to unmold, letting the caramel run down the sides.

Chef Tips

  • Watch the caramel closely as it cooks — it can go from perfectly amber to burnt very quickly.
  • The water bath is essential for a smooth, evenly set custard without cracking.
  • Chilling thoroughly before unmolding makes the process much cleaner and easier.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burning the caramel, which turns bitter rather than pleasantly rich.
  • Skipping the water bath, which can result in an unevenly cooked, cracked custard.
  • Unmolding before the bonet is fully chilled and set, causing it to lose its shape.

Cultural Note

Bonet is one of Piedmont's oldest documented desserts, its name derived from the small cap-shaped copper mold it was traditionally baked in, and remains a fixture of regional holiday tables.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, covered, for up to 4 days.

Reheating: Not applicable; served cold.

Serve with: Espresso
