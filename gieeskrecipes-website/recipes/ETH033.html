🫓 Ethiopian Lamb Firfir

ፍርፍር (የበግ) — Firfir (Lamb)

Ethiopian Lamb Firfir — the celebration version of firfir, made with lamb wat sauce (from Ye-Beg Key Wat) tossed with torn injera, kibbeh, and berbere. More prized than beef firfir — served the morning after a celebration using the previous night's lamb sauce.

Lamb firfir is the post-celebration morning dish — made from the previous night's lamb wat, it signals that the household has been generous enough to have lamb to spare. It is the breakfast of the day after a wedding or religious celebration in Ethiopia.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 3 pieces day-old injera — torn
  • 1 cup lamb wat sauce (ETH025 or similar)
  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • 1 onion — diced
  • 1 tsp berbere
  • 1 jalapeño
  • Salt

Method

  1. See ETH032 — substitute lamb wat sauce for beef Key Wat sauce.

Chef Tips

  • Lamb firfir is richer and more fragrant than beef firfir — the lamb fat in the sauce adds depth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Same as ETH032.

The Science

See ETH032 for firfir cooking science. Lamb wat sauce has a higher fat content than beef wat — this fat is absorbed by the injera more readily, producing a richer, more unctuous firfir.

Cultural Note

Lamb firfir is the morning that proves the previous evening was truly special — the leftover lamb sauce becoming a breakfast dish of even greater character than the wat it came from.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Ayib (ETH096), With a soft-boiled egg for breakfast
