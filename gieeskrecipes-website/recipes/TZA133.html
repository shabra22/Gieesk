🫓 Zanzibar Potato Fritters

Bhajia za Zanzibar

Zanzibar Bhajia — thin potato slices coated in a fragrant chickpea flour batter spiked with fresh coriander, green chilli, and cumin, deep-fried until crispy and golden. The most beloved Zanzibar street snack — eaten standing at the Forodhani and dipped in coconut chutney.

Bhajia (from the Indian bhaji tradition, transformed on the Zanzibar coast) are one of the most beloved Zanzibar snacks — thin potato slices in a crispy, aromatic chickpea batter. The chickpea batter is much crunchier than wheat flour and the Zanzibar spicing makes these completely distinctive.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mandoline or sharp knife for thin slicing, Deep frying pan, Paper towels

  • 4 large potatoes — peeled and sliced very thinly (2—3mm)

Chickpea Batter

  • 1 cup chickpea flour (besan)
  • ½ cup water
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp coriander
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — finely chopped
  • 1 green chilli — finely minced
  • Oil for deep frying

Method

  1. Slice potatoes very thinly — 2—3mm. Pat completely dry.
  2. Whisk chickpea flour with water and all spices until smooth batter.
  3. Add fresh coriander and green chilli. Mix.
  4. Dip potato slices in batter to coat thinly.
  5. Heat oil to 175°C.
  6. Fry in batches — do not overlap — for 3—4 minutes until golden and crispy.
  7. Drain on paper towels.
  8. Serve immediately with coconut chutney.

Chef Tips

  • Very thin potato slices are the key — thick slices do not become crispy.
  • Pat potatoes completely dry.
  • Thin coating of batter — not thick.
  • Serve immediately — they lose crispiness quickly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Thick potato slices.
  • Wet potatoes.
  • Too thick batter.

The Science

Chickpea flour contains more protein and less starch than wheat flour — it produces a crunchier, less chewy crust. The gluten-free nature of chickpea flour means there is no stretchy gluten network — the batter sets rigid and crispy rather than chewy.

Cultural Note

Zanzibar bhajia are the snack that everyone buys on the way to the beach, the market, or the evening prayer — the warm crispy fritters in a paper cone, dipped in coconut chutney, eaten while walking through the narrow lanes of Stone Town.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Briefly in hot oven.

Serve with: Chatni ya Nazi (TZA118), Pili Pili (TZA117), Urojo (TZA123)
