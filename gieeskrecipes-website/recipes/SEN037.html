🎣 Mbakhal, Fishermen’s Rice with Nététou

Mbakhal des pêcheurs — mbahal

Rice cooked in a tiof-head broth with two fingers of liquid over it, and a ball of pounded nététou buried in the rice, cooked 20 minutes, then crumbled on top.

Afrik’s mbakhal is a fishermen’s rice where the seasoning goes in twice. A tiof head and a piece of dried fish boil in salted water; 25 g of nététou is pounded with a chile and the onion and stirred in. The fish come out and the rice cooks in the broth — with ‘two fingers of liquid’ standing above it at the start. The remaining nététou is pounded with the second chile and salt, shaped into a ball, buried in a well in the rice, and removed after 20 minutes, to be crumbled over the finished plate. Indigenous butter or palm oil is drizzled on top.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid, Mortar and pestle

  • 1 kg rice
  • 125 g nététou (a possible misprint against 1 kg of rice — reported as printed)
  • 1 large onion
  • 2 chiles
  • 1 tiof head, or 600 g fresh fish
  • 1 piece of dried fish
  • 100 g indigenous butter (liquid or clarified butter), a high-fat butter
  • Lemons — quantity not printed
  • Salt

Method

  1. Put the tiof head and the dried fish in a pot of cold salted water. Bring to a boil.
  2. Pound 25 g of nététou with a chile and the onion. Reduce to a paste and stir it into the boiling pot.
  3. Take out the fish and keep it warm. Add the rice to the sauce — there should remain two fingers of liquid over the rice at the start; if not, add a little hot water.
  4. Pound the rest of the nététou with the second chile and salt and form it into a ball. Make a small hole in the centre of the rice, put the ball in, cover and continue cooking.
  5. After 20 minutes, remove the ball and set it aside.
  6. Eat the fish with the rice, crumbling the cooked nététou mixture over the top.
  7. Drizzle everything with indigenous butter or palm oil.

Chef Tips

  • Two fingers of liquid above the rice at the start is the printed measure — it replaces a water quantity and is easy to check.
  • The nététou ball is a vessel for the seasoning: it steams in the rice, then is crumbled over as a condiment.
  • Nététou is bought here. Taste it before adding salt; it is already salty.
  • If 125 g of nététou looks like too much for 1 kg of rice, it is the page’s own figure — start with less and keep the ball.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the rice too dry at the start.
  • Burying the ball and forgetting to take it out.
  • Over-salting before the nététou is in.

Health Benefits

  • Whole-fish broth gives protein and minerals.
  • Fermented locust bean (nététou) is a concentrated protein and fat source.
  • Gluten-free.

Cultural Note

The Afrik introduction, read by the second pass only, describes mbakhal as a dish of hand-rolled millet couscous with a peanut sauce — then the page prints a rice recipe. The page contradicts itself; both are recorded. It also says the dish is Wolof, a lunchtime dish shared from a communal bowl, and less famous than thiéboudiène or yassa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 1 day refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam gently with a splash of water.

Serve with: Lemon wedges, Palm oil or indigenous butter
