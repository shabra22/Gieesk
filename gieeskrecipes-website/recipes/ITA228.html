Gattò di Patate

A rich Neapolitan mashed potato bake layered with ham, cheese, and eggs, its name a Neapolitan adaptation of the French 'gâteau,' baked until the top turns deeply golden and crisp.

Gattò reflects the same French Bourbon-era influence found in sartù, its name a phonetic Neapolitan take on gâteau, transforming ordinary mashed potato into a substantial baked dish layered with cured meat and melting cheese.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot for boiling, Potato masher, Baking dish

  • 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) potatoes, peeled
  • 3 eggs
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) unsalted butter
  • 100 g (1¼ cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 150 g (5.3 oz) cooked ham, diced
  • 200 g (2 cups) mozzarella or provolone, diced
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) whole milk
  • 80 g (¾ cup) breadcrumbs
  • Pinch of nutmeg
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Boil the potatoes until very tender, then drain and mash while still hot.
  2. Beat in the butter, milk, eggs, Parmigiano, and nutmeg, mixing until smooth. Season well.
  3. Butter a baking dish and coat with a layer of breadcrumbs.
  4. Spread half the mashed potato mixture into the dish.
  5. Layer the diced ham and mozzarella evenly over the potato.
  6. Cover with the remaining mashed potato, smoothing the top.
  7. Scatter with more breadcrumbs and a little extra Parmigiano.
  8. Bake at 200°C (400°F) for 30–35 minutes until deeply golden on top.
  9. Let rest for a few minutes before slicing and serving.

Chef Tips

  • Mash the potatoes while still hot for the smoothest texture, and season generously, as potatoes need confident salting.
  • Let the dish rest briefly after baking, which helps it hold together for cleaner slices.
  • This dish is excellent served warm as a main course or cut into smaller squares as a side.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-seasoning the potato mixture, which needs a generous hand with salt to avoid tasting bland.
  • Using cold, previously mashed potatoes, which don't incorporate the eggs and butter as smoothly.
  • Skipping the breadcrumb topping, which is what gives the finished dish its characteristic crisp top.

Cultural Note

Gattò di patate reflects Naples' historic French Bourbon culinary influence, its name a Neapolitan phonetic adaptation of the French word gâteau.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat covered in the oven until warmed through.

Serve with: A simple green salad
