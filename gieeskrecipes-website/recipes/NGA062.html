🟡 Okpa

Okpa — okpa di oku, okpa wawa

Bambara nut flour steamed in leaf wraps for at least an hour — done when it is solid all over, and yielding twelve medium wraps.

Okpa is a steamed pudding of bambara nut flour and it is the breakfast of Enugu, so strongly associated with the Ninth Mile Corner that the place and the food are named together. Four hundred and fifty grams of flour, three quarters of a cup of red palm oil and a tablespoon of salt go into lukewarm water, wrapped and steamed for at least an hour. The seasoning is deliberately minimal to let the beans taste of themselves. Note the water figure: the ingredient list says one litre, but the page states 1.2 litres twice and says plainly that 1.2 is the quantity for the perfect texture.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, Whisk or wooden spoon, Large pot with a lid, Banana or uma leaves, or foil

  • 450 g okpa flour (bambara nut flour), 1 lb or 3 1/2 cups
  • 1.2 litres lukewarm water — see the note on the two figures
  • 3/4 cup red palm oil
  • 1 tbsp salt
  • Habanero pepper, to taste

For wrapping

  • Banana or uma leaves, or foil

Method

  1. Prepare 1.2 litres of lukewarm water.
  2. Put the okpa flour in a large bowl and add the palm oil, working it in so the flour takes up the oil evenly.
  3. Add the lukewarm water gradually, mixing all the time, until you have a smooth batter with no lumps.
  4. Add the salt and the habanero pepper to taste, and mix again.
  5. Spoon the batter into leaf wraps or foil parcels and seal them.
  6. Arrange the parcels in a pot with water at the base, not covering them.
  7. Cover and steam at least 1 hour.
  8. The okpa is done when it is solid all over.

Chef Tips

  • Work the palm oil into the dry flour before any water goes in. It coats the particles and stops them lumping.
  • Lukewarm water, added gradually. Hot water starts to cook the flour as it goes in and gives you lumps you cannot remove.
  • Solid all over is the test — not firm at the edges and soft in the middle. Press the centre of a parcel through its wrapping.
  • At least an hour. The source says 'at least', and under-steamed okpa is wet and grainy.
  • Keep the seasoning minimal. Bambara nut has a distinct flavour and the dish is built to show it off.
  • Water at the base of the pot, never over the parcels.
  • Twelve medium wraps is the stated yield from 450 g, which makes this a batch worth making for a week of breakfasts.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using hot water, which lumps the batter.
  • Adding the water before the oil.
  • Steaming for less than an hour.
  • Letting the water cover the parcels.
  • Over-seasoning it.

Health Benefits

  • Bambara nut is high in plant protein, fibre and lysine.
  • Steamed rather than fried.
  • Red palm oil supplies carotenoids and vitamin E.
  • Low glycaemic index and naturally gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Okpa and the Ninth Mile Corner outside Enugu are so tightly linked that the stretch of road is known for it — travellers stop to buy wraps still hot from the pot. Bambara groundnut is an underused African legume, drought-tolerant and high in protein, and okpa is the dish that makes the best case for it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated in the wrapping, 2 months frozen.

Reheating: Steam 10 to 15 minutes, or microwave in the leaf.

Serve with: Hot pap or akamu, Bread, Hot tea
