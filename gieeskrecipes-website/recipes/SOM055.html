Caano Geel Beddel — Somali Champagne

Not camel milk — the stand-in Somalis abroad invented for it, buttermilk and yogurt blended and fizzed with lemonade just before serving, chasing the soured, slightly curdled camel milk they grew up on.

This recipe is honest about what it is. Camel milk is the thing Somalis in the diaspora miss most and cannot buy, so households worked out ways to approximate its texture and its sour tang, and nearly every family has its own formula. What is being imitated is not fresh camel milk but the soured, faintly curdled kind that is most prized, and the nickname Somali champagne comes from the fizz rather than from anything alcoholic. If you can get real camel milk, drink that instead.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or immersion blender, Large jug

  • 1 L buttermilk
  • 750 g tub of 1% plain yogurt
  • 480 ml (2 cups) lemonade — Sprite, 7UP or similar
  • 3 tbsp sugar, to taste (optional)
  • 1/4 tsp salt (optional)

Method

  1. Blend the buttermilk and yogurt together with the sugar and salt, if using, until completely smooth.
  2. Cover and refrigerate. A day ahead is best; two days is better still.
  3. Mix in the lemonade just before serving, not before — the fizz is the point and it will be gone by morning.
  4. Pour and serve cold. Do not add ice.

Chef Tips

  • Make the base a day ahead. It genuinely improves after a day or two in the fridge, as the sourness settles.
  • The lemonade goes in at the last moment. Added early it goes flat and the drink tastes merely of sweet buttermilk.
  • No ice. Chill the jug properly instead — ice dilutes it and thins the body that is the whole point of the exercise.
  • Every Somali household has its own version of this. Adjust the yogurt-to-buttermilk ratio until it tastes like what you remember.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the lemonade hours early, which leaves a flat drink.
  • Adding ice, which waters down the texture being imitated.
  • Serving it the moment it is blended, before the base has had time in the fridge.
  • Presenting it as camel milk. It is a substitute, and Somalis who grew up on the real thing will tell you so immediately.

Health Benefits

  • Buttermilk and yogurt both supply protein, calcium and live cultures.
  • Made with 1% yogurt, which keeps it close to camel milk's naturally low fat content.
  • The sugar is optional and the drink works without it.

Cultural Note

The Horn of Africa has the largest camel population in the world and camels have been herded in Somalia for more than two thousand years. There is a Somali proverb that a mouthful of camel's milk will last a man half a day. That this substitute exists at all is a measure of how much its absence is felt.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The base keeps 3 to 4 days refrigerated. Add the lemonade per glass rather than to the jug if it will not all be drunk.

Reheating: Not applicable — served cold.

Serve with: Cad iyo Caano, Hilib Ari Karis, Muqmad
