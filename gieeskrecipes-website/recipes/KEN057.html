🍢 Kenyan Grilled Goat Skewers

Mishkaki ya Mbuzi

Mishkaki ya Mbuzi — Kenyan Grilled Goat Skewers — tender cubes of goat marinated in garlic, ginger, lemon, and spices then charcoal-grilled until smoky and juicy. Many consider goat mishkaki the superior version — richer and more flavourful than beef.

Goat mishkaki is often considered the traditional version of this classic Swahili street food — goat has been herded along the East African coast for centuries, long before beef became widely accessible. The richer flavour and slightly firmer texture of goat hold up beautifully to the charcoal and the spiced marinade.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill, Skewers, Mixing bowl, Tongs

  • 700g boneless goat — leg or shoulder, cut into 2—3 cm cubes
  • 12 skewers
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 3 tbsp lemon juice
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ½ tsp cayenne pepper — optional
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Combine all marinade ingredients. Add the goat cubes and mix well.
  2. Marinate for at least 2 hours — overnight is best.
  3. Thread goat cubes onto skewers with small gaps.
  4. Grill over glowing charcoal for 12—18 minutes, turning every 4 minutes.
  5. Goat takes slightly longer than beef — check one piece by cutting open. The inside should be just cooked through with no pink.
  6. Rest 5 minutes and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Goat takes slightly longer than beef to cook through on skewers — allow an extra 3—5 minutes.
  • Leg meat is ideal — more flavourful and holds its shape on the skewer.
  • The longer the marinade, the better the result with goat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Undercooking — goat must be cooked through, not served pink like beef.
  • Cutting pieces too large — they take too long to cook evenly.

The Science

Goat has less intramuscular fat than beef, which means it dries out more quickly if overcooked. The marinade — particularly the oil and acid from lemon juice — helps retain moisture during grilling by coating the surface and slightly denaturing the outer proteins.

Health Benefits

  • Lean protein — goat has less fat than beef or lamb.
  • Grilling uses minimal added fat.

Cultural Note

Goat mishkaki is the traditional version of this iconic Swahili street food, with roots going back centuries to the coastal trade routes that brought marinated skewered meat to East Africa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat in oven at 180°C for 5—7 minutes.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Chapati, Ugali, Pili Pili Sauce, Fresh Lemon Wedges
