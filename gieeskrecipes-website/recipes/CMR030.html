🥣 Water Fufu (Fermented Cassava Fufu)

Water fufu / fufu

Peeled cassava soaked 4–5 days until soft, blended, squeezed through cheesecloth, then stirred over medium heat 10–15 minutes until cooked through and shaped into ovals.

Cassava is peeled (frozen is fine), fully covered in water and left covered outside the fridge for a couple of days; the water is drained and refreshed daily. After about 4 to 5 days it is removed — the page says it ‘should be soft by now’ and notes that not all the cassava gets soft, in which case it can be cut smaller. It is blended in batches with a little water and squeezed in cheesecloth. Portions can be wrapped and frozen then thawed. The paste is cooked on medium heat, stirring vigorously, 10–15 minutes until cooked through, and shaped into ovals. The author’s tips: 1–2 teaspoons baking soda ‘to aid fermentation’, lime juice for acidity, a warm place, longer in cool weather; fermentation adds a light sour flavour. FERMENTATION CUE, STATED PLAINLY: the only observable cue is that the cassava should be soft at day 4–5, plus a faint sour flavour; no smell, bubble or colour cue is printed. SAFETY AS PRINTED: cassava is toxic raw, so always cook it through. The author also writes that fermentation ‘eliminates any potentially toxic compounds’; that is her claim, the page does not support it, and it should not replace thorough cooking.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large container with lid, Blender, Cheesecloth, Pot

  • 2 tubers cassava
  • Water, as much as needed
  • 1 lemon, juiced (optional)
  • 1–2 teaspoons baking soda (tip on the page)

Method

  1. Peel the cassava (frozen is fine). Place in a container and cover fully with water. Cover and keep outside the fridge.
  2. Drain and refresh the water daily. After about 4–5 days remove the cassava; it should be soft. If some pieces are not soft, cut them smaller.
  3. Blend in batches with a little water. Squeeze the pulp in cheesecloth to remove the liquid.
  4. Wrap portions and freeze, then thaw (as the page describes), or use at once.
  5. Cook the paste on medium heat, stirring vigorously, 10–15 minutes until cooked through. Shape into ovals.

Chef Tips

  • The only printed doneness cue for the ferment is that the cassava ‘should be soft by now’ at about 4–5 days.
  • A warm place speeds it up; it takes longer in cool weather.
  • Always cook the paste through — the page says raw cassava is toxic. A reader’s claim that 10 minutes’ boiling kills toxins is not the author’s statement.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Relying on fermentation to make raw cassava safe.
  • Skipping the daily water change.
  • Not squeezing out enough liquid so the fufu is sticky.

Cultural Note

The page does not attribute the recipe to Cameroon; it calls fufu a staple of many African countries. It is included because the same author pairs eru soup with water fufu.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The page suggests wrapping portions and freezing them.

Reheating: Thaw before cooking, as the page describes.

Serve with: Eru soup, Ndolé, Njama njama
