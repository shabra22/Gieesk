🍽️ Ethiopian Fasting Platter

ጾም ፍርፍር — Tsom Beyaynetu

Ethiopian Beyaynetu — the complete vegan fasting platter. Misir Wat, Kik Alicha, Shiro Wat, Gomen, Tikil Gomen, Fasolia, Timatim Selata, and Shimbra Wat on a large communal injera. The most important vegan feast tradition in the world — assembled during 200+ annual fasting days.

Beyaynetu (meaning 'variety') is the Ethiopian fasting platter — the complete spread of all the plant-based preparations assembled on one large injera. Ordered at Ethiopian restaurants or assembled at home on fasting days, it demonstrates that fasting food in Ethiopia is a celebration, not a deprivation. It is arguably the world's greatest vegan meal tradition.

Ingredients

Equipment: Multiple pots, Large mesob or serving plate

    Assemble from individual recipes:

    • Misir Wat — the red lentil centrepiece
    • Kik Alicha — golden mild peas
    • Shiro Wat — smooth chickpea stew
    • Kik Wat — spiced split peas
    • Gomen — collard greens
    • Tikil Gomen — spiced cabbage
    • Fasolia — string beans
    • Timatim Selata — tomato salad
    • Large injera base

    Method

    1. Day before: make Misir Wat, Kik Alicha, Shiro Wat, Kik Wat — these improve overnight.
    2. Morning of: make Gomen and Tikil Gomen.
    3. 30 minutes before: warm all wats. Make Fasolia.
    4. Immediately before: make Timatim Selata.
    5. Assemble: lay large injera. Place Misir Wat at centre. Surround with all other preparations.
    6. Serve with rolled injera in a basket alongside.

    Chef Tips

    • Misir Wat is always the centrepiece — the most important fasting dish.
    • Alternate red (Misir Wat, Kik Wat) and golden (Kik Alicha) with green (Gomen) for maximum visual impact.
    • Make the wats the day before — they are dramatically better.
    • Fresh salad goes on at the very last moment.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not preparing ahead — impossible to make all in one day well.
    • Under-seasoning the wats.

    Health Benefits

    • The fasting platter provides complete plant-based nutrition — all essential amino acids from the combination of legumes, complete vitamins from the greens, and extraordinary antioxidants from berbere. It may be the most nutritionally complete single meal tradition in Africa.

    Cultural Note

    The Ethiopian beyaynetu is one of humanity's greatest food traditions — a 2,000-year-old vegan meal tradition that emerged not from environmental philosophy but from religious observance, and that happens to be extraordinary by every measure: taste, nutrition, variety, and visual beauty. During the 200+ annual fasting days, millions of Ethiopians eat this platter, making Ethiopia the most significant vegan food culture in Africa.
