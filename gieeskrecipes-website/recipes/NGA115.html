🥥 Fried Coconut Rice

Fried coconut rice

Coconut milk boiled with stock and bay, rice cooked in it ten minutes to al dente, then stir-fried with vegetables and steamed five to ten minutes.

This is coconut rice and Nigerian fried rice collapsed into one dish, and the author is open that her cooking does not separate her British and Nigerian sides. A tin of coconut milk is boiled with a stock cube, thyme and bay for five minutes, the rinsed rice cooks in it for ten minutes until mostly absorbed and al dente, and meanwhile onion, mushrooms, peppers and carrot are stir-fried seven minutes with coriander, turmeric, garlic powder and ginger. The rice joins them, and the whole thing steams covered for five to ten minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium pot with a lid, Deep frying pan or wok, Sieve

  • 370 g long-grain or basmati rice
  • 400 ml tin coconut milk
  • 1 tbsp groundnut oil or coconut oil
  • 1 chicken stock cube
  • 1/2 tsp dried thyme
  • 2 dried bay leaves
  • 1/2 red onion, finely chopped
  • 6 closed-cup mushrooms, wiped and chopped
  • 1/2 green pepper, finely chopped
  • 1/2 yellow pepper, finely chopped
  • 1 large carrot, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1/4 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • Salt and white pepper

Method

  1. Boil the coconut milk with the stock cube, thyme and bay leaves for 5 minutes.
  2. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, then add it to the milk, just covered.
  3. Cook on medium-high, stirring occasionally, 10 minutes, until the liquid is mostly absorbed and the rice is al dente.
  4. Heat the oil in a deep frying pan over medium-high heat.
  5. Add the onion, mushrooms, peppers and carrot with the coriander, turmeric, garlic powder and ginger.
  6. Stir 7 minutes, until the vegetables begin to soften.
  7. Add the rice and season with salt and white pepper.
  8. Cover and steam on medium-low 5 to 10 minutes.
  9. Divide between four plates, or serve communally.

Chef Tips

  • Rinse until the water runs clear. For a dish that then gets stir-fried, loose starch is what makes it claggy.
  • Al dente at the ten-minute mark, deliberately. It finishes in the steaming stage and fully cooked rice here would go to mush.
  • Just covered by the coconut milk, not swimming. A 400 ml tin to 370 g of rice is a close ratio.
  • Seven minutes on the vegetables with the dry spices in from the start. The spices bloom in the oil rather than being stirred in later.
  • White pepper rather than black. It is the Nigerian fried rice convention and it keeps the colour clean.
  • This is a hybrid and the author says so. It is coconut rice and Nigerian fried rice in one pan — it serves both ideas and is faithful to neither.
  • Closed-cup mushrooms are not a traditional Nigerian ingredient. If you want this closer to Nigerian fried rice, swap them for liver or prawns.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not rinsing the rice.
  • Cooking the rice through before the steaming stage.
  • Adding the spices after the vegetables have softened.
  • Too much coconut milk, which gives porridge.
  • Expecting it to taste like either canonical dish.

Health Benefits

  • Coconut milk provides medium-chain fats.
  • A generous vegetable load for a rice dish.
  • Turmeric and ginger both have documented anti-inflammatory activity.

Cultural Note

Diaspora cooking is part of the Nigerian record rather than a departure from it, and this recipe is honest about being two things at once. Its author grew up in Croydon on memories of Nigerian boarding-school food, and the result is a dish that would be recognised in both places and claimed by neither.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam, or microwave with a splash of water.

Serve with: Hibiscus Chicken, Dodo (fried plantain), Grilled fish
