🐑 Ethiopian Lamb Shoulder Tibs

የትከሻ ጥብስ — Ye-Tikesha Tibs

Ethiopian Lamb Shoulder Tibs — bone-in lamb shoulder pieces sautéed in kibbeh with jalapeño, rosemary, onion, and tomato. More gelatinous and flavourful than boneless lamb tibs — the collagen from the shoulder bones enriches the pan sauce.

Shoulder tibs uses the bone-in shoulder rather than the tender leg cuts — the result is a richer, more complex tibs where the collagen from the bone enriches the pan sauce. It requires slightly longer cooking but the flavour reward is significant.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy wide pan

  • 700g bone-in lamb shoulder — chopped through the bone into pieces
  • 3 tbsp kibbeh
  • 1 onion — diced
  • 2 tomatoes — diced
  • 2 jalapeños
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp berbere
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Heat kibbeh until very hot.
  2. Sear lamb shoulder pieces on all sides — 3 minutes.
  3. Add onion. Cook 3 minutes.
  4. Add garlic, jalapeño, rosemary, berbere, and tomatoes.
  5. Cook 5—8 more minutes — the bone-in pieces need more time than boneless.
  6. Season. The pan sauce should be slightly more gelatinous than standard tibs.

Chef Tips

  • Bone-in lamb shoulder pieces — the specific cut.
  • Slightly longer cooking than boneless tibs — the bone conducts heat more slowly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expecting it to cook as fast as boneless tibs.

The Science

Bone-in cooking releases collagen and marrow from the bone surface — these dissolve into the pan sauce as the cooking progresses, producing the characteristic slightly sticky, gelatinous sauce.

Cultural Note

Shoulder tibs is the tibs of the knowledgeable Ethiopian restaurant-goer — ordered for the richer, more complex flavour that only bone-in cooking can produce.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Briefly in kibbeh.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Ayib (ETH096)
