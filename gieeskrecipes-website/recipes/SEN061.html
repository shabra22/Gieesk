🍅 Jollof Fonio

Jollof au fonio

Tomato paste and onion cooked until dark red but not burnt, simmered with broth until the oil rises, then four cups of cooked fonio folded through.

Fonio replaces rice in a short jollof. Oil, a little onion and two tablespoons of tomato paste are stirred over low heat about five minutes until the paste is dark red but not burnt; broth goes in with salt and pepper and simmers another 15 to 20 minutes until the oil rises to the surface; four cups of cooked fonio are folded in until well combined. The Lake Isle blogger who tested it reports her tomato paste failed to darken on her first attempt and, having doubled the broth by mistake, she got an oily dish; half the paste on the second try turned out ‘delicate, tangy’.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Wooden spoon

  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped onion
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 cup vegetable broth
  • 1 tsp salt, plus more if needed
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 4 cups cooked fonio

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a saucepan over medium. Add the onion and tomato paste, then lower the heat to low. Stir gently with a wooden spoon about 5 minutes, until the paste is dark red but not burnt. Add a little water or broth if it starts to scorch.
  2. Stir in the broth and season with the salt and pepper. Bring to a boil, lower the heat and simmer another 15 to 20 minutes, until the oil rises to the surface.
  3. Fold in the cooked fonio until well combined. Adjust the salt and pepper. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Dark red but not burnt’ and ‘the oil rises to the surface’ are the two printed cues — the same two that govern every jollof and ceebu.
  • If the paste doesn’t darken, the blogger’s second attempt used half the paste.
  • Add broth only as printed: she doubled it by mistake and the dish turned oily.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burning the paste.
  • Doubling the broth.

Cultural Note

The post does not mention Senegal, which is notable given that the author is Thiam’s publisher. It says only that jollof is a West African tomato rice dish with countries competing over whose version is best, and does not trace its origin.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Covered with a splash of water.

Serve with: Grilled fish or chicken
