🍝 Garganelli al Prosciutto

Garganelli al Prosciutto e Piselli

Garganelli al Prosciutto rolls fresh egg pasta squares around a ridged wooden comb to form small quills, tossed here with prosciutto, peas, and a light cream sauce.

Garganelli are shaped using a special ridged tool called a pettine (comb), a technique unique to Emilia-Romagna that gives the pasta ridged grooves on the outside and a hollow center, ideal for catching a light, creamy sauce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Garganelli comb (pettine) or gnocchi board, and a small dowel, Large pot, Large skillet

  • 1 batch Fresh Egg Pasta Dough
  • 150 g (5 oz) prosciutto, cut into strips
  • 150 g (1 cup) fresh or frozen peas
  • 1 small shallot, finely chopped
  • 30 g (2 tbsp) butter
  • 120 ml (½ cup) heavy cream
  • 60 g (¾ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, plus more for serving
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Roll the pasta dough thin and cut into squares about 4cm.
  2. Place a square diagonally on the ridged comb (or gnocchi board), and roll it around a thin dowel from one corner to the other, pressing gently to imprint the ridges, then slide off to form a small ridged tube.
  3. Repeat with all the dough, laying the shaped garganelli on a floured tray.
  4. Melt the butter in a skillet over medium heat. Add the shallot and cook until soft, about 3 minutes.
  5. Add the prosciutto and cook briefly, about 1–2 minutes.
  6. Add the peas and cream, and simmer gently for 3–4 minutes.
  7. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the garganelli for 2–3 minutes until tender and floating.
  8. Drain and toss with the sauce, adding the Parmigiano and tossing until glossy.
  9. Season with salt and pepper, and serve immediately with extra Parmigiano.

Chef Tips

  • If you don't have a traditional pettine, a clean hair comb or the ridged side of a gnocchi board works as a substitute.
  • Roll firmly but gently — too much pressure can flatten the pasta rather than forming a proper tube.
  • Add the prosciutto only briefly to the heat — overcooking makes it tough and salty.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rolling the garganelli too loosely, causing them to unravel during cooking.
  • Overcooking the prosciutto, which toughens it and intensifies its saltiness too much.
  • Using pre-shredded Parmigiano, which doesn't melt as smoothly into the sauce.

Cultural Note

Garganelli is a specialty of Romagna, particularly the towns of Bertinoro and Faenza, where the ridged pettine tool has been used for generations to shape this distinctive pasta.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Uncooked garganelli can be dried and stored at room temperature, or frozen for up to 1 month.

Reheating: Best prepared fresh; the cream sauce doesn't reheat particularly well.

Serve with: A crisp Lambrusco or Pignoletto wine, A simple green salad
