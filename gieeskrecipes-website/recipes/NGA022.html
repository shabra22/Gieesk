🫘 Moin Moin

Moin moin — moimoi, steamed bean pudding

Peeled beans blended very fine and frothy, then beaten a further eight minutes and five more after the oil — the air is what makes it set rather than go dense.

Moin moin is a steamed bean pudding and the whole thing turns on aeration. The beans are soaked only ten minutes, pulsed so each one splits and the skin falls off, finished by hand, then blended very fine with stock and peppers until frothy. After that comes the work: eight minutes of mixing to beat air in, then five minutes more once the oil and seasonings are folded through. Wrapped in banana leaves and steamed forty to fifty minutes, it sets into something smooth and light. Under-beaten, it comes out as a brick.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Stand mixer, hand mixer or a wooden spoon, Steamer or a deep pot, Foam sponge, for washing the leaves

  • 3 cups brown or black-eyed beans
  • Banana leaves, or any containers for steaming
  • 2 cooking spoons vegetable or sunflower oil
  • 1 cup chicken stock (optional)
  • 2 bell peppers (tatashe) or shombo
  • 1 onion
  • 1 atarodo (scotch bonnet)
  • 3 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 1 small boiled or smoked mackerel (titus)
  • Smoked or raw prawns, or corned beef (optional)
  • 4 boiled eggs
  • 2 bouillon cubes
  • Salt to taste

Used in the method but not listed in the original

  • Palm oil, melted and cooled

Method

  1. Soak the beans 10 minutes, then pulse a few times in a jug blender so each bean splits and the skin falls off. Finish peeling by hand.
  2. Blend the peeled beans with the stock, peppers and onion until smooth — very fine and frothy, with no lumps.
  3. Mix the batter approximately 8 minutes with a stand or hand mixer, or a wooden spoon, to incorporate air.
  4. Melt the palm oil if using, let it cool, and add it to the batter with the vegetable oil.
  5. Add the bouillon cubes, crayfish and salt. Adjust the consistency with stock or water until it is semi-fluid but definitely not watery, and mix 5 additional minutes.
  6. Shred the mackerel and fold it in with the other proteins.
  7. Wash the leaves with a foam sponge and salt, and remove the stalks.
  8. Fold the leaves in doubles, put 2 spoonfuls of batter in each, add a boiled egg whole or halved, and fold over.
  9. Arrange in a steamer, or line the pot with the leaf stalks and leaves. Keep the water beneath the moin moin, not submerging it.
  10. Cover tightly and steam 40 to 50 minutes on medium heat until set, topping up the water as needed.

Chef Tips

  • Thirteen minutes of beating, total, in two stages. This is not optional and it is what separates moin moin from a dense bean block.
  • Fold, do not beat, once the peppers and seasonings are in — over-mixing at that stage knocks the air back out.
  • Semi-fluid but definitely not watery. Too loose and it will not set; too thick and it steams unevenly.
  • Ten minutes is all the soak the beans need. Longer and they absorb water and go bland.
  • Pulse to peel, do not blend. You want each bean to split so the hull sloughs off.
  • Water beneath, never over. Submerged moin moin becomes bean soup.
  • Banana or uma leaves cook fastest, then foil, then plastic or aluminium plates — which take longest. Factor that into your timing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping or shortening the beating.
  • Over-mixing after the peppers go in.
  • Soaking the beans for hours.
  • Letting the water cover the parcels.
  • Batter too thin, so it never sets.

Cultural Note

Moin moin is one of the most portable foods in Nigeria — wrapped, steamed, eaten cold or hot, sold by hawkers and served beside jollof at every party. The leaf matters more than it looks: uma leaf and banana leaf both perfume the pudding in a way foil cannot, and moin moin elepo, the palm-oil version, is remembered by writers as the hawker's cry that reached the brain before the food did.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 2 months frozen in its wrapping.

Reheating: Steam 10 minutes, or microwave in the leaf.

Serve with: Jollof Rice, Eko, Pap or custard
