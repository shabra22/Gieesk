🍬 Simsim Sesame Snaps

Simsim

Half a kilo of sesame roasted with salt, then folded into caramel the moment it turns golden and patted flat with a fish slice before it sets — you get about thirty seconds.

Simsim snaps are sesame and caramel and nothing else, and they belong to northern Uganda where sesame is a staple rather than a garnish. Four tablespoons of sugar to five hundred grams of seed makes clear what this is: a sesame snack held together by a little caramel, not a sweet with seeds in it. The whole difficulty is the last thirty seconds — caramel sets fast, and everything has to be ready before the sugar goes in the pan.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large tray, Heavy frying pan, Wooden spoon, Fish slice or flat spatula, Sharp knife

  • 500 g sesame seeds, roasted
  • 60 ml (4 tbsp) sugar
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) salt

For the tray

  • A little oil or butter, to grease

Method

  1. Grease the tray and have it ready beside the stove.
  2. Roast or fry the sesame with the salt for about 10 minutes, then set it aside.

The caramel

  1. Add the sugar to the pan and let it caramelise, about 3 minutes.
  2. Mix it constantly with a wooden spoon until the caramel turns golden.
  3. Add the sesame and mix fast until it clumps together.
  4. Scoop it out onto the large tray, spread it out and pat it down quickly with a fish slice.
  5. Cut into whatever shapes you want while it is still warm.
  6. Let it cool for 5 minutes before serving, especially to children — it is very hot.

Chef Tips

  • Grease the tray and put it next to the stove before you start the caramel. Once the sesame goes in you have seconds, not minutes.
  • Roast the sesame with the salt first, separately. This is two operations in one pan, not one.
  • Stir the sugar constantly. Caramel goes from golden to burnt with no warning and burnt caramel tastes of nothing but burning.
  • Pat it down with a fish slice, fast. It sets as it cools and a thick uneven slab will not cut.
  • Cut it while warm. Cold, it shatters wherever it likes rather than where you want.
  • Five minutes of cooling before anyone eats it. Molten sugar burns badly and this is a children's snack.
  • Four tablespoons of sugar for half a kilo of sesame is correct. This is a sesame snack, not a sweet.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not having the tray ready.
  • Walking away from the caramelising sugar.
  • Adding cold sesame slowly instead of all at once.
  • Letting it cool before flattening.
  • Serving it straight from the tray.

Health Benefits

  • Sesame seeds are exceptionally high in calcium, magnesium, iron and zinc.
  • A good source of plant protein and healthy unsaturated fats.
  • Only four tablespoons of sugar across eight servings.
  • No dairy, no gluten and no oil beyond what greases the tray.

Cultural Note

Sesame — simsim — is a northern Ugandan staple, ground into pastes, mixed with groundnut, and eaten with vegetables as well as made into snaps like this. In the south it is more of a garnish. The distinction is one of the clearer north-south lines in Ugandan food.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 weeks in an airtight container. Keep it dry — humidity makes caramel sticky.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Ugandan tea, Roasted Simsim, Millet porridge
