🫘 Ayikple

Ayikple — bean and toasted corn one-pot

Taught at Flair Catering in Accra — adzuki beans simmered an hour with smoked herring and crayfish, then bound with up to two cups of toasted corn flour.

Ayikple is beans and toasted corn flour in one pot, and this version was taught by Barbara Baeta at her Flair Catering in Accra — which makes it one of the few recipes in the Ghanaian record traceable to a named professional kitchen. A cup of adzuki beans simmers about an hour with a third of a cup of smoked herring, a quarter cup of dried crayfish, ginger, salt and sliced jalapeño or kpakpo shito. A cup and a third to a cup and a half of tomato gravy goes in, and then up to two cups of toasted corn flour — ablemanu — to bind it. Her warning is specific: do not substitute cornmeal.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot, Strong wooden spoon, Knife

  • 1 cup adzuki beans (8 oz)
  • 1/3 cup smoked herring
  • 1/4 cup dried crayfish
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tsp ginger
  • About 1/4 cup sliced jalapeño, or 6 kpakpo shito
  • 1 1/3-1 1/2 cups tomato gravy
  • Up to 2 cups toasted corn flour (ablemanu)

Method

  1. Simmer the adzuki beans about 1 hour, until tender.
  2. Add the smoked herring, dried crayfish, salt, ginger and sliced chillies.
  3. Stir in the tomato gravy.
  4. Add the toasted corn flour gradually, stirring, until the mixture binds.
  5. Use up to 2 cups of corn flour depending on how stiff you want it.
  6. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Toasted corn flour — ablemanu — and not cornmeal. Her instruction is explicit: do not substitute corn meal, which is too coarse and gritty.
  • Add the corn flour gradually. Up to two cups is the ceiling, not the requirement, and how much you need depends on how wet the beans and gravy are.
  • An hour on the adzuki beans. They need to be properly tender before anything else goes in, because nothing after this stage softens them further.
  • Smoked herring and dried crayfish together are the savoury base. Neither is optional if you want this to taste like itself.
  • A quarter cup of sliced jalapeño or six kpakpo shito — the source offers both, and the kpakpo shito is the Ghanaian choice.
  • The tomato gravy is a separate preparation and is quantified by volume, so make it first — see Basic Tomato Gravy.
  • Toasted corn flour is the same ingredient as the tom brown in aprapransa, and the two dishes share the technique of binding a wet pot with it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using cornmeal instead of toasted corn flour.
  • Adding all two cups of flour at once.
  • Beans not fully tender before the rest goes in.
  • Leaving out either the herring or the crayfish.
  • Letting it catch — this mixture is thick and scorches easily.

Health Benefits

  • Adzuki beans are high in plant protein, folate, iron and soluble fibre.
  • Smoked herring and dried crayfish both supply protein, calcium and iodine.
  • Toasted maize adds fibre and B vitamins.
  • A complete one-pot meal with legume and cereal protein combined.

Cultural Note

Binding a pot of beans with toasted corn flour is the same move that makes aprapransa, and it belongs to a family of West African one-pots that turn a stew into a meal without a separate starch. That this one comes down through a named Accra caterer rather than a village kitchen is a reminder that professional Ghanaian cooking has its own transmission line.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated. It sets very firm.

Reheating: Low heat with water or extra gravy to loosen.

Serve with: Tomato Gravy, Fried fish, Shito
