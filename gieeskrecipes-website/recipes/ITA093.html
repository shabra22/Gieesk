Pollo alla Cacciatora

'Hunter-style' chicken braised with tomatoes, mushrooms, bell peppers, and olives in a rustic, savory sauce — the sort of hearty, one-pot meal Italian hunters historically brought home to share.

Cacciatora varies considerably by region, with northern versions leaning white-wine-and-mushroom and southern versions favoring tomato, peppers, and olives, but the dish always shares its rustic, forage-based spirit — simple, seasonal, and built to feed a hungry table.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven with lid

  • 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) chicken, cut into 8 pieces
  • Flour, for dredging
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 bell peppers, sliced
  • 200 g (7 oz) mushrooms, sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry white wine
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 80 g (½ cup) black or green olives, pitted
  • 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Season the chicken pieces and dredge lightly in flour, shaking off the excess.
  2. Warm the olive oil in a heavy pot and brown the chicken pieces well on all sides in batches, then set aside.
  3. In the same pot, sauté the onion and bell peppers until softened, then add the mushrooms and cook until golden.
  4. Add the garlic and cook briefly, then pour in the wine and let it reduce slightly, scraping up any browned bits.
  5. Add the crushed tomatoes, olives, and rosemary, and return the chicken to the pot.
  6. Cover and simmer gently for 40–45 minutes, until the chicken is very tender and the sauce has thickened.
  7. Uncover for the final 10 minutes if the sauce needs to reduce further.
  8. Season to taste and serve hot, directly from the pot.

Chef Tips

  • Browning the chicken thoroughly before braising builds essential depth of flavor in the final sauce.
  • Using bone-in, skin-on chicken pieces gives more flavor and moisture than boneless cuts through the long braise.
  • This dish improves with a day's rest, as the flavors continue to develop.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the browning step, which skips an essential layer of flavor.
  • Overcrowding the pot when browning, which steams rather than sears the chicken.
  • Simmering uncovered too long, which can dry out the chicken before the sauce properly thickens.

Cultural Note

Cacciatora, meaning 'hunter-style,' reflects a long Italian tradition of rustic, resourceful cooking built around whatever ingredients a day of hunting and foraging might yield.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; flavor improves the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, covered, until warmed through.

Serve with: Crusty bread or creamy polenta, A medium-bodied red wine
