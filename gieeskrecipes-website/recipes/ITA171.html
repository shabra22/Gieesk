🌰 Castagnaccio

Castagnaccio

A dense, naturally gluten-free Tuscan cake made entirely from chestnut flour, studded with pine nuts and raisins, and finished with fresh rosemary — a distinctly autumnal, unsweetened-tasting treat.

Castagnaccio has no added sugar beyond what the chestnut flour and raisins naturally contribute, resulting in a dense, earthy cake that reflects centuries of Tuscan mountain communities relying on chestnuts as a dietary staple during lean winters.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Whisk, Round baking pan, about 26cm

  • 400 g (3⅓ cups) chestnut flour
  • 500 ml (2 cups) water, approximately
  • Pinch of salt
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus more for the pan
  • 50 g (⅓ cup) raisins, soaked in warm water
  • 40 g (¼ cup) pine nuts
  • 2 sprigs fresh rosemary

Method

  1. Whisk the chestnut flour, water, and salt together into a smooth, thin batter, similar to a crepe batter, working out any lumps.
  2. Whisk in most of the olive oil.
  3. Let the batter rest for 30 minutes.
  4. Generously oil a round baking pan and pour in the batter, spreading to an even layer about 1.5cm thick.
  5. Scatter the drained raisins and pine nuts evenly over the top, and tuck rosemary sprigs into the batter.
  6. Drizzle with the remaining olive oil.
  7. Bake at 200°C (400°F) for 30–35 minutes, until the top develops characteristic deep cracks and a dark, slightly crusty surface.
  8. Cool before slicing — castagnaccio is traditionally served at room temperature.

Chef Tips

  • The deeply cracked, almost rustic-looking surface is entirely traditional and expected — don't worry if it looks quite different from a typical Western cake.
  • This cake is naturally unsweetened and gluten-free by nature, not by substitution, given its chestnut flour base.
  • Castagnaccio is best enjoyed the day it's made, though it keeps reasonably well for a couple of days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expecting a light, sweet cake — castagnaccio is dense and only mildly sweet, an acquired taste for some.
  • Making the batter too thick, resulting in an overly dense, gummy texture rather than the traditional thin layer.
  • Skipping the rosemary, which provides an essential aromatic contrast to the chestnut flavor.

Health Benefits

  • Chestnut flour is naturally gluten-free and provides fiber and complex carbohydrates.

Cultural Note

Castagnaccio has deep roots in Tuscany's mountainous Apennine communities, where chestnut flour was historically a dietary staple long before wheat became widely affordable.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store covered at room temperature for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: Fresh ricotta, for a modern accompaniment, A glass of Vin Santo
