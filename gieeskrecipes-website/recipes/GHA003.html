🍚 Omo Tuo

Omo tuo — rice balls

Two cups of jasmine rice to three of water, cooked until extremely soft and mashed hot — mushy, the source insists, but not mashed.

Omo tuo is the Sunday accompaniment to groundnut and palm nut soup across Akan Ghana, and it is rice cooked deliberately past the point anyone would want to eat it loose. Two cups of jasmine rice, washed until the water runs clear, go into three cups of water and cook covered on medium-low until extremely soft and tender. While it is still hot, portions are gently mashed and shaped between wet hands. The source's own warning is the whole technique: it should not look like mashed potatoes, it should look mushy but not mashed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot with a lid, Wooden spoon or rice paddle, Bowl of water, for wetting the hands

  • 2 cups jasmine rice, washed until the water runs clear
  • 3 cups water
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Wash the rice until the water runs clear.
  2. Bring the rice and water to the boil over medium-high heat with salt to taste.
  3. Reduce the heat to medium-low, cover, and cook until the rice is extremely soft and tender.
  4. While it is still hot, gently mash portions of the rice.
  5. Wet your hands and shape into balls.
  6. Serve in a bowl with soup poured around them.

Chef Tips

  • The texture rule, and it is the only one that matters: it should not look like mashed potatoes, it should look mushy but not mashed. Some grain structure survives.
  • Mash it hot. Cooled rice will not come together and the balls fall apart in the soup.
  • Extremely soft and tender is the cue, which means cooking well past normal rice doneness. Properly cooked rice makes bad omo tuo.
  • Wash until the water runs clear. The loose starch would make it gluey rather than mushy.
  • Wet hands between every ball. Rice sticks harder than fufu does.
  • Three documented versions disagree sharply on water — 1.5, 1.75 and about 2.33 cups per cup of rice. This is the driest of the three; if your rice is not collapsing, the next one up is Omo Tuo with Seven Cups of Water.
  • Gently is the operative word in the source's mashing instruction. Beating it develops the starch and gives you paste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the rice to normal doneness.
  • Mashing it once it has cooled.
  • Skipping the wash.
  • Mashing hard enough to make a smooth paste.
  • Dry hands when shaping.

Health Benefits

  • Fat-free as made.
  • Gluten-free.
  • Easy to digest, which is why it is often given to the very young and the unwell.
  • Lower in calories per serving than fufu or banku.

Cultural Note

Omo tuo is Sunday food in much of Ghana — the thing made after church to go with a long-cooked groundnut or palm nut soup, shaped into balls and set in the bowl for the soup to be poured over. The balls are eaten by pinching off pieces with the fingers of the right hand, the same way as fufu.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated, wrapped so they do not dry out.

Reheating: Steam briefly, or drop straight into hot soup.

Serve with: Groundnut Soup, Palm Nut Soup, Light Soup
