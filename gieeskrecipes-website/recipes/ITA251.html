🦪 Tiella di Riso Patate e Cozze

Tiella Barese

Bari's beloved layered bake of rice, potato, and mussels, cooked together in one pan until the rice absorbs all the briny mussel liquor - one of Puglia's most treasured one-pot dishes.

Tiella takes its name from the earthenware pan it's traditionally baked in, and getting the liquid ratio right is the dish's central challenge, since the raw rice must fully cook and absorb the mussel and vegetable juices without ever being stirred once assembled.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide, shallow baking dish or earthenware pan

  • 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) mussels in their shells, cleaned
  • 300 g (1 1/2 cups) short-grain rice
  • 4 potatoes, peeled and thinly sliced
  • 3 tomatoes, sliced
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated pecorino, plus more for topping
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • 150 ml (2/3 cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Open the mussels raw with a small knife (or steam very briefly just until they begin to open), reserving the mussel liquor and half the shells with the mussel still attached.
  2. Strain and reserve the mussel liquor.
  3. Oil a wide baking dish and layer half the potato slices on the bottom.
  4. Scatter half the onion and garlic over the potatoes, followed by half the mussels (in their half-shells), a layer of sliced tomatoes, and a scattering of parsley and pecorino.
  5. Sprinkle the raw rice evenly over this layer.
  6. Repeat with the remaining potato, onion, garlic, mussels, tomatoes, and parsley.
  7. Pour the reserved mussel liquor and enough water over everything to just cover the rice, and drizzle generously with olive oil.
  8. Season with salt and pepper, and top with a final scattering of pecorino.
  9. Bake at 190C (375F) for 45-55 minutes, without stirring, until the rice and potatoes are fully tender and the liquid has been absorbed.
  10. Let rest for 10 minutes before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Getting the liquid level right is crucial - too little and the rice won't cook through, too much and the dish becomes soupy.
  • The dish is never stirred once assembled and baking, which is essential to keeping the distinct layers intact.
  • Opening the mussels raw and keeping them on the half-shell gives the most attractive traditional presentation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring the dish while it bakes, which disrupts the layered structure this dish depends on.
  • Adding too little liquid, leaving the rice undercooked and chalky.
  • Slicing the potatoes too thick, which can leave them undercooked by the time the rice is done.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Tiella is one of Bari's most treasured culinary traditions, its precise layering and liquid ratio a genuine point of pride among Puglian home cooks.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in the oven, covered, with a splash of water.

Serve with: A chilled white wine from Puglia
