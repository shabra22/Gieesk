🍊 Jus de Madd, Casamance Madd Fruit Juice

Jus de madd (Saba senegalensis)

500 g of madd inner fruit, 1 litre of water and sugar to taste, chilled — the entire printed recipe for the juice of the orange-fleshed Casamance vine fruit.

The Slow Food and FAO booklet ‘Sénégal: de la terre à la table’ gives jus de madd as three ingredients — 500 g of madd (Saba senegalensis) inner fruit, 1 litre of water, sugar to taste — and says to chill the juice afterwards. NOTHING ELSE IS PRINTED: no temperatures, no times, no yield and no doneness cue, and the research notes record no method for extracting the inner fruit. FAO’s separate feature story on the fruit (11 August 2025, no byline) records that the fruit is gathered ‘by hand or with poles’ from vines sometimes over 40 metres tall, that local communities mainly eat it fresh, that only about two percent of collected fruit is processed into juices and preserves, and that the Madd de Casamance geographical indication was registered on 25 June 2024.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Sieve

  • 500 g madd inner fruit (Saba senegalensis)
  • 1 litre water
  • Sugar, to taste — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Extract the inner fruit of the madd. The source records no method for this step.
  2. Combine the 500 g of inner fruit with the 1 litre of water and sweeten to taste. The source records no method for combining, straining or timing.
  3. Chill the juice.

Chef Tips

  • The recipe prints no times, temperatures or cues.
  • Taste the sweetness yourself — ‘sugar to taste’ is the only guide.
  • Chill thoroughly; the booklet only says to chill.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expecting a method the source does not print.
  • Adding sugar before tasting.
  • Serving warm.

Health Benefits

  • Madd is eaten fresh and processed into juices and sweet and savoury preserves.

Cultural Note

‘Madd fruits are deeply rooted in the culture of the lower Casamance region.’ (FAO). The FAO story names no ethnic group.

Storage & Serving

Storage: No storage time is printed.

Reheating: Served cold.

Serve with: Cereal porridge (juice is often drunk with it, per the FAO feature), Meals
