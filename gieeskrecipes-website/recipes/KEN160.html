🍈 Kenyan Jackfruit Chips

Chips za Fenesi

Kenyan Jackfruit Chips — thin slices of young green jackfruit deep-fried until golden and crispy, lightly salted and spiced. A distinctive coastal Kenyan snack made from one of the largest and most underutilised fruits in the country — intensely satisfying and unlike any other chip.

Jackfruit (fenesi) grows prolifically along the Kenyan coast and in coastal-influenced areas, yet remains underutilised in Kenyan cooking. Young green jackfruit, before it ripens and sweetens, has a firm, neutral-flavoured flesh that crisps beautifully when fried. The resulting chips are distinctive — denser and more substantial than potato chips, with a slightly meaty texture when semi-crisp.

Ingredients

Equipment: Gloves (jackfruit sap is very sticky), Sharp knife, Mandoline or knife for slicing, Deep frying pan

  • 500g young green jackfruit — peeled and cut (use gloves — the sap is sticky)
  • Oil for deep frying
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp paprika
  • ¼ tsp garlic powder
  • ¼ tsp curry powder — optional

Method

  1. Wear gloves — jackfruit sap stains permanently. Oil your knife before cutting.
  2. Cut jackfruit into quarters. Remove the core and any seeds. Slice the flesh into thin pieces (3—4mm).
  3. Soak in cold salted water for 15 minutes to remove some of the starchy liquid. Drain and dry thoroughly.
  4. Heat oil to 170°C.
  5. Fry in small batches for 4—5 minutes until golden and crispy.
  6. Drain on paper towels.
  7. Season immediately with salt, paprika, and other spices.
  8. Cool completely before serving or storing.

Chef Tips

  • Always wear gloves when handling raw jackfruit — the latex sap is extremely sticky and stains permanently.
  • Oil your knife and cutting board before cutting to minimise sap sticking.
  • Young green jackfruit (not ripe yellow jackfruit) is essential for chips.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using ripe jackfruit — too sweet and soft for chips.
  • Not wearing gloves — the sap is notoriously difficult to remove.
  • Thick slices — chips rather than crisps; reduce the heat slightly for more even cooking.

The Science

Young jackfruit is high in starch and low in sugar, making it ideal for frying. The starch gelatinises during frying and then dehydrates to form a crispy structure. Ripe jackfruit has converted its starch to sugar, which would caramelise and burn before the chips dried out.

Health Benefits

  • Jackfruit is rich in vitamin C, potassium, and fibre.
  • Contains antioxidants including carotenoids.
  • Naturally gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Jackfruit chips represent the untapped potential of Kenya's coastal food culture — a remarkable fruit that grows in abundance yet is rarely used to its full potential. As awareness of its culinary versatility grows, jackfruit chips are appearing more frequently in Nairobi health stores and coastal market stalls.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight container for up to 5 days.

Reheating: Oven at 160°C for 5 minutes to re-crisp.

Serve with: Kenyan Masala Chai, Tamarind Dipping Sauce, Pili Pili Sauce, Cold Soda
