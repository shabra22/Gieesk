🌽 Githeri

Githeri — black beans and maize

Pre-boiled black beans and maize kernels fried together with tomato, garlic and curry until the pan dries, then simmered with a cup of water until it is gone.

Githeri is beans and maize cooked together, eaten across East Africa, and this version is explicitly a twist: the maize and the beans are mixed together while frying, which the traditional dish does not do. Think of it as an alternative way of eating beans and posho — and in a sense a kind of katogo, since katogo is exactly the habit of cooking a starch and a sauce in the same pot. Any dried bean works; the one firm rule is that the maize must be very well pre-boiled.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pan with a lid, Wooden spoon

  • 480 ml (2 cups) black beans, boiled and drained
  • 480 ml (2 cups) maize kernels, boiled and drained
  • 2 large tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 1 large green pepper, chopped
  • 1 clove garlic, crushed and chopped
  • 240 ml (1 cup) water
  • Ground coriander
  • Curry powder
  • Black pepper
  • A stock cube or chicken seasoning
  • Salt
  • Cooking oil

Method

  1. Put a clean pan on high heat and add oil.
  2. Add the garlic and onions and stir until an aroma is released.
  3. Add the tomatoes and cover the pan. Wait for them to become soft and tender.
  4. Add the boiled maize and the beans and stir until evenly distributed.
  5. Add the remaining ingredients except the water, and stir occasionally until they dry out.
  6. Add the water and stir.
  7. Let it simmer on low heat until all the water is gone.
  8. Take it off the heat and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Make sure the maize is very well pre-boiled and soft. Maize takes far longer than beans and it will not catch up in this pan.
  • Mixing the maize and beans while frying is a deliberate deviation — the traditional githeri does not do that. It is written this way on purpose.
  • Any type of dried bean works. Black beans are what this version uses but it is not a requirement.
  • Let it dry out before the water goes in. That stage is what fries the spice into the beans rather than boiling it off.
  • Simmer until the water is completely gone. Githeri is a dry dish, not a stew.
  • Season it however you like — the original is relaxed about this and lists a stock cube, curry powder and coriander as a starting point.
  • It is a reasonable way to think about this as katogo, since a starch and a protein are cooking together in one pot, which is exactly what katogo means.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using undercooked maize.
  • Adding the water before the pan has dried out.
  • Leaving liquid in it at the end.
  • Adding the beans before the tomatoes are soft.
  • Cooking the whole thing on high heat.

Health Benefits

  • Beans and maize together form a complete protein — the classic legume-and-grain pairing.
  • Very high in fibre, folate and magnesium.
  • Plant-based with a modest amount of oil.
  • Filling and cheap, which is why it is eaten across East Africa.

Cultural Note

Githeri is more associated with Kenya than Uganda and crosses the border freely, which is normal for food in this region. The provenance of this particular pot is worth noting: over five kilos of black beans from the author's grandmother, worked through as katogo, then bean stew, then patties, then this.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Warm in a pan with a splash of water.

Serve with: Sauteed Garlic Dodo, Chapati, Avocado
