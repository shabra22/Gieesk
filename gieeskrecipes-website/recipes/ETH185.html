🥚 Ethiopian Stuffed Eggs

ሙሉ እንቁላል — Mulu Inqiilal

Ethiopian Stuffed Eggs — hard-boiled eggs halved with yolks mixed with berbere, mitmita, silsi, and coriander. The Ethiopian devilled egg — where Western versions are mild and creamy, the Ethiopian version is fierce and fragrant.

Ethiopian stuffed eggs take the universal party food and make it completely Ethiopian — the yolk filling is mixed with berbere, mitmita, and silsi instead of mayonnaise and mustard, producing a devilled egg of heat and complexity.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Piping bag or spoon

  • 6 hard-boiled eggs — halved

Filling

  • 1 tsp berbere
  • ¼ tsp mitmita
  • 1 tbsp silsi or tomato paste
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 tsp oil
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander
  • ½ tsp salt

Garnish: pinch mitmita, coriander leaf

    Method

    1. Remove yolks from halved eggs.
    2. Mash yolks with berbere, mitmita, silsi, lemon, and oil until smooth.
    3. Add coriander. Season.
    4. Fill egg white cavities.
    5. Garnish with a pinch of mitmita and coriander.

    Chef Tips

    • The filling should be much drier than standard devilled egg — no mayonnaise.
    • The mitmita garnish is the visual signature.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding mayo — loses the Ethiopian character.

    The Science

    The egg yolk's emulsifying properties (lecithin) still work without mayonnaise — the small amount of oil and lemon juice create a stable mixture with the yolk proteins. The berbere's fat-soluble compounds dissolve into the yolk fat for uniform distribution.

    Cultural Note

    Ethiopian stuffed eggs demonstrate the Ethiopian cook's instinct — to take any format and apply the berbere tradition to it. The result is always more interesting and more satisfying than the original.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate 2 days.

    Reheating: Serve cold.

    Serve with: At Ethiopian parties, As a café starter, At celebration buffets
