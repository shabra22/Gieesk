🌿 Miondo (Fermented Cassava Sticks — Research-Scale Process)

Miondo

Whole cassava roots soaked unpeeled in drums 3 days until fermented and softened, peeled, mashed, pressed overnight, ground, formed into sticks, wrapped in ginger leaves and steamed 40–55 minutes.

The process documented in the paper ‘The effectiveness of cyanogen reduction during cassava processing into miondo’ (Transformation Alimentaire du Manioc, pp. 309–317, ORSTOM, 1995), studied at Bikoko Yabaki near Douala. Two-year-old cassava roots (local red-skin variety) have their heads and tails cut off and are cleaned, then soaked UNPEELED in large plastic drums of stream water for 3 days (ambient 28–32.5 °C; soak-water 29–31 °C; pH 4.32–6.23). The roots are peeled and mashed by hand to remove the central fibres and excess water, pressed overnight in an old fertilizer sack between tied wooden poles (pulp pH fell from 5.68–5.79 to 4.32–4.54), ground in a hammer mill and kneaded into cylinders 15–20 cm long and 1.5–2.0 cm in diameter. They are wrapped in large leaves (Zingiber officinale) and tied with raffia, 20 pieces per bundle, then steamed or boiled. SAFETY, AS PRINTED: fresh unpeeled roots held 396.2–512.0 mg HCN/kg (dry weight); the final product 1.4–2.8 mg HCN/kg, a mean reduction of 99.5%; soaking, mashing and dewatering were the most important steps; the authors call boiling ‘necessary’ for removing residual cyanogens. COOKING-ROOM GAP: this is a village-scale study with a hammer mill and drums; the paper prints no water quantity, no yield and no home-kitchen method.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large plastic drums, Fertilizer sack and wooden poles for pressing, Hammer mill, Steamer or large pot

  • Two-year-old cassava roots (local red-skin variety) — weight not printed
  • Stream water to cover — quantity not printed
  • Large leaves (Zingiber officinale) for wrapping
  • Raffia to tie (20 pieces per bundle)

Method

  1. Cut the heads and tails off the roots and clean them.
  2. Soak the roots unpeeled in drums of stream water for 3 days. On day 3 the roots should have fermented and softened.
  3. Peel the roots and mash them by hand, removing the central fibres and excess water.
  4. Press the pulp overnight in a sack between tied wooden poles.
  5. Grind in a hammer mill. Knead into cylinders 15–20 cm long and 1.5–2.0 cm in diameter.
  6. Wrap in large leaves, tie with raffia, 20 pieces per bundle.
  7. Steam or boil 40–55 minutes (the text says 40–55; the paper’s figure says 40–50).

Chef Tips

  • The only observable cue printed is that on day 3 the roots ‘had fermented and softened’; smell and colour are not described.
  • The paper reports pH measurements — a laboratory measure, not a kitchen test.
  • Boiling is described by the authors as necessary for removing residual cyanogens.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stopping the soak before the roots soften.
  • Skipping the pressing, which the paper calls one of the most important detoxifying steps.
  • Treating this as a home recipe — it is a documented village process.

  • Plain cassava starch; the paper reports a 99.5% reduction of cyanogens by this process.

Cultural Note

The paper documents one village’s practice (Bikoko Yabaki). Afrik articles use ‘miondo’ for a cassava stick and, in one case, as another name for nkui couscous — an inconsistency across that publication, not corrected here.
