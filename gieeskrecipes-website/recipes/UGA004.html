🍠 Classic Katogo with Tubers and Beans

Katogo

Irish potatoes, purple yams and sweet potatoes layered into a garlic and tomato base, topped with boiled beans and cooked in one pan until the potatoes give — three starches in one pot and no measuring.

This is katogo as a category rather than a recipe: three different tubers, beans on top, one pan, no scales. It is deliberately unmeasured, and the cook who published it says so — you go by how many people you are feeding. The order matters more than the amounts. Irish potatoes first because they take longest, then yams, then sweet potatoes, then the beans over the top so they steam rather than sink. The tubers give it an earthy warmth and the beans make it rich enough for cold weather.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan with a lid

    No weights — go by how many you are feeding

    • Irish potatoes, peeled and halved
    • Purple yams, halved
    • Sweet potatoes, halved
    • Beans, already boiled
    • Tomatoes, chopped
    • Onions, chopped
    • Garlic cloves, finely chopped
    • Cooking oil
    • A few drops of chilli oil
    • Curry powder
    • Salt
    • About half a glass of water

    To garnish

    • Green onion, finely chopped

    Method

    1. Put the saucepan on high heat with the cooking oil and let it get hot.
    2. Add the garlic and onions and wait for them to turn translucent.
    3. Add the tomatoes, chilli oil, curry powder and salt, and stir.
    4. Add the Irish potatoes first, then the yams, then the sweet potatoes.
    5. Top with the boiled beans and cover the pan for about 10 minutes.
    6. Add about half a glass of water — more if you want it soupy — and stir so the beans are evenly distributed.
    7. Let it cook until the Irish potatoes are soft and tender.
    8. Take it off the heat, scatter over the green onion, and serve warm.

    Chef Tips

    • The order of the tubers is the recipe. Irish potatoes go in first because they are the slowest; sweet potatoes last because they collapse.
    • Beans on top, not stirred in. They steam above the tubers and stay whole.
    • Boil the beans beforehand. Dried beans thrown in raw will still be hard when everything else is mush.
    • This is published without measurements on purpose — the cook's own note is that when making katogo, instincts are used depending on how many people are to be cooked for. Do not go looking for a ratio.
    • The yams should be the purple kind if you can get them.
    • Half a glass of water gives a thick katogo. Add more for a soupy one.
    • It is good cold-weather food and that is when it gets made.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Putting all three tubers in at once.
    • Stirring the beans down into the pan.
    • Using unboiled beans.
    • Adding so much water it becomes a soup you did not want.
    • Letting the sweet potatoes go in early and disintegrate.

    Health Benefits

    • Three different tubers give a broad spread of carbohydrate, potassium and vitamin C.
    • Beans add plant protein and a lot of fibre.
    • Sweet potatoes bring beta-carotene the others do not.
    • Entirely plant-based and very filling for the calories.

    Cultural Note

    That one word covers a dish made of bananas, a dish made of offal and a dish made of three tubers tells you what katogo is: not a recipe but a way of cooking. It is the most flexible thing in the Ugandan repertoire and the most honest about what was actually in the house.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Warm in a covered pan with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Dodo, Ugandan tea, Avocado
