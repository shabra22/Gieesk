🫀 Ethiopian Organ Meat Mix

ዱለት — Dulet

Ethiopian Dulet — a mixture of minced raw or lightly cooked beef tripe, liver, and lean beef, seasoned with mitmita, jalapeño, onion, and kibbeh. The offal celebration dish — eaten at feasts where whole animals are slaughtered, honouring the animal completely and wasting nothing.

Dulet is the Ethiopian philosophy of whole-animal eating made into a dish — when a cattle or sheep is slaughtered for a celebration, the organs are mixed with lean meat, seasoned with mitmita and jalapeño, and eaten as a celebration in themselves. Nothing is discarded; everything is honoured.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 200g beef or lamb tripe — cleaned and finely minced
  • 150g beef or lamb liver — finely minced or chopped
  • 150g lean beef — finely minced
  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • 1 tsp mitmita
  • 1 onion — very finely diced
  • 2 jalapeños — finely minced
  • 2 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Combine all three meats with onion, jalapeño, garlic, and salt.
  2. Heat kibbeh in a pan.
  3. Add meat mixture. Cook, stirring, 5—8 minutes.
  4. Dulet can be served raw (leb-leb) or cooked through.
  5. Add mitmita. Stir through.
  6. Serve immediately on injera.

Chef Tips

  • The freshest possible organ meats are essential.
  • The balance of tripe, liver, and lean beef is traditional — each contributes differently.
  • Cook to your preferred level — many eat dulet leb-leb (slightly warmed but not fully cooked).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Poor quality organ meats.
  • Over-cooking — liver becomes rubbery.

The Science

Liver contains extremely high concentrations of B12, iron, and vitamin A — these nutrients are heat-labile to varying degrees. Lightly cooking (leb-leb) preserves more of these nutrients than fully cooking. Tripe's collagen partially dissolves even in brief cooking, releasing gelatin that gives dulet its characteristic slightly sticky consistency.

Cultural Note

Dulet is the dish of wholeness — the celebration that honours the entire animal, the acknowledgment that every part of what was sacrificed deserves respect. In Ethiopian celebration cooking, nothing from a slaughtered animal is discarded.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Eat immediately — no storage for raw. Cooked: refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Briefly in kibbeh.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Ayib (ETH096), With tej at celebrations
