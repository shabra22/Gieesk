🥜 Kenyan Kashata

Kashata

Kashata are Kenya's most beloved traditional sweets — coconut and groundnut clusters cooked in a spiced sugar syrup until golden and brittle, with flavours of cardamom and cinnamon. A Swahili coastal confection sold at every market and celebration.

Kashata come in two varieties — coconut (kashata ya nazi) and groundnut (kashata ya karanga) — often sold side by side at coastal markets. The sugar syrup is cooked until it reaches the soft crack stage, then the coconut or groundnuts are stirred in and the mixture is spooned onto a greased surface to set. The result is sweet, slightly chewy, fragrant with cardamom, and completely addictive.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Swahili Coast — centuries of Arab and Indian Ocean trade influence

Kashata are traditional Swahili confections made from coconut, groundnuts, or both, cooked in sugar syrup until they set into brittle, sweet clusters. They have been made along the East African coast for centuries, sold at markets, celebrations, and street stalls. The name is believed to derive from Arabic, reflecting the long trade relationship between the Swahili coast and the Arabian Peninsula.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Wooden spoon, Greased baking tray or greased parchment

  • 2 cups desiccated coconut or roasted groundnuts (or 1 cup each)
  • 1 cup sugar
  • ½ cup water
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • Pinch of salt
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tbsp butter or coconut oil

Method

  1. Grease a baking tray or line with parchment. Set aside.
  2. Combine sugar and water in a heavy saucepan over medium heat.
  3. Stir until sugar dissolves, then stop stirring and allow to boil.
  4. Cook without stirring for 8—10 minutes until the syrup reaches 130°C or forms a soft ball when dropped in cold water.
  5. Add cardamom, cinnamon, salt, vanilla, and butter. Stir quickly.
  6. Add the coconut and/or groundnuts. Stir vigorously to coat everything in the syrup.
  7. Working quickly, spoon heaped tablespoons onto the greased tray.
  8. Flatten slightly with the back of the spoon.
  9. Allow to cool and set completely — about 15—20 minutes.
  10. Store in an airtight container.

Chef Tips

  • Work very quickly once the nuts or coconut are added — the syrup sets fast.
  • Have the greased tray ready before you start cooking the syrup.
  • The soft ball stage (130°C) is critical — too low and they stay sticky; too high and they become too hard.
  • Wet your hands slightly if you want to shape them by hand instead of spooning.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not working fast enough — the mixture sets in the pan.
  • Sugar syrup too hot — kashata become rock-hard instead of pleasantly chewy.
  • Not greasing the tray — they stick permanently.

The Science

Cooking sugar syrup to the soft ball stage (130°C) produces a supersaturated solution that crystallises around the coconut or nut particles as it cools. The cardamom and cinnamon essential oils are captured in the crystallising sugar matrix, releasing their fragrance with every bite.

Cultural Note

Kashata are among the most recognisable traditional Swahili sweets — present at every coastal market stall, celebration table, and tea break across Kenya. They represent centuries of coastal confectionery tradition.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 weeks.

Reheating: Not applicable — serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Kenyan Masala Chai, As a standalone sweet, With fresh fruit
