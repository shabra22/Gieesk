🫓 Teff Injera

የጤፍ እንጀራ — Ye-Tef Injera

Ethiopian Pure Teff Injera — 100% dark teff grain soaked, ground, and fermented for the full 3 days, producing the most authentic, most sour, most nutritious, and most texturally complex injera possible. The highland tradition — the deep brown injera of Tigray and Amhara that Ethiopians consider the finest.

100% teff injera is the gold standard of injera — the full 3-day fermentation with nothing but teff and water produces an injera of extraordinary depth, sourness, and nutritional density. Dark teff injera has a richer, earthier flavour than the lighter versions; white teff injera is more delicate and slightly less sour. This recipe uses dark (brown/red) teff — the highland standard.

Ingredients

Equipment: Stone grinder or high-powered blender, Large clay or glass fermentation vessel, Mitad or 30cm non-stick pan, Lid

  • 2 cups dark teff — whole grain
  • 3 cups water (for soaking and grinding)
  • 2 tbsp ersho (fermentation starter from previous batch)

Day 3: ½ cup cold water for thinning

    Method

    1. Day 1 — Soak: cover teff grain with 2 cups water. Soak 8 hours.
    2. Grind soaked teff with soaking water until smooth. Add ersho.
    3. Cover. Ferment at room temperature.
    4. Day 2 — Do not disturb much. Stir once gently.
    5. Day 3 — The batter should be deeply sour, bubbly, and slightly separated.
    6. Thin with cold water to the right consistency — thinner than pancake batter.
    7. Cook as per ETH001 — medium heat, covered, never flipped.

    Chef Tips

    • Starting from whole grain and grinding gives a more authentic texture than pre-ground teff flour.
    • 3 full days is the minimum for whole grain injera — the grinding releases natural yeasts from the grain surface.
    • Dark teff injera has a more sour, complex flavour than white teff.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Short fermentation — insufficient sourness.
    • Not soaking the grain before grinding.

    The Science

    Whole grain teff has natural wild yeasts on its surface (as wheat does) — grinding the soaked grain releases these yeasts directly into the batter. This produces a more complex fermentation microbiome than batter made from pre-ground flour, which has lost many of its surface microorganisms during milling.

    Cultural Note

    100% teff injera is the injera that Ethiopians in the highlands consider the real thing — the injera that has been made the same way for 3,000 years, from the same grain that grows on the Ethiopian highlands and nowhere else on earth naturally. It is the taste of Ethiopian identity at its most fundamental.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Room temperature in cloth 3 days. Refrigerate 1 week.

    Reheating: Damp cloth. Microwave 30 seconds.

    Serve with: All Ethiopian stews and wats, Particularly suited to richly spiced wats
