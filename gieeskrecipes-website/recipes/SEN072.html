🫘 Accara, Black-Eyed Pea Fritters from West Africa

Accara / Akara

A pound of dried black-eyed peas soaked overnight, skinned, blended with shallots or onion and garlic to a paste ‘neither too runny nor too thick’, shaped into lemon-sized balls and fried in coconut oil.

30 minPrep

Marie-Claude Mendy’s accara, from the Oldways Cultural Food Traditions collection. A pound of peas soaks overnight and is rubbed free of skins; a quicker route is also printed — pulse the peas briefly with water, cover with water until foaming and pour off the water and the floating skins. The skinless peas are blended to a fine paste with ¾ cup of chopped shallots or onion, optional garlic and a pinch of pepper, seasoned with salt, shaped into lemon-sized balls and fried in three tablespoons of unrefined coconut oil, turning once or twice, five to seven minutes on medium heat. They are served at room temperature with kanni hot sauce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor or blender, Large skillet, Slotted spoon, Paper towels

  • 1 pound dried black-eyed peas
  • 4 cups water, or enough to cover the beans for soaking overnight
  • 3/4 cup chopped shallots or onion
  • 2 tbsp chopped garlic (optional)
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • 3 tbsp unrefined coconut oil
  • Salt, to taste — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Soak the peas overnight in enough water to cover, covered. Drain, rub off the skins with the palms and discard them. Faster: pulse the peas in a food processor with water for a few seconds, add a little more water and pulse again; transfer to a bowl, cover with water until foaming, and pour off the water and the floating skins.
  2. Blend the skinless peas in a food processor or blender into a fine paste with the shallots or onion and garlic, adding water slowly while the machine runs. The paste should be neither too runny nor too thick.
  3. Add salt to taste and a pinch of black pepper.
  4. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-low heat until it is hot (no temperature printed).
  5. Shape the mixture into lemon-sized balls and fry in small batches, flipping once or twice, until golden brown on both sides; the page says it usually takes 5 to 7 minutes on medium heat.
  6. Remove with a slotted spoon, drain on paper towels and serve at room temperature with kanni.

Chef Tips

  • Printed paste target: ‘neither too runny nor too thick’.
  • The quick-soak foaming step is the page’s own shortcut when there is no time to leave the peas overnight.
  • These are served at room temperature, not piping hot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • A runny paste that falls apart in the oil.
  • Not draining the skins off the peas.
  • Frying a big batch at once and lowering the oil temperature.

Health Benefits

  • Black-eyed peas supply plant protein, fibre and folate.
  • Coconut oil is high in saturated fat; use sparingly.

Cultural Note

Mendy is described on the Oldways page as a Chopped winner who owned a Senegalese restaurant in Boston, and the page calls accara a West African dish; Oldways collects culinary heritage recipes. The accara-and-kanni pairing is the classic Senegalese breakfast sandwich filling on a baguette.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 1 day refrigerated.

Reheating: Recrisp in a hot oven.

Serve with: Kanni, Baguette sandwiches, Sosu kaani
