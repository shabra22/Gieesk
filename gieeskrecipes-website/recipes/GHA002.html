🥄 Fufu from Flour

Fufu flour — instant fufu

One part cassava-plantain fufu flour to two parts water, cooked and stirred five to ten minutes — the ratio comes from the institute that developed the flour.

Fufu flour exists because pounding is hard work and a mortar is not portable, and the ratio for it comes from the CSIR Food Research Institute in Accra, which developed and taught the technology. One part flour to two parts water, always, cooked five to ten minutes with constant stirring. It will not have the elastic pull of pounded fufu — the same institute's own research on texture profiles exists precisely because the flour version measures differently — but it is honest about what it is, and it is what most Ghanaian kitchens abroad actually use.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Strong wooden spoon or spatula, Bowl of water, for moulding

  • 1 part cassava-plantain fufu flour
  • 2 parts water

Typical household batch

  • 1 cup fufu flour
  • 2 cups water

Method

  1. Measure the flour and water at one part flour to two parts water.
  2. Bring most of the water to the boil in a heavy saucepan.
  3. Add the flour, stirring hard from the moment it goes in.
  4. Cook 5 to 10 minutes, stirring constantly so no dry pockets or lumps form.
  5. Work it against the side of the pan until it is smooth and pulls away as one mass.
  6. Wet your hands or a bowl and mould into balls.
  7. Serve with soup poured around it.

Chef Tips

  • One to two, flour to water. The CSIR report states it flatly — 'the ratio of the flour to water is always 1:2' — and it is the only published fufu ratio of any kind.
  • Five to ten minutes, stirring constantly. Stop stirring and you get lumps you cannot work out afterwards.
  • Hold back a little of the water. If it tightens too fast you have something to loosen it with.
  • Work it against the side of the pan rather than folding it. That is what develops whatever stretch the flour can give.
  • It will not match pounded fufu and nobody claims it does — the institute that developed the flour also published texture-profile research comparing the two, which tells you they are measurably different.
  • Different brands blend cassava with plantain, yam or cocoyam in different proportions, so taste before you decide a batch is wrong.
  • No source gives a doneness cue beyond the time, so go by the look: smooth, glossy and leaving the pan clean.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the flour to cold water.
  • Any pause in the stirring.
  • Cooking beyond ten minutes, which makes it gluey.
  • All the water in at once, leaving nothing to adjust with.
  • Expecting the pull of pounded fufu.

Health Benefits

  • Gluten-free and essentially fat-free.
  • Retains the resistant starch of the plantain and cassava it is milled from.
  • No added salt or sugar.
  • Far quicker than pounding, which in practice means the dish gets eaten at all.

Cultural Note

Fufu flour is a Ghanaian institutional invention rather than a foreign shortcut — the CSIR Food Research Institute in Accra developed the technology and ran the training that put it into commercial production. That a national research institute took the trouble says something about how much the pounding labour was worth removing.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Eat at once. It sets hard as it cools.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: Light Soup, Groundnut Soup, Palm Nut Soup
