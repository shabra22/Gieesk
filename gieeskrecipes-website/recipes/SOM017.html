🍖 Hilib Ari Karis

Hilib Ari Karis

Bone-in goat meat slow-boiled with whole spices until falling-off-the-bone tender, then finished with a quick fry in ghee for a dish that's both a celebration centerpiece and everyday staple.

This two-stage technique - a long, gentle boil followed by a final fry - is common across Somali meat cookery, the boiling stage tenderizing tougher cuts while the finishing fry in ghee develops a deeply savory crust and rich aroma just before serving.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large skillet

  • 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) bone-in goat meat, cut into pieces
  • 1 onion, quartered
  • 3 cardamom pods
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, whole
  • 80 g (1/3 cup) ghee
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced, for finishing
  • Fresh cilantro, chopped
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Place the goat meat in a large pot with the quartered onion, cardamom, cinnamon, bay leaves, ginger, and whole garlic cloves.
  2. Cover generously with water and bring to a boil, skimming off any foam.
  3. Reduce to a gentle simmer and cook, covered, for 1-1.5 hours, until the meat is very tender.
  4. Remove the meat from the broth (reserving the broth for another use, such as maraq) and pat dry.
  5. Melt the ghee in a large skillet and saute the sliced onion until golden.
  6. Add the boiled goat pieces and fry for 8-10 minutes, turning occasionally, until deeply browned and slightly crisp on the edges.
  7. Season with salt, finish with fresh cilantro, and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • The initial long boil is essential for tenderizing the goat meat before the finishing fry.
  • Do not discard the flavorful cooking broth - it makes an excellent base for maraq or another soup.
  • The final fry in ghee is what gives this dish its rich aroma and deeply savory crust.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the initial boil, which leaves the meat tough rather than properly tender.
  • Skipping the final fry, missing the flavorful crust and aroma this step develops.
  • Discarding the cooking broth, wasting a flavorful base that could be used elsewhere.

Cultural Note

This two-stage boil-then-fry technique is common across Somali meat cookery, valued both as an everyday preparation and as a centerpiece for larger family gatherings.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a pan with a little extra ghee.

Serve with: Bariis Iskukaris, Basbaas
