Baccalà alla Potentina

Salt cod simmered in a rustic tomato and pepper sauce, a landlocked Basilicata take on Italy's beloved preserved fish, brought inland via ancient trade routes long before refrigeration.

Despite Basilicata's limited coastline, salt cod has long been a staple there, transported inland via trade since it needed no refrigeration, and this version simmers the reconstituted fish in a rustic sauce brightened with the region's dried peppers.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl for soaking, Heavy-bottomed pan with lid

  • 800 g (1.75 lb) dried salt cod
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 2 dried peperoni cruschi or a dried chili, crumbled
  • 2 potatoes, peeled and cubed
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Soak the salt cod in cold water for 24 hours, changing the water several times to remove excess salt.
  2. Drain, remove any skin and bones, and cut into large pieces.
  3. Warm the olive oil in a heavy pan and sauté the onion and garlic until softened.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes and crumbled dried peppers, and simmer for 10 minutes.
  5. Add the potatoes and enough water to almost cover, and simmer for 15 minutes until the potatoes begin to soften.
  6. Add the cod pieces and simmer gently for another 15 minutes, until the fish is tender and the potatoes are fully cooked.
  7. Finish with fresh parsley and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Soaking the cod thoroughly and changing the water several times is essential to remove excess salt.
  • The dried Basilicata peppers add a distinctive, mild smoky sweetness rather than intense heat.
  • Let the potatoes get a head start cooking before adding the delicate fish, which needs less time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not soaking the cod long enough, resulting in an overly salty dish.
  • Adding the fish too early, causing it to overcook and fall apart before the potatoes are done.
  • Using a thin, bland tomato sauce that doesn't provide enough flavor for the rich cod.

Cultural Note

Salt cod's long shelf life made it a practical staple even in landlocked parts of Basilicata, transported inland via historic trade routes long before modern refrigeration existed.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A dry white wine
