🥟 Fataya with Sosu Kaani

Fataya — tuna turnovers

A milk-and-oil pastry rolled to 2.5 mm and cut into 10 cm rounds, filled with a food-processed paste of tuna and fried aromatics, frozen, then fried at about 170 °C and served with a cooked habanero pepper sauce.

230 minTotal time
120 minPrep
60 minCook
16Servings
190Calories
HardDifficulty

The most fully specified fataya in the set. A baking-powder dough of flour, whole milk and oil is kneaded to a smooth non-sticky ball and rested 45 minutes to an hour. Red onion, spring onion, green chilli, garlic, parsley and habanero are pulsed in 30-second intervals, stir-fried 5 to 7 minutes with fish sauce, cooled 10 minutes and blended with drained tuna to a paste. Rounds about 9.5 to 10 cm across are filled with about a teaspoon, sealed, forked and frozen; they are fried from frozen at about 170 °C for about 5 minutes, five or six at a time. The sauce is blended tomatoes, onion, chillies and garlic cooked down with tomato paste, fish sauce and lime.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor, Rolling pin, 9.5 to 10 cm round cutter, Lined baking tray, Medium saucepan, Thermometer (optional)

    Pastry

    • 250 g all-purpose flour
    • 1/2 tsp baking powder
    • 1/2 tsp salt
    • 140 ml whole milk at room temperature
    • 1 1/2 tbsp oil

    Filling

    • 100 g red onion, chopped
    • 50 g spring onion, chopped
    • 50 g green chilli, chopped
    • 10 g garlic
    • 1/2 cup parsley
    • 1/2 habanero, de-seeded
    • 2 tbsp oil
    • 2 tsp fish sauce
    • 170 g canned tuna, drained
    • 3 cups oil for frying

    Sosu kaani

    • 200 g tomatoes, chopped
    • 100 g red onion, chopped
    • 1 green chilli, chopped
    • 2 garlic cloves, chopped
    • 1 habanero
    • 1 bay leaf
    • 1/4 cup oil
    • 1 tbsp tomato paste
    • 1/2 tsp salt
    • 2 tsp fish sauce
    • 1/4 tsp black pepper, to taste
    • 2 limes, juiced
    • Store-bought ketchup, for serving — quantity not printed
    • Water to blend the sauce — appears in the method, not the list

    Method

      Pastry

      1. Sift the flour, baking powder and salt into a large bowl. Add the milk and oil, combine lightly with a fork and knead on a clean counter until a smooth, non-sticky ball forms. Cover and rest 45 minutes to 1 hour.

      Filling

      1. Cut the red onion, spring onion, green chilli, garlic, parsley and habanero into large pieces. Pulse in 30-second intervals for 1 to 2 minutes until finely chopped.
      2. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a small pan over medium-low for 2 to 3 minutes. Stir-fry the chopped mixture 5 to 7 minutes, until fragrant and cooked. Stir in the fish sauce, take off the heat and cool 10 minutes.
      3. Add the drained tuna and the cooked aromatics to the processor and blend 2 to 3 minutes, until the mixture forms a paste.

      Shaping

      1. Flour the counter and halve the dough, keeping one half wrapped in cling film. Roll to 0.25 cm and cut circles of about 9.5 to 10 cm, re-rolling scraps. Put about 1 teaspoon of filling in the centre of each, dampen the edges, fold, press to seal and press with a fork. Arrange on a lined tray and freeze.

      Sosu kaani

      1. Blend the tomatoes, red onion, green chilli, garlic and habanero with water for about 1 minute. Heat the 1/4 cup oil over medium, add the blended mixture and bay leaf and cook about 15 minutes, stirring often, until reduced and most of the water has evaporated. Add the tomato paste and cook 5 minutes. Add the salt, fish sauce, pepper and lime juice, cook 1 more minute and cool.

      Frying

      1. Heat 3 cups of oil in a medium saucepan over medium about 5 minutes, to approximately 170 °C. Fry the frozen pastries in batches of 5 to 6, about 5 minutes, turning once, until golden on both sides. Do not overcrowd. Drain on paper towels and serve hot with ketchup and the sosu kaani.

      Chef Tips

      • Freezing the filled pastries before frying is part of the printed method — they are fried from frozen.
      • Keep the half of the dough you are not rolling wrapped so it does not dry.
      • The printed oil temperature is ‘approximately 170 °C’, from a single pass of the page.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Crowding the pan: batches of 5 or 6 only.
      • Rolling the dough thicker than 0.25 cm, which leaves a doughy edge.
      • Leaving the filling wet — it should be a paste, not a loose mince.

      Cultural Note

      The page says these ‘delectable snacks’ are a beloved and widely popular street food and a staple on Ramadan streets. Roseanne Orim’s habanero may be replaced by red bird’s-eye chilli, the page adds.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Frozen unfried pastries keep for weeks; fried ones 1 day.

      Reheating: Fry from frozen; or recrisp fried ones in a hot oven.

      Serve with: Sosu kaani (below), Ketchup, Hot mint tea
