Testaroli al Pesto

Testaroli are Italy's oldest known pasta, a simple crepe-like batter cooked on a flat griddle, cut into diamonds, and briefly boiled — traditionally tossed with fragrant Ligurian pesto.

Believed by some food historians to be a direct ancestor of pasta itself, dating back over two thousand years to Etruscan and Roman times, testaroli is cooked entirely differently from typical pasta — first baked in a special pan called a testo, then cut and briefly simmered.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large flat skillet or crepe pan, Mixing bowl, Whisk, Large pot

    Testaroli batter

    • 300 g (2½ cups) all-purpose flour (traditionally a mix with some whole wheat)
    • 700 ml (3 cups) water
    • ½ tsp salt

    To serve

    • 1 batch pesto (as in ITA036)
    • Extra grated Parmigiano-Reggiano and Pecorino

    Method

    1. Whisk the flour, water, and salt together into a smooth, thin batter, similar to a crepe batter. Let rest for 30 minutes.
    2. Heat a large, flat skillet or crepe pan over medium heat until very hot.
    3. Pour in a thin layer of batter, swirling to coat the pan evenly, and cook until set and lightly browned on the bottom, about 3–4 minutes, without flipping (testaroli is traditionally cooked on one side only, covered).
    4. Remove and let cool slightly, then repeat with the remaining batter to make several flat rounds.
    5. Once cooled, cut the rounds into large diamond or triangle shapes.
    6. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Turn off the heat, then add the testaroli pieces and let them sit in the hot water for 2–3 minutes until just warmed through and tender — they should not be boiled vigorously, as they are already cooked.
    7. Drain and toss immediately with the pesto.
    8. Serve with extra grated cheese.

    Chef Tips

    • Testaroli is already cooked once on the griddle — the "boiling" step is really just a brief warming, not full cooking.
    • Do not flip the testaroli while cooking on the griddle; it is traditionally cooked through on one side with residual heat and a lid.
    • A well-seasoned cast iron pan works beautifully in place of a traditional testo.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Boiling the testaroli vigorously like regular pasta, which can make it mushy since it's already cooked.
    • Making the batter too thick, resulting in a dense, bread-like texture rather than the traditional thin, tender testaroli.
    • Overcooking on the griddle, drying it out before the pasta stage.

    Cultural Note

    Testaroli is closely tied to the Lunigiana area straddling Tuscany and Liguria, and its ancient cooking method — baked, then boiled — is considered one of the most direct living links to pasta's earliest origins in Italy.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: The uncooked griddle rounds keep refrigerated for up to 2 days before cutting and finishing.

    Reheating: Best prepared fresh; the final hot-water step should happen just before serving.

    Serve with: Pesto Genovese, A simple tomato sauce as an alternative, A crisp Ligurian white wine
