🏝️ Mbakhalou Saloum, Peanut Rice of the Saloum Islands

Mbakhalou Saloum — Mbaxal bu Saloum

Rice cooked in a sauce of onions, meat or fish, nététou, guedj, yet, dried Saloum shrimp and 200 g of peanut powder, then steamed under a cloth.

1h 30mTotal time
30mPrep
1hCook
6Servings

HardDifficulty

Lamine Faye of Louma by Seggfaye presents mbakhalou saloum as the emblem of the Saloum islands, ‘where the groundnut meets the mangrove’. Onions and meat are fried, nététou goes into the hot oil, 200 g of peanut powder is slaked in warm water and added with the guedj and yeet, and the sauce simmers 30 minutes. The rice is then added to the sauce and cooked until the liquid is fully absorbed, and the pot is finished by steaming under a cloth. The fish version is closer to the coast and islands; the meat version is common inland.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid, Clean cloth for steaming

  • 1 kg rice
  • 500 g meat or fish
  • 2 onions
  • Tomato concentrate — quantity not printed
  • Mixed vegetables — quantity not printed
  • Salt and pepper — quantities not printed
  • 200 g peanut powder (guerté noflay)
  • 1 tbsp nététou
  • 1 piece of guedj (butter-style)
  • 1 small piece of dried yeet (Cymbium)
  • 100 g dried Saloum shrimp
  • Hot chile purée — to taste

Method

  1. Fry the onions and the meat in oil. Add the nététou to the hot oil.
  2. Slake the peanut powder in lukewarm water and add it to the pot with the guedj and yeet.
  3. Add the tomato concentrate, vegetables and the dried shrimp. Simmer 30 minutes over medium heat, until the sauce begins to take body.
  4. Add the rice to the sauce and cook until the liquid is fully absorbed.
  5. Finish by steaming under a cloth: the rice finishes swelling in the steam.
  6. Add chile purée to taste and serve.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Cook until absorption is complete’ is the cue for the rice; the water quantity is not printed.
  • ‘The sauce must be rich and fragrant’ is the page’s taste cue before the rice goes in.
  • The cloth-steam finish is part of the dish — don’t skip it.
  • Guedj and yeet are bought, already fermented; no fermentation is performed.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the peanut powder dry to the hot oil.
  • Skipping the steam finish.
  • Over-salting before tasting the dried seafood.

Cultural Note

‘Celui de ma terre natale, les Îles du Saloum’, writes Lamine Faye — the dish of the author’s own home islands. Neither page that prints it names an ethnic group; it is attributed to a region only and is not labelled Serer here.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 1 day refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam gently with a splash of water.

Serve with: Hot chile purée, Lemon wedges
