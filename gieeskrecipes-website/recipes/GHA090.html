🟡 Agbeli Kaklo

Agbeli kaklo — bankye krakro, cassava balls

Grated cassava with the juice extracted, blended with onion and deep-fried at constant medium heat — the comfort food of the southern Volta.

Agbeli kaklo is the cassava ball of the Volta Region, sold for as little as twenty Ghana pesewas and described as the comfort food from Volta. One large cassava is soaked overnight, grated, and the juice extracted, then the onion is blended into the drained cassava with salt and optional chilli and spring onion. It deep-fries at constant medium heat, avoiding overcrowding, until golden brown. It is known as agbeli kaklo in Ewe and bankye krakro in Ashanti — and a former president is recorded as having stopped his convoy at Anloga to buy it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grater, Cloth or sack, for extracting the juice, Blender, Deep pan, Slotted spoon

  • 1 large cassava
  • 1 large onion
  • Sunflower or coconut oil, for frying
  • 1 green chilli (optional)
  • 15 g chopped spring onions (optional)
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Soak the peeled cassava overnight.
  2. Grate it finely.
  3. Extract the juice, pressing the grated cassava in a cloth.
  4. Blend the onion into the drained cassava.
  5. Season with salt, and add the chilli and spring onions if using.
  6. Shape into small balls.
  7. Deep fry at constant medium heat, avoiding pan overcrowding.
  8. Fry until golden brown, then drain and serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Constant medium heat is her instruction and it is the key to this snack. High heat browns the outside while the cassava inside stays raw, and raw cassava is not just unpleasant but unsafe.
  • Extract the juice properly. Wet grated cassava will not hold a ball and will spit violently in oil.
  • Avoid overcrowding the pan — her own words. Crowded cassava balls steam and fall apart.
  • Soaking overnight is her step and it softens the tuber for grating. Another documented version drains the grated cassava under a stone instead.
  • Golden brown is the only cue any source gives. Nobody publishes a time or a temperature.
  • Blend the onion INTO the cassava rather than mixing it in. It distributes through the dough properly that way.
  • The names are regional: agbeli kaklo in Ewe, bankye krakro in Ashanti, for the same thing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Frying at high heat, leaving raw cassava inside.
  • Not extracting enough juice.
  • Overcrowding the pan.
  • Skipping the soak, which makes grating much harder.
  • Mixing the onion in rather than blending it.

Health Benefits

  • Cassava is gluten-free and supplies resistant starch.
  • Vegan as written.
  • Draining the juice removes some of cassava's water-soluble cyanogenic compounds.
  • Cheap and filling, which is the point of the snack.

Cultural Note

Agbeli kaklo at twenty pesewas a ball is street food at its most democratic, and the story of a head of state halting a motorcade at Anloga to buy some is the best endorsement any Ghanaian snack has on record. In Ashanti it is bankye krakro and nobody would mistake it for the plantain version.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Same day. They go soft and heavy.

Reheating: A hot oven briefly.

Serve with: Shito, Fried fish, Bambara nuts
