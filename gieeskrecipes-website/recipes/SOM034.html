Surbiyaan — Zurbian

Lamb shoulder braised in yogurt and tomato with cardamom and cinnamon, then layered under saffron rice with browned butter and orange blossom water — the wedding rice of the Arabian Sea coast.

Surbiyaan is a biryani by descent, eaten in Yemen, across the Arabian Peninsula and along the Somali coast, and the name is generally read as an inversion of the first two letters of ruz biryani. It arrived with the dhow trade and stayed as celebration food: weddings, Eid, and the meal put in front of a guest you want to honour. The signature is that the stew underneath stays loose — a surbiyaan that comes to the table dry has been overcooked.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy wide pot with a tight lid, Large pot, for boiling the rice, Mortar and pestle, for the saffron

    For the meat

    • 60 ml (1/4 cup) vegetable oil
    • 1 medium onion, chopped
    • 4 cloves garlic, minced
    • 2 tsp fresh ginger, grated
    • 680 g (1 1/2 lb) bone-in lamb shoulder, in 6 cm cubes
    • 2 tsp xawaash spice blend
    • 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
    • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
    • 1/4 tsp ground turmeric
    • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
    • 2 tsp xawaash spice blend plus 2 tsp salt, or 4 tsp seasoned bouillon powder
    • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
    • 240 ml (1 cup) plain yogurt
    • 590 ml (2 1/2 cups) boiling water
    • 2 medium potatoes, quartered
    • 80 g (1/2 cup) fresh cilantro, chopped

    For the rice

    • 414 g (2 cups) basmati rice
    • 3 L (12 cups) water
    • 3 tbsp salt

    To finish

    • 1 tsp saffron threads, ground and steeped in 60 ml hot water
    • 3 tbsp browned butter
    • 1 tsp orange blossom water

    Method

    1. Fry the onion in the oil over medium heat for 3 minutes, add the garlic and ginger and cook for a minute, then add the lamb and cook for 3 minutes.
    2. Add the xawaash, cardamom, cinnamon, turmeric, black pepper and the seasoning, and cook for a minute. Add the tomatoes, stir, cover and cook 3 minutes.
    3. Stir in the yogurt until it combines with the sauce rather than splitting, then add the boiling water. Cover and cook over medium heat for 30 minutes.
    4. Add the quartered potatoes, stir, cover and cook for another 30 minutes.
    5. Meanwhile grind the saffron and steep it in the hot water.
    6. Bring the 3 litres of water to the boil with the 3 tbsp salt. Wash the rice, add it, and boil for exactly 10 minutes — it will still be underdone. Drain.
    7. Stir the cilantro into the meat, then spread the drained rice over the top and level it. Do not stir the rice into the stew.
    8. Spoon over the saffron water, the browned butter and the orange blossom water.
    9. Cover tightly and leave on the very lowest heat for 20 minutes, then bring the whole pot to the table and serve from it.

    Chef Tips

    • Bone-in lamb shoulder is the right cut. It is more tender than leg and stays moist through an hour of braising.
    • There should still be loose tomato and yogurt stew at the bottom of the pot when you serve. That is the dish, not a mistake.
    • Ten minutes is deliberately short for the rice. It finishes in the steam of the meat, which is where it picks up the flavour.
    • Orange blossom water is what makes it smell like a Somali wedding. Rose water is the acceptable substitute; vanilla is not.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Stirring the rice into the meat, which turns a layered dish into a wet pilaf.
    • Cooking it until dry. Without the stew at the base the rice has nothing to absorb and the dish eats stiff.
    • Adding cold yogurt straight into a hot pan so it splits into curds — stir it in steadily and let it come together first.
    • Boiling the rice until tender before layering, after which the final 20 minutes turns it to paste.

    Health Benefits

    • Lamb provides protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
    • Yogurt contributes calcium and live cultures, though the cooking reduces them.
    • Saffron and cardamom season generously with very little salt.

    Cultural Note

    Surbiyaan is the dish that signals an occasion on the Banaadir coast. It belongs to the same family as the Hyderabadi biryanis carried west by Indian Ocean trade, and turns up at Somali weddings from Mogadishu to Minneapolis, cooked in a pot wide enough that the rice can be layered rather than mixed.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Sprinkle with water, cover and warm through in a low oven so the rice steams rather than fries.

    Serve with: Green salad, Basbaas, Shaah Cadays
