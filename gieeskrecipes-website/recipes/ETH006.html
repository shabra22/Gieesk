🍞 Eritrean-Ethiopian Sweet Bread

ሕምባሻ — Himbasha

Himbasha — the round, wheel-patterned slightly sweet Eritrean and Ethiopian bread. Very similar to Ambasha but with a distinctive wheel or sun-ray pattern cut into the surface and a slightly different spice balance using cardamom and anise alongside fenugreek.

Himbasha is the bread that crosses the Ethiopian-Eritrean boundary most comfortably — deeply beloved on both sides, made the same way by both communities, and carried to every corner of the diaspora. The wheel pattern on its surface is both decorative and functional — it creates a ready-made score for breaking the bread into portions.

Ingredients

Equipment: Rolling pin, Sharp knife for wheel pattern, Large flat pan with lid

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp yeast
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 tsp ground fenugreek
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp ground anise or fennel
  • 1 cup warm water
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tbsp black sesame seeds (optional — scattered on top)

Method

  1. Dissolve yeast and 1 tsp sugar in warm water. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Combine flour, remaining sugar, salt, and all spices.
  3. Add yeast mixture and oil. Knead 8 minutes.
  4. Rest covered 90 minutes.
  5. Press into a large round 2cm thick.
  6. Scatter sesame seeds. Press lightly.
  7. Cut wheel pattern: score from centre to edge like spokes of a wheel — 8 cuts. Then score a circle midway.
  8. Cook in a covered pan on medium-low heat: 20 minutes per side.
  9. Alternatively, bake at 180°C for 35 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • The anise is the distinguishing spice between himbasha and ambasha.
  • Score more deeply than you think — the pattern expands during cooking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Shallow scoring — pattern disappears.
  • Too high heat.

The Science

See ETH005 for pattern science. The anise in himbasha contains anethole — a highly fragrant compound that is significantly more volatile than fenugreek's sotolone, producing a more immediately intense fragrance when the bread is hot.

Cultural Note

Himbasha is the bread that unites Ethiopians and Eritreans in the diaspora — regardless of political boundaries, both communities recognise and cherish this bread as home. At Ethiopian and Eritrean coffee ceremonies worldwide, himbasha is almost always present.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Room temperature 3 days. Freeze 2 months.

Reheating: Warm in dry pan.

Serve with: Honey, Butter, Sweet tea, At any Ethiopian celebration
