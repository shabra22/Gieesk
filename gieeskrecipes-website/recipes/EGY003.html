🥬 Molokhia with Garlic Taqliya (Egyptian Jute Leaf Soup)

Molokhia — ملوخية

Minced jute mallow leaves whisked into warm broth and finished with a sizzling taqliya of garlic and coriander seeds, served over rice with pickled onions.

35mTotal time
10mPrep
25mCook
6Servings
110Calories
MediumDifficulty

A thick green soup made from frozen minced molokhia leaves warmed in broth with cilantro and crushed garlic, without covering the pot or letting it boil. A tasha of garlic and coriander seeds pounded to a paste is fried in ghee until fragrant, crisp and orange-tinted and poured straight in. A quick pickled onion of vinegar, sumac and red onion rests in the fridge for at least an hour.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Whisk, Mortar and pestle, Small frying pan, Jar with lid

  • Frozen bag of minced green Molokhia, 400 grams, see notes
  • ¼ cup fresh minced cilantro, optional
  • 2 ½ cups broth, chicken broth, beef broth or vegetable broth
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed

Tasha (taqliya)

  • 5 whole garlic cloves
  • 1 tablespoon coriander seeds
  • Salt and pepper (no quantity printed)
  • 2 tablespoons ghee (quantity from the method)
  • ¼ teaspoon salt (quantity from the method)

Pickled onions (quantities not printed)

  • Red vinegar, salt, pepper, olive oil, sumac and sliced red onions

Method

  1. Defrost the molokhia overnight in the fridge.
  2. Warm 2 to 2½ cups of broth on low, just warm to the touch and not too hot. Whisk in the molokhia, the cilantro and the crushed garlic clove. Do not cover the pot and do not let it boil, or the colour darkens.
  3. Tasha: pound ¼ teaspoon salt, the 5 garlic cloves and the coriander seeds to a fine paste. Fry in 2 tablespoons ghee on medium for a minute or two over low heat, until the paste turns fragrant, crispy and orange-tinted.
  4. Add the tasha to the simmering soup at once; it will sizzle.
  5. Pickled onions: shake the vinegar, salt, pepper, oil, sumac and sliced onions in a jar and rest at least 1 hour in the fridge.
  6. Serve over rice or with bread, with the pickled onions.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: tasha is ready when fragrant, crispy and orange-tinted; the soup is kept below a boil and uncovered. The pickling rest is NO CUE, TIME ONLY (at least 1 hour in the fridge) and is a quick onion pickle, not a ferment.
  • If you only have whole frozen leaves, the page says to blend them first with 1 cup of warm broth.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Covering the pot, which the page says darkens the colour.
  • Boiling the soup after adding the molokhia.

Cultural Note

Nermine Mansour writes that Egyptian women used to be judged on their molokhia, and that the cook traditionally gasps loudly (teshhag) as the hot tasha hits the pot. She cites a food historian who found no evidence the name means 'royal'; the earliest confirmed record she gives is from the 11th century.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate covered up to 2 days (our estimate; the page gives none).

Reheating: Warm gently without boiling to protect the colour.

Serve with: Rice with vermicelli, Aish baladi
