Beer iyo Basal

Lamb or goat liver sliced thin, rinsed until the water runs clear, and thrown into a screaming-hot pan of onion and pepper for barely a minute — the Somali breakfast that punishes hesitation.

Liver is breakfast food in Somalia, eaten with anjero and sweet tea, and most households prefer goat or lamb to calf. The entire dish turns on one number: about a minute in the pan. Liver cooked properly is moist and faintly sweet; liver cooked for three minutes instead of one is dry, grainy and bitter, and no amount of onion covers it up.

Ingredients

Equipment: Sharp knife, Colander, Large frying pan or wok

  • 450 g (1 lb) lamb or goat liver
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 1 small red bell pepper, sliced
  • 1 small green bell pepper, sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp vegetable seasoning mix, or 2 bouillon cubes
  • 1 tsp xawaash spice blend
  • 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper

Method

  1. Peel the membrane off the liver and cut it into slices about 5 mm thick.
  2. Put the slices in a colander and rinse under cold running water until the water runs clear. This is what keeps the finished dish from tasting metallic.
  3. Get the pan properly hot, then add the olive oil.
  4. Add the onions and both peppers and stir.
  5. Add the minced garlic, then the vegetable seasoning, then the chopped cilantro, stirring after each.
  6. Add the liver to the very hot pan, spreading it in one layer.
  7. Add the xawaash and toss everything together.
  8. Cook for about one minute only, until the liver has just lost its rawness, then take the pan off the heat. Finish with black pepper and serve at once with anjero.

Chef Tips

  • Rinse until the water runs clear. It is the difference between liver that tastes clean and liver that tastes of iron.
  • The pan has to be hot before anything goes in. A moderate pan stews the liver in its own juices, which is the same as overcooking it.
  • Slice thin and even. Thick pieces cannot cook through in a minute, and a minute is all you get.
  • Camel liver with a little of the hump fat is the version many Somalis rate above all others, cooked exactly the same way.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking, which is the only mistake that matters here: the liver turns dry and bitter within a minute of being done.
  • Skipping the rinse, leaving a strong metallic taste through the dish.
  • Crowding the pan so the liver steams rather than sears.
  • Salting the liver before it goes in, which draws out moisture and toughens it.

Health Benefits

  • Liver is one of the most nutrient-dense foods there is, exceptionally high in vitamin A, iron, copper and B12.
  • Very high in protein for the calories.
  • Because of the vitamin A, it is traditionally eaten in moderation rather than often — which is also the current dietary advice.

Cultural Note

Liver is a breakfast side across Somalia, alongside goat meat, kidneys and dried oodkac. Somali cooks buy it the same day they cook it, eat it in moderation, and treat a fresh liver as something worth cooking properly rather than fitting in around other food.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately. Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm very briefly in a hot pan — any longer and it overcooks on reheating.

Serve with: Canjeero, Sabaayad, Shaah Cadays
