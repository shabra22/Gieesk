🫘 Makonde Cassava and Bean Stew

Mchuzi wa Muhogo na Maharage — Wamakonde

Makonde Cassava and Bean Stew — fresh cassava chunks and kidney beans simmered together with peanuts, tomatoes, and onions in the traditional Makonde style of Mtwara. Simple, nutritious, and completely local — the daily meal of the great carvers.

Makonde cassava and bean stew reflects the Makonde Plateau's ecology — cassava grows better than any other staple in the semi-arid conditions, and beans and peanuts provide the protein. This stew is the embodiment of Makonde food wisdom: use what grows here, make it nutritious and filling, waste nothing.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Makonde people of Mtwara and the Makonde Plateau — renowned worldwide for their wood carving

The Makonde (Wamakonde) people of southern Tanzania's Mtwara region are world-famous for their intricate ebony wood carvings — considered among the finest sculptural traditions in Africa. Their homeland, the Makonde Plateau, is semi-arid and cassava grows better than any other crop here. Makonde cuisine is built around cassava in every form — fresh, dried, pounded — combined with beans and peanuts for protein. The Makonde cassava and bean stew is the daily meal of the plateau.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 400g fresh cassava — peeled and cut into chunks
  • 1.5 cups cooked kidney beans
  • 3 tbsp groundnut paste
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1.5 tsp salt
  • 2 cups water

Method

  1. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden.
  2. Add tomatoes. Cook 6 minutes until thick.
  3. Add cassava chunks and water.
  4. Cover and cook 20—25 minutes until cassava is completely tender.
  5. Add cooked beans.
  6. Mix groundnut paste with ½ cup warm water. Add to pot.
  7. Simmer 10 more minutes until sauce is thick and well-integrated.
  8. Season with salt.

Chef Tips

  • Cassava must be completely tender — test with a fork.
  • The groundnut paste added near the end gives the Makonde version its distinctive richness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-cooking cassava.
  • Adding groundnut paste too early — it scorches.

Cultural Note

Makonde cassava stew is the food that sustains the great carvers of southern Tanzania — simple, filling, and made entirely from what the plateau provides. The same hands that create the world's most intricate wood sculptures prepare this honest, direct stew.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: As a complete meal, With ugali on feast days
