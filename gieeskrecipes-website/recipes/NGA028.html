🧆 Akara

Akara — bean fritters, kosai

Beans blended thick, then whisked ten minutes — or stirred vigorously in one direction for ten to twelve — and fried at about 180°C for three to four minutes.

Akara is the deep-fried bean fritter eaten at breakfast across Nigeria, and like every bean dish in the repertoire it lives or dies on air. The batter is blended thick, deliberately short of water, then beaten until it is very fluffy — and the instruction here is specific in a way that matters: if you are using a wooden spoon, stir vigorously in ONE direction for ten to twelve minutes. The peppers are folded in at the end, lightly, so the air you worked in does not come straight back out.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or mill, Whisk, or a wooden spoon, Deep pan or pot for frying, Sieve, Slotted spoon

  • 3 cups brown or white beans
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • 1 red bell pepper (tatashe)
  • 1 scotch bonnet (atarodo)
  • 1 medium-size onion
  • 1 tbsp ground pepper (atagungun), optional
  • 2 bouillon cubes
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Pick and wash the beans, then soak 5 minutes in lukewarm water and peel — pour into the blender and pulse a couple of times so the skins come off, then finish by hand.
  2. Blend the peeled beans till smooth in a mill or electric blender. Do not add too much water; you need to end up with a thickish consistency.
  3. Blend the bell pepper, onion and scotch bonnet roughly with as little water as you can. Sieve if there is excess water.
  4. Incorporate air into the batter. Whisk for at least 10 minutes till it becomes very fluffy, or use a wooden spoon and stir vigorously in one direction for 10 to 12 minutes.
  5. Add the blended pepper, bouillon cubes, ground pepper and salt. Mix lightly, or just fold the pepper in — do not over-mix or you will lose the air you have worked up.
  6. Heat the oil to approximately 180°C.
  7. Scoop the batter in and fry till golden brown, about 3 to 4 minutes, turning them over for even frying.
  8. Drain on a paper-towel-lined plate.

Chef Tips

  • One direction, if you are using a spoon. Ten to twelve minutes, always the same way round. Changing direction knocks the air back out.
  • Thickish batter. Too much water and the akara will spread and absorb oil instead of puffing.
  • Fold the peppers in. The beating is finished by then and the mixing from here is only to distribute.
  • 180°C. Cooler and they soak up oil; hotter and the outside colours before the inside sets.
  • Five minutes in lukewarm water is all the soak the beans need before peeling — and note this author soaks for ten for her moin moin, without explaining why.
  • Sieve the pepper blend if it is wet. Water in the batter is water in the oil.
  • Eaten with hot pap, custard or oatmeal in the morning, or with soft white bread — agege bread is the classic partner.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring back and forth instead of one way.
  • A batter loose enough to pour.
  • Over-mixing after the peppers go in.
  • Frying in oil that is not hot enough.
  • Crowding the pan, which drops the oil temperature.

Cultural Note

Akara is breakfast and it is street food, sold from a single wide pan at the roadside from before dawn. It crossed the Atlantic with enslaved West Africans and became acarajé in Bahia, where it is fried in dendê — palm oil — rather than vegetable oil, and received Brazilian heritage status in 2004. In the north the same fritter is kosai, and the Hausa version is eaten with koko rather than pap.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best within the hour. A day refrigerated at most.

Reheating: 5 minutes in a hot oven or air fryer. Never the microwave, which makes them soggy.

Serve with: Agege Bread, Eko, Pap, custard or oatmeal
