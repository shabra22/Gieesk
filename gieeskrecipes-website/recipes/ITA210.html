🐟 Branzino al Sale

Branzino al Sale

A whole sea bass encased in a thick shell of salt and baked until the crust hardens, trapping in moisture and gently seasoning the fish without ever tasting overly salty itself.

Despite using an enormous quantity of coarse salt, the finished fish emerges perfectly, delicately seasoned rather than oversalted, the hardened crust acting almost like a natural oven that steams the fish in its own moisture.

Ingredients

Equipment: Baking sheet, Large mixing bowl

  • 1 whole sea bass (branzino), about 1kg (2.2 lb), scaled and gutted
  • 2 kg (4.4 lb) coarse sea salt
  • 3 egg whites
  • Fresh rosemary and lemon slices, for the cavity
  • Extra virgin olive oil, for serving
  • Lemon wedges, for serving

Method

  1. Stuff the fish cavity with rosemary sprigs and a few lemon slices.
  2. Whisk the egg whites lightly and combine with the coarse salt, mixing until the salt is evenly moistened, similar to wet sand.
  3. Spread a bed of the salt mixture on a baking sheet, about the length and width of the fish.
  4. Place the fish on top and pack the remaining salt mixture over and around it, covering completely and pressing firmly to form a solid shell.
  5. Bake at 220C (425F) for 30-35 minutes until the salt crust is hard and golden.
  6. Let rest for a few minutes, then crack open the salt crust with the back of a knife and carefully lift it away.
  7. Brush away any remaining salt, fillet the fish, and drizzle with good olive oil and lemon before serving.

Chef Tips

  • The egg white binds the salt into a solid, crackable shell -- don't skip it.
  • Despite the large amount of salt, the fish itself absorbs very little, remaining delicately seasoned rather than oversalted.
  • Crack the salt crust firmly with the back of a heavy knife, then carefully brush away any loose salt before serving.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not packing the salt shell thickly or evenly enough, which can lead to uneven cooking.
  • Cutting into the fish itself while cracking the crust, rather than tapping gently around the shell first.
  • Skipping the aromatics in the cavity, missing an easy way to add extra flavor.

Health Benefits

  • Sea bass is a good source of lean protein and omega-3 fatty acids.

Cultural Note

Cooking fish in a salt crust is a technique found along Italy's coastlines, prized for how effectively it seals in moisture while the fish remains only delicately seasoned.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; refrigerate leftover fish for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Reheat gently to avoid overcooking the delicate fish further.

Serve with: A simple green salad, A dry Ligurian white wine
