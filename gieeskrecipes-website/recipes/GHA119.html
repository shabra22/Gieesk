🥟 Ghanaian Meat Pie

Meat pie

Four cups of flour to a cup and a third of cold salted butter, chilled thirty minutes, rolled a quarter inch and cut into six-inch rounds.

The Ghanaian bakery pie, sold warm in paper bags at every bus station. Four cups of all-purpose flour, a cup and a third of cold salted butter and half a cup of vegetable shortening are pulsed to small chunks with a quarter teaspoon of baking powder, then six tablespoons of ice cold water until the dough clumps, and chilled thirty minutes. The filling is ground chicken or turkey with onion, carrot and scallion, cooked in stages — onions five minutes, carrot ten on low, then the scallions and meat for ten. The dough divides into eight, rolls to a quarter inch, and is cut into six-inch rounds. It bakes at 350 for forty-five minutes until golden.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor, Rolling pin, 6-inch round cutter, Baking sheet, Oven, Pastry brush

    Pastry

    • 4 cups all-purpose flour
    • 1 1/3 cups salted butter, in its cold state
    • 1/2 cup vegetable shortening
    • 1/4 tsp baking powder
    • 6 tbsp ice cold water
    • Egg wash

    Filling

    • 1 lb ground chicken or turkey
    • 1/2 medium onion, small dice
    • 1 medium carrot, cubed
    • 2 scallions, thinly sliced
    • 2 tbsp garlic-and-coconut chilli sauce — the source names a branded product
    • 1/2 tsp garlic powder
    • 1/2 tsp onion powder
    • 1/2 tsp ginger powder
    • Salt and pepper, to taste

    The pastry step also calls for black pepper, which is not in the printed ingredient list. See the note.

      Method

      1. Cook the onions 5 minutes.
      2. Add the carrot and cook 10 minutes on low.
      3. Add the scallions and the meat and cook 10 minutes.
      4. Season the filling with the chilli sauce, garlic powder, onion powder, ginger powder, salt and pepper, and let it cool.
      5. For the pastry: pulse the flour, the cold butter and the shortening with the baking powder to small chunks.
      6. Add the ice cold water until the dough clumps.
      7. Chill 30 minutes.
      8. Preheat the oven to 350 degrees Fahrenheit.
      9. Divide the dough into 8, roll to 1/4 inch and cut 6-inch rounds.
      10. Fill each round, fold, seal the edges and brush with egg wash.
      11. Bake at 350 degrees for 45 minutes, until golden.

      Chef Tips

      • Butter in its cold state, and ice cold water. That is the whole of pastry and the source is right to specify it twice.
      • Small chunks, not breadcrumbs. Visible pieces of butter are what make it flaky rather than short.
      • Thirty minutes chilling is a minimum. An hour is better and the dough rolls without fighting you.
      • Cool the filling completely before it goes near the pastry. Warm filling melts the butter and you lose the layers.
      • A quarter inch and six-inch rounds gives eight large pies. Ghanaian bakery pies are substantial, not canapés.
      • Forty-five minutes at 350 is a long bake for a small pie, and it is what gets the thick pastry cooked through. Judge by golden.
      • Note the source names a branded garlic-and-coconut chilli sauce. Any garlicky chilli sauce works, or lean on the three powders.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Warm butter or tap water.
      • Processing the fat to crumbs.
      • Filling the pastry while the meat is still warm.
      • Rolling thinner than a quarter inch, which splits.
      • Pulling them at twenty-five minutes, when the pastry is still raw inside.

      Health Benefits

      • Ground chicken or turkey is leaner than the beef most meat pies use.
      • Carrot contributes beta-carotene.
      • Ginger and garlic carry documented anti-inflammatory compounds.
      • High in protein and genuinely filling.

      Cultural Note

      The meat pie came to West Africa with the British and was kept because it travels — sold warm from glass cases at lorry parks, eaten one-handed on a tro-tro. The Ghanaian version runs smaller on spice and larger on pastry than the Nigerian, and the corned-beef turnover is its own well-loved branch.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: 3 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen baked or unbaked.

      Reheating: A 350-degree oven for 10 to 15 minutes. Never the microwave, which makes the pastry limp.

      Serve with: Shito, Sobolo, Bissap
