🌺 Ethiopian Hibiscus Tea

ቅጠላ ሻይ — Hilb Shai

Ethiopian Hibiscus Tea — dried hibiscus flowers (roselle) steeped in hot water until deeply ruby red, sweetened with honey and served hot or cold with lemon. One of Africa's most beautiful herbal drinks — Ethiopia grows hibiscus (roselle) in its western regions.

Ethiopian hibiscus tea (made from Hibiscus sabdariffa, also called roselle) produces a deeply red, tart, antioxidant-rich drink that is both beautiful and medicinal. Grown in western Ethiopia, it is increasingly popular across the country.

Ingredients

Equipment: Teapot or saucepan, Sieve

  • 4 tbsp dried hibiscus flowers (roselle)
  • 4 cups hot water
  • 3 tbsp honey
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • Ice (for cold version)

Method

  1. Pour hot water over hibiscus flowers.
  2. Steep 5—8 minutes until deeply ruby red.
  3. Strain. Add honey and lemon juice.
  4. Serve hot, or cool and serve over ice.

Chef Tips

  • The colour deepens the longer it steeps — 8 minutes for maximum colour and flavour.
  • Lemon brightens the tartness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-steeping — pale and mild.

The Science

Hibiscus anthocyanins are pH-sensitive — in acidic conditions (lemon juice) they turn brighter red; in alkaline conditions they turn blue-purple. The addition of lemon juice not only flavours the drink but maintains the vivid ruby colour.

Health Benefits

  • Anthocyanins — very high antioxidant activity.
  • Studies show hibiscus lowers blood pressure.
  • Very high vitamin C.
  • Diuretic properties.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian hibiscus tea is the ruby drink of western Ethiopia — beautiful to look at, tart to drink, and full of the most powerful antioxidants available in any tea. It is both medicine and ceremony.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days.

Reheating: Warm gently.

Serve with: Hot in the morning, Cold in warm weather, At celebrations as a colourful non-alcoholic drink
