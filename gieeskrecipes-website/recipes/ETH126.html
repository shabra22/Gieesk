🫛 Ethiopian Okra Stew

ዘምቢሏ ወጥ — Bamya Wat

Ethiopian Okra Stew — fresh okra cooked with tomato, onion, garlic, and a touch of berbere. A southern Ethiopian fasting dish — the okra's natural mucilage thickens the sauce into something surprisingly rich.

Okra stew is particularly popular in southern Ethiopia where okra grows abundantly in the warmer climate. The okra's natural thickening agent produces a sauce of remarkable body.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 400g fresh okra — trimmed, sliced into rounds
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • ½ tsp berbere
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Heat oil. Cook onion until golden.
  2. Add garlic, berbere, and turmeric. Stir 30 seconds.
  3. Add tomatoes. Cook 4 minutes.
  4. Add okra. Stir gently.
  5. Cook covered 10—12 minutes.
  6. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Dry okra before cutting — excess moisture increases sliminess.
  • Handle gently — do not over-stir or it becomes very mucilaginous.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-stirring — maximum mucilage released.

The Science

Okra's mucilage is composed of galactan oligosaccharides — a type of soluble fibre that forms a gel when heated with water. This gel thickens the stew naturally. Less stirring means less mucilage release; some is desirable for sauce body, too much is unpleasant.

Health Benefits

  • Mucilage is prebiotic.
  • High in vitamin C and K.
  • Supports gut health.

Cultural Note

Okra stew is the southern Ethiopian contribution to the fasting platter — bringing a different texture and thickening quality from the highlands' legume-based stews.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best fresh. Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), As a southern Ethiopian fasting side
