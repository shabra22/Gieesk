🥛 Ashta Kadaba (Homemade Cream Filling)

Eshta — قشطة

A thick mastic-scented cream of milk, cream, cornstarch and flour thickened for about 2 minutes, with cream cheese and rose water; for atayef, kunafa and basbousa.

2h 15mTotal time
10mPrep
5mCook
8Servings

EasyDifficulty

Milk, cream, cornstarch, flour, sugar and mastic are cooked over medium heat, stirring constantly, to a boil, then boiled until thick and custard-like, about 2 minutes. Cream cheese and rose water are stirred in. The cream cools about an hour and chills at least an hour; it makes about 2 cups.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Whisk

  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 2 tablespoons cornstarch
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon granulated sugar
  • 2-4 mastic resins
  • ¼ cup cream cheese
  • 1 teaspoon rose water

Method

  1. Whisk the milk, cream, cornstarch, flour, sugar and mastic in a pan.
  2. Raise to medium heat and stir constantly to a boil. Boil until the mixture reaches a thick, custard-like consistency, about 2 minutes.
  3. Stir in the cream cheese and rose water.
  4. Cool about 1 hour, then refrigerate at least 1 hour.

Chef Tips

  • Cue: thick, custard-like. The cooling and chilling are NO CUE, TIME ONLY. The card says it keeps 1 week; the body mentions 3 days in one note.
  • Ashta means cream and kadaba means fake. The author says this is not the cream used in Om Ali, for which she uses whipped cream.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not stirring constantly, which scorches the bottom.

Cultural Note

Nermine Mansour explains that ashta means cream and kadaba means fake, and that it fills atayef, kunafa and basbousa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days (the shorter of the page's two figures).

Reheating: Serve cold.

Serve with: Atayef, konafa and basbousa
