🍛 Tanzanian Rice with Stew

Wali wa Mchuzi

Wali wa Mchuzi — plain steamed rice served with a rich, spiced beef or chicken stew. The everyday lunch of urban Tanzania — simple, satisfying, and found at every restaurant, cafeteria, and mama lishe (food vendor) across the country.

Wali wa Mchuzi is the combination that defines Tanzanian everyday eating — plain white rice alongside a rich, flavourful stew. At every mama lishe in Dar es Salaam, this is what workers eat for lunch. The rice is always plain; the stew does the talking.

Ingredients

Equipment: Rice pot with lid, Stew pot

  • 2 cups long-grain rice — washed
  • 3 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Simple Beef Stew

  • 400g beef — cubed
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 cup water

Method

  1. Cook rice by absorption method: combine rice, water, and salt. Bring to a boil. Cover. Lowest heat for 18 minutes. Rest 5 minutes.
  2. Brown beef in oil. Remove.
  3. Cook onions until golden. Add garlic, ginger, tomatoes, spices.
  4. Cook 8 minutes until thick. Return beef. Add water.
  5. Simmer covered 30—40 minutes until tender.
  6. Serve rice with stew alongside.

Chef Tips

  • The rice and stew are separate — never mix them.
  • The stew should be rich and thick — not watery.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Watery stew.
  • Mixing rice and stew before serving.

The Science

Serving rice and stew separately allows each diner to control the ratio — a cultural practice that reflects individual preference.

Cultural Note

Wali wa Mchuzi is Tanzania's everyday lunch — the meal that sustains the country's workers, students, and families through the middle of every day. Every mama lishe cook has their own version of the stew.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate components separately 3 days.

Reheating: Separately, gently.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Pili Pili Sauce, Fried Plantain
