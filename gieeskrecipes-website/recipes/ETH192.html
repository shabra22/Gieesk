🍯 Ethiopian Style Baklava

ባቅላዋ — Baqlawa

Ethiopian Style Baklava — layers of buttered phyllo pastry filled with a walnut-honey mixture spiced with cardamom, baked golden and drenched in Ethiopian honey-cardamom syrup. The Ethiopianised Arab sweet — distinguished by the prominent cardamom and the use of Ethiopian mountain honey in the syrup.

Ethiopian baklava is the Eid celebration centrepiece of the Muslim community — made with more cardamom than standard baklava, using Ethiopian honey in the syrup rather than simple syrup, and sometimes incorporating local walnuts or almonds from the Ethiopian highlands.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Ethiopian Muslim communities — baklava arrived via Arab trade routes and became Ethiopianised

Baklava arrived in Ethiopia through the Arab and Ottoman trade connections that shaped the food culture of Ethiopia's Muslim communities. Ethiopian baklava differs from the standard Middle Eastern version in key ways: it uses Ethiopian honey instead of simple syrup, incorporates cardamom (Ethiopian spice tradition) prominently in both the nut filling and the honey syrup, and is sometimes made with a teff-wheat mixed pastry. It is the centrepiece sweet of Ramadan Eid celebrations in Ethiopian Muslim communities.

Ingredients

Equipment: 30×20cm baking tin, Pastry brush, Oven at 180°C, Saucepan

  • 250g phyllo pastry sheets
  • 150g unsalted butter — melted

Filling

  • 200g walnuts or almonds — finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ¼ tsp ground cinnamon

Ethiopian Honey-Cardamom Syrup

  • ½ cup raw Ethiopian honey
  • ½ cup water
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp rose water (optional)
  • Juice of ½ lemon

Method

  1. Make syrup: combine honey, water, sugar, cardamom, and lemon. Bring to a boil. Simmer 5 minutes. Add rose water. Cool completely.
  2. Mix filling: nuts, sugar, cardamom, and cinnamon.
  3. Brush tin with butter.
  4. Layer 8 sheets of phyllo, brushing each with butter.
  5. Scatter half the nut filling.
  6. Layer 4 more buttered sheets.
  7. Add remaining filling.
  8. Top with remaining sheets, buttering each.
  9. Cut into diamond shapes before baking — all the way through.
  10. Bake at 180°C for 30—35 minutes until deeply golden.
  11. While hot, pour cool syrup over. The syrup should sizzle.
  12. Cool completely before serving — minimum 4 hours.

Chef Tips

  • The syrup must be cool and the baklava must be hot — or vice versa. This temperature contrast creates the best texture.
  • Cut completely before baking — cutting after baking shatters the pastry.
  • Ethiopian mountain honey in the syrup produces dramatically better flavour than simple syrup.
  • The cardamom in the syrup is the Ethiopian signature — be generous.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Hot syrup on hot baklava — stays soft instead of crispy.
  • Not cutting before baking — impossible to cut after.
  • Insufficient syrup — dry baklava.

The Science

Pouring cool syrup on hot baklava creates a rapid temperature differential that causes the syrup to contract and penetrate every layer simultaneously — if both are hot, the syrup sits on top rather than being absorbed. The sugar crystals in the cooled syrup nucleate during this rapid absorption, creating the characteristic slightly crystalline texture of properly made baklava.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian baklava is the sweet that marks Eid ul-Fitr — after 30 days of Ramadan fasting, the first dessert at the Eid feast is baklava. The smell of cardamom-honey syrup and the crunch of phyllo pastry is the taste of Eid for Ethiopian Muslims, connecting their community to the broader world of Islamic food culture while remaining distinctly Ethiopian.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight container at room temperature — 1 week. Refrigerate 2 weeks.

Reheating: Room temperature — never microwave.

Serve with: At Eid celebrations, With Ethiopian spiced coffee, As a Ramadan iftar dessert
