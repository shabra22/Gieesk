🌿 Palmnut Soup with Selim Pods

Abenkwan — dedetsi

An Ewe cook's spice-forward palmnut soup — selim pods, dawadawa powder, African nutmeg and smoked paprika, with a kilo each of goat and smoked fish.

This is the most aromatic documented palmnut soup and the only one by an Ewe cook, for whom the soup is dedetsi rather than abenkwan. It is built on a commercial concentrate let down with a litre and a half of warm water, and what makes it distinctive is the spicing: three selim pods, a tablespoon of dawadawa powder, a teaspoon of blended African nutmeg and a tablespoon of smoked paprika, alongside three tablespoons of shrimp powder. A kilo of goat, a kilo of assorted smoked fish, okra, garden eggs and mushrooms. The method is video-only, so only the quantities are published.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Sieve, Blender, Mortar, for the selim pods

  • 1 packet palmnut concentrate — packet weight not given
  • 1.5 litres warm water
  • 1 kg goat meat
  • 1 kg assorted smoked fish
  • 3 large onions
  • 2 large tomatoes
  • 150 g okra
  • 100 g garden eggs
  • 150 g mushrooms
  • 3 habanero chillies
  • 10 g pettie belle chillies
  • 4-5 mild green chillies
  • 20 g ginger
  • 3 selim pods
  • 3 tbsp shrimp powder
  • 1 tbsp dawadawa powder
  • 1 tsp blended African nutmeg
  • 1 tbsp smoked paprika
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

    The published method for this recipe is video-only. No written steps exist in the source.

    1. Let the palmnut concentrate down with the 1.5 litres of warm water and strain.
    2. Cook the goat meat with the aromatics until tender.
    3. Add the palm liquid and bring to a simmer.
    4. Add the selim pods, dawadawa powder, African nutmeg, smoked paprika and shrimp powder.
    5. Add the smoked fish, okra, garden eggs and mushrooms.
    6. Simmer until the oil rises and the palm taste has cooked out.

    The order and timings above are inferred from the other documented palmnut soups, not from this source.

      Chef Tips

      • Be aware that the method here is reconstructed. The source publishes a full ingredient list but its instructions are in a video, so the step order above follows the other documented palmnut soups rather than this cook's own sequence.
      • Selim pods — hwentia, Ethiopian pepper — are the signature of this soup. Bruise them and add them whole so they can be fished out; they are smoky and faintly bitter and nothing substitutes.
      • Dawadawa powder is fermented locust bean and it is the deepest savoury note available in West African cooking. A tablespoon is a lot, and it should be.
      • African nutmeg (calabash nutmeg, ehuru) is not nutmeg and tastes nothing like it. If you cannot get it, leave it out rather than substituting.
      • Three kinds of chilli — habanero, pettie belle and mild green — layered for aroma rather than heat alone. The pettie belle at 10 g is a small amount by weight.
      • Smoked paprika is the one non-traditional item in the list and the cook includes it deliberately; it reinforces the smoked fish.
      • This is an Ewe cook's version, where the soup is dedetsi. The Akan abenkwan versions at Palm Nut Soup and Abenkwan, the Palm Oil Version are plainer.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Treating the reconstructed step order as the author's own.
      • Grinding the selim pods into the soup so they cannot be removed.
      • Substituting ordinary nutmeg for African nutmeg.
      • Leaving out the dawadawa, which carries the savoury backbone.
      • Adding the okra early, so it disintegrates.

      Health Benefits

      • Palm fruit concentrate supplies provitamin-A carotenoids and tocotrienols.
      • Goat is leaner than beef or lamb.
      • Smoked fish adds omega-3 fats, protein and iodine.
      • Selim pods and African nutmeg both carry documented antimicrobial aromatic compounds.

      Cultural Note

      The Ewe call this soup dedetsi and spice it far more heavily than Akan cooks do, which is the kind of regional difference that gets lost when a dish is published under one national name. Selim pods and dawadawa are the markers — smoky and fermented respectively, and both older in the regional kitchen than any of the chillies alongside them.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

      Reheating: Simmer gently. Remove the selim pods before storing or they turn the soup bitter.

      Serve with: Akple, Banku, Fufu
