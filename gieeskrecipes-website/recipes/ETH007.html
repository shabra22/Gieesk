🫓 Ethiopian Flatbread

ቂጣ — Kita

Ethiopian Kita — a simple unleavened Ethiopian flatbread made from wheat flour and water, cooked on a dry pan. The quick-cooking, everyday alternative to injera — made in 25 minutes, used as a base for Chechebsa (Kita Firfir), or eaten with any stew.

Kita is the quick bread of Ethiopian daily life — when there is no time for injera fermentation or no injera in the house, kita is made. Thicker than a tortilla and thinner than a pita, it is slightly chewy and neutral in flavour, serving as the perfect vehicle for the bold flavours of Ethiopian stews.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Dry pan or mitad

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour or whole wheat flour
  • ¾ cup water
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp oil (optional)

Method

  1. Combine flour and salt. Add water gradually, mixing.
  2. Knead 5 minutes until smooth. Add oil if using.
  3. Rest 10 minutes covered.
  4. Divide into 4 balls. Roll each 3—4mm thick.
  5. Cook in a dry pan over medium heat: 3—4 minutes per side until cooked through and lightly browned.
  6. Keep wrapped in a cloth.

Chef Tips

  • Kita should be thicker than a tortilla but thinner than a pita.
  • Cook over medium heat — high heat burns outside before inside cooks.
  • Keep wrapped in a cloth after cooking — they stay soft.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too thin — becomes a cracker.
  • Too high heat.

The Science

Unlike injera, kita has no fermentation — it is a simple starch-gluten system. The gluten network formed during kneading creates elasticity; without the acid of fermentation to partially break down this gluten, kita is slightly chewier and more dense than injera.

Cultural Note

Kita is the bread of practicality — made when time is short, when injera has run out, when the children are hungry. It is not a lesser bread but a different one — honest, direct, and perfectly functional.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Room temperature 2 days. Freeze 2 months.

Reheating: Warm in dry pan or microwave.

Serve with: Any Ethiopian stew, As the base for Chechebsa (ETH010), With honey and butter for breakfast
