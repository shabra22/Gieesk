Risotto alla Milanese

Milan's signature golden risotto gets its color and distinctive aroma from saffron, traditionally served alongside osso buco but glorious enough to stand entirely on its own.

The technique of risotto — toasting the rice, adding hot stock gradually while stirring, and finishing with butter and cheese for a creamy 'all'onda' consistency that ripples like a wave — is showcased perfectly in this simple, saffron-scented Milanese classic.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide, heavy-bottomed pan, Ladle, Small saucepan for stock

  • 320 g (1½ cups) Carnaroli or Arborio rice
  • 1.2 L (5 cups) hot chicken or vegetable stock
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 100 ml (scant ½ cup) dry white wine
  • 1 generous pinch saffron threads
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) unsalted butter, divided
  • 60 g (¾ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Keep the stock warm in a saucepan over low heat throughout cooking.
  2. Steep the saffron threads in a small ladle of the hot stock to release their color and aroma.
  3. Melt half the butter with the olive oil in a wide pan and sauté the onion until soft and translucent, without browning.
  4. Add the rice and toast, stirring constantly, for 2 minutes until the grains turn slightly translucent at the edges.
  5. Pour in the wine and stir until fully absorbed.
  6. Begin adding the hot stock one ladle at a time, stirring frequently and waiting until each addition is mostly absorbed before adding the next.
  7. Continue for about 18 minutes, adding the saffron-infused stock partway through, until the rice is creamy but still has a slight bite at the center.
  8. Remove from the heat and beat in the remaining butter and Parmigiano vigorously to create a glossy, rippling consistency.
  9. Let rest for 1 minute off the heat, then serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Keep the stock at a steady simmer throughout — adding cold stock shocks the rice and slows cooking unevenly.
  • The final vigorous beating in of butter and cheese, called mantecatura, is what gives good risotto its signature creamy, wave-like consistency.
  • Risotto waits for no one — have your diners ready, as it should be served the moment it's finished.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the stock at once instead of gradually, which prevents the rice from releasing its starch properly.
  • Overcooking the rice until it loses all bite, rather than stopping while the center still has a slight resistance.
  • Skipping the mantecatura step, leaving the risotto looser and less luxuriously creamy than it should be.

Health Benefits

  • Saffron contains antioxidant compounds; the dish provides energy from the rice and calcium from the cheese.

Cultural Note

Risotto alla Milanese is said to trace back to a Milanese cathedral glassmaker who used saffron to tint glass and, as legend goes, jokingly added it to a wedding risotto, giving rise to this now-iconic dish.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately; risotto does not hold or reheat particularly well.

Reheating: Reheat gently with a splash of stock or water, stirring constantly, though texture will suffer somewhat.

Serve with: Osso buco, traditionally, A dry Lombardy white wine
