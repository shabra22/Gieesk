🫘 Maraq Digir

Maraq Digir

Adzuki beans boiled soft, onion caramelised slowly, xawaash stirred in and a purée of blended tomato, coriander and green chilli poured over — then simmered down and some of the beans mashed to thicken it.

The two decisions that make this dish are both about texture. The aromatics are blended raw into a purée rather than chopped, which gives the stew its smooth body instead of leaving visible pieces of tomato and onion floating in it. And some of the beans get mashed against the side of the pot as it finishes, which thickens it without flour or reduction. Adzuki is the specified bean and it is the right one — the same bean that goes into cambuulo.

Ingredients

Equipment: Frying pan, for the spices, Coffee grinder or mortar and pestle, Blender, Saucepan

    The xawaash for this dish, if you are making it fresh

    • 20 ml (4 tsp) cumin seeds
    • 20 ml (4 tsp) coriander seeds
    • 10 ml (2 tsp) black peppercorns
    • 2 cm piece flat cinnamon bark
    • 5 ml (1 tsp) cardamom seeds
    • 5 ml (1 tsp) whole cloves

    The stew

    • 200 g dried adzuki beans
    • 1 onion, peeled and sliced
    • 3 medium tomatoes
    • 1 to 2 green chillies
    • 1 bunch fresh coriander
    • 1/2 thumb fresh ginger (optional)
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) olive oil
    • 10 ml (2 tsp) xawaash
    • 10 ml (2 tsp) tomato paste, level
    • Salt, to taste

    Method

      The spice blend

      1. Dry-heat the cumin, coriander, peppercorns, cinnamon bark, cardamom seeds and cloves in a frying pan over medium heat for 2 minutes, until fragrant.
      2. Cool, then grind to a powder in a coffee grinder or with a mortar and pestle. This makes about 35 g and keeps in a jar.

      The stew

      1. Blend the fresh coriander, tomatoes and chillies — and the ginger if you are using it — into a purée.
      2. Boil the adzuki beans for about 20 minutes, until tender.
      3. Meanwhile, fry the sliced onion slowly in the olive oil until caramelised. Do not rush this.
      4. Stir in the xawaash, then add the coriander and tomato purée with the tomato paste and salt.
      5. Simmer for about 20 minutes, adding water as needed.
      6. Add the beans and cook another 15 to 20 minutes, mashing some of them against the side of the pot as you go, until it thickens.

      Chef Tips

      • Blend the aromatics raw. Chopped, you get a chunky tomato stew; blended, you get the smooth body that makes this maraq digir.
      • Mash some of the beans at the end. That is the thickener — no flour, no long reduction.
      • Caramelise the onion slowly before the spice goes in. Rushed onions leave the whole pot thin and one-dimensional.
      • Adzuki is the bean, not white or kidney. Kidney beans work as a substitute and the flavour shifts noticeably.
      • Tinned fava beans are given as the shortcut alternative if you have no dried beans and no time.
      • Note the flat cinnamon bark rather than a rolled quill — it is the cassia-style bark, and it is what Somali xawaash uses.
      • Feta, black olives, olive oil and za'atar with pita is the author's own serving suggestion, and it is very good, though it is a diaspora plate rather than a Mogadishu one.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Chopping the tomatoes and coriander instead of blending them.
      • Skipping the mashing, so it stays watery.
      • Frying the onion fast on high heat.
      • Adding the xawaash to a dry pan where it scorches — get it into the oil with the onion already there.
      • Under-boiling the beans before they go in.

      Health Benefits

      • Adzuki beans are high in plant protein, fibre, folate and iron.
      • Entirely plant-based, with one tablespoon of oil for two generous servings.
      • Cumin, coriander, cinnamon and cloves all arrive with the xawaash.
      • Very filling for the calorie count, which is why it is everyday food.

      Cultural Note

      Beans carry more of Somali eating than the meat-and-rice reputation allows for, and maraq digir is the dish people name when they talk about the food that means home. It is cheap, it is filling, it is vegetarian without trying to be, and every household's version tastes of its own xawaash.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days. Freezes well for 3 months.

      Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of water — it thickens considerably overnight.

      Serve with: Sabaayad, Canjeero, Bariis Iskukaris
