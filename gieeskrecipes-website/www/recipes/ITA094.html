Vitello Tonnato

An elegant Piedmontese classic pairing thinly sliced, poached veal with a creamy, tangy sauce built from tuna, anchovies, and capers — an unlikely combination that has endured for centuries.

Despite the surf-and-turf pairing sounding unusual to modern ears, vitello tonnato has graced Piedmontese tables since at least the 18th century, traditionally served chilled as a refined summer antipasto, the tuna sauce's briny richness playing beautifully against the delicate poached veal.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot for poaching, Blender or food processor, Sharp knife for slicing

  • 800 g (1.75 lb) veal roast (such as eye of round)
  • 1 onion, quartered
  • 1 carrot, halved
  • 1 celery stalk, halved
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) dry white wine
  • 200 g (7 oz) canned tuna in olive oil, drained
  • 4 anchovy fillets
  • 2 egg yolks (or 3 tbsp good-quality mayonnaise)
  • 2 tbsp capers, plus more for garnish
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Place the veal in a pot with the onion, carrot, celery, bay leaves, and wine, and add enough water to cover.
  2. Bring to a gentle simmer and poach for 50–60 minutes, until the veal is cooked through but still tender, not tough.
  3. Remove the veal and let it cool completely, then refrigerate, wrapped, until well chilled — this makes it much easier to slice thinly.
  4. For the sauce, blend the tuna, anchovies, egg yolks, and capers until smooth.
  5. With the blender running, drizzle in the olive oil slowly to emulsify into a creamy sauce, similar to making mayonnaise.
  6. Thin with a little of the veal poaching liquid if needed, and season with lemon juice, salt, and pepper.
  7. Slice the chilled veal very thinly against the grain and arrange on a platter.
  8. Spoon the tuna sauce generously over the veal slices, garnish with a few whole capers, and chill until ready to serve.

Chef Tips

  • Chilling the veal thoroughly before slicing is essential for achieving thin, clean slices rather than tearing.
  • If using raw egg yolks feels risky, a good-quality store-bought mayonnaise makes a very reasonable base substitute.
  • This dish is meant to be served cold, making it an excellent make-ahead option for entertaining.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the veal, which toughens it rather than leaving it tender and easy to slice thinly.
  • Slicing the veal while still warm, which tends to tear rather than produce clean, thin slices.
  • Making the sauce too thick, which should be pourable enough to coat the veal in a thin, even layer.

Cultural Note

Vitello tonnato has been a staple of Piedmontese festive tables since the 18th century, often appearing as a starter for Christmas and other special family gatherings.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, covered, for up to 2 days — the flavors meld further overnight.

Reheating: Not applicable; this dish is always served cold.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A chilled white wine
