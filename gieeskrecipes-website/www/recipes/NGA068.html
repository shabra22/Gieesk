🌿 Editan Soup

Editan

Two cups of editan leaf — shredded, pounded and washed before it ever reaches the pot — with uziza and okra, cooked three to five minutes at the end.

Editan is an Ibibio soup of Akwa Ibom and the leaf gives it its name and its character. It is a bitter leaf that needs real preparation: shredded, pounded, and washed before use, which is how the bitterness is brought down to something pleasant. Two cups of it go in at the very end with a tablespoon of sliced uziza and six fingers of okra chopped into rounds, for three to five minutes only. The target consistency is stated by reference — just like afang or edikang ikong, so thick rather than soupy.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Mortar and pestle, Colander, Sharp knife

  • 2 cups editan leaf, shredded, pounded and washed
  • 1 tbsp sliced uziza leaf
  • 8 pieces beef
  • 4 pieces snails
  • 8 pieces cow skin (pomo)
  • Stockfish (optional)
  • A handful of dried prawns
  • 6 fingers okro, chopped in circular chunks
  • Scotch bonnet pepper, coarsely ground, to taste
  • 1 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 2 cooking spoons palm oil
  • 1 cup water
  • Seasoning, to taste
  • Salt to taste

Used in the method but not listed in the original

  • Smoked fish

Method

  1. Shred, pound and wash the editan leaf thoroughly and set it aside.
  2. Wash and season the beef with seasoning, salt and pepper, then steam it in its own juice until that dries up.
  3. Add water and cook until the beef is almost soft.
  4. Add the snails, cow skin, smoked fish, prawns and ground crayfish, and cook until all the meats are soft.
  5. Add 1 cup of water and bring to the boil.
  6. Add the palm oil, taste and correct the seasoning, and cook about 3 minutes.
  7. Add the editan, the uziza and the chopped okro, and cook 3 to 5 minutes.
  8. Serve with a swallow of choice.

Chef Tips

  • Shred, pound, wash — in that order, and wash thoroughly. Editan is bitter and the washing is what makes it edible rather than punishing.
  • Three to five minutes for the leaves at the very end. Any longer and the bitterness comes back up.
  • Steam the beef in its own juice until it dries up before any water goes in. Same opening move as edikang ikong.
  • Three minutes on the palm oil before the greens, so it is properly incorporated.
  • Okra chopped in rounds, not sliced thin. It is there for body and a little draw.
  • The consistency target is given by comparison — just like afang or edikang ikong, which means thick enough to sit on a swallow.
  • Smoked fish is in the method but not the ingredient list. A medium piece is the usual amount for this size of pot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-washing the editan, which leaves it harshly bitter.
  • Cooking the leaves longer than five minutes.
  • Adding water before the beef juice has dried.
  • Adding the palm oil with the greens rather than before them.
  • Making it soupy rather than thick.

Health Benefits

  • Bitter leaves like editan are traditionally used for digestion and blood sugar.
  • Snails are very lean and rich in iron and magnesium.
  • Okra mucilage is a soluble fibre.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.

Cultural Note

Editan is one of several Akwa Ibom soups built on a single named leaf, and the pattern across them is consistent: the leaf is prepared at length before cooking and then barely cooked at all. Where a Yoruba soup might fry its base for twenty minutes, these soups do their work in the washing and the pounding, and the pot is only the last five minutes.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat, briefly.

Serve with: Iyan, Eba or garri, Akpu or fufu
