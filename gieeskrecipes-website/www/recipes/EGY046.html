🫓 Aish Baladi (Egyptian Whole Wheat Pita)

Aish baladi — عيش بلدي

A whole wheat dough proofed with yeast, rolled into five-inch rounds and baked on a 500°F stone until puffed and golden, or cooked in a hot pan.

3h 35mTotal time
20mPrep
30mCook
8Servings
200Calories
MediumDifficulty

Warm water, a teaspoon of sugar or honey and yeast stand 10 minutes until bubbly or foamy; 1 cup of the flour is mixed in and left half an hour as a pre-ferment, then the remaining flour and salt are kneaded until smooth and elastic. The dough rises about 1½ hours until doubled, is divided into 8, flattened to about 5 inches and rests 30 minutes, then bakes on a stone at 500°F for 6 to 8 minutes, flipping halfway, until puffed up and golden.

Ingredients

Equipment: Baking stone or heavy baking sheet, Stand mixer (optional), Rolling pin

  • 300 g warm water (1 1/4 cups)
  • 6 g sugar or honey (1 tsp)
  • 6 g dry yeast (1 tsp)
  • 385 g whole wheat flour (2 3/4 cups)
  • 8 g salt (1 tsp)
  • cracked wheat or whole wheat flour for dusting

Method

  1. Mix the yeast, water and sugar or honey and let stand 10 minutes, until bubbly or foamy.
  2. Mix in 1 cup (140 g) of the flour and leave the pre-ferment for half an hour.
  3. Add the remaining flour and the salt. Knead until smooth and elastic (a stand mixer takes 2 minutes, then 3 more, on low).
  4. Cover and let rise about 1½ hours, until doubled.
  5. Punch down, divide into 8 pieces and flatten each to about 5 inches (12 cm) in diameter. Cover loosely and rest 30 minutes.
  6. Set a stone on the middle rack and preheat to 500°F (260°C). Bake in batches 6 to 8 minutes, flipping halfway, until puffed up and golden. Or cook in a pan on medium-high heat for 1 to 1½ minutes per side until the bottom is golden and the top puffs.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: yeast bubbly or foamy; dough doubled; breads puffed and golden. The pre-ferment and final rest are NO CUE, TIME ONLY.
  • Dust with cracked wheat or flour so the rounds do not stick.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • A cool oven or stone, which prevents the pocket from puffing.
  • Rolling too thick.

Cultural Note

Jean Pendziwol writes that baladi means traditional or country and aish means life: 'In Egypt, bread is life.' She says it is one of Egypt's most commonly baked breads.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Wrap once cool; best the same day (our note).

Reheating: Warm briefly in a hot oven or pan to soften.

Serve with: Ful medames, Ta'meya
