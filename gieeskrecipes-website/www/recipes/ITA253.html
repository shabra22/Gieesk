🥞 Timballo di Scrippelle

Scrippelle Mbusse

Thin Abruzzese crepes rolled around Parmigiano, then 'drowned' in hot chicken broth - a comforting dish whose Teramo dialect name literally translates to 'wet crepes.'

Scrippelle are essentially thin, savory crepes, rolled around a simple cheese filling and softened by a generous ladle of hot broth poured directly over them just before serving, a specialty most closely associated with the city of Teramo.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Non-stick crepe pan or skillet

  • 4 eggs
  • 150 g (1 1/8 cups) 00 flour
  • 250 ml (1 cup) water
  • Pinch of salt
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, plus more for the pan
  • 150 g (1 3/4 cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1.2 L (5 cups) hot chicken broth
  • Fresh parsley, chopped

Method

  1. Whisk the eggs, flour, water, salt, and olive oil into a smooth, thin batter, similar to a crepe batter.
  2. Lightly oil a non-stick pan and heat over medium heat.
  3. Pour a thin layer of batter into the pan, swirling to coat, and cook briefly on each side until just set, without browning.
  4. Repeat with the remaining batter, stacking the cooked crepes as you go.
  5. Sprinkle each crepe generously with grated Parmigiano and roll up into a tight cylinder.
  6. Slice the rolled crepes into rounds, or leave whole depending on preference, and arrange in serving bowls.
  7. Ladle the hot chicken broth generously over the crepes just before serving, allowing them to soften slightly.
  8. Finish with fresh parsley and an extra scattering of Parmigiano.

Chef Tips

  • Cook the crepes very briefly on each side, just until set, without letting them brown, which keeps them tender.
  • Pour the broth over the crepes only right before serving so they soften gently without becoming completely mushy.
  • These crepes freeze well unfilled, making it easy to prepare a batch ahead of time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Browning the crepes too much, which changes their intended delicate, tender texture.
  • Adding the broth too far ahead of serving, causing the crepes to fall apart.
  • Making the crepes too thick, losing the delicate quality that defines this dish.

Cultural Note

Scrippelle mbusse is a defining specialty of Teramo in Abruzzo, its playful dialect name reflecting the dish's essential final step of drowning the rolled crepes in hot broth.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Unfilled crepes keep refrigerated for up to 2 days, or frozen for 1 month.

Reheating: Reheat the broth and assemble fresh; already-assembled scrippelle do not hold well.

Serve with: Crusty bread
