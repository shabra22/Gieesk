🍯 Ethiopian Honey Wine

ጠጅ — Tej

Ethiopian Tej — the ancient fermented honey wine of Ethiopia. Raw honey dissolved in water with gesho twigs (or hops as a substitute), fermented for 2—3 weeks into a lightly alcoholic, slightly bitter, deeply fragrant traditional wine. The drink of Ethiopian royalty and celebration.

Tej is Ethiopia's most important traditional beverage — drunk at celebrations, offered to guests as the highest hospitality, and served in tej houses across the country. The traditional fermentation uses gesho (Rhamnus prinoides) — an Ethiopian plant that provides bitterness similar to hops. The result is a honey wine of genuine complexity.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Ethiopia — the ancient mead that predates written history

Tej is Ethiopia's most ancient beverage — a fermented honey wine that has been consumed in the Ethiopian highlands for at least 2,000 years and likely much longer. Made from Ethiopian mountain honey and gesho (Rhamnus prinoides) — a bitter hops-like plant endemic to Ethiopia — tej was historically the drink of Ethiopian royalty and is still served at celebrations and in tej houses (tej bets) across the country. The gesho provides bitterness and acts as a natural preservative. Tej ranges from very sweet and mildly alcoholic (new ferment) to dry and strongly alcoholic (fully fermented).

Ingredients

Equipment: Large glass or clay fermentation vessel, Cloth for covering, Straining cloth

  • 1 kg raw Ethiopian honey (or good quality raw honey)
  • 3 litres warm water

Gesho (traditional)

  • 50g gesho twigs — or 30g dried hops

No yeast needed — honey's natural wild yeasts drive fermentation

    Method

    1. Dissolve honey in warm (not hot) water.
    2. Add gesho twigs or hops.
    3. Pour into fermentation vessel.
    4. Cover with a cloth.
    5. Ferment at room temperature for 7—14 days.
    6. Stir gently daily.
    7. After 7 days: strain out gesho.
    8. Continue fermenting 7—14 more days until desired sweetness/alcohol level.
    9. Strain through cloth. Serve in traditional berele (flask-shaped glasses).

    Chef Tips

    • Raw honey contains wild yeasts on its surface — no commercial yeast needed.
    • Warmer room temperature = faster fermentation.
    • Taste daily — fresh tej (sweet, mild) to older tej (dry, alcoholic) are both valid.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using pasteurised honey — kills the wild yeasts.
    • Too hot water — kills the yeasts.

    The Science

    Honey fermentation is driven by wild Saccharomyces cerevisiae strains and other yeasts naturally present on honey. The honey's high sugar concentration initially inhibits fermentation — dilution with water is what activates the yeasts. Gesho's bitterness (from compounds similar to hops' alpha acids) inhibits bacterial contamination while allowing yeast activity.

    Cultural Note

    Tej is the drink of Ethiopian civilization — served at the court of Emperor Haile Selassie, drunk by Sheba's Queen, and today served in tej houses from Addis Ababa to the smallest highland village. The berele flask-shaped glass from which it is drunk is one of the most distinctive objects of Ethiopian material culture.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Sealed container — refrigerate after desired fermentation level reached. Keeps weeks to months.

    Reheating: Not applicable — serve at room temperature.

    Serve with: At Ethiopian celebrations, With Doro Wat, As a gift of hospitality
