🐟 Ethiopian Smoked Fish

ጢስ አሣ — Tis Asa

Ethiopian Smoked Lake Fish — whole fish salted, spiced with berbere and garlic, and slowly smoked over fragrant wood until deeply flavoured and preserved. The traditional lake community preservation technique — smoked fish is used in stews, eaten as-is, and carried on long journeys.

Smoked fish is the preservation technique of Ethiopia's lake communities — when the catch is larger than can be eaten fresh, the fish is salted, spiced, and smoked. The smoking adds flavour complexity and extends the shelf life dramatically. Smoked fish is used in stews and eaten as a snack.

Ingredients

Equipment: Smoker or covered grill with indirect heat, Fish rack

  • 1 kg whole fish — tilapia or catfish
  • 2 tbsp coarse salt
  • 1 tbsp berbere
  • 1 tsp garlic powder

Wood for smoking: olive wood, acacia, or fruit wood

    Method

    1. Clean fish. Score deeply.
    2. Rub salt, berbere, and garlic over all surfaces and into score marks.
    3. Rest 1 hour.
    4. Prepare smoker at 70—80°C (low temperature, cold smoke).
    5. Smoke fish for 6—8 hours, refuelling as needed.
    6. The fish is ready when deeply golden, firm, and fragrant.

    Chef Tips

    • Low temperature — this is preservation smoking, not cooking smoking.
    • The berbere adds complexity to the smoke flavour.
    • Smoked fish keeps 2 weeks at room temperature in a dry place.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Too high temperature — cooks rather than smokes.

    The Science

    Cold smoking at 70—80°C produces volatile phenol compounds from the wood combustion — these phenols deposit on the fish surface and have antimicrobial properties, inhibiting bacterial and fungal growth. Combined with the salt's desiccating effect, they extend shelf life dramatically.

    Cultural Note

    Smoked lake fish is the food preservation wisdom of Ethiopia's lake communities — developed over centuries of observation to make the most of abundant fishing seasons and provide protein through lean periods.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Wrapped in cloth 2 weeks at room temperature. Refrigerate 1 month.

    Reheating: Use in stews or eat as-is.

    Serve with: In fish stews, As a standalone snack, As a travel food
