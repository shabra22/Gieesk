🦐 Risotto alla Pescatora

Risotto alla Pescatora

A luxurious seafood risotto from Italy's coastline, built on a quick shellfish stock and finished with mussels, shrimp, and squid — a showpiece dish for special occasions.

Unlike creamy inland risottos, seafood risotto is traditionally made without cheese or excessive butter, letting the natural brininess of the shellfish and a well-made seafood stock carry the dish, finished with just a touch of olive oil for shine.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide, heavy-bottomed pan, Large pot with lid for mussels, Ladle

  • 320 g (1½ cups) Carnaroli or Arborio rice
  • 500 g (1.1 lb) mussels, cleaned
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) shrimp, shell-on
  • 250 g (9 oz) squid, cleaned and sliced into rings
  • 1.2 L (5 cups) fish or shellfish stock, hot
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry white wine
  • 200 g (7 oz) cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Salt and chili flakes, to taste

Method

  1. Peel the shrimp, reserving the shells, and simmer the shells in the fish stock for 15 minutes to deepen its flavor, then strain and keep warm.
  2. Steam the mussels in a covered pot with a splash of water until they open, about 4–5 minutes; discard any that stay closed, and reserve the liquid, straining it into the stock.
  3. Warm half the olive oil in a wide pan and sauté the onion and garlic until softened.
  4. Add the rice and toast for 2 minutes, then add the wine and stir until absorbed.
  5. Stir in the cherry tomatoes, then begin adding the hot stock one ladle at a time, stirring frequently.
  6. After about 12 minutes, add the squid, continuing to add stock gradually.
  7. A few minutes before the rice is done, add the shrimp and mussels to warm through and finish cooking.
  8. Once the rice is creamy with a slight bite, remove from the heat, stir in the remaining olive oil and chili flakes, and finish with parsley before serving immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Simmering the shrimp shells briefly in the stock adds real depth that plain stock alone won't provide.
  • Add delicate seafood like shrimp and mussels late so they don't overcook while the rice finishes.
  • Traditionally no cheese is added to seafood risotto, letting the seafood flavors stand out clearly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the seafood at the start, resulting in overcooked, rubbery shrimp and squid by the time the rice is ready.
  • Discarding the mussel cooking liquid, which is full of flavor and should go into the stock.
  • Adding Parmigiano out of habit, which clashes with the delicate seafood flavors in this particular risotto.

Health Benefits

  • An excellent source of lean protein, iron, and omega-3 fatty acids from the mixed seafood.

Cultural Note

Seafood risotto appears in variations all along Italy's coastline, each region drawing on whatever shellfish and fish are freshest from local waters that day.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately; seafood risotto does not hold or reheat well.

Reheating: Not recommended; the seafood toughens significantly upon reheating.

Serve with: A crisp, dry white wine such as Vermentino
