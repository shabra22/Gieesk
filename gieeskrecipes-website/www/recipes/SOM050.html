Macsharo — Mkate wa Maashara

A honeycomb rice cake from Brava — a fermented rice-flour batter thinned with cardamom syrup until it is almost watery, poured into a smoking wok and steamed under a lid into a spongy, hole-riddled round.

The Bravanese call this the rich man's maanda. Their everyday bread is corn-based; rice was the scarcer commodity, so a rice cake was what you served to a guest you wanted to impress, alongside meat dishes. The name comes from the deep bowl it was served in — maashara, a word for a large bowl also used in parts of Yemen — and in Chimbalazi, the Swahili dialect of Brava, another of its names means simply to pour.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wok, or a deep frying pan with a lid, Large mixing bowl, Saucepan

    The yeast starter

    • 2 tbsp fine semolina
    • 1 tbsp sugar
    • 1 tbsp instant dry yeast
    • 60 ml (4 tbsp) water

    The batter

    • 450 g (3 3/4 cups) rice flour — non-glutinous, not glutinous rice flour
    • 1 egg
    • 1/4 tsp baking powder
    • 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
    • 350 ml (1 1/2 cups) water

    The syrup

    • 450 g (2 1/4 cups) sugar
    • 530 ml (2 1/4 cups) water
    • 1/2 tsp ground cardamom

    For cooking

    • 2 tbsp oil per cake

    Method

    1. Mix the semolina, sugar, yeast and 60 ml water and leave it to stand for at least 15 minutes, until active.
    2. Put the rice flour in a large bowl, add the egg, then the yeast starter, then the water gradually, mixing well. Stir in the baking powder and cardamom.
    3. Leave the batter to ferment for 3 hours. It can go into the fridge overnight and be cooked the next day instead.
    4. For the syrup, put the sugar in a pan, add the water and boil for 5 minutes. Stir in the cardamom and let it cool completely.
    5. Add the cooled syrup to the batter. It will now be very watery — that is exactly right, and thickening it here is the commonest way this goes wrong.
    6. Put 2 tbsp oil in the wok over high heat and wait until it is very hot, just starting to smoke.
    7. Pour in about 2 cups of batter and cook uncovered for 1 to 2 minutes.
    8. Cover, drop the heat to its lowest setting and cook for 20 minutes, until the top is set and honeycombed through.
    9. Let it cool in the wok before lifting it out, and rest it on a curved dish rather than a flat plate.

    Chef Tips

    • It has to be plain non-glutinous rice flour, the kind sold in Asian groceries. Glutinous rice flour makes a cake that falls apart.
    • The batter is supposed to be watery after the syrup goes in. A thick batter gives a dense cake that never goes spongy.
    • The oil must be smoking before the batter goes in. That first blast of heat is what sets the base and starts the honeycomb.
    • It can also be baked, at 180C / 350F for 15 minutes, if a wok on high heat is not practical.
    • White patches on the finished cake mean too little oil in the wok or a lid that was not sealing.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using glutinous rice flour, which produces something that will not hold together at all.
    • Thickening the batter because it looks too thin, which is the single most common failure.
    • Holding back the syrup to make it less sweet — the syrup is what makes it light; without it the cake is very dense.
    • Turning it out while hot, or onto a flat plate, either of which tears the top off.

    Health Benefits

    • Naturally gluten-free, being made entirely from rice flour.
    • The fermentation makes the batter easier to digest.
    • Dairy-free as written.

    Cultural Note

    Macsharo belongs to a family of honeycomb rice cakes made all over Asia and along the Swahili coast, where it is also called mkate wa siniyah. In Brava it is a status dish: another of its names translates as rice bread, and rice was the expensive grain. It is eaten as a snack and, paired with samosas, to break the fast in Ramadan.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Covered, at room temperature up to 2 days.

    Reheating: Warm briefly in a low oven. It dries quickly, so cover it.

    Serve with: Sambuusa, Xalwo Muus, Shaah Cadays
