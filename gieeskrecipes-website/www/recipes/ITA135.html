🫒 Bagna Cauda

Bagna Càuda

A warm, pungent Piedmontese dip of garlic and anchovies slowly melted into olive oil, traditionally kept simmering at the table over a small burner for dipping raw and cooked vegetables.

Bagna cauda, meaning 'hot bath,' is as much a social ritual as a recipe, a communal dish meant to be shared slowly around a table loaded with an array of seasonal vegetables, each dipped into the same warm, garlicky pot.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small saucepan or fonduta pot, Small burner for tableside serving (optional)

  • 150 g (5.3 oz) anchovy fillets
  • 8 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) extra virgin olive oil
  • 60 g (¼ cup) unsalted butter
  • Assorted raw and cooked vegetables (bell peppers, cardoons, celery, fennel, cabbage), for dipping

Method

  1. Rinse the anchovy fillets if very salty, and roughly chop.
  2. Combine the garlic and olive oil in a small saucepan and warm over very low heat.
  3. Cook gently for 15–20 minutes, until the garlic is completely soft and mellow, taking care it never browns or fries.
  4. Add the anchovies and stir, letting them dissolve completely into the garlic-infused oil, about 5 minutes.
  5. Stir in the butter until melted and the sauce is smooth.
  6. Transfer to a small warming pot or fondue-style burner to keep warm at the table.
  7. Serve surrounded by a colorful array of raw and lightly cooked vegetables for dipping.

Chef Tips

  • The garlic must cook low and slow until fully mellowed — any browning turns it bitter rather than sweet and soft.
  • Traditionally the sauce is kept gently warm throughout the meal using a small tabletop burner, turning it into a shared, leisurely dining experience.
  • A wide variety of vegetables, both raw and lightly blanched, makes for the most traditional and enjoyable spread.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the garlic over heat that's too high, causing it to brown and turn bitter.
  • Rushing the process, which doesn't allow the garlic and anchovy flavors to properly mellow and meld.
  • Serving only a narrow selection of vegetables, missing the communal, varied spirit of the dish.

Health Benefits

  • Anchovies provide omega-3 fatty acids; the accompanying vegetables add fiber and vitamins.

Cultural Note

Bagna cauda is deeply woven into Piedmontese social life, traditionally served during the autumn grape harvest and enjoyed as a slow, communal meal among family and friends.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; reheat gently before serving.

Reheating: Reheat very gently over low heat, stirring, without letting it come to a boil.

Serve with: A robust Piedmontese red wine
