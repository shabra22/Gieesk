🍚 Thieboudienne for Twelve

Thieboudienne — SBS version

Three or four whole fish fried in batches, vegetables simmered in a nokkos-and-tomato sauce with a teaspoon of nététou, and a kilo of broken rice stirred into the broth.

2h 20mTotal time
50mPrep
1h 30mCook

650Calories
HardDifficulty

Aissatou Ba’s version for SBS Food feeds twelve. A stuffing of parsley, onion, garlic, habanero and capsicum is pushed into deep slits in thick fish cutlets; a nokkos of onions, garlic, capsicums, stock cubes and one teaspoon of nététou is blended smooth. The fish fries 6–7 minutes a side in 750 ml of oil, three-quarters of the nokkos fries five minutes with the tomato paste, and the vegetables simmer in the broth. Fish and heads return with okra and chillies for 15 minutes; tamarind concentrate is stirred into a cup of the sauce for drizzling; fish sauce seasons the broth, which ‘should be a little saltier than you think’. Steamed broken rice is stirred in and finished over low heat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor, Deep frying pan, Large pot, Steamer, Slotted spoon

    Main

    • 3–4 lean whole fish with firm flesh (barramundi, grouper, sea bream, bass, pike, hake, tilapia or snapper), about 1.5–2 kg, scaled and gutted
    • 750 ml (3 cups) vegetable oil
    • 2 small pieces sea snail (optional; the page says ‘only available in Senegal’)
    • 4 tbsp tomato paste
    • 2 carrots, peeled and cut into chunks
    • 2 sweet potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
    • 1 cassava root, peeled and cut into chunks
    • 1 eggplant, cut into 3 wedges
    • 1 cabbage, cut into wedges
    • 3 bay leaves
    • 4 okra
    • 2 habanero chillies
    • 1 kg broken rice or jasmine rice
    • 1 tbsp tamarind concentrate
    • 2 tbsp fish sauce
    • Sliced tomato, red and green capsicum, to serve — quantity not printed

    Stuffing

    • 1/2 bunch curly parsley, chopped
    • 1/2 onion, chopped
    • 3 garlic cloves, peeled
    • 1/2 habanero chilli
    • 1/2 red capsicum
    • 1/2 green capsicum
    • 1/2 tsp salt
    • Freshly ground black pepper, to taste

    Nokkos

    • 3 onions, roughly chopped
    • 5 garlic cloves
    • 2 red capsicum, roughly chopped
    • 2 green capsicum, roughly chopped
    • A pinch of ground black pepper
    • 1 tbsp salt
    • 2 vegetable stock cubes
    • 1 tsp nététou

    Method

    1. Process all the stuffing ingredients to a smooth paste (a mortar and pestle may be used).
    2. Cut the fish into thick cutlets, reserving the head. Make a few deep slits in each piece and push the stuffing into the slits.
    3. Process all the nokkos ingredients, including the nététou, until smooth.
    4. Heat the oil in a deep frying pan over high. Fry the fish in batches, 6–7 minutes per side. Set aside.
    5. Add three-quarters of the nokkos to the same oil and fry 5 minutes. Add the sea snail if using, then the tomato paste and 250 ml (1 cup) of water. Simmer covered 10 minutes.
    6. Add 1 1/2–2 litres of water, then the carrot, sweet potato, cassava, eggplant, cabbage and bay leaves. Simmer 20 minutes, or until just tender.
    7. Meanwhile wash and rinse the rice 3 times, drain well, and steam 10–15 minutes, or until half cooked.
    8. Add the fried fish and reserved heads to the vegetables with the okra and fresh chillies. Cover and simmer 15 minutes. Remove the fish and vegetables with a slotted spoon.
    9. Stir the tamarind into 1 cup of the sauce in a small bowl. Pour another cup of sauce over the fish.
    10. Add the remaining nokkos to the remaining sauce. Season generously with salt, then add the fish sauce — it should be a little saltier than you think.
    11. Gently stir the steamed rice into the sauce and cook over low heat 10–15 minutes, until just cooked.
    12. Tip the rice onto a platter, top with vegetables and fish, drizzle with the tamarind sauce and garnish with sliced tomato and capsicum.

    Chef Tips

    • Season the sauce ‘a little saltier than you think’ before the rice goes in; the rice drinks the salt.
    • The rice is steamed ‘half cooked’ first, then finished in the sauce until ‘just cooked’.
    • Step 4 has no doneness cue; 6–7 minutes per side is a time only.
    • Nététou is bought and raw-blended into the nokkos. The page gives no preparation, time or cue for it.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Under-seasoning the sauce.
    • Over-steaming the rice before it goes in.
    • Skipping the slits, which hold the stuffing.

    Cultural Note

    The page’s own gloss: ‘Very popular in Senegalese cooking, netetou (also known as dawadawa and sumbala)…’. It spells the seasoning ‘netetou’ and never uses ‘nététou’, ‘guedj’ or ‘yeet’.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

    Reheating: Steam gently with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Sliced tomato and capsicum, Tamarind sauce
