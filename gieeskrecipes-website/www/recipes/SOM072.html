🥭 Casiir Cambe

Casiir Cambe

Mango pulp blended with milk, ice cream and a pinch of salt, poured over a spoonful of strawberry jam in the bottom of the glass and dusted with cardamom — a Ramadan shake for breaking the fast.

This is the mango shake older sisters make for the family at iftar, and the details are what make it Somali rather than generic. A pinch of salt, which sharpens the mango rather than salting it. Butterscotch ice cream rather than vanilla. And the jam spooned into the bottom of the glass so the shake sits on top of it — the one documented Somali layering trick, and the reason it looks like something from a juice bar rather than a blender.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Tall glasses

  • 180 ml (3/4 cup) mango pulp
  • 240 ml (1 cup) milk
  • 2 scoops butterscotch or vanilla ice cream
  • A pinch of salt
  • A few ice cubes

To serve

  • Strawberry jam
  • Ground cardamom

Method

  1. Put the mango pulp, milk, ice cream, salt and a few ice cubes in a blender and blend well.
  2. Spoon a layer of strawberry jam into the bottom of each glass.
  3. Pour the shake on top of the jam.
  4. Dust with ground cardamom and serve cold.

Chef Tips

  • The pinch of salt is not a typo. It lifts the mango the way salt lifts any sweet fruit, and leaving it out makes the shake noticeably flatter.
  • Butterscotch ice cream is the specified choice and it is better than vanilla here — the caramel note sits well against the mango.
  • The jam goes in the glass, not the blender. Blended in, it just muddies the colour; layered, it gives you a sweet-sharp mouthful at the bottom.
  • Alphonso or Kesar pulp, tinned, is more reliable than fresh mango outside the season and is what most people actually use.
  • The jam and the cardamom can both go on at once — they are not alternatives.
  • Some versions use yoghurt in place of the ice cream, which makes it a lassi rather than a shake. Both are drunk; they are different drinks.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the salt.
  • Blending the jam in.
  • Using a stringy, fibrous mango instead of pulp, which no amount of blending fixes.
  • Serving it at room temperature.

Health Benefits

  • Mango is high in vitamins A and C.
  • Milk supplies calcium and protein — useful when you are breaking a fast.
  • The ice cream makes this a treat rather than a health drink, and it is meant to be.

Cultural Note

Iftar drinks carry a particular weight: after a day without water, what you reach for first matters, and in a lot of Somali households it is this. It belongs to Ramadan and to hot afternoons, and it also gets drunk straight after a savoury meal, which is not how most cultures treat a milkshake.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Drink immediately.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Sambuusa, Bajiye, Dates, at iftar
