🫘 Ethiopian Lentil and Spinach Stew

ምስር ስፒናጭ ወጥ — Misir Spinach Wat

Ethiopian Lentil and Spinach Stew — red lentils cooked in a berbere sauce with spinach added in the final 5 minutes. The nutritionally supercharged Misir Wat — the combination of lentil iron with spinach iron and vitamin C for maximum absorption.

Lentil and spinach stew takes Misir Wat and adds the nutritional power of spinach — both are very high in iron, and the spinach's vitamin C dramatically improves the iron bioavailability of the lentils. A modern nutritional upgrade to the classic dish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot

  • 1.5 cups red lentils
  • 200g spinach — washed
  • 3 onions
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 3 tbsp berbere
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 2 cups water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Make Misir Wat base (ETH101) through the simmering stage.
  2. When lentils are almost done, add spinach.
  3. Stir spinach into the hot lentil sauce.
  4. Cook 3—4 minutes until wilted.
  5. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Add spinach at the very end — it only needs 3 minutes.
  • The spinach wilts dramatically — add generously.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding spinach too early.

The Science

Non-haem iron from lentils and spinach is poorly absorbed on its own — typically only 5—12% bioavailability. However, vitamin C in the spinach converts non-haem iron from Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺, increasing absorption to 20—40%. Cooking the spinach briefly preserves much of its vitamin C while breaking down the cell walls to release the iron.

Health Benefits

  • Iron from two sources.
  • Vitamin C for iron absorption.
  • Double the plant protein.
  • Very high in folate.

Cultural Note

Lentil and spinach stew demonstrates how Ethiopian fasting cuisine continues to evolve — taking the ancient berbere-lentil tradition and enriching it with modern nutritional understanding.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days (spinach shortens shelf life).

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), On the fasting platter
