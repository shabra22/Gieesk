Asun — peppered goat meat

Grilled goat chopped into bites, then tossed with peppers and onions fried fifteen minutes until they lose their water — finished when the onions caramelise a bit.

Asun is the party dish — chunks of smoky grilled goat in a coarse pepper and onion mixture, eaten with a toothpick and a cold drink. The sequence matters: the meat is cooked and grilled whole first, then chopped, so the smoke gets into it before the surfaces multiply. The peppers and one onion are fried for about fifteen minutes until they have lost their water, the meat goes in for ten, and a second chopped onion goes in at the end for freshness. It is done when those onions have caramelised a little.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grill or oven, Wok or large skillet, Sharp knife

  • 1 kg cooked and grilled goat meat
  • Scotch bonnet (atarodo) peppers — as much as you can tolerate
  • Green and bell peppers (optional)
  • 2 large onions
  • 2 bouillon cubes
  • Salt to taste

Used in the method but not listed in the original

  • Vegetable oil

Method

  1. Coarsely blend or chop the peppers and one onion, and set aside.
  2. Chop the grilled goat into bite-sized chunks.
  3. Heat a wok or skillet. Add oil, then the peppers and onions.
  4. Saute with the seasoning and salt about 15 minutes, until the peppers lose their water.
  5. Add the meat and fry 10 minutes.
  6. Add the second chopped onion and stir.
  7. Add more green peppers if using, and fry 5 to 6 minutes until the onions caramelise a bit.

Chef Tips

  • Grill the meat whole and chop it afterwards. Chopping first multiplies the surfaces and you lose the smoke into the fire.
  • Fifteen minutes until the peppers lose their water. If there is liquid in the pan when the meat goes in, the meat will steam instead of frying.
  • Coarse, not smooth. Asun should have visible pieces of pepper clinging to the meat.
  • The onions caramelising is the cue. Not browning, not burning — just turning sweet at the edges.
  • The second onion goes in near the end for bite and freshness. Both onions cooked down and you have lost that contrast.
  • Female native goat works best, according to another named source — the meat cooks fast and the skin is softer.
  • Traditionally asun is smoked, not grilled. The smoked version is the real thing and this is the domestic approximation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Chopping the goat before grilling it.
  • Adding the meat to a wet pepper mixture.
  • Blending the peppers smooth.
  • Cooking both onions down to nothing.
  • Overcooking the meat, which is already cooked and grilled.

Health Benefits

  • Goat is leaner than beef or lamb and lower in saturated fat.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.
  • High in iron, zinc and B12.
  • Capsaicin from the scotch bonnets is associated with raised thermogenesis.

Cultural Note

Asun belongs to the owambe — the Yoruba party — and it arrives on a tray with toothpicks, to be eaten standing up with a drink in the other hand. The word itself points at smoke, and the smoked version made over a drum is what the dish aspires to; grilling is what most kitchens can manage. It is bar food as much as party food, and it is almost never a main course.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated, 2 months frozen.

Reheating: Hot pan, briefly. It is better slightly charred than gently warmed.

Serve with: Cold beer or stout, Boiled yam, Dodo (fried plantain)
