🍜 Spicy Obuwowoolo Noodle Soup

Obuwowoolo — buwowoolo

Street-bought fried cow lung refried with chilli oil while the noodle water becomes the broth — nothing is drained away, and the flavour packets go in the bin.

Obuwowoolo is pieces of cow lung slow-fried in their own fat, almost a confit, bought at markets like Kalerwe and eaten at twilight with friends alongside roasted maize and fried cassava. This is the only written record of it, and it is a shortcut dish: instant noodles, street-bought lung, pre-made chilli oil. What makes it work is that the noodle water is kept, seasoned with salt and soy sauce and boiled into a broth, and the meat comes to the bowl still sizzling.

Ingredients

Equipment: Two pans, or one pan and one pot, Bowls

  • 2 packs instant noodles — discard the flavour packets
  • 240 ml (1 cup) fried cow lung (obuwowoolo), or more
  • 480 ml (2 cups) water
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) soy sauce
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) chilli oil
  • 120 ml (1/2 cup) cabbage, thinly sliced
  • Salt
  • Simsim, to garnish

Method

  1. Over medium heat, refry the fried lung meats. Add salt and the chilli oil.
  2. Reduce the heat and let them cook on low while you prepare the broth.
  3. Boil the water. Add the noodles and stir until they become loose, about 3 minutes.
  4. Take the noodles off the heat and drain them — but do not discard the water.
  5. Put the water back on the heat, add salt and the soy sauce, and let it come to the boil.
  6. Put the noodles in a bowl.
  7. Add the sizzling hot meat.
  8. Add the sliced cabbage.
  9. Pour the broth over the noodles.
  10. Sprinkle with simsim and eat while hot.

Chef Tips

  • Keep the noodle water. Seasoned with salt and soy sauce and brought back to the boil, it is the broth — and this is the same keep-the-cooking-liquid instinct that runs through kikomando and matooke porridge.
  • Throw the flavour packets away. The point is the lung fat and the chilli oil.
  • The meat goes into the bowl still sizzling. Keeping it on low heat while you make the broth is what gets it there.
  • The cabbage goes in raw and wilts under the hot broth. Do not cook it.
  • Three minutes for the noodles, just until loose. They finish in the bowl.
  • This is called a lazy way to have great noodle soup by the person who wrote it — instant noodles, street-bought meat and pre-made chilli oil. It is a real Kampala dish assembled from convenient parts, not a traditional one.
  • Obuwowoolo itself is what to look for at a market like Kalerwe: cow lung slow-fried in its own fat. The traditional way to eat it is at twilight with friends, with roasted maize and fried cassava alongside, everything still hot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Discarding the noodle water.
  • Using the flavour packets.
  • Letting the meat go cold before assembly.
  • Cooking the cabbage.
  • Overcooking the noodles in the pot.

Health Benefits

  • Lung is high in protein, iron and vitamin B12, and very low in fat compared with most offal.
  • Cabbage goes in raw, keeping its vitamin C.
  • Simsim adds calcium and healthy fats.
  • The instant noodles are high in sodium, and the flavour packets being discarded helps.

Cultural Note

Obuwowoolo is a reminder that Kampala street food goes well beyond the rolex, and that whole categories of it have never been written down. That the only published account of it is inside an instant-noodle recipe says a lot about how much of this cuisine still lives only in markets and memory.

Storage & Serving

Storage: None — assemble and eat.

Reheating: Not worth it. The noodles go to pieces.

Serve with: Roasted maize, Fried cassava, Chilli oil
