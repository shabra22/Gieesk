Peperoni Cruschi

Sun-dried Senise peppers flash-fried until they turn shatteringly crisp within seconds, a distinctive Basilicata specialty eaten as a snack or scattered over pasta for crunch.

Made from the sweet, thin-walled peppers grown around Senise and dried in the sun for weeks, peperoni cruschi transform almost instantly in hot oil, puffing and crisping so quickly that a moment's inattention will burn them.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep frying pan, Slotted spoon

  • 20 dried Senise peppers (peperoni di Senise), whole
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Wipe the dried peppers clean if needed, removing any stems.
  2. Heat vegetable oil to about 160°C (320°F) — hot but not smoking.
  3. Carefully lower a few peppers into the oil at a time.
  4. Fry for just a few seconds — the peppers will puff and turn crisp almost immediately.
  5. Remove quickly with a slotted spoon the moment they crisp, as they burn very easily.
  6. Drain on paper towels and season with salt while still warm.
  7. Serve as a crunchy snack, or crumble over pasta or eggs for added texture.

Chef Tips

  • Watch these constantly while frying — they cook in mere seconds and burn just as quickly.
  • Frying in small batches gives you better control and reduces the risk of burning any of them.
  • These are traditionally used both as a standalone snack and as a crunchy garnish over other dishes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Walking away even briefly while frying, which can result in burnt, bitter peppers.
  • Using oil that's too hot, which speeds up the already very quick cooking time even further.
  • Overcrowding the pan, causing uneven cooking.

Health Benefits

  • Peppers retain some vitamin C even after drying, though frying does add fat.

Cultural Note

Peperoni cruschi are a protected specialty of Senise in Basilicata, celebrated with a dedicated local festival each August honoring the region's distinctive sun-dried pepper tradition.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container at room temperature for a few days; they lose crispness over time.

Reheating: Not applicable; these are meant to be eaten fresh and crisp.

Serve with: As a garnish over pasta, A simple snack on their own
