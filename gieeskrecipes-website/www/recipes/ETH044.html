🐑 Ethiopian Spiced Lamb Shoulder

የበግ ትከሻ — Ye-Beg Tikesha

Ethiopian Whole Lamb Shoulder — marinated overnight in berbere, kibbeh, garlic, and ginger, slow-roasted for 2 hours until falling tender. The celebration centrepiece — carved at the table on injera and eaten communally.

The whole lamb shoulder roast is the Ethiopian celebration showpiece — marinated overnight in berbere and kibbeh, roasted until falling-off-the-bone tender, and brought whole to the table where guests tear pieces directly. It represents the pinnacle of Ethiopian hospitality.

Ingredients

Equipment: Roasting pan, Oven at 150°C, Foil

  • 1.5 kg bone-in lamb shoulder

Overnight Marinade

  • 4 tbsp berbere
  • 3 tbsp kibbeh
  • 6 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Pierce shoulder deeply all over. Rub marinade in thoroughly. Refrigerate overnight.
  2. Bring to room temperature 1.5 hours before roasting.
  3. Roast covered with foil at 150°C for 90 minutes.
  4. Remove foil. Raise to 200°C for 20—30 minutes to caramelise.
  5. Rest 20 minutes. Pull apart or carve.

Chef Tips

  • The overnight marinade penetrates deeply through the score marks.
  • Low and slow for 90 minutes creates falling-tender meat.
  • The high-heat finish caramelises the berbere coating magnificently.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Short marination.
  • Insufficient resting.

The Science

See ETH036 for roast science. Lamb shoulder has more connective tissue than leg — requiring the longer cooking time to convert collagen to gelatin, producing the falling-tender result.

Cultural Note

The whole lamb shoulder arriving at the table is the ultimate Ethiopian hospitality statement — 'for you, we have spared nothing'.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days.

Reheating: Covered in oven at 160°C.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Tikel Gomen (ETH087), Gomen (ETH086)
