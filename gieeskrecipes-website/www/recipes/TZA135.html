🫓 Tanzanian Coconut Chapati

Chapati ya Nazi

Tanzanian Chapati ya Nazi — chapati made with coconut milk instead of water, creating a richer, more fragrant, slightly sweeter flatbread with a more tender texture. The coastal version of the everyday chapati — all the layers, twice the flavour.

Coconut chapati is the coastal upgrade of Tanzania's everyday flatbread — replacing water with fresh coconut milk produces a chapati that is more tender, more fragrant, and slightly richer than the standard version. On the coast, where coconut milk is as abundant as water, this is simply how chapati is made.

Ingredients

Equipment: Rolling pin, Flat pan or griddle, Pastry brush

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 200ml warm coconut milk
  • ½ cup warm water
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil — plus more for cooking

Method

  1. Combine flour, salt, and sugar. Add oil and rub in.
  2. Add coconut milk and water gradually, mixing to a soft dough.
  3. Knead 8—10 minutes until smooth.
  4. Rest covered 30 minutes.
  5. Divide into 8 balls. Roll each thin.
  6. Brush with oil. Fold, brush, fold again into a layered disc. Roll out again.
  7. Cook in a dry pan over medium heat 2—3 minutes per side.
  8. Brush with oil during cooking. Keep wrapped.

Chef Tips

  • Coconut milk produces a more tender dough than water — do not over-knead.
  • The fold-and-roll technique creates the flaky layers.
  • Keep cooked chapatis wrapped — they go hard quickly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-kneading coconut chapati — toughens.
  • Not wrapping finished chapatis.

The Science

Coconut milk's fat content coats the flour proteins — partially inhibiting gluten development. This produces a more tender, less chewy chapati than water-based dough, while the fat also creates greater flakiness in the layered structure.

Cultural Note

Coconut chapati is the coast's most generous gift to the chapati tradition — richer, softer, and more fragrant than the mainland version, it is the bread that makes every coastal Tanzanian meal complete.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Wrap in cloth for 2 days. Freeze 2 months.

Reheating: Warm in dry pan.

Serve with: Any Tanzanian stew, Maharage ya Nazi (TZA091), Grilled fish
