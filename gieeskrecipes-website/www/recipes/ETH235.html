🫘 Tigrayan Tsebhi Birsen

ጽብሒ ብርሰን — Tsebhi Birsen

Tigrayan Tsebhi Birsen — red lentils cooked in the Tigrayan berbere-style sauce with a touch of dried lemon (left lemon) for acidity. The Tigrinya version of Misir Wat — more acidic, slightly different spice profile, representing the ancient northern food tradition.

Tsebhi Birsen follows the same dry-caramelised onion technique as Misir Wat but uses the Tigrayan spice approach — a touch more acid, a specific blend that references the ancient Aksumite spice trade.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Tigray region — northern Ethiopia, the heartland of the Aksumite civilisation

Tigray is the northernmost highland region of Ethiopia — the heartland of the ancient Aksumite civilisation (1st—7th century CE) that was one of the world's first Christian kingdoms. Tigrayan cuisine (called Tigrinya cuisine) is closely related to Eritrean food and uses similar ingredients to the broader Ethiopian tradition but with distinctive preparation methods. Tsebhi (the Tigrinya word for wat) is made with more acidity (from tamarind or dried lemon) and a specific spice blend called Berbere Tigrinya that differs from standard berbere. Birsen means lentils — Tsebhi Birsen is the Tigrayan lentil wat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot

  • 2 cups red lentils
  • 4 onions — finely diced
  • 4 tbsp oil
  • 4 tbsp berbere
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 1 tbsp dried lemon rind (or 1 tsp tamarind paste)
  • 1 tsp korerima (Ethiopian cardamom)
  • 3 cups water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Dry caramelise onions 20 minutes.
  2. Add oil and berbere. Fry 3 minutes.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, dried lemon, and korerima.
  4. Add lentils and water. Simmer 30—35 minutes.
  5. Season.

Chef Tips

  • The dried lemon is the Tigrayan signature — it adds a citrus-tart note absent from standard Misir Wat.
  • Korerima is used in Tigrinya cooking more than elsewhere.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the dried lemon.

Cultural Note

Tigrayan food carries the weight of the Aksumite civilisation — the cuisine of the people who built the obelisks of Aksum, who received the Ark of the Covenant according to Ethiopian tradition, and who gave Ethiopia its first Christian church. Tsebhi Birsen is a lentil stew with 1,500 years of history.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001) or Tigrayan taita (thicker injera), At Tigrayan celebrations
