🟤 Tanzanian Tamarind Juice

Juisi ya Ukwaju

Tanzanian Juisi ya Ukwaju — tamarind pulp dissolved in cold water and sweetened to the perfect sweet-sour balance, lightly spiced with ginger and cardamom, served over ice. The most refreshing drink on the Tanzanian coast — simultaneously cooling, energising, and deeply flavourful.

Tamarind juice is the drink of the Swahili coast — the sweet-sour flavour of tamarind dissolved in ice-cold water is one of the most refreshing beverages in East Africa. Sold by vendors from large clay pots at every coastal market, it is the drink that stops the heat of the midday sun.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Sieve, Pitcher

  • 100g block tamarind — or 5 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 6 cups cold water
  • 4—5 tbsp sugar (to taste)
  • ½ tsp ground ginger
  • Pinch of ground cardamom
  • Pinch of salt
  • Ice cubes

Method

  1. If using block tamarind: break into pieces, soak in 2 cups warm water for 10 minutes. Squeeze and knead with your hands until pulp dissolves. Strain through a sieve, pressing firmly. Discard fibres and seeds.
  2. If using paste: dissolve directly in 2 cups warm water.
  3. Add remaining cold water, sugar, ginger, cardamom, and salt.
  4. Stir until sugar dissolves.
  5. Taste — adjust sweet-sour balance.
  6. Serve over ice.

Chef Tips

  • The drink should be distinctly sour — add sugar to balance, not to eliminate the sourness.
  • A pinch of salt amplifies both the sweetness and sourness.
  • Block tamarind produces a deeper flavour than paste — worth the extra effort.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-sweetening — loses its refreshing sour character.
  • Not straining thoroughly — fibrous result.

The Science

Tamarind contains tartaric acid (3—6%) — one of the strongest natural food acids. This acid stimulates salivary gland secretion more effectively than citric or malic acid, creating the particularly refreshing, thirst-quenching sensation of tamarind drinks. The salt in the recipe suppresses bitterness perception, amplifying the sweet-sour flavour.

Health Benefits

  • Tamarind is exceptionally high in iron.
  • Contains B vitamins.
  • The tartaric acid aids digestion.
  • Very refreshing in heat.

Cultural Note

Tamarind juice is the drink of Zanzibar's warmest hours — sold by vendors who have been filling clay pots from the same tamarind trees for generations. To drink it cold at the Forodhani waterfront is to cool down in the most Zanzibari way possible.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days without ice.

Reheating: Not applicable — serve cold.

Serve with: Zanzibar Mix (TZA124), Bhajia (TZA133), Any street food
