🥟 Bur

Bur

Somalia's own filled fried pastry, its half-moon shape and thicker, more pliable dough setting it apart from the crisp, sharp-cornered sambuusa, typically filled with spiced meat or vegetables.

Bur and sambuusa are often confused by outsiders, but Somali cooks distinguish them clearly - bur uses a softer, more bread-like dough folded into a half-moon rather than sambuusa's thin, crisp pastry folded into a tight triangle, giving bur a chewier, more substantial bite.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Deep frying pan

  • 400 g (3 cups) all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp active dry yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 220 ml (scant 1 cup) warm water
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, plus more for frying
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) cooked, spiced ground meat filling (as for Sambuusa)

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast and sugar in the warm water and let sit for 10 minutes.
  2. Combine the flour and salt, then add the yeast mixture and oil, kneading into a smooth, soft dough.
  3. Let rise, covered, for 30 minutes.
  4. Divide the dough into balls and roll each out into a round about 12cm across.
  5. Place a spoonful of the meat filling on one half of each round.
  6. Fold over into a half-moon shape, pressing the edges firmly to seal.
  7. Heat vegetable oil to 175C (345F).
  8. Fry the bur in batches for 3-4 minutes per side, until deeply golden.
  9. Drain on paper towels and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Bur dough is softer and more yeasted than sambuusa thin pastry wrapper, giving it a distinctly chewier bite.
  • Seal the edges firmly, crimping if needed, to prevent the filling from leaking during frying.
  • These are best served warm, shortly after frying.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rolling the dough too thin, losing bur characteristic softer, more substantial texture.
  • Not sealing the edges well enough, allowing the filling to escape into the oil.
  • Overfilling, which makes the pastries difficult to seal properly.

Cultural Note

Bur is a distinct cousin to sambuusa in Somali cooking, its softer, yeasted dough giving it a different character prized in its own right rather than as a simple variation.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; refrigerate leftovers for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Reheat in a hot oven to help re-crisp.

Serve with: Basbaas (hot sauce), Shaah Cadays
