🍛 Jollof Rice with Black-Eyed Peas

Jollof — Oldways

Brown rice cooked in tomato liquid while onion, garlic, carrot, cabbage, tomato paste and black-eyed peas simmer with turmeric and thyme, then mixed together.

An American whole-grain adaptation from the Oldways Africa Heritage Diet: the liquid drained from a can of diced tomatoes plus water makes 4 cups of cooking liquid for 2 cups of brown rice, simmered 30 to 35 minutes. Meanwhile onion and garlic soften in olive oil about 5 minutes, carrot, cabbage, tomato paste, the tomatoes, black-eyed peas, turmeric, thyme and red pepper flakes simmer under a lid until the vegetables are done, and the rice is mixed through or served under it. Oldways describes jollof as the basis of Senegal’s national dish, a meal of red rice and fish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium pot with lid, Large pan with lid, Measuring cup

  • 1 (15-ounce) can diced tomatoes, drained (save the liquid)
  • 4 cups liquid total (tomato liquid plus water)
  • 2 cups uncooked brown rice
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 large onion, chopped (about 2 cups)
  • 2–3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 large carrot, chopped (about 1 cup)
  • 1/4 head green cabbage, chopped (about 2 cups)
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 (15-ounce) can black-eyed peas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp thyme
  • 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes
  • Parsley, for garnish

Method

  1. Drain the liquid from the diced tomatoes into a measuring cup and add enough water to make 4 cups. Put it in a medium pot with the brown rice, bring to a boil, cover and simmer until the rice is tender to your taste — about 30 to 35 minutes.
  2. While the rice cooks, heat the oil in a large pan. Cook the onions and garlic until the onion is soft and pale, about 5 minutes.
  3. Add the carrots, cabbage, tomato paste, tomatoes, black-eyed peas and spices. Simmer with the lid on for a few minutes on low heat until the vegetables are done to your taste. Adjust the spices.
  4. When the rice is done, mix it with the vegetables and beans, or serve the rice with everything on top. Garnish with parsley.

Chef Tips

  • Two of the page’s three cues are preference statements — ‘tender to your taste’ and ‘done to your taste’ — so taste the rice.
  • ‘Soft and pale’ is the only observable cue, for the onion.
  • Save the canned tomato liquid; it replaces part of the water.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Draining the tomatoes and discarding the liquid, leaving the rice under-hydrated.

Cultural Note

An adaptation, labelled so by its source: Oldways attributes the Wolof Empire origin of the name ‘jollof’ and says jollof is the basis of Senegal’s thiebou dienn, ‘a meal of red rice and fish’. Brown rice, turmeric and canned beans make it a diaspora kitchen version, not a Senegalese one.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam with a splash of water.

Serve with: Parsley, Grilled fish (not in the source)
