🥥 Coconut Cream and Milk

Coconut cream and milk, and chilled coconut water

One coconut yields about two thirds of a cup of water to drink, then hot water blended with the meat gives milk — squeeze until as much liquid is removed as possible.

The component the coconut rice assumes, and a Ghanaian kitchen skill in its own right. The three eyes of the coconut are punctured with a hammer and an ice pick and the water drained through a strainer — about two thirds of a cup, which is a drink rather than an ingredient. That reserved water plus about half a cup more is heated until nearly boiling, then blended with the coconut meat in batches for about ten minutes in total, strained through cheesecloth and squeezed until as much of the liquid is removed as possible. Repeating with fresh water gives a lighter second milk. Left in the fridge, the cream rises to the top.

Ingredients

Equipment: Hammer and an ice pick, screwdriver or clean nail, Knife and a vegetable peeler, Blender, Strainer and cheesecloth, Small pan

  • 1 fresh coconut
  • The reserved coconut water — about 2/3 cup
  • About 1/2 cup water, plus more for a second lighter milk

Nothing else. This is an extraction, not a recipe with seasonings.

    Method

    1. Locate the three eyes at one end of the coconut and puncture all three with a hammer and an ice pick.
    2. Invert the coconut over a strainer set on a cup or bowl and drain the coconut water, shaking to get it all out. One coconut yields about 2/3 cup.

    Chill that water and drink it. That is the first recipe.

    1. Strike the coconut with the hammer until it breaks into pieces.
    2. Pry the white meat away from the hard brown shell with a knife.
    3. Remove the thin brown layer with a knife or vegetable peeler so only white pieces remain.
    4. Heat the reserved coconut water plus about 1/2 cup of water until nearly boiling.
    5. Blend the meat with the hot water in batches, for about 10 minutes in total.
    6. Strain through cheesecloth and squeeze it until as much of the liquid is removed as possible.

    For a lighter second milk, repeat with fresh water over the same chaff.

    1. Refrigerate. The cream rises to the top and can be spooned off.
    2. Freeze the leftover chaff for another use.

    Chef Tips

    • All three eyes, not one. Puncturing one gives you a vacuum and a mess; three lets it pour.
    • Drain the water through a strainer and drink it chilled. It is not an ingredient — one coconut gives about two thirds of a cup and that is the whole point of the first recipe.
    • Hot water, nearly boiling, not cold. Heat is what releases the fat from the meat and it roughly doubles your yield.
    • Ten minutes of blending in total, in batches. That is far longer than instinct suggests and it is where the richness comes from.
    • Squeeze until as much liquid as possible is out. The cheesecloth twist is not optional if you want cream.
    • The cream rises in the fridge, so make it the day before and spoon the cream off the top. That is what the coconut rice calls for separately.
    • A second pass with fresh water over the same chaff gives a thin milk — fine for cooking rice, not for cream.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Puncturing one eye instead of three.
    • Discarding the coconut water.
    • Blending with cold water.
    • Stopping at a couple of minutes of blending.
    • Not squeezing the cheesecloth.

    Health Benefits

    • Coconut water is naturally high in potassium and a genuine electrolyte drink.
    • Fresh coconut milk has no added gums, stabilisers or sulphites.
    • Medium-chain fats are metabolised differently from long-chain.
    • Vegan and dairy-free.

    Cultural Note

    Coconut sellers along the Ghanaian coast machete the top off and hand it over to drink, then split it so you can scrape out the jelly — the water first, the meat second, which is exactly the order these two recipes follow. Making your own milk is a Saturday job, and no tin tastes like it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 3 days refrigerated; the cream rises and can be spooned off. It freezes well, as does the chaff.

    Reheating: Warm gently. Boiling splits it.

    Serve with: Coconut Rice, Mpotompoto, Groundnut Toffee
