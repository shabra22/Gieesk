🫘 Kelewele with Calabash Nutmeg

Kelewele

A tablespoon of black peppercorns and ten cloves alongside calabash nutmeg and aniseed — and her own flag is that the spices will not stick.

The most aggressively spiced documented kelewele: two large ripened plantains against one calabash nutmeg or six allspice pods, ten cloves, a tablespoon of black peppercorns, a teaspoon of aniseed, thirty grams of ginger, an onion and a habanero or five dried red chillies. Black peppercorns in kelewele appear in no other source. Her honest note is the problem the dish actually has — difficulty getting the spices to stick to the ripened plantain when it fries — and she says the video covers it. The method is video-only, so the steps here are reconstructed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or mortar, Deep pan, Slotted spoon, Paper towels

  • 2 large ripened plantains
  • 1 medium onion
  • 30 g fresh ginger
  • 1 tsp aniseed
  • 1 calabash nutmeg, or 6 allspice pods
  • 10 cloves
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorns
  • 1 habanero chilli, or 5 dried red chillies
  • Salt, to taste
  • Oil, for frying

Method

    The published method for this recipe is video-only. No written steps exist in the source.

    1. Grind the aniseed, calabash nutmeg, cloves and black peppercorns.
    2. Blend the onion, ginger and chilli with a little water.
    3. Combine the ground spices with the blended aromatics and salt.
    4. Cube the plantains and coat them with the spice mixture.
    5. Rest briefly so the coating adheres.
    6. Fry in hot oil in batches until deep golden and caramelised at the edges.
    7. Drain and serve warm.

    The order above is inferred from the other documented kelewele, not from this source.

      Chef Tips

      • Her own flag is the real problem with kelewele and worth knowing before you start: it is difficult to get the spices to stick to ripened plantain when it fries. Resting the coated cubes and keeping the marinade thick rather than watery both help.
      • A tablespoon of black peppercorns is unique to this version among all documented kelewele. It gives a sharper, less sweet result.
      • Calabash nutmeg — ehuru — is not true nutmeg and tastes nothing like it. Six allspice pods is her stated alternative, and that is a real substitution rather than an equivalent.
      • Ten cloves for two plantains is a very high ratio. If that sounds like a lot, it is, and it is printed as such.
      • Grind the whole spices yourself. Pre-ground loses the volatile oils that make the difference here.
      • Two plantains makes this the smallest documented batch, which suits the heavy spicing — scale the plantain up and the spices will overwhelm.
      • The method is reconstructed from the other documented kelewele. Nothing in the step order is this author's.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Treating the reconstructed step order as the author's own.
      • Using true nutmeg in place of calabash nutmeg.
      • A watery marinade, which will not cling.
      • Scaling the plantain up without scaling the spices.
      • Frying before the coating has had a moment to adhere.

      • Ripe plantain supplies potassium, vitamin A and vitamin B6.

      • Vegan and gluten-free.

      Cultural Note

      Spicing kelewele this heavily — peppercorns, ten cloves, calabash nutmeg — pushes it toward the aromatic end of Ghanaian cooking that the Volta Region favours generally, the same instinct that puts selim pods and dawadawa into palmnut soup. The street version in Accra is far plainer.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Eat at once.

      Reheating: A hot oven briefly.

      Serve with: Roasted groundnuts, Red Red, Bambara nuts
