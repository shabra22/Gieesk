🥩 Seswaa

Seswaa — loswao

Beef on the bone simmered four hours in nothing but salted water, then pounded with a wooden spoon until it flakes into fine fibres — three ingredients, and the technique is the entire recipe.

Seswaa is the oldest kind of cooking there is: meat, water, salt, time and a stick. It is called seswaa in northern Botswana and loswao in southern Botswana and the western parts of South Africa, so a Setswana speaker in the North West is as likely to say loswao. The pounding is not a garnish on the method — it is the method, because crushing the meat works its own fat back through the fibres, which is where the soft, rich texture comes from. It is cooked in a large pot for weddings and for funerals.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy stewing pot, or a cast-iron three-legged pot, A large wooden spoon, a forked stick, or a mortar and pestle, Kitchen shears (optional)

  • 2 to 3 kg lean beef on the bone (shin, chuck or similar) — goat or mutton also work
  • 1 1/2 tsp salt, or to taste
  • Water, to cover

Optional

  • A pinch of chilli powder

Method

  1. Cut the meat into small pieces, about 4 cm, keeping it on the bone. Put it in a heavy pot and cover with water.
  2. Add the salt, and the chilli powder if you are using it.
  3. Boil over a low heat for about 4 hours, stirring occasionally, until the meat is completely tender and comes away from the bone easily.
  4. Extract the bone marrow and set it aside.
  5. When the pot has boiled itself almost dry, add the reserved marrow and lightly fry the meat in it.
  6. Pound the meat in the pot with a large wooden spoon or a forked stick, removing every bone carefully as you go.
  7. Keep pounding until the meat flakes into fine fibres. Shears can be used to refine the fibres further.
  8. Check the salt and serve hot with phaleche or bogobe.

Chef Tips

  • Do not add onion, garlic, bay or stock. The two Botswana-published versions are meat, salt and water only, and the whole character of the dish is that it tastes of beef rather than of seasoning.
  • The marrow is the fat that makes it. Fish it out, keep it, and fry the meat in it once the water has gone.
  • Pound, do not shred. Shredding with forks pulls the fibres apart; pounding crushes the fat through them, which is a different result entirely.
  • No source gives a pounding time — the endpoint is the instruction. Stop when it flakes into fine fibres rather than when a clock says so.
  • The four hours is not negotiable on a low heat. This is a big-pot dish and it does not scale down in time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding aromatics and turning it into an ordinary stew.
  • Discarding the bone marrow.
  • Shredding it with forks instead of pounding.
  • Rushing the simmer, which leaves fibres that will not break down however hard you pound.
  • Missing a bone. Go through it twice.

Health Benefits

  • Very high in protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
  • No added fat at all — everything in it came out of the bone.
  • Naturally free of gluten, dairy and carbohydrate.

Cultural Note

Seswaa is Botswana's national dish and it crosses the border with the language: Setswana is spoken on both sides of it, and the North West province cooks the same food under the name loswao. It belongs to occasions rather than to weeknights — it is cooked in quantity, by men, at weddings and at funerals, and there is no small version of it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Freezes well for 3 months.

Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of water. It dries out on high heat.

Serve with: Phaleche or stiff maize porridge, Morogo wa Dinawa, Boiled butternut
