🌰 Sellou (Sfouf, Toasted Flour and Almond Energy Mix)

Sellou / sfouf / zmita (سلو - سفوف)

A dense, nutty mix of toasted flour, sesame, fried almonds, anise and cinnamon bound with clarified butter, eaten by the spoonful at fast-breaking.

7hTotal time

Sellou is a labour of several days: almonds blanched and dried, sesame toasted, flour browned, butter clarified. The author learned this Fez variation from her mother-in-law. The ground sesame and almonds are rubbed with the sifted flour, sugar and spices for at least 10 minutes, then clarified butter is gradually kneaded in until the mixture is glistening, stiff but pliable. The author adds that it is Moroccan tradition to enjoy it when breaking the fast.

Ingredients

Equipment: Oven, Large baking trays, Food processor or grinder, Very large bowl, Fine sieve

  • 2.2 lbs whole raw almonds, or a little more
  • 2.2 lbs unhulled sesame seeds, or a little more
  • 2.2 lbs all purpose flour
  • 2 1/4 cups powdered sugar, or to taste
  • 4 tbsp ground cinnamon, or to taste
  • 2 tbsp ground anise, or to taste
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp mastic gum (several drops, ground to a powder; optional)
  • 1 lb unsalted butter, approx.
  • Vegetable oil, for frying almonds

Method

  1. Several days ahead: blanch the almonds 1 to 2 minutes, drain and peel. Dry them on a towel several hours or overnight until completely dry.
  2. Toast the sesame at 400°F (200°C) for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring, until the seeds are slightly darker, crunchy and nutty in aroma.
  3. Brown the flour at 400°F for 30 minutes or longer, stirring about every 5 minutes, until evenly coloured light to medium golden brown. Do not burn. Sift it several times.
  4. Clarify the butter by melting it until the milk solids separate to the bottom and foam forms on top; refrigerate overnight.
  5. Fry the almonds in about 1/2 inch of oil over medium heat for 5 to 10 minutes until light to medium golden brown.
  6. Sift the browned flour, sugar, spices, mastic and salt into a very large bowl. Grind the sesame to a near paste and the almonds half to a paste and half to a powder, and add.
  7. Rub and toss for at least 10 minutes. Gradually knead in the clarified butter until the mixture is glistening, stiff but pliable. Use less butter for a powdery sellou.
  8. Cool for several hours before covering.

Chef Tips

  • Cues quoted from the source: sesame darker, crunchy and nutty; flour light to medium golden, do not burn; almonds golden; final mix glistening, stiff but pliable.
  • The author calls zmita the healthier honey-and-olive-oil version; no recipe is given.
  • Browned flour burns quickly at the end; stir often.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burning the flour.
  • Not drying the almonds completely.
  • Adding all the butter at once.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

The author writes that it is Moroccan tradition to enjoy this nutty, energizing treat when breaking the fast, and that this is a Fez variation taught by her mother-in-law.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps for several weeks in an airtight container (the source gives no shelf-life figure).

Reheating: Not reheated.

Serve with: Mint tea
