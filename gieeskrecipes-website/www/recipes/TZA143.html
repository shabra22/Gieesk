🥤 Tanzanian Tamarind Drink

Sherbet ya Ukwaju

Tanzanian Sherbet ya Ukwaju — chilled tamarind water sweetened with sugar, lightly spiced with ginger and cardamom, and garnished with fresh mint. Zanzibar's most refreshing drink — the sweet-sour-spiced cooling beverage that accompanies every street food meal.

Tamarind drink is the beverage of the Zanzibar street food scene — sour, sweet, slightly spiced, and deeply refreshing in the coastal heat. It is the essential companion to urojo, mishkaki, and bhajia at the Forodhani Gardens.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pitcher, Whisk or spoon

  • 4 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 4 cups cold water
  • 3 tbsp sugar (or to taste)
  • ½ tsp ground ginger
  • Pinch of ground cardamom
  • Fresh mint leaves
  • Ice

Method

  1. Dissolve tamarind paste in 1 cup warm water.
  2. Strain through a sieve if using block tamarind.
  3. Add remaining cold water, sugar, ginger, and cardamom.
  4. Stir until sugar dissolves.
  5. Taste — adjust sweet-sour balance.
  6. Serve over ice with fresh mint.

Chef Tips

  • The sweet-sour balance is personal — start with less sugar and add.
  • Fresh ginger grated instead of ground is more vibrant.
  • Very cold is essential.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-sweetening — tamarind's sourness should be present.
  • Not cold enough.

The Science

Tamarind contains tartaric acid — one of the strongest natural food acids. At the concentrations used in sherbet, it stimulates salivation more effectively than other sour agents, creating the refreshing sensation characteristic of tamarind drinks.

Cultural Note

Tamarind drink is the flavour of Zanzibar's evenings — sipped at the Forodhani Gardens as the sun sets over the Indian Ocean, it is one of the island's most evocative sensory experiences.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Not applicable — serve cold.

Serve with: Urojo (TZA123), Zanzibar Mix (TZA124), Any street food
