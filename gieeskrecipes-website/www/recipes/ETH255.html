🫘 Somali (Ethiopia) Cambuulo

ካምቡሎ — Cambuulo

Ethiopian Somali Cambuulo — adzuki beans slow-cooked until very soft, mixed with sesame oil and honey. The beloved Somali sweet-savoury bean dish eaten by Ethiopia's Somali communities — simultaneously a main dish and a dessert.

Cambuulo is one of the most distinctive dishes of Somali food culture — the small adzuki beans cooked until completely tender, then dressed with sesame oil and honey. It is eaten both as a main dish (during Ramadan) and as a sweet at celebrations.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 2 cups dried adzuki beans — soaked overnight
  • 3 tbsp sesame oil
  • 3 tbsp honey
  • ¼ tsp cardamom
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Cook soaked adzuki beans 40—45 minutes until very tender.
  2. Drain. While still warm, dress with sesame oil, honey, and cardamom.
  3. Season with salt.
  4. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • The beans must be very soft — almost melting.
  • Dress while warm — absorbs the oil and honey better.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-cooking — must be very tender.

The Science

Adzuki beans contain high concentrations of molybdenum (crucial for enzyme function) and are among the most digestible legumes.

Cultural Note

Cambuulo is the sweet that the Somali diaspora carries with them everywhere — one of the most beloved comfort foods of Somali culture, appearing wherever Somali communities gather.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: At Ramadan, At Ethiopian Somali celebrations
