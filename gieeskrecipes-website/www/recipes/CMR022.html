🫘 Koki (Gâteau de cornille)

Koki / gâteau de cornille

White beans soaked overnight, skinned, crushed with chilli and mixed with palm oil and water to a smooth, fairly liquid batter, tied in banana leaves and cooked 4 hours in a leaf-lined pot.

The Afrik printing calls koki a ‘gâteau de cornille’ of Cameroonian origin from the Mbo culture (known as moin-moin in Nigeria). The white beans are soaked the evening before, then rubbed against each other next day to remove the white skin. A thick-bottomed pot is lined with banana leaves (central rib removed) and 1 litre of water. The palm oil is warmed until lukewarm; the beans are crushed with the chilli, mixed with half the oil and, once absorbed, the rest, and salted. Half the water is added gradually, mixing 5 minutes, then the rest, until the paste is smooth and fairly liquid. The batter goes into banana leaves (or foil), tied tightly with string, and cooks 4 hours in the lined pot. Served hot.

Ingredients

Equipment: Thick-bottomed pot, Blender or mortar

  • 500 g koki (white beans / haricot blanc)
  • 2 chillies
  • 1/2 litre water (printed; the method also uses 1 litre in the pot)
  • 30 cl palm oil
  • Banana leaves (or aluminium foil)
  • Kitchen string
  • Salt — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Soak the beans the evening before in plenty of water. Next day rub them together to remove the white skin.
  2. Line a thick-bottomed pot with banana leaves, central rib removed, and add 1 litre of water. Heat.
  3. Warm the palm oil until lukewarm.
  4. Crush the beans with the chillies. Mix with half the oil; when absorbed, add the rest and salt.
  5. Add half the water gradually, mixing 5 minutes, then the rest. The paste should be smooth and fairly liquid.
  6. Pour into banana leaves or foil and tie tightly with string. Cook 4 hours in the lined pot. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • The printed cue for the batter is ‘pâte lisse et assez liquide’ (smooth and fairly liquid).
  • Tie the parcels tightly.
  • There is no printed doneness cue; the page gives only the 4-hour time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the skins on the beans.
  • Adding all the water at once.
  • Letting the pot dry out over the 4 hours.

Cultural Note

The page says the dish is of Cameroonian origin from the Mbo culture and calls it the same as Nigerian moin-moin. In this printing ‘koki’ names the beans themselves.
