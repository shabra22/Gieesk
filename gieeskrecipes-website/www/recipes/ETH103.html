🫘 Ethiopian Yellow Split Pea Alicha

ቅቅ አልጫ — Kik Alicha

Ethiopian Kik Alicha — yellow split peas simmered until completely soft in a golden sauce of onion, turmeric, garlic, and ginger without berbere. The mild, golden-yellow counterpart to Misir Wat on the fasting platter — gentle, creamy, and deeply nourishing.

Kik Alicha is the mild partner to Misir Wat — the same position that Alicha Wat holds to Key Wat in the meat world. Yellow split peas cooked with turmeric and onion (no berbere) produce a golden, mild stew that provides balance on the fasting platter. Its gentle flavour makes it the most accessible Ethiopian dish for those new to the cuisine.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot

  • 2 cups yellow split peas — washed
  • 3 onions — finely diced
  • 4 tbsp oil
  • 1.5 tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp ground ginger
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 3 cups water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Cook onion in oil until very golden — 10 minutes.
  2. Add turmeric and ground ginger. Stir 1 minute.
  3. Add garlic and fresh ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add washed split peas. Stir to coat.
  5. Add water. Bring to a boil.
  6. Reduce heat. Simmer covered 40—45 minutes until completely soft.
  7. Stir occasionally — peas will partially dissolve.
  8. Season. The stew should be thick and golden.

Chef Tips

  • No berbere — the gentleness is the point.
  • The peas should be very soft — nearly dissolving.
  • Turmeric is generous here — the golden colour is important.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding berbere.
  • Under-cooking — peas must be very soft.

The Science

Yellow split peas (dehulled and split yellow field peas) cook faster than whole peas because the hull has been removed — the cotyledons absorb water directly. The turmeric's curcumin dissolves into the oil, producing the characteristic golden colour throughout the stew.

Cultural Note

Kik Alicha is the gentle welcome of Ethiopian fasting cuisine — the dish that even the most heat-sensitive guest can eat with joy. Its golden colour and mild flavour make the fasting platter complete by providing the essential contrast to the red berbere wats.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently with added water.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Alongside Misir Wat and Shiro on the fasting platter, With Gomen (ETH106)
