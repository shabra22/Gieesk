The Ethiopian drinks guide assembles the full spectrum — from the 2,000-year-old coffee ceremony to the modern Addis Ababa juice bar layered drink, from the ancient tej to the everyday lemon-ginger. Together they tell the complete story of what Ethiopia drinks.

Ingredients

Equipment: Jebena for coffee, Fermentation vessels for tej and tella, Blender for juices

    Assemble the full Ethiopian drinks experience:

    • Coffee Ceremony — the centrepiece
    • Spiced Tea — for non-coffee drinkers
    • Tej — the ancient honey wine
    • Tella — the grain beer
    • Multi-Layer Juice — the modern visual
    • Lemon Ginger — the health drink
    • Hibiscus Tea — the ruby herbal
    • Warm Spiced Milk — for children and non-alcohol drinkers

    Method

    1. Days before: begin tej fermentation. Begin tella brewing.
    2. Day of: roast coffee beans for the ceremony.
    3. Prepare all non-alcoholic drinks to order.
    4. The coffee ceremony is the anchor — everything else radiates from it.
    5. Serve in sequence: start with coffee ceremony, then branching to individual preference.

    Chef Tips

    • The coffee ceremony should last 1—2 hours — plan accordingly.
    • Tej and tella take days to prepare — plan far ahead.
    • The juice bar drinks are made to order and immediate.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not starting tej and tella days ahead.

    Cultural Note

    Ethiopia's drinks tell the country's complete food story — the birthplace of coffee, the ancient honey wine, the grain beer of the highlands, the modern juice bar culture of Addis Ababa, and the healing lemon-ginger of grandmothers. From the first cup of the coffee ceremony to the last layered juice at the bar, Ethiopia's beverages are as extraordinary as its food.
