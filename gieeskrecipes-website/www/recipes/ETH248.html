🥔 Gamo Amicho

አሚቾ — Amicho

Gamo Amicho — boiled yam or false yam (Dioscorea) dressed with butter, berbere, and salt. The staple food of the Gamo people around Arba Minch and the Rift Valley lakes — the root vegetable tradition that complements the fish of the lake region.

Amicho is the Gamo alternative to injera — boiled and dressed with the simplest of preparations, the false yam or true yam provides the starchy base that accompanies fish from the nearby Rift Valley lakes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 800g yam (or sweet potato as substitute) — peeled, cubed
  • 2 tbsp butter or kibbeh
  • ½ tsp berbere
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Boil yam in salted water 20—25 minutes until very tender.
  2. Drain.
  3. Dress with butter or kibbeh, berbere, and salt while hot.
  4. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Dress while hot — the butter melts and absorbs into the yam.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Dressing cold yam — butter doesn't absorb.

The Science

Yam starch gelatinises during boiling. The warm yam's surface channels and pores absorb the melted butter through capillary action — producing a uniform seasoning throughout.

Cultural Note

Gamo Amicho is the lakeside staple — eaten with the fresh fish from the Rift Valley lakes that has sustained the Gamo people for centuries.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten warm and fresh.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: With Rift Valley lake fish, At Gamo community meals
