🍲 Tanzanian Beef Stew

Mchuzi wa Nyama

Mchuzi wa Nyama — Tanzania's foundational beef stew. Beef simmered low and slow with tomatoes, onions, garlic, ginger, and Tanzanian spices until the sauce is rich, thick, and the meat is falling-tender. The stew that accompanies ugali and rice at every Tanzanian table.

Mchuzi wa Nyama is the backbone of Tanzanian everyday cooking — the rich, slow-cooked beef stew that appears alongside ugali and rice at every family meal, every mama lishe lunch counter, and every celebration table. The spicing is warm rather than hot — cumin, coriander, and a touch of pilipili give it depth without fire.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot with lid

  • 800g beef — bone-in pieces
  • 2 large onions — diced
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp coriander
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 2 cups water
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander

Method

  1. Brown beef in batches in hot oil. Remove and set aside.
  2. In the same pot, cook onions until deeply golden — 8 minutes.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, and all spices. Stir 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook 8—10 minutes until completely broken down and thick.
  5. Return beef. Add water.
  6. Bring to a boil. Reduce heat. Cover and simmer 45—55 minutes until meat is very tender.
  7. Adjust seasoning. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Bone-in beef adds far more flavour to the sauce.
  • The tomato-onion base must be cooked until completely reduced before adding water.
  • Low and slow — rushing produces tough, dry meat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the simmer.
  • Adding water before the tomato base is reduced.

The Science

Bone-in beef releases collagen from the bones during slow cooking — this converts to gelatin, giving the sauce a silky, rich body impossible to achieve with boneless meat alone.

Cultural Note

Mchuzi wa Nyama is the taste of home for every Tanzanian — the stew that mothers make and children grow up eating. Its smell filling a house means a meal is coming and all is well.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: Ugali (TZA001), Wali wa Nazi (TZA002), Chapati (TZA004)
