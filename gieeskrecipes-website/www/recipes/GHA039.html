Nsiho — white kenkey

Made from dehulled maize so it comes out white — steeped forty-eight hours to pH 3.58, then a short twelve-hour dough ferment to pH 3.50.

Nsiho is white kenkey, and the whiteness comes from dehulling the maize before anything else. It is the best-quantified fermentation in the Ghanaian literature because two University of Ghana theses measured it stage by stage. Traditional steeping runs forty-eight hours, during which pH falls from 5.99 to 3.58 and titratable acidity climbs from 0.03 to 0.30 per cent. The dough then ferments only twelve hours, pH falling from 5.94 to 3.50. One thesis optimised the process at thirty to forty-five hours of steeping at thirty to thirty-five degrees followed by twelve hours of dough fermentation.

Ingredients

Equipment: Nonreactive container, for steeping, Mill or grinder, Heavy wooden paddle, Large pot and steamer

  • White maize, dehulled
  • Water, for steeping and for the dough

For wrapping

  • Maize husks or plantain leaves

Quantities are not published in any named source. See the note.

    Method

    1. Dehull the white maize — this is what makes nsiho white rather than cream.
    2. Steep the dehulled grain in water 48 hours at ambient temperature, or 30 to 45 hours at 30 to 35 degrees for the optimised process.

    STEEPING ENDPOINT: pH falls from about 5.99 to about 3.58, with titratable acidity rising from about 0.03 to 0.30 per cent.

    1. Mill the steeped grain to a dough.
    2. Ferment the dough 12 hours.

    DOUGH ENDPOINT: pH falls from about 5.94 to about 3.50, with titratable acidity rising from about 0.27 to 0.36 per cent.

    1. Cook part of the dough into a thick paste and fold the raw remainder back in.
    2. Shape, wrap, and steam or boil until cooked through.

    The finished product should be whitish, with a fruity odour and a smooth, non-sticky texture, and only mildly sour.

      Chef Tips

      • Dehulling is the whole point. Leave the hulls on and you have ordinary kenkey; take them off and you get the pale colour and the much milder flavour nsiho is wanted for.
      • Two separate ferments with very different lengths: forty-eight hours for the steep, only twelve for the dough. Most of the souring happens before the grain is even milled.
      • The target is mildly sour, not strongly so. Consumers in the published study liked a whitish colour, a fruity odour, and a smooth non-sticky texture with minimal sourness.
      • A fruity odour rather than a sharply sour one is the sensory marker — this is the gentlest of the kenkey family.
      • Thirty to forty-five hours at thirty to thirty-five degrees is the optimised steep. A warm spot shortens it; a cool kitchen lengthens it.
      • Vendor samples measured pH 4.07 to 4.54 in the finished product, which is higher — less sour — than ordinary kenkey's 3.7.
      • Published as a process. No named source gives household quantities and none have been invented.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Skipping the dehulling.
      • Giving the dough the same long ferment as the steep.
      • Fermenting to the sourness of ordinary kenkey.
      • Letting it go sticky, which the published consumer work identifies as a fault.
      • Expecting home measurements, which do not exist in print.

      Health Benefits

      • Dehulling removes some fibre but also most of the phytate, improving mineral absorption.
      • Double fermentation raises B-vitamin content.
      • Gluten-free and fat-free.
      • Milder acidity makes it easier on sensitive stomachs than ordinary kenkey.

      Cultural Note

      White kenkey is the gentler member of the family — paler, fruitier, barely sour — and it is often what gets given to children or to someone who finds ordinary kenkey too sharp. It is also, by a wide margin, the best-documented fermented food in Ghana, thanks to two University of Ghana theses that measured every stage.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Shorter-keeping than ordinary kenkey because it is less acidic. 2 to 3 days refrigerated.

      Reheating: Re-steam.

      Serve with: Shito, Fried fish, Pepper sauce
