🥣 Soupou Kandja, Okra Sauce with Dried Fish

Soupou kandja — sauce gombo

Finely chopped okra cooked low and slow with rehydrated dried fish, shrimp powder and red palm oil until viscous and coating.

1h 25mTotal time
20mPrep
45mCook
4Servings
330Calories
EasyDifficulty

A compact kissafrica version: 500 g of okra chopped fine or blended, 150 g of dried fish (Nile perch, croaker or sea bream) soaked 20 minutes in lukewarm water, and two tablespoons of dried shrimp powder. Onions and garlic are cooked translucent in three tablespoons of hot red palm oil, the fish, whole chile, shrimp powder and a little water simmer 10 minutes, and the okra goes in with a bouillon cube for 20 to 25 minutes on low heat, until a viscous, coating texture is reached. It is served hot with fragrant white rice, and the page suggests crab or other seafood for a richer sauce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Knife or blender for the okra

  • 500 g fresh okra (or frozen)
  • 150 g dried fish (Nile perch, croaker, or African sea bream)
  • 2 tbsp dried shrimp powder (or whole dried shrimp)
  • 2 onions
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 3 tbsp red palm oil
  • 1 whole chile (bird’s eye or Scotch bonnet)
  • 1 bouillon cube
  • Salt and pepper — quantities not printed
  • White rice, to serve

Method

  1. Wash the okra, trim the ends, then chop very finely or blend. Set aside.
  2. Soak the dried fish pieces in lukewarm water for 20 minutes. Drain and set aside.
  3. Sauté the onions and garlic in hot palm oil until translucent.
  4. Add the fish pieces, whole chile, shrimp powder and a little water. Simmer 10 minutes.
  5. Stir in the chopped okra, bouillon cube, salt and pepper. Cook over low heat 20 to 25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until a viscous, coating texture is achieved.
  6. Serve hot with white rice.

Chef Tips

  • The printed cue is texture: ‘a viscous and coating texture’ — it should coat a spoon.
  • Soak the dried fish in lukewarm water, not hot; hot water cooks the outside before the inside softens.
  • Cook on low heat; fast boiling splits the okra and palm oil.
  • Page tip: swap the fish for smoked tofu and add more shrimp powder, or use vegetable broth, for a lighter version.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling instead of simmering.
  • Skipping the soak for the dried fish.
  • Adding salt before tasting the stock cube and dried fish.

Cultural Note

The page calls it ‘an iconic Senegalese dish’ and is English-only; no French or Wolof wording is printed. It is a clean illustration of the structure of a kandia — okra, palm oil, dried fish — without the multiple fermented seasonings of the Casamance versions.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with a little water.

Serve with: Steamed white rice, Crab or other seafood (page suggestion)
