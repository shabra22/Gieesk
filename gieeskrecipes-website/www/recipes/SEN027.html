🦐 Okra and Seafood Stew with Fish Sauce

Soupoukandia

Okra simmered in fish stock until it falls apart, enriched with palm oil and fish sauce, and finished with mussels and shrimp in the last four minutes.

2h 30mTotal time
25mPrep
2h 5mCook
7Servings
250Calories
MediumDifficulty

Where the Afrik soupou kandia leans on dried and fermented seafood, the Saveur one swaps in commercial fish sauce for every fermented ingredient and builds a clean, silky okra broth. Twelve cups of fish or vegetable stock are cooked with sliced okra, bay and onion about an hour and a half, until the okra is very tender and the stock has reduced by a quarter. Palm oil, fish sauce, aubergine and halved chiles go in for another 30 minutes, until the soup has thickened and the okra falls apart. Mussels and shrimp cook covered for about four minutes, until the mussels open, and the rest of the fish sauce is stirred in off the heat.

Ingredients

Equipment: 6-quart saucepan or pot, Slotted spoon

    Vegetable broth

    • 12 cups fish or vegetable stock
    • 4 cups thick-sliced okra
    • 3 bay leaves
    • 2 medium yellow onions, roughly chopped

    Seafood soup

    • 6 tbsp palm oil
    • 4 tbsp fish sauce, divided — 3 tbsp early, remainder off the heat
    • 1 large eggplant, cut into large chunks, or 4 small Thai eggplants
    • 2 habanero or Scotch bonnet chiles, slit in half lengthwise
    • 24 mussels, cleaned and debearded
    • 16 medium shrimp, peeled and deveined
    • Freshly ground black pepper, to taste
    • Cooked white rice or fonio, for serving

    Method

    1. Bring the stock to a boil in a 6-quart saucepan over high heat. Add the okra, bay leaves and onions, reduce to medium-low, and cook, stirring occasionally, until the okra is very tender and the stock has reduced by one-quarter — about 1 1/2 hours.
    2. Add the palm oil, 3 tablespoons of the fish sauce, the eggplant and chiles. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the soup has thickened and the okra falls apart — about 30 minutes.
    3. Add the mussels and shrimp, cover, and cook until the mussels open and the shrimp are cooked through — about 4 minutes. Discard any mussels that stay shut.
    4. Remove from the heat. Stir in the remaining fish sauce and the pepper.
    5. Ladle into bowls and serve with rice or fonio.

    Chef Tips

    • The cues are ‘stock reduced by one-quarter’ and ‘okra falls apart’; both are visible and both beat the clock.
    • Hold back part of the fish sauce and stir it in off the heat — it keeps a fresher, sharper edge.
    • Discard any mussel that has not opened by the end of the four minutes.
    • Prick or split the chiles rather than chopping them if you want the heat in the broth but not in every bite.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding mussels early so they toughen.
    • Stirring in all the fish sauce at the start.
    • Not discarding unopened mussels.

    Health Benefits

    • Okra and aubergine give soluble fibre.
    • Mussels are a strong source of B12, iron and zinc.
    • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

    Cultural Note

    The headnote calls this ‘undoubtedly a predecessor of Louisiana-style gumbo’. That is Saveur’s assertion, stated with no citation; it is recorded as a claim, not a fact. Compare Soupe Kandia, built on dried and fermented seafood, which is a materially different dish under the same name.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 days refrigerated — reheat only once.

    Reheating: Gentle heat, just to hot, so the shellfish do not toughen.

    Serve with: Steamed white rice, Fonio
