🥬 Aunt Marie’s Sauce Feuille, Leafy Beef Stew

Sauce feuille

Beef shoulder braised in tomato paste, then simmered with blanched cabbage and chopped collards (or cassava leaves) until the meat is tender and the oil rises.

Thiam’s family recipe from ‘Simply West African’: cabbage and collards are blanched in a gallon of heavily salted water, chilled in ice water, squeezed and finely chopped. Beef shoulder browns five minutes; onion cooks until translucent; garlic and ginger cook two to three minutes to a light golden colour; tomato paste cooks to a dark brick colour. Stock goes in, simmers partly covered until the oil rises to the surface, then the greens and a whole chile go in for 40 to 50 minutes more, until the meat is tender. Traditionally the leaves are moringa, cassava or sweet-potato leaves; collards are the accessible substitute.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot for blanching, Bowl of ice water, Dutch oven or heavy pot

  • 1 1/2 cups kosher salt, for the blanching water
  • 1 gallon water, for blanching
  • 1 small cabbage, cored and thinly sliced, about 4 cups
  • 1 pound collard greens or cassava leaves, stems removed
  • 2 tbsp peanut or vegetable oil
  • 2 pounds beef shoulder, cut into 2-inch cubes
  • 2 tsp fine sea salt, plus more as needed
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1/2 cup chopped yellow onion
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger
  • 1/4 cup tomato paste
  • 4 cups chicken stock or water
  • 1 habanero or Scotch bonnet chili (optional)
  • Rice or fonio, for serving — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Bring the kosher salt and 1 gallon of water to a boil over high heat. Set out a bowl of ice water. Blanch the cabbage 3 minutes, move it to the ice water, drain well and squeeze out excess water. Blanch the collards the same way — no time is printed for the greens — drain, squeeze and finely chop.
  2. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over high heat. Season the beef with 1 teaspoon sea salt and 1/2 teaspoon pepper and brown it on all sides, about 5 minutes. Add the onion and cook about 5 minutes more, until translucent.
  3. Add the garlic and ginger and cook 2 to 3 minutes, stirring constantly, until a light golden colour. Lower the heat.
  4. Stir in the tomato paste and cook about 5 minutes more, stirring often, until it is a dark brick colour.
  5. Add the stock, bring to a boil over high heat, reduce to low and simmer partly covered about 15 minutes, until the oil rises to the surface.
  6. Stir in the remaining 1 teaspoon salt, 1/2 teaspoon pepper and the whole chile if using. Add the cabbage and greens, stirring gently so the chile does not burst.
  7. Simmer 40 to 50 minutes more, until the meat is tender.
  8. Adjust the seasoning, remove the chile and set it aside for anyone who wants more heat. Serve warm with rice or fonio.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Dark brick color’ is the tomato-paste cue, and ‘until the oil rises to the surface’ is the signal to add the greens.
  • Squeeze the blanched greens hard — wet leaves dilute the stew.
  • Cassava leaves are preferred if you can get them; collards are the accessible substitute.
  • Add the whole chile gently so it doesn’t burst, and lift it out before serving.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not squeezing the blanched greens.
  • Adding greens before the oil has risen.
  • Letting the chile burst.

Cultural Note

The headnote frames the dish through co-author Lisa Katayama’s first trip to Dakar and Thiam’s Aunt Marie; he calls it ‘a true representation of his roots’. The traditional leaves are moringa, cassava or sweet-potato.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat with a splash of stock.

Serve with: Steamed rice, Fonio
