🌶️ Ewa Aganyin with Rehydrated Chillies

Ewa aganyin

A full cup of palm oil, onions browned ten to fifteen minutes, and the doneness cue nobody else gives — the pepper changing from bright red to dark red.

This version sits between the other two: a full cup of palm oil, preheated until very hot but with no bleaching instruction, and a sauce built on dried chillies and dried red bell peppers rehydrated overnight or for two hours in hot water. Its real contribution is a usable colour cue. Where one source says very dark but not burnt and another says very dry but not burnt, this one tells you what to watch: the pepper changes from bright red to dark red. The beans take two to two and a half hours and are mashed to preference rather than to collapse.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large stockpot, Potato masher or wooden spoon, Blender, Heavy frying pan

    For the beans

    • 1 lb beans
    • 6-8 cups water
    • Salt to taste

    For the sauce

    • 3/4 cup dried chilli
    • 2 dried red bell peppers
    • 1 medium onion
    • 1 tsp ginger
    • 2 tsp crayfish
    • 2 seasoning cubes
    • 1 cup palm oil
    • 1/4 cup water, for blending
    • Salt to taste

    Method

    1. Pick the dirt from the beans, then rinse and drain several times.
    2. Pour into a stockpot with the water and cook until very tender, 2 to 2 1/2 hours.
    3. Mash with a potato masher or wooden spoon to the texture you prefer.
    4. Rehydrate the dried chillies and peppers overnight, or in hot water for 2 hours.
    5. Blend the rehydrated peppers with the onions, ginger and a quarter cup of water.
    6. Preheat the palm oil until very hot.
    7. Add the sliced onion and cook 10 to 15 minutes, until browned.
    8. Stir in the blended peppers and cook 10 minutes.
    9. Add the seasoning cubes, crayfish and salt, and cook 5 to 10 minutes more, until the pepper has changed from bright red to dark red.

    Chef Tips

    • Bright red to dark red is the cue, and it is the most usable one any version gives. Watch the colour rather than the clock.
    • Ten to fifteen minutes browning the onions before anything else goes in. That is a long time and it is where the sweetness comes from.
    • Overnight is better than two hours for the dried peppers, if you can plan ahead.
    • Mash to preference here, not to collapse. This version keeps some bean texture where the Lagos street version does not.
    • Two to two and a half hours on the beans is longer than the other versions because the pot is larger and no salt management is specified.
    • A quarter cup of water in the blender and no more. You will be frying this and every extra drop has to go.
    • Nigerian honey beans — ewa oloyin — are named as the alternative to black-eyed peas, and they are sweeter and better suited to the dish.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Rushing the onion browning.
    • Blending the peppers with too much water.
    • Stopping while the pepper is still bright red.
    • Under-cooking the beans, which leaves them grainy.
    • Scaling the oil down so far that the sauce stews instead of frying.

    Cultural Note

    Three named cooks, three ewa agoyin sauces, and three different positions on the single question of how hot the palm oil should get. Taken together they map the real range of the dish: bleached and almost black at one end, warmed and red at the other, and this one in the middle with the clearest instruction for telling when you have arrived.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. Store the sauce separately.

    Reheating: Low heat, loosening the beans with a little water.

    Serve with: Agege Bread, Dodo (fried plantain), Boiled yam
