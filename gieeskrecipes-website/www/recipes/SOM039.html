🍲 Maraq Ari

Maraq Ari

Lamb or goat browned with ground fenugreek, xawaash and turmeric, then stewed down in blended tomato with potato and carrot added late so they still hold their shape.

Maraq is the broad Somali word for a stew or soup, and maraq ari is the version that arrives with anjero in front of it. Somali cooks associate it with the Gu, the long rains from April to June, when the herds are fat and there is meat to spare. The fenugreek is the note that makes it taste Somali rather than generic, and the oil is not stinginess-proof: it is what keeps the stew from catching and comes to the top at the end where you can lift it off.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a lid, Blender, Spice grinder, for the fenugreek

  • 80 ml (1/3 cup) canola or vegetable oil
  • 1 large onion, sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 2 tsp fenugreek seeds, ground
  • 1 tbsp xawaash spice blend
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 680 g (1 1/2 lb) lamb or goat, on the bone
  • 800 g (4 large) tomatoes, blended
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tsp salt, to taste
  • 350 g (2 medium) potatoes, cut small
  • 230 g (2 medium) carrots, sliced
  • 1 green pepper, chopped
  • 1 tbsp green chilli sauce, or to taste
  • 80 g (1/2 cup) fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 1/2 tsp ground black pepper

Method

  1. Sauté the sliced onion in the oil over medium heat for 3 minutes, add the garlic and fry for a minute, then stir in the grated ginger.
  2. Add the ground fenugreek, the xawaash and the turmeric, mixing well after each.
  3. Add the meat immediately, before the spices can catch, and brown it for 3 minutes.
  4. Pour in the blended tomatoes and mix.
  5. Stir in the tomato paste and the salt, cover, and cook for 30 minutes.
  6. Add the potatoes and carrots, cover, and cook for 20 minutes.
  7. Add the green pepper and cook for a further 10 minutes.
  8. Stir in the chilli sauce and the cilantro.
  9. Finish with the black pepper and serve hot with anjero.

Chef Tips

  • Cut the potatoes small. Tomato is acidic and potatoes soften slowly in it — large cubes will still be firm when the meat is falling apart.
  • Do not reduce the oil. It fries the stew rather than boiling it, stops the base catching, and separates out on top at the end where it is easy to skim.
  • The order is the recipe. Meat first and long, root vegetables in the middle, peppers at the end — so the meat is tender and the vegetables still have shape.
  • Ground fenugreek is what people cannot place when they taste a Somali stew for the first time. It is worth grinding your own; the pre-ground kind fades quickly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the spices and then leaving them in the hot oil while you fetch the meat — they burn in seconds and the whole pot turns bitter.
  • Putting the potatoes in with the meat at the start, so they disintegrate into the sauce.
  • Cutting back the oil, after which the stew sticks and scorches on the bottom.
  • Using boneless meat. The bone is where the body of the maraq comes from.

Health Benefits

  • Lamb and goat provide protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
  • Goat is notably leaner than lamb or beef.
  • Potato, carrot and pepper make it a one-pot meal with vegetables built in.

Cultural Note

Somalis tie maraq ari to the Gu, the main rainy season between April and June, when pasture is good and the herds can spare an animal. It is served with anjero, soor, rice, sabaayad, bread or pasta — which is to say with whatever the household has, because the stew is the fixed point and the starch is not.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Better on the second day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the hob, adding a little water if it has thickened.

Serve with: Canjeero, Soor iyo Maraq, Sabaayad
