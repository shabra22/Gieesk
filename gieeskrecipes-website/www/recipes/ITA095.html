🐟 Baccalà alla Vicentina

Baccalà alla Vicentina

Dried salt cod, soaked for days to remove its salt, then simmered for hours in milk, onion, and anchovies until it breaks down into a rich, creamy stew unique to Vicenza.

This dish, technically made with stockfish (air-dried, unsalted cod) rather than true salted baccalà despite the name, requires patience above all — a long soak to rehydrate and a very slow simmer that gradually breaks the fish down until it nearly melts into the milk-based sauce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl for soaking, Heavy-bottomed pot with lid

  • 800 g (1.75 lb) dried stockfish or salt cod
  • 2 onions, thinly sliced
  • 4 anchovy fillets
  • 500 ml (2 cups) whole milk
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus more as needed
  • 40 g (¼ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Creamy polenta, for serving

Method

  1. Soak the dried stockfish or salt cod in cold water for 2–3 days, changing the water several times daily, until softened and much of the salt has been drawn out.
  2. Drain, remove any skin and bones, and flake the fish into large pieces.
  3. Warm a little olive oil in a heavy pot and sauté the onions until soft, then add the anchovies, stirring until they dissolve.
  4. Layer the flaked fish over the onions in the pot, season lightly, and pour in the milk and a generous amount of olive oil.
  5. Cover and cook over the lowest possible heat for 3–4 hours, stirring only occasionally and gently, allowing the fish to slowly break down into the sauce.
  6. Add more olive oil during cooking if the mixture looks dry, as the dish should remain rich and saucy.
  7. Stir in the Parmigiano near the end and check the seasoning.
  8. Serve hot, spooned generously over creamy polenta, finished with fresh parsley.

Chef Tips

  • This dish genuinely cannot be rushed — the long, gentle cooking is what transforms tough dried fish into something silky.
  • Stir gently and infrequently rather than vigorously, to avoid breaking the fish down too much too quickly.
  • A generous hand with olive oil throughout cooking is traditional and essential to the dish's final creamy texture.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not soaking the fish long enough, leaving it too salty or tough to properly break down.
  • Cooking over heat that's too high, which can cause the milk to scorch or separate.
  • Being too sparing with olive oil, resulting in a dry rather than lusciously rich final dish.

Cultural Note

Baccalà alla Vicentina is so closely tied to the city of Vicenza that a dedicated confraternity exists to protect and promote the traditional recipe, insisting on stockfish rather than true salted cod.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; flavor deepens further the next day.

Reheating: Reheat very gently on the stovetop over low heat, stirring occasionally.

Serve with: Creamy polenta, traditionally, A dry white wine from the Veneto
