🥧 Torta della Nonna

Torta della Nonna

'Grandmother's cake' layers a buttery shortcrust shell with silky lemon pastry cream, topped with pine nuts and a dusting of powdered sugar — a beloved Tuscan classic.

This homely, comforting tart earns its name from the sort of unfussy, deeply satisfying dessert a grandmother might have always on hand, its lemon-scented custard filling and pine-nut topping a familiar sight in Tuscan bakery windows.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Tart pan, about 24cm, Saucepan for custard, Whisk

  • 300 g (2¼ cups) 00 flour
  • 150 g (¾ cup) cold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 120 g (⅔ cup) granulated sugar
  • 2 egg yolks
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • Pinch of salt
  • 500 ml (2 cups) whole milk
  • 4 egg yolks, for the custard
  • 100 g (½ cup) granulated sugar, for the custard
  • 40 g (⅓ cup) cornstarch
  • 50 g (⅓ cup) pine nuts
  • Powdered sugar, for dusting

Method

  1. Combine the flour, cold butter, sugar, egg yolks, lemon zest, and salt, working quickly into a smooth dough. Wrap and rest in the refrigerator for 1 hour.
  2. For the custard, warm the milk in a saucepan until just below boiling.
  3. Whisk the egg yolks, sugar, and cornstarch together in a bowl until smooth.
  4. Gradually pour the warm milk into the egg mixture, whisking constantly, then return everything to the saucepan.
  5. Cook over medium heat, whisking continuously, until the custard thickens, about 5 minutes. Remove from heat and let cool, covered with plastic wrap pressed directly onto the surface.
  6. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F). Roll out two-thirds of the dough and line a tart pan, trimming the edges.
  7. Pour the cooled custard into the tart shell and spread evenly.
  8. Roll out the remaining dough and place over the custard as a top layer, sealing the edges, or leave open-faced and scatter pine nuts directly over the custard.
  9. Bake for 35–40 minutes until golden, then cool completely before dusting with powdered sugar and slicing.

Chef Tips

  • Keep the butter cold and work the pastry dough quickly to avoid it becoming tough.
  • Whisk the custard constantly while cooking to prevent lumps or scorching on the bottom of the pan.
  • This tart is often made open-faced with just a bottom crust, which lets the pine nuts toast directly and beautifully atop the custard.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overworking the pastry dough, which develops gluten and makes the crust tough rather than tender.
  • Not whisking the custard enough while it thickens, resulting in lumps.
  • Slicing the tart before it has fully cooled, causing the custard filling to run.

Cultural Note

Torta della Nonna is a fixture of Tuscan home baking and trattoria dessert menus alike, evoking the comforting simplicity its name suggests.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, covered, for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature or chilled; this dessert is not reheated.

Serve with: A cup of espresso, A glass of Vin Santo dessert wine
