🥜 Tanzanian Beef Peanut Stew

Mchuzi wa Nyama na Karanga

Mchuzi wa Nyama na Karanga — Tanzanian beef and peanut stew. Tender beef simmered in a rich sauce of ground peanuts, tomatoes, and spices. A thick, nourishing, deeply satisfying stew from central Tanzania — the peanut sauce provides protein and richness that transforms the dish.

Peanut stew is one of central Tanzania's most beloved dishes — the ground peanuts create a sauce of extraordinary richness and depth that coats the beef perfectly. It is a dish of warmth, nourishment, and deep satisfaction.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender or mortar for peanuts

  • 700g beef — cubed
  • 1 cup raw peanuts — roasted and ground (or 4 tbsp peanut butter)
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 4 tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 3 cups water
  • ¼ cup coriander

Method

  1. Roast peanuts in a dry pan until golden. Cool and grind coarsely.
  2. Brown beef. Remove.
  3. Cook onions until golden. Add garlic, ginger, spices.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook 8 minutes.
  5. Return beef. Add water. Simmer covered 40 minutes.
  6. Mix ground peanuts with ½ cup warm water to a paste. Add to pot.
  7. Simmer 15—20 more minutes until sauce is thick and richly flavoured.
  8. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Freshly roasted and ground peanuts are vastly better than commercial peanut butter.
  • Mix peanut paste with warm water first — prevents lumping.
  • The peanut sauce should be thick and coating.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding dry ground peanuts directly — lumps.
  • Commercial peanut butter substituted without adjusting salt.

The Science

Ground peanuts contain protein and fat that emulsify into the stew liquid during cooking, creating a naturally thick, rich sauce without the need for flour or starch. The peanut fat carries the spice flavours throughout the sauce.

Cultural Note

Beef peanut stew represents the culinary traditions of Tanzania's interior — where groundnuts have been grown and cooked with for centuries, long before the coast's coconut influence reached inland.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently — peanut sauce can stick.
