🥬 Njama Njama and Corn Fufu (Adamu)

Njama njama

Blanched nightshade greens (or kale or spinach) cooked with tomatoes, onion, Scotch bonnet and smoked turkey or fish in oil, served with a corn fufu.

50 minTotal time
20 minPrep
30 minCook
5Servings
310Calories
EasyDifficulty

Njama njama is the author’s name for a popular Cameroon green vegetable, especially in the North West Region (Solanum scabrum, in her notes). The greens are blanched 2–3 minutes in hot water until wilted, cooled, drained and squeezed. Onion is sautéed 2–3 minutes; tomatoes, Scotch bonnet and bouillon are added and cooked, scraping up the browned bits, with the paprika and smoked turkey or fish. After about 10 minutes the greens go in for 2–3 minutes. The page pairs it with a corn fufu.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 1 medium onion, sliced
  • 5–6 fresh tomatoes, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon cayenne pepper
  • 5 pounds nightshade greens or water cress (or 3 pounds frozen kale or spinach)
  • 1/2–1 cup canola oil or palm oil
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 1 teaspoon paprika (‘1- teaspoon’ printed)
  • 1/2 pound chopped smoked turkey or fish
  • 1 teaspoon paprika, optional (listed a second time)
  • 1 tablespoon bouillon powder or Maggi cube, optional
  • 1–2 Scotch bonnet or habanero peppers

Method

  1. Blanch the greens in hot water for 2–3 minutes until wilted. Cool in cold water, drain and squeeze.
  2. Sauté the onion about 2–3 minutes.
  3. Add the tomatoes, Scotch bonnet and bouillon and scrape up the browned bits. Add the paprika, smoked turkey or fish and crayfish if using.
  4. Cook about 10 minutes.
  5. Add the greens and cook 2–3 minutes. Adjust the seasoning. Serve with corn fufu.

Chef Tips

  • The printed cue for blanching is ‘until wilted’.
  • Squeeze the blanched greens hard before they go in.
  • A reader on the page warns that many Solanum species have toxic leaves or fruit — identify the plant.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using the wrong nightshade species.
  • Skipping the squeeze so the dish is watery.
  • Treating the doubled paprika lines as 2 teaspoons — one is marked optional.

Cultural Note

Adamu writes it is a popular green vegetable ‘especially in the North West Region’. The page prints ‘5 pounds’ of greens for 4–5 servings, which looks large; it is printed as is.

Storage & Serving

Serve with: Corn fufu, Kati kati, Achu
