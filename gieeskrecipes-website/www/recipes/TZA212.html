🍍 Tanzanian Pineapple Cardamom Dessert

Nanasi wa Iliki

Tanzanian Nanasi wa Iliki — pineapple rings caramelised in a cardamom-honey pan sauce, served warm with a dusting of cinnamon. Tanzania's most effortless hot fruit dessert — the pineapple's natural acidity caramelises magnificently in a hot pan.

Caramelised pineapple with cardamom is the fastest hot dessert in Tanzania — pineapple rings in a hot pan with honey and cardamom for 5 minutes produces a dessert of genuine quality. The pineapple's bromelain breaks down as it cooks, its natural sugars caramelise, and the cardamom perfumes everything.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 1 ripe pineapple — peeled, cored, sliced into rings
  • 2 tbsp coconut oil or butter
  • 2 tbsp honey
  • ¾ tsp ground cardamom
  • ¼ tsp ground cinnamon
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tbsp lime juice

Serve with: coconut ice cream (TZA164) or yogurt

    Method

    1. Heat coconut oil in a wide pan over medium-high heat.
    2. Add pineapple rings in a single layer.
    3. Cook without moving for 3—4 minutes until golden caramelised.
    4. Flip. Cook other side 2—3 minutes.
    5. Add honey, cardamom, cinnamon, and salt. Toss to coat.
    6. Cook 1 more minute until honey bubbles and glazes.
    7. Add lime juice. Remove from heat.
    8. Serve immediately with coconut ice cream.

    Chef Tips

    • Do not move the pineapple — contact time creates caramelisation.
    • High heat is essential.
    • Lime at the end brightens everything.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Moving pineapple too early.
    • Low heat — steams instead of caramelises.

    The Science

    Pineapple contains 12—15% natural sugars. When pan-cooked at high temperature, these sugars caramelise (above 160°C) through pyrolysis reactions — producing hundreds of flavour compounds including diacetyl and furans. The bromelain enzyme denatures above 60°C, which is why cooked pineapple is sweeter and less acidic than raw.

    Cultural Note

    Caramelised pineapple is Tanzania's quick-assembly dessert — the fruit that grows abundantly, cooked in 10 minutes into something that can be served at the finest table. It represents the confidence of Tanzania's modern food culture.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately.

    Reheating: Briefly in pan.
