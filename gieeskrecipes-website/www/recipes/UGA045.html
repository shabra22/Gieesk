🥤 Lemongrass Passion Fruit Juice

Obutunda

Ten passion fruits and ten blades of lemongrass squeezed together by hand with sugar for five minutes, until the seeds turn completely black — which is how you know the pulp is off them.

Passion fruit juice is everywhere in Uganda and this version puts lemongrass in the bowl while you work the fruit, so the herb infuses cold rather than being boiled. The doneness cue is one of the best in Ugandan cooking: you squeeze by hand for about five minutes and stop when the seeds have gone completely black, because that means all the orange pulp has come off them. No heat involved at any stage.

Ingredients

Equipment: Fairly deep bowl, Spoon, Strainer, Two pitchers

  • 10 ripe passion fruits
  • 10 blades lemongrass
  • 45 ml (3 tbsp) sugar, plus more to finish
  • Cold water, to top up

Method

  1. Cut the fruits in half and scoop the contents into a fairly deep bowl with a spoon.
  2. Wash the lemongrass and add it to the bowl.
  3. Add the 3 tablespoons of sugar and, with clean hands, mix and squeeze everything together.
  4. Keep going about five minutes, until the passion seeds are completely black.
  5. Remove the lemongrass.
  6. Strain the juice off the seeds.
  7. Add more cold water and sugar to taste.
  8. Chill, then serve.

Chef Tips

  • Black seeds are the cue. All the orange on them is pulp, and when they are clean and black you have got everything out of the fruit.
  • Squeeze by hand. A blender cracks the seeds and makes the juice bitter, which is why nobody does it that way.
  • The lemongrass goes in for the squeezing, not into boiling water. It infuses cold and stays fresh rather than stewed.
  • The first three tablespoons of sugar help draw the pulp off the seeds. The rest is sweetening at the end, to taste.
  • Cold water, preferably. It keeps the aroma, which warm water flattens.
  • It makes two pitchers from ten fruits, so it stretches further than you would expect.
  • Chill it properly before serving. Room-temperature passion juice tastes markedly more acidic.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Blending it.
  • Stopping before the seeds are black.
  • Boiling the lemongrass.
  • Topping up with warm water.
  • Serving it unchilled.

Health Benefits

  • Passion fruit is very high in vitamin C, vitamin A and fibre.
  • No cooking, so none of the vitamin C is lost to heat.
  • Lemongrass contains citral and is traditionally taken to settle the stomach.
  • Sweetened to taste, so you control the sugar entirely.

Cultural Note

Passion fruit grows readily in Uganda and fresh juice is offered at functions and in homes as a matter of course, usually in large jugs. The hand-squeezing method is universal and the black-seed test is the kind of practical knowledge that never appears in a written recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days in a covered jug.

Reheating: Not applicable — served cold.

Serve with: Rolex, Samosas, Mandazi
