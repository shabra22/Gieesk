🍆 Melanzane alla Parmigiana

Parmigiana di Melanzane

Layers of fried eggplant, tomato sauce, mozzarella, and Parmigiano baked until bubbling — one of southern Italy's most beloved comfort dishes, and surprisingly not merely 'eggplant with parmesan.'

Despite its name, parmigiana likely has nothing to do with Parma at all — the word may derive from the Sicilian 'parmiciana,' referring to the overlapping wooden slats of a shutter, which the layered eggplant slices visually resemble.

Ingredients

Equipment: Colander, Deep frying pan, Baking dish

  • 3 large eggplants, sliced lengthwise about ½cm thick
  • Salt, for drawing out moisture
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • 800 g (1.75 lb) tomato passata or crushed tomatoes
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • Fresh basil leaves
  • 400 g (14 oz) fresh mozzarella, sliced
  • 100 g (1¼ cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Salt the eggplant slices generously and let sit in a colander for 20 minutes to draw out bitterness, then pat dry.
  2. Fry the eggplant slices in batches in hot vegetable oil until golden on both sides, then drain on paper towels.
  3. Meanwhile, warm the olive oil in a saucepan, sauté the onion and garlic until softened, then add the passata and simmer for 20 minutes with torn basil, salt, and pepper.
  4. Spread a thin layer of tomato sauce over the base of a baking dish.
  5. Layer fried eggplant, more sauce, mozzarella slices, and a scattering of Parmigiano, repeating until all ingredients are used.
  6. Finish with a final layer of sauce and a generous topping of both cheeses.
  7. Bake at 190°C (375°F) for 30–35 minutes until bubbling and golden on top.
  8. Let rest for 10–15 minutes before slicing and serving — this dish holds together much better once slightly cooled.

Chef Tips

  • Salting the eggplant is essential — it removes bitterness and excess moisture that would otherwise make the dish watery.
  • Letting the baked parmigiana rest before slicing helps the layers hold together for clean portions.
  • This dish is often even better the next day, once the flavors have had time to meld overnight.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the salting step, resulting in a bitter, watery final dish.
  • Slicing immediately after baking, before the layers have had a chance to set.
  • Using a thin, bland tomato sauce that doesn't stand up to the richness of the eggplant and cheese.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Melanzane alla parmigiana is claimed by both Campania and Sicily as a regional specialty, and remains one of the most widely beloved dishes across all of southern Italy.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days; flavor improves the next day.

Reheating: Reheat covered in the oven until warmed through.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A simple green salad
