🥔 Nafaqo

Nafaqo

A whole boiled egg wrapped in spiced mashed potato and deep-fried until the shell of potato crackles — Somalia's iftar and street-corner snack, and a close cousin of the Scotch egg.

Nafaqo is what a Somali cook makes when there are eggs, potatoes and a fryer: cheap, filling and impossible to eat only one of. It belongs to the same family of fried Ramadan snacks as sambuusa and bajiye, and appears on the same iftar plate as both. The trick of it is all in the potato — dry enough to hold, seasoned enough to matter, and pressed round the egg with no seam for the oil to find.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Potato masher, Deep pan or fryer, Slotted spoon

  • 6 eggs, plus 1 extra egg for coating
  • 900 g (2 lb) floury potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 1 small onion, very finely chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tsp xawaash spice blend
  • 1 green chilli, finely chopped (optional)
  • 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
  • Oil, for deep-frying

Method

  1. Hard-boil the 6 eggs for 9 minutes, then cool them under cold running water and peel them carefully.
  2. Boil the potatoes in salted water until completely tender, then drain them very well and leave them in the hot dry pan for a minute to steam off.
  3. Mash the potatoes smooth with no butter or milk — any added liquid will make the casing collapse in the oil.
  4. Work in the onion, garlic, xawaash, chilli, cilantro, salt and pepper, then let the mash cool to room temperature. It firms up as it cools.
  5. Divide the mash into six. Flatten a portion in your palm, set an egg in the middle and bring the potato up around it, sealing it closed with no cracks.
  6. Beat the extra egg and roll each ball in it to give the surface something to crisp.
  7. Chill the shaped nafaqo for 20 minutes so they hold their shape in the oil.
  8. Heat the oil to 180C / 350F and fry two or three at a time for 3 to 4 minutes, turning, until deep golden.
  9. Drain on paper towels and serve hot with basbaas.

Chef Tips

  • Floury potatoes, drained hard and steamed dry, are the difference between a casing that holds and one that slides off in the oil.
  • Cool the mash before shaping. Warm potato is soft, sticky and will not seal.
  • Nine minutes gives a fully set yolk, which is what you want — a soft yolk breaks when the ball is shaped.
  • Fry in small batches. Crowding the pan drops the oil temperature and the casing soaks up oil instead of crisping.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding butter or milk to the mash out of habit, which makes a casing too soft to fry.
  • Leaving a seam or a crack when sealing, so hot oil gets in and the nafaqo breaks apart.
  • Frying in oil that is too cool, giving a pale, greasy shell.
  • Frying in oil that is too hot, which browns the potato before the inside is heated through.

Health Benefits

  • Eggs provide complete protein, choline and vitamin B12.
  • Potatoes contribute potassium and vitamin C.
  • Vegetarian, and satisfying enough to work as a light meal rather than only a snack.

Cultural Note

Nafaqo sits on the Ramadan iftar plate beside sambuusa and bajiye, and is sold from street stalls the rest of the year. The name shares a root with nafaqa, sustenance or provision — an apt name for a snack built to fill someone up after a day of fasting.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best straight from the fryer. Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat in a hot oven for 8 to 10 minutes to crisp the shell. A microwave makes it soggy.

Serve with: Basbaas, Sambuusa, Bajiye
