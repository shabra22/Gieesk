🥜 Miyan Taushe, Blended

Miyan taushe

The smooth version — pumpkin cooked separately with the peppers and blended, peanuts blended in at the end, and the greens off the heat immediately.

This is the other way to make miyan taushe and the difference is textural rather than a matter of ingredients. Here the pumpkin is cooked separately with pepper, tomatoes and onion and then blended smooth, which gives a velvety soup rather than the coarse-mashed one. The peanuts are blended and stirred in near the end for two minutes, the smoked fish gets three, and the chopped greens go in with the heat turned off immediately after. It serves two, and the author is candid that this was her first northern Nigerian recipe after years of blogging.

Ingredients

Equipment: Two pots, Blender, Wooden spoon

  • 5 pieces of beef
  • 1 large bulb of onion
  • 2 scotch bonnet peppers
  • Seasoning cube
  • 1/2 large pumpkin, peeled and diced
  • 2 large tomatoes
  • 2 cooking spoons of palm oil
  • Small piece of smoked fish
  • A handful of peanuts
  • 2 tbsp crayfish
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 handfuls of chopped green vegetables — green, spinach or efo tete

Method

  1. Boil the beef with onion, seasoning, salt and pepper until soft. Keep the stock.
  2. Cook the pumpkin, pepper, tomatoes and onion separately, then blend them together.
  3. Blend the peanuts.
  4. Heat the palm oil and stir-fry some onions.
  5. Add the blended pumpkin mixture and the beef stock, and simmer 5 minutes.
  6. Add the beef and season with the crayfish.
  7. Stir in the blended peanuts and simmer 2 minutes.
  8. Add the smoked fish and simmer 3 minutes.
  9. Add the chopped green vegetables and turn off the heat immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Blending gives a velvety soup. If you want texture, mash in the pot instead — that is the other documented method and it is a genuinely different dish.
  • Cook the pumpkin with the peppers and tomatoes before blending, so everything is softened together and blends smooth.
  • Peanuts blended and added at the end, two minutes only. Longer and they can catch and taste scorched.
  • Greens in, heat off, immediately. That is the instruction and it keeps them green.
  • No pumpkin-to-groundnut ratio can be derived from this recipe — 'half a large pumpkin' and 'a handful of peanuts' are not absolute quantities. For the one documented ratio, 250 g to 150 g, see Miyan Taushe.
  • The seasoning cube carries no quantity on the source page either. One cube for this quantity of beef is the usual reading.
  • Serves two, which makes this the practical version for a small household.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Blending the peanuts into the pumpkin mixture rather than adding them separately at the end.
  • Leaving the greens on the heat.
  • Cooking the peanut paste for more than two minutes.
  • Expecting the coarse texture of the mashed version.
  • Scaling up from 'half a large pumpkin' without weighing anything.

Health Benefits

  • Pumpkin is an exceptional source of beta-carotene.
  • Peanuts add plant protein, niacin and healthy fats.
  • Smooth-blended vegetables are easy to digest.
  • High in protein from beef and smoked fish.

Cultural Note

That one of Nigeria's established food bloggers wrote her first northern recipe after years of blogging, and apologised for it, says something true about where the written record is thick and where it is thin. The miyan soups are eaten daily across a vast area and documented in a fraction of the detail that the southern soups are.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of stock.

Serve with: Tuwo Shinkafa, Tuwo Masara, Masa
