🫘 Bajiye

Bajiye

Golden, spiced Somali bean fritters, mashed black-eyed peas or lentils bound with onion and chili and fried until crisp, a favorite street food snack sold hot from roadside stalls.

Bajiye reflects a broader East African and South Asian fritter tradition, and in Somalia it's especially popular as an affordable, filling street food, sold by vendors and eaten on the go, often with a squeeze of lime and a dab of hot basbaas.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor or blender, Deep frying pan

  • 300 g (1 1/2 cups) black-eyed peas or split red lentils, soaked for a few hours
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1-2 green chilies, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Drain the soaked beans or lentils and blend with a small amount of water into a thick, coarse paste.
  2. Stir in the onion, garlic, green chilies, cumin, cilantro, and salt, mixing thoroughly.
  3. Let the mixture rest for 10 minutes to firm up slightly.
  4. Heat vegetable oil to 175C (345F).
  5. Drop spoonfuls of the mixture into the hot oil and fry for 3-4 minutes per side, until deeply golden and cooked through.
  6. Drain on paper towels and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • The batter should be thick enough to hold its shape when dropped into the oil - add a little more soaked, drained bean mixture if too loose.
  • Frying at a steady, moderate temperature ensures the fritters cook through without burning outside.
  • These are best eaten fresh and hot, straight from the fryer.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Blending the beans too smooth, losing some of the pleasant, rustic texture.
  • Making the batter too wet, causing the fritters to fall apart in the oil.
  • Frying at too high a temperature, browning the outside before the inside cooks through.

Cultural Note

Bajiye is a beloved Somali street food, sold hot from roadside stalls and markets, prized as an affordable, satisfying snack throughout the day.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; refrigerate leftovers for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Reheat in a hot oven to help re-crisp.

Serve with: Basbaas (hot sauce), Lime wedges
