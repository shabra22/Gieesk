🧆 Kenyan Beef Stew

Beef Stew

Kenyan Beef Stew is the ultimate comfort dish — tender beef slow-simmered in a rich tomato gravy with onions, garlic, ginger, and warm spices. Found on nearly every Kenyan family table, it is the perfect companion to ugali, chapati, or rice.

Every Kenyan home has its version of beef stew, and every version tells a story. The secret is patience — browning the beef first for deep caramelised flavour, then simmering low and slow until the meat practically falls apart in the gravy. Prepared for Sunday lunches, celebrations, and ordinary weekday dinners alike, this is the dish that makes a house smell like home.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed sufuria or Dutch oven, Wooden spoon, Knife, Chopping board, Measuring cups and spoons

  • 1 kg stewing beef — cut into 3–4 cm cubes
  • 2 large onions (300g) — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes (500g) — chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste — optional but recommended
  • 3 tbsp vegetable or sunflower oil
  • 3 cups (720ml) beef stock or water
  • 2 medium carrots — sliced
  • 1 green bell pepper — chopped (optional)
  • 2 tsp salt — adjust to taste
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp curry powder — optional
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped, for garnish

Method

  1. Wash the beef, pat it completely dry with a cloth, and season lightly with salt and black pepper.
  2. Heat the oil in a heavy sufuria over medium-high heat until shimmering.
  3. Brown the beef in batches — do not crowd the pot. Sear each batch until deeply caramelised on all sides, then remove and set aside. This step builds the entire foundation of flavour.
  4. In the same pot, add the onions and cook over medium heat until deeply golden, about 8 minutes.
  5. Add the garlic and ginger, stirring for 1 minute until fragrant.
  6. Stir in the chopped tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the tomatoes completely break down into a thick, dark sauce — about 10 minutes.
  7. Return the browned beef to the pot, stirring to coat every piece in the tomato base.
  8. Pour in the beef stock and bring to a gentle boil.
  9. Reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 60–75 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the beef is nearly tender.
  10. Add the carrots and bell pepper during the last 20 minutes of cooking.
  11. Continue simmering uncovered for the final 10 minutes to thicken the gravy to your liking.
  12. Taste and adjust seasoning. Garnish generously with fresh coriander and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Use beef with some marbling — chuck or shin cuts give the richest, most tender result.
  • Never skip the browning step — it adds a depth of flavour you simply cannot get any other way.
  • Simmer gently, never boil rapidly — a rolling boil will toughen the beef.
  • Make it the day before — beef stew always tastes even better the next day as the flavours deepen overnight.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking on high heat throughout — makes the beef tough and dry.
  • Skipping the browning step — results in a flat, grey stew without depth.
  • Adding all vegetables at the start — carrots and peppers will turn to mush.
  • Too little liquid — causes the bottom to scorch before the beef is tender.

Cultural Note

Beef stew is a cornerstone of Kenyan family cooking — prepared for Sunday lunches, celebrations, and ordinary weekday dinners. While every household has its own recipe, the combination of beef, onions, tomatoes, and slow cooking is deeply familiar across all communities. It is considered the ultimate Kenyan comfort food.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 4 days. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop over low heat, adding a splash of water or stock if the sauce has thickened. Alternatively microwave in short intervals, stirring between each.

Serve with: White Ugali, Sukuma Wiki, Brown Ugali, Chapati, Rice, Mukimo, Mashed Potatoes, Steamed Green Vegetables
