🍐 Cotognata Siciliana

Cotognata

A dense, jewel-toned quince paste, cooked down slowly with sugar until thick enough to slice, its deep amber color and intense fruit flavor a beloved Sicilian autumn tradition.

Cotognata requires patience above all, the quince pulp simmered and stirred for a long stretch until it reduces to a thick paste that will set firm enough to slice cleanly once cooled, traditionally poured into decorative molds.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Food mill or fine sieve, Shallow molds or dish

  • 1.5 kg (3.3 lb) quinces, washed
  • 600 g (3 cups) granulated sugar, approximately (equal weight to the cooked pulp)
  • Juice of 1 lemon

Method

  1. Cut the quinces into chunks (no need to peel or core first) and simmer in water to cover until very soft, about 30-40 minutes.
  2. Pass the cooked quince through a food mill or fine sieve to remove skins and seeds, yielding a smooth pulp.
  3. Weigh the pulp and combine with an equal weight of sugar in a clean pot.
  4. Add the lemon juice and cook over low heat, stirring very frequently, for 45-60 minutes, until the mixture becomes very thick and pulls away from the sides of the pot.
  5. Pour the thick paste into shallow molds or a dish lined with parchment, smoothing the top.
  6. Let set at room temperature for at least 8 hours, ideally overnight, until firm enough to slice.
  7. Cut into pieces and serve, or store for later use.

Chef Tips

  • Stir frequently during the long cooking stage to prevent scorching, especially as the mixture thickens.
  • The paste is ready when a spoon dragged through it leaves a clear trail that does not immediately fill back in.
  • This keeps remarkably well, making it an excellent make-ahead treat or homemade gift.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not cooking the paste long enough, resulting in a mixture too soft to slice once cooled.
  • Stirring infrequently, which risks scorching the thick mixture on the bottom of the pot.
  • Rushing the setting time, cutting into it before it has properly firmed up.

Cultural Note

Cotognata is a beloved Sicilian autumn tradition, its intense quince flavor and jewel-like color making it a favorite paired with aged cheese or enjoyed on its own.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container at room temperature for several weeks, or refrigerate for longer.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Aged pecorino cheese, Espresso
