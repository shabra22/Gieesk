🥣 Fresh Pepper Sauce

Fresh pepper sauce — the Ghanaian answer to salsa

Crushed in an asanka with a rocking motion for ten or fifteen minutes — because texture is very important in Ghanaian cuisine.

This is the raw pepper sauce that sits beside kenkey and fried fish, and the reason it is made in an asanka rather than a blender is stated plainly by its teacher: texture is very important in Ghanaian cuisine. A large onion, a little over eight ounces of plum tomatoes — about a cup and a half chopped — about two tablespoons of minced chili peppers and a teaspoon of salt are crushed in the earthenware bowl with a rocking motion for ten or fifteen minutes. It can optionally be simmered twenty to thirty minutes. Taught by Barbara Baeta.

Ingredients

Equipment: Asanka (earthenware grinding bowl) and wooden grinder, Knife, Jar, for storing

  • 1 large onion
  • Plum tomatoes — a little over 8 oz, or about 1 1/2 cups chopped
  • Chili peppers, about 2 tbsp minced, adjust to taste
  • Salt, about 1 tsp or to taste

Method

  1. Chop the onion, tomatoes and chillies roughly.
  2. Put them in the asanka with the salt.
  3. Crush with a rocking motion for about 10 or 15 minutes.

Optional: simmer the finished sauce about 20 to 30 minutes.

  1. Refrigerate and use within a day or so.

Chef Tips

  • The rocking motion in the asanka is the technique, for ten or fifteen minutes. Her teacher's reason is the whole point: texture is very important in Ghanaian cuisine, and a blender gives you a smooth purée instead of a crushed sauce.
  • Ten or fifteen minutes is a long time to crush by hand and it is not negotiable if you want the texture.
  • The simmer is optional. Raw is fresher and sharper; simmered keeps longer and tastes rounder.
  • Use within a day or so — her own instruction. This is a fresh sauce and it does not keep like shito.
  • Two tablespoons of minced chilli for a cup and a half of tomato is a good starting ratio, and it is adjustable.
  • She calls it the Ghanaian answer to salsa, which is the most useful single description of it.
  • If you have no asanka, a mortar and pestle is closer than a blender. Pulse a food processor at most.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a blender.
  • Crushing for two minutes instead of ten or fifteen.
  • Making a large batch — it does not keep.
  • Simmering it when you wanted the fresh version.
  • Skipping the salt, which draws the juice out as you crush.

Health Benefits

  • Raw, so it keeps the full vitamin C of the tomatoes and chillies.
  • Very low in calories with no added oil.
  • Onion and chilli both carry documented bioactive compounds.
  • Vegan and gluten-free.

Cultural Note

An asanka is a ribbed earthenware bowl and food crushed in one comes out quite unlike anything a blade produces — uneven, juicy, with recognisable pieces. That a professional Accra caterer taught this sauce with an insistence on the vessel, rather than the ingredients, says most of what needs saying about Ghanaian cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: A day or so refrigerated, per the author. The simmered version keeps a few days longer.

Reheating: Not needed. Served cold or at room temperature.

Serve with: Kenkey, Fried fish, Banku
