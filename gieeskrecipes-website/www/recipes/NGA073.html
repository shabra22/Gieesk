🥬 Otong Soup

Otong — Efik okra soup

Half the okra pounded in a mortar and half left chopped — the pounded half draws, the chopped half keeps its texture.

Otong is the southern okra soup and the clever move here is splitting the okra. Two cups are chopped fine, and half of that goes into a mortar to be pounded — pounded okra gives up its mucilage and thickens the broth, while the chopped half stays in visible pieces. The pounded half goes in with the chopped at the same time, after the meat and crayfish have simmered ten minutes, then ugu, uziza and palm oil follow for five to seven minutes. The source's own warning is to be sure not to overcook the vegetables.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium pot, Mortar and pestle, or a blender, Sharp knife

  • 2 cups ugu leaves, chopped
  • 2 cups okra, finely chopped — half of it to be pounded
  • 3 cups water
  • 1/4 cup palm oil
  • 1/4 cup uziza leaves (optional)
  • 1/4 cup blended crayfish
  • 1 tsp dried pepper
  • 2 bouillon cubes
  • Meat and seafood of choice, pre-cooked
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Boil the 3 cups of water on medium heat.
  2. Pound half the chopped okra in a mortar, or pulse it in a blender.
  3. Add the crayfish, bouillon, pre-cooked meat, dried pepper and salt, and simmer 10 minutes.
  4. Add both the pounded and the chopped okra and stir.
  5. Add the ugu, the uziza and the palm oil, and simmer 5 to 7 minutes — be sure not to overcook the vegetables.
  6. Serve with a swallow.

Chef Tips

  • Pound half, chop half. The pounded okra is your thickener and the chopped okra is your texture, and you want both.
  • A mortar does it better than a blender. Pounding bruises the okra and releases the mucilage; blending shreds it.
  • Five to seven minutes at the end, and the source says plainly not to overcook the vegetables. Okra past that point goes grey and slimy rather than drawing.
  • Three cups of water to two cups of okra is a loose soup by Efik standards. Reduce the water if you want it thicker.
  • Palm oil goes in with the greens rather than earlier. It is for richness and colour rather than as a frying base.
  • Uziza is optional here and it is worth including — a quarter cup gives a peppery lift the okra alone does not have.
  • The same dish is ila asepo in Yoruba, which this source states directly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Pounding all the okra, which loses the texture.
  • Blending instead of pounding.
  • Cooking the greens past seven minutes.
  • Adding the palm oil at the start.
  • Boiling hard once the okra is in.

Health Benefits

  • Okra mucilage is a soluble fibre that moderates blood sugar.
  • Ugu supplies iron, calcium and vitamins A and C.
  • Very low in carbohydrate and calories.
  • Uziza carries piperine-family compounds.

Cultural Note

Okra soup is one of the few dishes that exists in essentially every Nigerian cuisine, which makes its names a useful map: otong among the Efik, ila asepo in Yorubaland, and plain okra soup across the southeast. The split-okra method here — half pounded, half chopped — is the kind of refinement that only shows up once a cook has made something hundreds of times.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. The draw slackens after a day.

Reheating: Warm gently. Boiling destroys the okra texture.

Serve with: Iyan, Eba or garri, Akpu or fufu
