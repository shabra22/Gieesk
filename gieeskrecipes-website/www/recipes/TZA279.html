🌿 Hadzabe Wild Tuber Roast

Mizizi ya Msitu — Wahadzabe

Hadzabe Wild Tuber Roast — a collection of root vegetables (sweet potato, yam, and cassava used as accessible equivalents to the Hadza wild tubers) roasted directly in the coals of an open fire. The ancient Hadzabe carbohydrate source — dug from the earth and cooked in the fire.

The Hadzabe identify over 20 species of edible wild tubers in the Lake Eyasi ecosystem — the most important carbohydrate source in their diet. Hadzabe women are expert tuber-diggers who can identify and excavate wild tubers invisible to untrained eyes. This preparation uses cultivated equivalents to honour the tradition and technique.

Ingredients

Equipment: Open fire or charcoal, Tongs or green sticks

  • 2 medium sweet potatoes
  • 1 yam section (300g)
  • 2 cassava sections

Coarse salt for eating

    Method

    1. Build a fire with good-sized coals.
    2. Place all unpeeled roots directly in the coals.
    3. Cover partially with ash and coals.
    4. Roast 35—45 minutes, turning every 10 minutes.
    5. Test with a skewer — no resistance means done.
    6. Remove from fire. Allow to cool slightly.
    7. Peel hot and eat with a pinch of salt.

    Chef Tips

    • Each root cooks at a different rate — check each individually.
    • The char on the outside is flavour — do not avoid it.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not checking each root individually.

    Cultural Note

    Hadzabe wild tubers are the carbohydrate that has sustained one of humanity's oldest peoples for tens of thousands of years. The Hadzabe women who dig these roots carry knowledge of their landscape that cannot be replaced — each tuber species, its season, its location, its preparation. It is ecological knowledge as ancient as human knowledge itself.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately.

    Reheating: Briefly in coals.
