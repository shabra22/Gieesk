Spaghetti alla Puttanesca

Spaghetti alla Puttanesca is a bold, briny, deeply savory Neapolitan classic — olives, capers, anchovies, and garlic simmered with tomato into a sauce that punches well above its short cooking time.

The colorful, debated origin of the name aside, this dish is a masterclass in umami layering — anchovies dissolve into the oil as a savory backbone, while olives and capers provide bursts of briny intensity throughout.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large skillet

  • 400 g (14 oz) spaghetti
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 4–6 anchovy fillets
  • ½ tsp red pepper flakes
  • 150 g (1 cup) pitted black olives (such as Gaeta), roughly chopped
  • 3 tbsp capers, rinsed
  • 500 g (18 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 3 tbsp chopped fresh parsley
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the spaghetti until al dente.
  2. Meanwhile, heat the olive oil in a skillet over medium-low heat. Add the garlic and anchovies, cooking and mashing the anchovies into the oil until they dissolve, about 2 minutes.
  3. Add the red pepper flakes, olives, and capers, cooking for 1–2 minutes.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes and simmer for 12–15 minutes until slightly thickened.
  5. Drain the pasta, reserving a little pasta water, and add directly to the sauce.
  6. Toss over medium heat for 1–2 minutes, adding pasta water as needed.
  7. Stir in the parsley and taste before adding salt — the olives, capers, and anchovies bring significant saltiness already.
  8. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Let the anchovies fully dissolve into the oil — this builds a savory backbone without any distinctly fishy taste.
  • Taste before adding extra salt, as the olives, capers, and anchovies are already quite salty.
  • This dish is traditionally served without cheese, letting the briny, savory sauce stand on its own.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-salting before tasting, since the briny ingredients already provide plenty of seasoning.
  • Skimping on the olives or capers, which are essential to the dish's bold, defining character.
  • Adding cheese, which is not traditional and can clash with the briny sauce.

Cultural Note

The name's origin is fiercely debated, with the most repeated (if unverified) legend claiming it was a quick, inexpensive dish made by Neapolitan women between other duties, using whatever pantry staples were on hand.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate the sauce for up to 4 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a pan, then toss with freshly cooked pasta.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A crisp Campanian white wine
