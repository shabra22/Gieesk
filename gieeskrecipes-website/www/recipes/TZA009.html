🍌 Tanzanian Cooked Bananas

Ndizi za Kupikwa

Ndizi za Kupikwa — green cooking bananas simmered in a spiced tomato and onion sauce until completely tender and starchy. The staple food of Tanzania's banana-growing regions — Kilimanjaro, Kagera, and the lake regions — where green bananas replace ugali as the primary starch.

In Tanzania's banana-growing highlands — particularly around Kilimanjaro and in Kagera near Lake Victoria — green cooking bananas (ndizi) are as central to the diet as ugali is elsewhere. Cooked in a spiced tomato-onion sauce until completely tender, they absorb all the surrounding flavours while contributing their characteristic starchy sweetness.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid

  • 6 large green cooking bananas — peeled
  • 2 medium onions — chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 2 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 cups water
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander

Method

  1. Peel bananas wearing gloves. Halve or cut into chunks.
  2. Submerge in salted water immediately to prevent browning.
  3. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden.
  4. Add garlic, tomatoes, and curry powder. Cook 6 minutes.
  5. Drain bananas and add to the pot.
  6. Add water. Bring to a boil.
  7. Cover and simmer 25—30 minutes until completely tender.
  8. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Wear gloves when peeling — latex sap stains permanently.
  • Keep peeled bananas in salted water — they brown rapidly.
  • They are done when a knife slides in without resistance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not wearing gloves.
  • Browning bananas.
  • Undercooking.

The Science

Green cooking bananas are high in resistant starch and low in sugar — opposite to ripe bananas. Cooking converts some resistant starch to digestible starch, producing the characteristic tender, slightly starchy texture.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in resistant starch — a prebiotic fibre.
  • Good source of potassium.
  • Naturally gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Ndizi za Kupikwa are the staple of Tanzania's banana highlands — the food of Kilimanjaro's slopes where the Chagga people have cultivated hundreds of banana varieties for centuries.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: Nyama ya Kukaanga, Mchuzi wa Samaki, Pili Pili Sauce
