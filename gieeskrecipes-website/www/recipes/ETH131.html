🧈 Ethiopian Shiro with Kibbeh

ሽሮ ፍርፍር ቅቤ — Shiro be-Qibe

Ethiopian Shiro with Kibbeh — Shiro Wat enriched with a generous spoonful of kibbeh stirred in at the end. The non-fasting-day version of Shiro — the kibbeh transforms the already excellent chickpea stew into something extraordinary.

Shiro with kibbeh is the non-fasting version of Ethiopia's most practical dish — the addition of kibbeh's spiced, rich, aromatic butter turns Shiro Wat from excellent into exceptional. This is how Shiro is served at Ethiopian celebration meals when fasting is not in effect.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan

  • 1 cup shiro powder
  • 2 onions
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 tsp berbere
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Make Shiro Wat as per ETH102.
  2. When cooked through and thick, remove from heat.
  3. Stir in kibbeh. The kibbeh melts into the hot shiro.
  4. Season. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Add kibbeh off the heat — it should melt gently, not fry.
  • A generous spoon of kibbeh completely changes the character of the shiro.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding kibbeh too early — it burns.
  • Insufficient kibbeh.

The Science

The kibbeh's spice infusion (see ETH092) combines with the shiro's chickpea base — the fat-soluble aromatic compounds from the kibbeh distribute throughout the smooth shiro, creating a unified flavour more complex than either alone.

Cultural Note

Shiro with kibbeh is the difference between the fasting table and the celebration table — the same dish, transformed by one spoonful of butter. It demonstrates how Ethiopian cooking uses the same building blocks for both restriction and abundance.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days.

Reheating: Gently — stir in fresh kibbeh after reheating for best result.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), On the non-fasting celebration platter
