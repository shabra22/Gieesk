🐑 Skopo

Skopo — smiley, inhloko, intloko yemvu

A cleaned, halved sheep's head simmered three hours with onion and stock until the meat gives, then finished in the oven with spice and oil until golden — the cheeks and the ears are what people queue for.

It is called a smiley because the lips draw back in the cooking and the head appears to grin. Vendors boil it in salted water alone for about an hour and let customers spice it themselves, which tells you how good the meat is on its own; the home version goes longer and finishes in the oven. Buy the head cleaned and halved from a butcher — removing the fur and preparing it is genuine labour and there is no shame in skipping it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Very large stockpot, Kettle, kept on, Oven pan, Tongs

  • 1 sheep's head, cleaned, de-furred and halved
  • Boiling water, enough to cover
  • 2 onions, chopped small
  • 2 chilli beef stock cubes
  • Brown onion soup powder, to thicken — about 2 tbsp
  • Barbecue spice, to taste
  • Steak and chops spice, to taste
  • Olive oil, a little, for the oven stage

To serve

  • Pap

Method

  1. Buy the head already cleaned and halved unless you intend to do it yourself. The requirement is the same either way: the fur removed, the head cleaned and split.
  2. Bring a large pot of water to the boil and add the halved head.
  3. Add the chopped onion and set the heat to medium.
  4. Boil for 30 minutes before adding the chilli beef stock cubes, so the meat has a seasoned base to sit in.
  5. Boil for another hour or so, stirring in the brown onion soup powder to make a sauce.
  6. Keep a kettle of boiling water ready and top the pot up whenever it threatens to run dry.
  7. Keep cooking and stirring for a further 1 1/2 to 2 hours, depending on how soft you want the meat, then switch off and let it sit in the broth.
  8. At this point it can be eaten as it is, with pap and the broth.
  9. To take it further, lift the head out of the broth into an oven pan.
  10. Season with barbecue spice and steak and chops spice, add a little olive oil, and put it in a hot oven for about 15 minutes, until golden brown.

Chef Tips

  • Keep the kettle on. A pot that boils dry over three hours is the one real hazard, and it is a big pot.
  • The cheeks and the ears are the prized parts, and the tongue after them. That is what to look for when it comes out.
  • Vendors use salt and nothing else in the pot, and let people spice their own portion. If you want to taste what the meat actually does, try it that way once.
  • Three hours is a guide, not a rule — the meat is ready when it gives, and older animals take longer.
  • The broth is not a by-product. Serve it over the pap.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting the pot run dry.
  • Salting hard early, given the stock cubes and soup powder that follow.
  • Rushing it. Under three hours the meat will not release from the bone.
  • Attempting the cleaning and de-furring without knowing how — this is a job for a butcher or someone who has done it before.

Health Benefits

  • Very high in protein and collagen, and rich in iron and zinc.
  • Essentially free of carbohydrate.
  • Uses a cut that would otherwise be discarded, which is the whole economic logic of the dish.

Cultural Note

Smileys, mogodu, amanqina and walkie talkies became township trades because apartheid barred Black women from formal employment and formal markets refused these cuts, so women built businesses on what was left. Describing that food as recycled meat, as some writing still does, erases skilled cooking and a real economy. The trade is now everywhere from taxi ranks to expensive restaurants, and the women who built it are the reason.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Strip the meat off the bone and refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Warm the stripped meat in its broth, or crisp it in a hot oven.

Serve with: Pap, Chakalaka, Umngqusho
