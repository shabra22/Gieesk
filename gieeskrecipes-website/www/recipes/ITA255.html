Spaghetti alla Bottarga

A minimalist Sardinian pasta showcasing bottarga, the island's prized cured fish roe, grated generously over spaghetti tossed simply in garlic and olive oil.

Bottarga, salted and air-dried grey mullet or tuna roe, carries an intensely briny, almost umami depth that needs nothing more than good olive oil, garlic, and a little lemon to become one of Sardinia's most sought-after delicacies.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large saute pan

  • 400 g (14 oz) spaghetti
  • 60 g (2.1 oz) bottarga, finely grated, plus extra shavings for serving
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1 dried chili, crumbled (optional)
  • 100 ml (scant 1/2 cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Juice of 1/2 lemon
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Black pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of lightly salted water to a boil (bottarga is already quite salty, so go easy on the salt) and cook the spaghetti until al dente.
  2. Meanwhile, warm the olive oil in a large pan and gently saute the garlic and chili until fragrant, taking care not to brown the garlic.
  3. Drain the pasta, reserving some cooking water, and add it to the pan.
  4. Remove from the heat and toss in most of the grated bottarga along with a splash of pasta water, letting the residual heat melt it into a light sauce.
  5. Finish with lemon juice, parsley, and black pepper.
  6. Plate and top with extra shavings of bottarga just before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Go light on salt in the pasta water, since bottarga itself is quite salty.
  • Toss the bottarga in off the heat, letting the residual warmth melt it gently rather than cooking it directly, which can make it bitter.
  • A little bottarga goes a long way - this is meant to be a delicate showcase, not an overwhelming amount.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-salting the pasta water, resulting in an overly salty finished dish given bottarga own saltiness.
  • Cooking the bottarga directly over heat for too long, which can turn it bitter.
  • Adding cheese, which is never used alongside seafood in Italian tradition and would clash with the bottarga.

Health Benefits

  • Bottarga provides omega-3 fatty acids and protein, though it is high in sodium.

Cultural Note

Bottarga is one of Sardinia's most prized delicacies, sometimes called Mediterranean caviar, and this simple pasta remains the classic, most respected way to showcase it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately; not suited to storage or reheating.

Reheating: Not recommended; best enjoyed fresh.

Serve with: A crisp Sardinian white wine such as Vermentino
