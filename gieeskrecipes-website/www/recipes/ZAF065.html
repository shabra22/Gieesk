🫘 Boereboontjies

Boereboontjies

Green beans, potato and onion boiled together in one salted pot until everything is soft enough to mash, then crushed by hand with butter and a great deal of black pepper — deliberately lumpy.

Boereboontjies is green beans mashed into potato, and it is the standard Sunday-roast side of Afrikaans cooking. There is one rule every source agrees on and it is about texture: chunky, mashed by hand with a potato masher, lumps wanted. A ricer or a stick blender turns it into pale green wallpaper paste. Everything else — whether the onion is boiled with it or fried first, whether bacon belongs, whether it is one pot or two — is genuinely open, and every household is certain its own way is correct.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan with a lid, Colander, Potato masher — not a ricer and not a blender

  • 450 g green beans, topped, tailed and cut into 1 cm pieces
  • 2 large potatoes, peeled and cut into large chunks
  • 1 large onion, cut into large chunks
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) salt
  • Water, to cover
  • 25 g butter
  • Plenty of freshly ground black pepper

Optional

  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) full-cream milk

Method

  1. Put the potatoes, beans and onion in a pan, cover with cold water and add the salt.
  2. Bring to the boil, put the lid on, and boil for 15 to 20 minutes until everything is soft enough to mash.
  3. Drain thoroughly in a colander.
  4. Add the butter, the black pepper and the milk if you are using it.
  5. Mash to a nice chunky texture, leaving plenty of lumps.
  6. Taste, adjust the seasoning, and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Chunky, always. Foodle Club's instruction not to use a potato ricer is the clearest statement of it, and a stick blender is worse still.
  • Drain it properly or the mash will be watery and the butter will not take.
  • Plenty of black pepper. This is a bland dish without it and every source leans on it.
  • Nutmeg is often said to belong in boereboontjies, but it does not appear in any published recipe I could find — ten were checked, Afrikaans sources included. Add a scrape if that is how your family made it, and know that it is a home variation rather than the canon.
  • Bacon is a real variation: fry a packet with the onion first, then cook the beans and potatoes in the same pot with just enough water to cover, about 30 minutes.
  • Frying the onion first rather than boiling it gives a sweeter, rounder result. Both are traditional and neither is wrong.
  • Start the potatoes in cold water with everything else. It is one pot, and staging the additions is more fuss than this dish deserves.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mashing it smooth.
  • Not draining it.
  • Being shy with the pepper.
  • Overcooking the beans to grey — soft enough to mash, not disintegrated.
  • Skipping the butter, which is what carries the whole thing.

Cultural Note

Boereboontjies is the least documented of the boerekos classics, which is usually a sign that a dish never needed writing down — everyone's mother made it and nobody consulted a book. That the published record has no nutmeg in it while so many households swear by it is a neat illustration of how much of this cooking lives outside the cookbooks.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Warm in a pan with a splash of milk.

Serve with: A Sunday roast leg of lamb, Potjiekos, Pampoenkoekies
