🌯 Ekpang Nkukwo

Ekpang nkukwo

Grated cocoyam wrapped a teaspoonful at a time in cocoyam leaves, layered rather than stirred, and shaken loose after about twenty minutes — never stirred.

Ekpang nkukwo is a technique dish and the technique is the wrapping. Six medium cocoyams are peeled, grated or blended, loosened with about half a cup of water, and then taken up a teaspoonful at a time and wrapped in cocoyam leaves — each wrap placed gently on top of the last until all the paste is used, so the pot is layered rather than mixed. After about twenty minutes you shake the pot to loosen them, and you never stir. Periwinkle, crab, dry fish and shrimp cook alongside, and the palm oil goes in near the end.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grater or blender, Large pot with a lid, Cocoyam leaves, Ladle

  • 6 medium-size cocoyam
  • About 1/2 cup water, to loosen the paste
  • Cocoyam leaves, segmented
  • 1/2 cup unshelled periwinkle (optional)
  • 6 dressed crabs (optional)
  • 1 medium-size dry fish
  • 1/2 cup fresh shrimps
  • 1 cup stockfish pieces (optional)
  • 3 tbsp crayfish
  • 4 leaves utasi, or 8 ntong leaves
  • 2 tbsp dry pepper
  • 2 cooking spoons palm oil
  • 2 crayfish seasoning tablets
  • 750 ml water — see the note on the two figures
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Peel, wash and grate or blend the cocoyam, then mix in about half a cup of water to soften it further.
  2. Using your fingers, take about a teaspoon-size of the paste at a time and wrap it in the leaves, placing each one gently in the pot. Place each wrap on top of the other until all the paste is wrapped.
  3. Add 2 cups of water gradually and bring to the boil.
  4. Add the fish, shrimps, crabs and seasonings and cook on low heat.
  5. After about 20 minutes, shake the pot to loosen the wraps — do not stir.
  6. Add the remaining water and cook 10 minutes.
  7. Add the crayfish and cook 5 minutes.
  8. Add the palm oil, lift the wraps gently with a spoon so it distributes, and cook 5 minutes.
  9. Adjust the salt and cook a final 5 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • Teaspoon-size portions. Small wraps cook through; large ones stay raw in the middle.
  • Layer, do not mix. Each wrap goes on top of the last and the pot is built up rather than stirred together.
  • Shake the pot at twenty minutes, do not stir. Same logic as ikokore and kekefiyai — the parcels need to come loose without being broken.
  • Lift the wraps gently with a spoon when the palm oil goes in, so it coats everything without disturbing the layers.
  • Utasi or ntong, and the ratio is given: four utasi leaves or eight ntong. Utasi is twice as strong.
  • On the water: the ingredient list says 750 ml but the method calls for 2 cups and then 'the remaining water'. Two cups is roughly 475 ml, so the remaining is roughly 275 ml. The discrepancy is in the source.
  • Another named source wraps a tablespoon rather than a teaspoon — a threefold difference in portion size. Both are published and neither is adjusted.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Wrapping portions too large to cook through.
  • Stirring the pot at any point.
  • Adding all the water at the start.
  • Adding the palm oil early, where it stops the wraps cooking.
  • Using utasi and ntong interchangeably at the same count.

Health Benefits

  • Cocoyam is a low-allergen starch rich in potassium and resistant starch.
  • Periwinkle, crab and shrimp are all very lean, high-iron proteins.
  • Red palm oil supplies carotenoids and vitamin E.
  • A complete one-pot meal.

Cultural Note

Ekpang nkukwo is the most laborious dish in the Efik repertoire and it shows: dozens of individual teaspoon-sized parcels, each wrapped by hand in a cocoyam leaf, layered into a pot and then never touched with a spoon. It belongs to the same family of no-stir techniques as ikokore and kekefiyai, where the whole skill is building something in a pot that would be destroyed by mixing.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of water, no stirring.

Serve with: On its own, as a one-pot, Cold palm wine, Boiled plantain
