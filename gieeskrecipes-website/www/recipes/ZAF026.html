🥨 Koeksisters

Koeksister

Plaited dough fried until golden and plunged straight from the oil into syrup chilled overnight — the temperature shock is what makes them crisp outside and syrup-logged inside.

This is the Afrikaans koeksister: three legs of dough plaited tight, deep-fried, and dropped into syrup that has been in the fridge all night. It is a different dish from the Cape Muslim koe'sister, which is a spiced yeast bun soaked in hot syrup and rolled in coconut. The story is that Dutch settlers brought two recipes to the Cape — a fried doughnut and a thin bowtie made from pasta dough — and someone combined them and started plaiting. The hot-into-cold is not a flourish; it is the recipe.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Rectangular airtight container, for the syrup, Large deep saucepan, for frying, Slotted spoon, Cooling rack

    The syrup, made the day before

    • 1 kg granulated sugar
    • 475 ml (2 cups) water
    • 2 cinnamon sticks
    • 1 x 2.5 cm piece fresh ginger, peeled and sliced
    • 1 lemon, juice and zest

    The dough

    • 280 g cake flour
    • 1/2 tsp sea salt
    • 2 tbsp baking powder
    • 4 tbsp butter, softened
    • 1 large free-range egg, lightly beaten
    • 7 tbsp water

    To fry

    • 1.4 L (6 cups) vegetable oil

    Method

    1. Make the syrup first, the day before. Dissolve the sugar in the water over low heat, stirring frequently, and do not let it boil before the sugar has completely dissolved.
    2. Once dissolved, boil for 1 minute, then add the cinnamon, ginger, lemon juice and zest.
    3. Reduce the heat and simmer a further 5 minutes. Cool, transfer to a rectangular airtight container and chill overnight.
    4. For the dough, sift the flour, salt and baking powder together and rub in the butter until it looks like breadcrumbs.
    5. Mix in the beaten egg with a palette knife, then add the water a little at a time until the dough comes together. Add a little more flour if it is overly sticky.
    6. Knead for 30 seconds into a ball and rest it, covered, at room temperature for at least an hour.
    7. Roll out on a floured surface to 1 cm thick. Cut a rectangle, then cut that into strips about 12 x 4 cm, and turn each strip short-side up.
    8. Cut two incisions 1 cm apart down the length of each strip, leaving the tops intact so you have three legs joined at one end.
    9. Plait the three legs tightly, pinch the loose ends together, and fold the top in slightly so both ends match.
    10. Heat the oil in a large saucepan. When hot, add 3 koeksisters at a time and deep-fry for about 3 minutes, turning and pressing them lightly into the oil.
    11. When golden brown, lift them out, drain very briefly on kitchen paper, and plunge them straight into the ice-cold syrup. Coat them thoroughly and let them soak for a minute.
    12. Lift onto a rack to cool and dry slightly.

    Chef Tips

    • The syrup must be genuinely cold, which means made the day before and left in the fridge. Cool syrup is not cold syrup, and the difference shows in the crunch.
    • Do not let the sugar syrup boil before every grain has dissolved, or it crystallises and stays grainy.
    • Plait tightly. A loose plait comes apart in the oil and unwinds in the syrup.
    • Three at a time, no more. Each batch drops the oil temperature, and a cool fryer gives greasy koeksisters.
    • Barely drain them before they go into the syrup — seconds, not a minute. They need to be hot when they hit it.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using syrup that is merely cool, which is the single commonest reason koeksisters go soggy instead of crisp.
    • Boiling the syrup before the sugar has dissolved, which makes it grainy.
    • Plaiting loosely, so they unravel.
    • Draining them on paper until they cool, after which the syrup cannot get in.
    • Calling the Cape Muslim koe'sister by this name. Different dough, opposite syrup temperature, different dish.

    Health Benefits

    • Portioned small — a batch of twelve from 280 g of flour.
    • The ginger and cinnamon in the syrup are traditionally used to settle digestion.
    • No dairy beyond the butter in the dough.

    Cultural Note

    The koeksister is one of the few South African foods with a monument to it, in Orania. It is also the dish most often confused with the Cape Muslim koe'sister, and Cape Town will correct you on the difference. World Koe'sister Day, for the other one, was first held in 2019 and now falls on the first Sunday in September.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Airtight at room temperature 3 days. They soften as they sit.

    Reheating: Not reheated. Eaten cold, and they are meant to be.

    Serve with: Strong coffee, Rooibos tea
