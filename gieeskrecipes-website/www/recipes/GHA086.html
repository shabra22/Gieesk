🍻 Burukutu

Burukutu — blukutu

Soured over a six-to-seven-hour hold from pH 4.6 to 4.05, then a hundred and twenty hours of alcoholic fermentation to pH 2.88 and four and a half per cent alcohol.

Burukutu is the other northern grain beer, and it is the best-measured fermentation in the Ghanaian drinks literature — sampled at four real production sites in Tema, Zenu, Ashaiman and Accra Newtown. The souring stage runs over a six-to-seven-hour holding period, with pH falling from about 4.63 to 4.05. The alcoholic fermentation then runs a full hundred and twenty hours, pH dropping from 3.36 to 2.88, soluble solids from 7.5 to 3.3 per cent and alcohol climbing from 0.99 to 4.47 per cent. No sensory endpoint is published, so the pH figures are the only guide.

Ingredients

Equipment: Steeping and germination vessels, Mill, Large fermentation vessel, pH strips, Straining cloth

  • Sorghum or millet grain, malted
  • Water

No household quantities are published. See the note.

    Method

    1. Malt the grain — steep, germinate and dry as for pito.
    2. Grind and mash with water.
    3. Hold for souring 6 to 7 hours.

    SOURING ENDPOINT: pH falls from about 4.63 to about 4.05 over that holding period.

    1. Begin the alcoholic fermentation at room temperature, about 28 degrees.
    2. Ferment 120 hours.

    ALCOHOLIC ENDPOINT: pH falls from about 3.36 to about 2.88; soluble solids fall from about 7.5 to 3.3 per cent; alcohol rises from about 1 to about 4.5 per cent.

    1. Strain and serve.

    Chef Tips

    • A hundred and twenty hours is five days, which is far longer than pito's twelve to twenty-four. Burukutu is a slower, sourer, stronger drink.
    • The souring hold is only six to seven hours and the pH moves from 4.63 to 4.05 — a small, measurable shift. Strips are genuinely useful here.
    • Final pH of 2.88 is very acid, more so than most commercial beers. That sourness is characteristic rather than a fault.
    • Watch the soluble solids if you can measure them: 7.5 per cent down to 3.3 is the sugar being eaten, and it tracks the alcohol rising.
    • No sensory endpoint is published anywhere. If you have no strips, the honest answer is that the literature cannot tell you when to stop.
    • A second study on a different batch gives pH 2.93 and 4.76 per cent alcohol at the same hundred and twenty hours, which is close enough to be reassuring.
    • Blukutu is the Volta spelling of the same drink, and it is made across the north and into Nigeria.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cutting the alcoholic fermentation to pito's timescale.
    • Expecting a sensory cue the literature does not provide.
    • Skipping the short souring hold.
    • Fermenting much warmer than about 28 degrees.
    • Judging it by sweetness, which falls throughout.

    Health Benefits

    • B vitamins from the yeast and lactic acid bacteria.
    • Sorghum and millet are both gluten-free.
    • The long lactic fermentation improves mineral availability.
    • Around 4.5 per cent alcohol, comparable to a standard beer.

    Cultural Note

    Burukutu is sold from drinking spots across northern Ghana and into northern Nigeria, and the five-day fermentation makes it markedly sourer and stronger than pito. That researchers sampled four working production sites rather than brewing in a lab is why these are the most trustworthy fermentation figures in the Ghanaian drinks record.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: A few days at most, like pito. It continues to ferment.

    Reheating: Not applicable.

    Serve with: Drunk on its own, Tuo Zaafi, Tubaani
