🫘 Ethiopian Broad Bean Stew

ባቄላ ወጥ — Baqela Wat

Ethiopian Broad Bean Stew — dried broad beans (fava beans) soaked and slow-cooked in a berbere sauce. A substantial, filling fasting stew — broader in flavour than lentils, with the distinctive earthiness of fava beans absorbing the berbere.

Broad bean stew is an important part of Ethiopia's legume-based fasting cuisine — the fava bean's earthy, slightly meaty flavour makes it one of the most satisfying fasting proteins available.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot

  • 2 cups dried broad beans (fava beans) — soaked overnight, outer skins removed
  • 3 onions
  • 4 tbsp oil
  • 3 tbsp berbere
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 3 cups water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Remove outer skins from soaked beans.
  2. Boil beans 40 minutes until tender.
  3. Dry caramelise onions 18 minutes.
  4. Add oil and berbere. Fry 3 minutes.
  5. Add garlic and ginger.
  6. Add cooked beans with some cooking liquid.
  7. Simmer 15 minutes. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Remove the outer skins after soaking — they cause digestive discomfort.
  • The beans should remain whole but be very tender.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not removing outer skins.
  • Under-cooking.

The Science

Fava beans contain vicine and convicine — compounds that can cause digestive problems (and haemolytic anaemia in G6PD-deficient individuals). Removing the outer skins and boiling reduces these compounds dramatically.

Health Benefits

  • High plant protein.
  • Very high iron and folate.
  • L-dopa content may support neurological health.

Cultural Note

Broad bean stew is one of Ethiopia's most ancient fasting foods — fava beans were among the earliest cultivated plants in the Ethiopian highlands, and their use in fasting cuisine connects to agricultural traditions thousands of years old.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), On the fasting platter
