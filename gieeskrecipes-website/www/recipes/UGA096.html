🥔 Steamed Sweet Potatoes with Groundnut Sauce

Ebinyebwa ebitokose

Sweet potatoes wrapped in banana leaves and steamed over half an hour on a bed of banana stems, with a groundnut sauce built from tomato and onion boiled in water and thickened with paste mixed cold.

This is the signature Busoga pairing, and it is the clearest written example of the leaf-steaming that the kingdom is known for — steaming food is a popular way of cooking in Busoga, and the rig is banana stems in water with a leaf laid over them. The potatoes go on top in their own leaf parcel, covered with another leaf. The sauce follows the universal Ugandan rule: tomato and onion boiled first, groundnut paste mixed with cold water to a fluid consistency and only then stirred in.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep pan with a tight lid, Banana stems and leaves, Second saucepan, Mortar and pestle, for pounding the peanuts, Bowl

    Steamed sweet potatoes

    • Sweet potatoes
    • Banana leaves
    • Banana stems
    • Water

    Groundnut sauce

    • Peanuts, or ready-made groundnut paste
    • Water
    • Tomato, diced
    • Onion, diced

      Method

        The potatoes

        1. Peel, cut and wash the sweet potatoes.
        2. Place banana stems in a pan with water.
        3. Layer a banana leaf over the water-covered stems.
        4. Wrap the sweet potatoes in banana leaves and set them on the leaf, then cover the pan with another leaf.
        5. Steam for over 30 minutes.

        The sauce

        1. Pound the peanuts into a paste, or use ready-made paste.
        2. Chop the tomatoes and onions.
        3. Boil the tomatoes and onions with water.
        4. Mix the peanut paste with cold water to a fluid consistency.
        5. Add it to the boiling liquid and simmer, stirring, until thick.
        6. Serve the potatoes with the sauce.

        Chef Tips

        • Banana stems in the water, a leaf over them, the parcel on top, another leaf as the lid. That layered rig is the Busoga steaming method and it is worth learning as a technique rather than as one recipe.
        • Over thirty minutes is the source's own phrasing, and it means what it says — this is not a twenty-minute job.
        • Mix the groundnut paste with cold water to a fluid consistency before it goes in. Every Ugandan groundnut sauce turns on this and the ones that go lumpy skipped it.
        • Boil the tomatoes and onions in water first. The paste goes into a hot liquid, not the other way round.
        • Pounding the peanuts yourself is said to release the oils better than machine grinding, and it is the traditional preference.
        • Simmer and stir until thick. That is the only doneness cue given and it is enough.
        • No quantities are documented. One figure in the source — about 100 kg of sweet potato — is annual consumption per person, not a recipe quantity, and it should not be mistaken for one.
        • Busoga is a Bantu kingdom and one of four constitutional monarchies in Uganda, ruled by an elected Kyabazinga. Its food is thinly documented and mostly served by tour-operator pages, which is why this chapter matters.

        Common Mistakes to Avoid

        • Letting the water touch the potato parcel.
        • Mixing the groundnut paste into hot liquid.
        • Steaming for twenty minutes.
        • Skipping the covering leaf.
        • Reading the 100 kg consumption figure as an ingredient quantity.

        Health Benefits

        • Sweet potatoes are high in fibre, potassium and vitamin C.
        • Groundnut paste supplies plant protein, niacin, folate and vitamin E.
        • Steaming preserves more of the potatoes' nutrients than boiling.
        • Contains peanuts.

        Cultural Note

        Busoga is fishing and farming country around Jinja, and it is one of the worst-documented food regions in Uganda despite being one of its four kingdoms — most of what is online about it comes from safari operators. That its one properly written chapter is about steaming sweet potatoes says something about what has and has not been recorded.

        Storage & Serving

        Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Keep the sauce and potatoes separate.

        Reheating: Steam the potatoes; warm the sauce with a splash of water.

        Serve with: Mugoyo, Sauteed Garlic Dodo, Sauteed Mukene
