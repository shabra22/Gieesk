🐟 Guedj, Salted Fermented Dried Fish

Guedj / guedge / kethiakh family

Gutted, scaled fish salted with coarse salt, fermented 2 to 3 days at ambient 25 to 30 °C and sun-dried 2 to 4 days, turned at midday — finished when semi-dry, firm, light brown and strongly pungent. The seasoning fish of ceebu jën.

Guedj is the salted, fermented, sun-dried fish that gives ceebu jën, soupou kandia and many sauces their depth. All production routes include washing, gutting, salting, fermenting and drying, in a variable order. Small whole fish are covered with coarse salt; large fish are opened and salted or brined. Fermentation is spontaneous at ambient temperature — 2 to 3 days at 25 to 30 °C in the 2019 review, 3 to 4 days in Fall et al. 2017, overnight to 2 days with salting in FAO’s Senegal row — then the fish is dried 2 to 4 days (FAO: 3 to 5, up to 7) on raised platforms, turned at midday. The finished product is, in FAO’s words, a semi-dry product with a strong pungent smell and a light brown colour, and it should stay whole and firm. THERE IS NO IN-PROCESS DONENESS TEST: the endpoint is the description of the finished fish plus instrumental thresholds that cannot be read in a kitchen.

Ingredients

Equipment: Basin or concrete vat, Raised drying platforms or racks

  • Fresh fish (small whole fish, or large fish opened) — quantity not printed
  • Coarse salt — no guedj-specific weight is printed; GRET et al. (1993) give 30–40% of fish weight as a general figure for traditional salted fish
  • Seawater or brine for large fish — immersion brines are generally above 30% (w/v) and often reach 80% (w/v) per the 2019 review

Method

    Prepare and salt

    1. Gut and scale the fish and wash it in clean running water; drain. Small whole fish are covered with coarse salt; large fish are opened and salted or put in brine.

    Ferment

    1. Leave the salted fish at ambient temperature for 2 to 3 days in the hottest seasons (25 to 30 °C) — the sources’ figures range from overnight to 4 days. The fermentation is spontaneous; salt is generally the only technological agent.

    Dry

    1. Dry on raised platforms in the sun for 2 to 4 days (FAO: 3 to 5, up to 7), turning the fish at midday.

    Judge the finished fish

    1. Finished guedj is semi-dry, light brown, strongly pungent, and stays whole and firm — it does not crumble. Re-dry it if it is damp; FAO notes it needs redrying to avoid spoilage and keeps about three months if well dried.

    Chef Tips

    • The only published sensory description is of the finished product: ‘a semi-dry product with a strong pungent smell and a light brown colour’ (FAO 329).
    • REJECTION THRESHOLD: a fermented product at pH 6.5 or above is considered poor quality (Yankah 1988, cited by Fall et al. 2018). Measured guedj sits at pH 6.28 to 6.56, so pH is not a progress marker.
    • TVB-N should stay below 500 mg N/100 g; the ITA reference product measured 155 to 237 against 228 to 335 for market fish.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Treating it as a casual home project: histamine, TVB-N and contaminants cannot be checked in a kitchen.
    • Drying in damp air — insects, mould and fragmentation follow when it is poorly handled.
    • Under-salting: salt in finished flesh ran 6.6–10.8% in the ITA and market samples.

    Health Benefits

    • Very salty and a protein-dense seasoning.
    • Fermented fish carries a histamine risk; the maximum permitted level cited is 20 mg/100 g.

    Cultural Note

    SAFETY, from the sources: ‘L’histamine peut provoquer des effets néfastes … allant d’irritations jusqu’à la mort en cas de consommation à forte dose’ (FAO Senegal study, cited), with a limit of 20 mg/100 g; some producers apply chemicals unfit for human consumption; fermented fish is rarely kept cold in Senegal’s processing chain. This entry documents a traditional process; buying guedj from a trusted source is the stated default.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: About three months if well dried (FAO); a well-dried high-salt product can keep 6–12 months.

    Reheating: Not applicable — a seasoning, used in small pieces.

    Serve with: Ceebu jën, Soupou kandia, Nokoss
