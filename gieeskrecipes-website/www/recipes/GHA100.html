🥫 Jollof Sauce

Jollof sauce

A litre of sauce simmered thirty to forty minutes — closer to forty with tinned tomatoes, to cook out the acidity.

The sauce the one-pot jollof is built on, and it is a separate preparation worth keeping. An onion is fried until translucent, half a tablespoon each of curry and chilli powder go in for two minutes, then ginger, garlic, an optional scotch bonnet, optional thyme, a heaped tablespoon of the jollof dry spice mix, tinned or fresh tomatoes, tomato purée, salt and stock. It is blended with a stick blender and simmered thirty to forty minutes — closer to forty if using tinned tomatoes, in order to cook out their acidity. It makes a litre and keeps three days chilled.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Stick blender, Wooden spoon, Airtight container

  • Sunflower or other cooking oil — no quantity is printed
  • 1 large onion, finely sliced
  • 1/2 tbsp curry powder
  • 1/2 tbsp chilli powder
  • 2.5 cm fresh root ginger, grated — unpeeled if organic
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 1 scotch bonnet chilli, deseeded and roughly chopped (optional)
  • 25 g thyme leaves, chopped (optional)
  • 1 heaped tbsp jollof dry spice mix
  • 1 x 400 g tin tomatoes, or 250 g fresh tomatoes, diced
  • 1 heaped tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 tsp sea salt, or to taste
  • 100 ml good quality chicken or vegetable stock

Method

  1. Fry the onion until translucent.
  2. Add the curry powder and chilli powder and cook 2 minutes.
  3. Add the ginger, garlic, scotch bonnet, thyme and the jollof dry spice mix.
  4. Add the tomatoes, tomato purée, salt and stock.
  5. Blend with a stick blender.
  6. Simmer over medium-low heat 30 to 40 minutes — closer to 40 minutes if using tinned tomatoes, in order to cook out the acidity of the tomatoes.
  7. Cool and store, or use straight away.

Chef Tips

  • Forty minutes if you use tinned tomatoes, thirty if fresh. Her reasoning is explicit — the tinned need longer to cook out their acidity — and it is the kind of distinction most recipes skip.
  • Blend AFTER everything is in and BEFORE the long simmer. A stick blender in the pan is what she specifies.
  • The oil carries no quantity at all in the source. Two tablespoons for a litre of sauce is a reasonable reading, offered as such.
  • Twenty-five grams of thyme leaves is a very large amount of fresh thyme, and it is optional. Taste before committing to all of it.
  • Keeps up to three days chilled. Make a double batch if you are planning jollof, since the one-pot takes five hundred millilitres of it.
  • This is a different sauce from chalé sauce, which underpins her groundnut soups — chalé is a raw blended base, this is a cooked and spiced sauce.
  • An imperial printing of the same recipe gives a flat forty minutes rather than the thirty-to-forty range, so forty is the safer figure.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Thirty minutes with tinned tomatoes, leaving it sharp.
  • Blending after the simmer.
  • Confusing it with chalé sauce.
  • All twenty-five grams of thyme without tasting.
  • Making only one batch when the jollof needs half a litre.

Health Benefits

  • Cooked tomato is high in lycopene, more bioavailable with fat and heat.
  • Thyme, ginger and garlic all carry documented antimicrobial compounds.
  • Vegetarian with vegetable stock, and low in calories.
  • Capsaicin from the chilli powder and scotch bonnet.

Cultural Note

Keeping a jollof sauce in the fridge is the move that makes a party dish into a weeknight one, and the distinction between thirty minutes for fresh tomatoes and forty for tinned is the sort of detail that only emerges when someone cooks the same thing hundreds of times.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Up to 3 days chilled, per the author. It freezes well.

Reheating: Gentle heat. It thickens on standing.

Serve with: Jollof One-Pot Rice, Grilled chicken, Rice
