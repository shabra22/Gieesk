🍞 Tanzanian Oven Bread

Mkate wa Sinia

Tanzanian Mkate wa Sinia — a soft, sweet, enriched Swahili bread baked in a round tin, flavoured with cardamom, coconut milk, and sesame seeds on top. The festive Zanzibar bread that signals celebration — baked for Eid, weddings, and honoured guests.

Mkate wa Sinia (literally 'tray bread') is the festive bread of Zanzibar — round, soft, slightly sweet, fragrant with cardamom, and decorated with sesame seeds on top. It is baked for Eid morning, for weddings, and whenever guests important enough to receive the finest hospitality arrive.

Ingredients

Equipment: Round baking tin (24cm), Oven (180°C)

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tsp active dry yeast
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 200ml warm coconut milk
  • ½ cup warm water
  • 2 eggs
  • 3 tbsp butter — softened

Topping

  • 1 egg — beaten
  • 2 tbsp sesame seeds

Method

  1. Dissolve yeast and 1 tbsp sugar in warm water. Rest 10 minutes until foamy.
  2. Mix flour, remaining sugar, cardamom, and salt.
  3. Beat eggs into warm coconut milk. Add to dry ingredients with yeast.
  4. Knead 8—10 minutes until smooth and elastic.
  5. Add softened butter. Knead 3 more minutes.
  6. Cover and rest 90 minutes until doubled.
  7. Shape into a round and place in greased tin.
  8. Rest 30 minutes.
  9. Brush with beaten egg. Scatter sesame seeds.
  10. Bake at 180°C for 25—30 minutes until golden.

Chef Tips

  • The dough should be slightly soft and tacky — resist adding too much flour.
  • The sesame seeds are the visual signature — be generous.
  • Cool completely before slicing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-flouring — dense bread.
  • Cutting hot.

The Science

The egg and butter in the enriched dough create a fine, soft crumb structure. The fats coat the gluten strands, preventing them from forming the tight, chewy network of lean breads — producing the characteristic soft, tender crumb of enriched breads.

Cultural Note

Mkate wa Sinia is the bread of Zanzibar's most important moments — the loaf that signals a celebration is happening. Its smell of cardamom and sesame from the oven is the smell of Eid morning in Zanzibar.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Room temperature 3 days. Freeze 2 months.

Reheating: Warm briefly in oven.

Serve with: Chai ya Tangawizi, Butter and honey, Mbaazi (TZA104)
