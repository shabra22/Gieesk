🍲 Nkatenkwan from Asamankese

Nkatenkwan

Learnt from a cook in Asamankese — palm oil with the groundnut paste until the oils start to separate, about ten okro, and a litre of hot water.

This version was learnt in Asamankese in the Eastern Region and it is the only documented groundnut soup that fries the paste in palm oil before anything else goes in: heat the palm oil, add the groundnut paste and work it until the oils start to separate, then the tomatoes, then thirty minutes of simmering. The cooked vegetables are ground and returned, and it continues until the chicken is tender and the oils collect on the surface. A litre of hot water is added. Roughly three hundred grams of groundnuts and about ten okro — most of the rest is unquantified, honestly so.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot, Blender or grinder, Wooden spoon

  • A whole chicken
  • About 300 g groundnuts, or peanut butter
  • Okro, about 10
  • 1 litre hot water
  • 1 can tomatoes
  • 3 onions
  • Root ginger
  • Scotch bonnets
  • Palm oil
  • Stock cube

To serve

  • Rice

Method

  1. Steam the chicken with the aromatics.
  2. Make the groundnut paste, if starting from whole groundnuts.
  3. Heat the palm oil and add the paste, working it until the oils start to separate.
  4. Add the tomatoes and simmer 30 minutes.
  5. Grind the cooked vegetables and return them to the pot.
  6. Add 1 litre of hot water.
  7. Continue until the chicken is tender and the oils collect on the surface.
  8. Serve with rice.

Chef Tips

  • Frying the groundnut paste in palm oil first is unique to this version, and it is the step that gives the deepest flavour of any documented groundnut soup. The oils separating at this early stage is the cue.
  • Note that the oils separate twice in this recipe — once when you fry the paste, and again at the end when they collect on the surface. Both are cues and both matter.
  • Steam the chicken rather than boiling it. Less water in at the start means a more concentrated soup.
  • About ten okro is the printed amount. It is the only documented groundnut soup besides Nkatenkwan with Okra and Eggplant to include okra.
  • Three hundred grams of groundnuts for a whole chicken sits in the middle of the documented range, and if you grind them yourself you get a far better soup than peanut butter gives.
  • Grind the cooked vegetables and return them — the same remove-blend-return technique the light soups use.
  • Most of this recipe is unquantified and that is recorded rather than filled in. Palm oil, ginger, scotch bonnet and the stock cube carry no amounts in the source.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the palm oil fry and adding the paste to liquid.
  • Boiling the chicken rather than steaming it.
  • Adding the litre of hot water before the vegetables are returned.
  • Serving before the oils collect on the surface.
  • Assuming the unquantified items are negligible — the palm oil in particular is substantial.

Health Benefits

  • Groundnuts ground fresh retain more vitamin E than commercial peanut butter.
  • Red palm oil supplies provitamin-A carotenoids.
  • Okra adds soluble fibre and folate.
  • High in protein from the whole chicken.

Cultural Note

A recipe learnt over three months in one woman's kitchen in Asamankese reads differently from one developed for a magazine — it is mostly technique and cues, with almost no numbers, because that is how it was taught. The palm-oil-first step is the kind of thing that only survives being learnt in person, and it is the single best idea in the documented record for this soup.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat with water to loosen.

Serve with: Rice, Fufu, Omo Tuo
