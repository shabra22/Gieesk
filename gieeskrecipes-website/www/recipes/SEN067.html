🥟 Pastels au Poisson

Pastel — chausson frit au poisson

Half-moon turnovers of a plain flour dough filled with sardinella or tuna cooked down with onion, garlic and tomato paste, deep-fried and served with a hot onion-tomato sauce.

90 minTotal time
45 minPrep
30 minCook
6Servings
310Calories
MediumDifficulty

The page that fixes the vocabulary: in Senegal the traditional pastel is a small fried turnover filled with fish, most often sardinella or tuna, and nearly always served with a lightly spiced tomato sauce of onions and chilli that is ‘an integral part of the tasting’. A fataya, by the same page, is generally the same turnover filled with minced beef — though in practice names vary by family and neighbourhood, and some people use the two words interchangeably. The dough here is the plainest of the four printed in this collection: flour, water, a little sugar and milk powder, salt, no egg and no fat. Fish and onions are softened in oil with garlic and tomato paste, the dough is rolled out, filled, folded to half-moons, sealed with a fork and fried; the sauce is onions cooked down with tomato paste and chilli.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, Saucepan or frying pan, Rolling pin, Round cutter or glass, Fork, Deep pan for frying

    Dough

    • 500 g flour
    • 250 to 300 ml water
    • 20 g sugar
    • 2 tbsp milk powder
    • 1/2 tsp salt

    Filling

    • 1 large tin sardinella (or tuna), about 400 g
    • 2 large onions
    • 3 cloves garlic
    • 2 tbsp tomato paste
    • A little chilli
    • Salt, pepper and seasoning cube — quantities not printed
    • Oil for softening the onions — quantity not printed

    Sauce

    • 3 large onions
    • 2 tbsp tomato paste
    • A little chilli
    • Salt, pepper and seasoning cube — quantities not printed

    Frying

    • Oil for deep-frying — quantity not printed

    Method

      Filling

      1. Soften the onions in oil. Add the flaked sardinella (or tuna), the garlic and the tomato paste with a little water, and simmer. Season with salt, pepper, chilli and the seasoning cube. Let it cool.

      Dough

      1. Mix the flour, milk powder, sugar and salt. Add the water gradually until the dough is soft and firm but not sticky. Rest it briefly (no time printed).

      Shaping

      1. Flatten small portions into circles. Put a spoonful of filling on each, fold into half-moons and seal the edges with a fork.

      Frying

      1. Deep-fry over medium heat until golden on both sides. Drain.

      Sauce

      1. Cook the onions with the tomato paste and chilli, add a little water, and simmer until smooth. Season.
      2. Serve the pastels hot with the sauce and, if you like, a fresh salad.

      Chef Tips

      • The dough target is ‘soft, firm, not sticky’ — add the water gradually rather than all at once.
      • The page prints neither a frying temperature nor a frying time. Judge by the colour: golden on both sides.
      • Seal the edges firmly with a fork so the filling does not escape in the oil.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Adding all the water at once and ending with a sticky dough.
      • Frying over very high heat, which colours the dough before it is cooked.
      • Under-seasoning the filling — the sauce is meant to be spicy but the filling carries the fish.

      Cultural Note

      The page’s own distinction — pastel for fish, fataya for minced meat — is the one most Senegalese cooks would recognise, but the source says outright that usage varies by family and neighbourhood. Fataya is of Lebanese origin; the Mbaye pages in this research say it has become so embedded in Senegalese cooking that its origin is forgotten.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Best eaten the day they are fried; keeps 1 day in the fridge.

      Reheating: Recrisp in a hot oven for 8 to 10 minutes.

      Serve with: Hot onion-tomato sauce, Fresh salad, Sosu kaani
