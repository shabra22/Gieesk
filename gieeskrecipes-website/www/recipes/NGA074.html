🦀 Fisherman's Soup

Fisherman soup

Everything fresh goes in at once for five minutes, then okra, then palm oil, then greens — four timed stages and no quantities given anywhere.

Fisherman's soup is exactly what the name says: a pot of fresh fish, crabs, shrimps and smoked fish cooked quickly together, with okra for body and ugu or spinach at the end. No source consulted gives a single quantity for it, which is recorded here rather than papered over — what survives is the sequence and the timings, and those are clear. Five minutes for the seafood, five to seven for the okra, five for the palm oil, five to seven for the greens. The origin is also contested in a way worth knowing about.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Sharp knife, Ladle

  • Fresh fish
  • Fresh crabs
  • Dried or smoked fish, or both
  • Stockfish (optional)
  • Shrimps
  • Dried crayfish
  • Pepper
  • Fresh spinach or pumpkin leaves (ugwu)
  • Fresh basil leaves
  • Onions, chopped into large chunks
  • Palm oil
  • Okra, sliced
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Chop the onions into large chunks. Chop the peppers and the basil.
  2. Add the fresh fish, shrimps, crabs, boiled dry fish, onions, peppers and crayfish to the pot and cook 5 minutes.
  3. Add the sliced okra and cook 5 to 7 minutes, until done.
  4. Add the palm oil and cook 5 minutes.
  5. Add the chopped spinach or ugwu with salt and cook 5 to 7 minutes.
  6. Serve hot, optionally with garri or pounded yam.

Chef Tips

  • Large chunks of onion, not diced. In a soup this quick they are there as a vegetable, not a base.
  • Everything fresh goes in together. There is no browning stage and no stock — the seafood makes its own broth in five minutes.
  • Okra after the seafood, not with it. It needs five to seven minutes and the fish does not.
  • Fresh basil, chopped, at the start with the peppers. It is the aromatic signature of the dish.
  • No quantities exist for this soup in any source consulted. Build it by eye: enough fish and shellfish to crowd the pot, a couple of handfuls of okra, a cooking spoon of palm oil.
  • Judge the salt only at the end. Smoked fish, dried fish and crayfish all bring their own and it is easy to overshoot.
  • Serve it loose. This is a soup you can eat with a spoon as much as with a swallow.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the okra with the seafood.
  • Dicing the onion.
  • Salting at the start.
  • Cooking the fresh fish longer than five minutes before the okra goes in.
  • Letting the greens go past seven minutes.

Health Benefits

  • Exceptionally high in lean protein, iodine, selenium and omega-3 fats.
  • Okra mucilage is a soluble fibre.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.
  • Crab and periwinkle-family shellfish are rich in iron and zinc.

Cultural Note

Fisherman's soup is the dish of a coast where the catch decides the menu — there is no fixed ingredient list because there was never meant to be one, and the absence of quantities in the written record may be less a gap than an accurate reflection of how it is cooked. Whether it came up from São Tomé or grew in the Cross River estuary is not settled by anything in print.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. Seafood soups do not keep well.

Reheating: Low heat, briefly. Overheating ruins the shellfish.

Serve with: Eba or garri, Iyan, On its own with a spoon

But this named Nigerian newspaper states it 'originates from São Tomé and Príncipe… and has become popular in Nigeria, particularly in Akwa Ibom State.' That is a substantive claim about a non-Nigerian origin from a named source, and it conflicts with the usual framing. It is filed here as Akwa Ibom, following the source, with the conflict stated.
