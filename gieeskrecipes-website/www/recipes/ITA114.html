🧀 Frico Friulano

Frico

A rustic cake of shredded potatoes, onions, and Montasio cheese, pan-fried until the outside turns deeply golden and crisp while the cheese inside stays meltingly soft.

Frico comes from Friuli-Venezia Giulia in Italy's northeast corner, where it began as a way to use up aging cheese rinds, and has since evolved into this beloved potato-and-cheese cake, crisp outside and irresistibly gooey within.

Ingredients

Equipment: Non-stick skillet, Plate for flipping

  • 600 g (1.3 lb) potatoes, peeled and grated
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 400 g (14 oz) Montasio cheese (or a mix of aged and young), grated
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter, divided
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Squeeze the grated potatoes well to remove excess moisture.
  2. Melt half the butter in a non-stick skillet and sauté the onion until soft and golden.
  3. Add the grated potatoes to the pan with the onion, season, and press down into an even layer.
  4. Cook over medium heat for 8–10 minutes until the underside is golden and crisp.
  5. Scatter the grated cheese evenly over the potatoes.
  6. Continue cooking until the cheese begins to melt and the bottom is deeply golden, about 5 more minutes.
  7. Carefully flip the frico using a large plate, then slide it back into the pan to crisp the second side.
  8. Cook for another 8–10 minutes until both sides are deeply golden and crisp.
  9. Slide onto a board, let rest for a couple of minutes, then slice into wedges and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Squeezing as much moisture as possible from the grated potatoes is key to achieving a properly crisp result.
  • A good non-stick pan makes the flipping process far easier and less risky.
  • Montasio is traditional, but a mix of any good melting and aged cheeses works well as a substitute.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the moisture-squeezing step, resulting in a soggy rather than crisp frico.
  • Flipping too early, before the bottom has properly set and crisped.
  • Using only mild, low-flavor cheese, which misses the savory depth good frico depends on.

Cultural Note

Frico is a defining dish of Friuli-Venezia Giulia, historically made by shepherds and farmers as a way to use up cheese scraps, now celebrated as a regional specialty in its own right.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; refrigerate leftovers for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat in a dry skillet to help re-crisp; the microwave will leave it soft and greasy.

Serve with: Soft polenta, A simple green salad
