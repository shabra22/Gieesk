🎣 Taraaqad

Taraaqad — Nguurhu

Kingfish poached in a shallow broth of onion, garlic and sesame oil, sharpened with white vinegar at the last second — the Bravanese way with fish, and about as plain as good cooking gets.

The Bravanese are said to have more than two hundred names for fish, which tells you how central it is to their diet — served at breakfast, lunch and supper without ever feeling repetitive, because fish takes so many forms. This recipe is characteristic of the town's cooking: five minutes of preparation, one pan, and nothing to hide behind. The vinegar goes in off the heat, where it stays bright.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan with a lid, Fish slice

  • 4 kingfish steaks or fillets, about 2.5 cm thick
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp light sesame oil (untoasted), or extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 1/2 tsp vegetable seasoning salt, or a crumbled stock cube
  • 480 ml (2 cups) water
  • 1 tbsp white vinegar
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper

Method

  1. Soften the diced onion in the sesame oil over medium heat until translucent.
  2. Add the minced garlic and cook for a further minute.
  3. Add the vegetable seasoning and the water, cover the pan and bring to a boil.
  4. Slide in the fish, cover again, and cook for 10 minutes, until it flakes at the touch of a fork.
  5. Add the black pepper and the vinegar, then turn off the heat immediately.
  6. Serve with the pan broth spooned over, alongside maanda to soak it up.

Chef Tips

  • Untoasted sesame oil is the traditional fat here and tastes quite different from the dark toasted kind, which would overwhelm the fish. Olive oil is the better substitute.
  • The two tablespoons of oil are part of the dish, not something to drain off — they emulsify into the broth.
  • Cod and other firm white fish work in place of kingfish, with the same timing.
  • Cooking the onions first and boiling the liquid before the fish goes in means the fish spends the shortest possible time in the pan.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking, which is the only real way to ruin this dish — the fish turns tough and no amount of broth rescues it.
  • Adding the vinegar while the pan is still on the heat, which cooks off its sharpness.
  • Using dark toasted sesame oil, which tastes nothing like the untoasted oil the recipe intends.
  • Putting the fish into cold liquid and bringing it up together, so it poaches far longer than 10 minutes.

Health Benefits

  • Poached rather than fried, so it stays low in fat.
  • High in protein, omega-3 fatty acids and vitamin B12.
  • The broth carries most of the seasoning, so little salt is needed on the plate.

Cultural Note

Fish is at the centre of the Bravanese diet at every meal of the day, and the town's recipes are characteristically simple and straightforward — the result of having fish fresh enough that elaboration would be a waste of it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate in its broth up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm gently in the broth on the hob, just until hot through.

Serve with: Muufo Baraawe, Bariis Iskukaris, Basbaas
