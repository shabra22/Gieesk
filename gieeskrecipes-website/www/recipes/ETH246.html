🫓 Dorze Injera (Chencha)

ዶርዜ እንጀራ — Dorze Injera

Dorze Injera — the famous giant injera of the Dorze people, made from teff with a proportion of enset starch. The Dorze injera is so large it is said to last three days — eaten from the edges inward. The master weavers' bread.

Dorze injera is famous across Ethiopia for its size — made on a very large mitad, it is served folded and enough for a family meal. The addition of enset starch (from the false banana) gives it a distinctive chewiness.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Dorze people — around Chencha in the Omo Valley highlands

The Dorze people are famous across Ethiopia for two things: their extraordinary weaving skills (Dorze cotton cloth is among the finest in Ethiopia) and their injera made from the Dorze banana-leaf preserved enset mixed with teff. Dorze injera is significantly larger than standard injera — so large it is said one Dorze injera lasts three days as the outer edges are eaten while the centre remains fresh. The Dorze live in remarkable beehive-shaped bamboo houses that can be moved, and their food reflects both their ingenuity and their highland Omo Valley location.

Ingredients

Equipment: Very large mitad or large flat pan

    Make as standard injera (ETH001) with 20% enset starch added

      Or: 2.5 cups teff flour + ½ cup enset/arrowroot starch

      • 2.5 cups teff flour
      • ½ cup arrowroot starch
      • 3 cups water
      • Starter from previous batch

      Method

      1. Mix teff flour and starch. Add water and starter.
      2. Ferment 2—3 days as per ETH001.
      3. Cook on the largest possible mitad — the bigger the injera the more authentic.
      4. Cook one side only as per standard injera method.

      Chef Tips

      • The Dorze injera is massive — use the largest available cooking surface.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Standard size — misses the point of Dorze tradition.

      Cultural Note

      Dorze injera is a statement of abundance — the weaver people who create some of Ethiopia's finest cloth also create Ethiopia's largest injera. Their hospitality is expressed in size.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Keeps fresh 3 days at room temperature.

      Reheating: Eat at room temperature.

      Serve with: With any Ethiopian stew, At Dorze celebrations
