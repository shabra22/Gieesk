🌱 Tanzanian Boiled Arrowroot

Nduma za Kuchemsha

Nduma za Kuchemsha — arrowroot boiled until tender and served with honey, salt, or coconut cream. An ancient Tanzanian breakfast tuber — mildly flavoured, easily digestible, and deeply traditional in the northern highlands.

Arrowroot (nduma) is one of Tanzania's oldest cultivated foods — grown on the fertile slopes of Kilimanjaro and in highland areas. Boiled until its characteristic mildly earthy, slightly floury flesh is perfectly tender, nduma is one of the most easily digestible carbohydrates available.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan

  • 600g fresh arrowroot — scrubbed
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Honey or salt to serve

Method

  1. Scrub arrowroot. Halve if large.
  2. Place in pot with cold salted water.
  3. Bring to a boil. Cook 20—25 minutes until a fork slides in easily.
  4. Drain. Peel (skins peel easily after cooking).
  5. Serve with honey or salt.

Chef Tips

  • Cook with skin on — peels easily after.
  • Do not overcook — becomes gluey.
  • A pinch of salt on the served arrowroot sharpens its mild flavour.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking — gluey texture.

The Science

Arrowroot starch gelatinises at a lower temperature than potato starch, making it very easily digestible. This is why arrowroot has traditionally been used as food for the sick and the very young.

Health Benefits

  • One of the most easily digestible starches.
  • Good for sensitive stomachs.
  • Gluten-free and hypoallergenic.

Cultural Note

Nduma represent Tanzania's ancient agricultural heritage — one of the oldest cultivated tubers in the East African highlands, still eaten daily in the communities that have grown them for centuries.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Steam briefly.

Serve with: Chai ya Tangawizi, Honey, As a standalone breakfast
