🍢 Muchomo

Muchomo

Small pieces of beef marinated in the juice of smashed onions, threaded onto sticks and roasted over coals damped with ash so the heat goes deep instead of just crisping the outside.

Muchomo is not nyama choma. The Kenyan dish is a slab sold by weight at a butchery; muchomo is small pieces on sticks, sold at roadside stalls along the major routes and in the Kampala suburbs. The marinade is the surprise: no spice blend, just the juice of onions smashed to release it, sometimes ginger liquid, and for chicken nothing but salt. The other real technique is sprinkling ash on the charcoal — it drops the heat and slows it, so the inside cooks before the outside is done.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill or sigiri, Skewers or sticks, Mortar, or a heavy bowl, for smashing the onions

  • 1 kg beef, cut into small bite-sized pieces

The marinade

  • 3 large onions, smashed for their juice
  • A thumb of ginger, smashed for its liquid (optional)
  • Salt

Optional, to speed the cooking

  • Juice of 1/2 a pineapple, or of 1 lemon

For the fire

  • Charcoal
  • A handful of wood ash

Also good this way

  • Chicken pieces, salted only
  • Sausages

Method

  1. Smash the onions hard in a mortar or bowl until they give up their juice, and strain the juice off.
  2. Do the same with the ginger if you are using it.
  3. Toss the beef pieces in the onion juice with salt, and the pineapple or lemon juice if you want it to cook faster.
  4. Leave to marinate about 30 minutes.
  5. Light the charcoal and let it burn down to embers.
  6. Sprinkle a handful of ash over the coals to damp the heat down — this gives a slower, deeper heat.
  7. Thread the beef onto sticks, packed but not crushed together.
  8. Roast over the ashed coals, turning often, until done the way you want it — well done, medium, or completely dry.
  9. Serve straight off the fire.

Chef Tips

  • Ash on the coals is the technique that matters. Too much heat gives you a crunchy exterior that fools you while the inside is still raw — the ash gives the slow, deep heat you want.
  • The marinade is onion juice, not onion. Smash them and use the liquid; the pieces would just burn on the stick.
  • For chicken, only salt is used and no other spice. That is the documented practice and it is worth respecting.
  • Pineapple or lemon juice tenderises and speeds it up, and it is optional rather than standard.
  • Small pieces on sticks. That is what makes it muchomo rather than nyama choma, and it changes how it eats.
  • Customers order it by doneness — well done, medium, or completely dry — so ask before you cook for people.
  • No source names the cut of beef used. Stalls say only beef, so pick something that suits fast, hot cooking and know that this detail is genuinely undocumented.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking it over full-heat coals with no ash.
  • Using chopped onion instead of onion juice.
  • Spicing the chicken version.
  • Cutting the pieces too large, so they char before they cook.
  • Turning it only once.

Health Benefits

  • Beef gives protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
  • Grilled over coals, so a good deal of the fat renders away.
  • The marinade is onion juice and salt — no sugar and no oil.
  • Naturally free of gluten and dairy.

Cultural Note

Muchomo is sold along the roads out of Kampala — Lukaya, Namawojjolo, Nakulabye — and buying it is part of a journey rather than a meal. The word is Swahili and traditionally signals the act of celebration that follows a victorious finish, which is roughly the mood at a stall on a Friday evening.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately. Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Briefly over coals or in a hot pan. It dries out fast.

Serve with: Gonja, Kasooli, A cold beer
