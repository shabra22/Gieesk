🍜 Chicken Light Soup

Nkrakra

Everything into the pot at once, boiled hard twenty minutes, then the aromatics lifted out and blended back — and torn basil at the end.

The simplest documented light soup, and the only one credited to a named home cook with a named narrator: everything goes into the pot together — a whole soup chicken, four tomatoes, two onions, garlic, chillies, ginger and a litre and a half of water — and boils hard for twenty minutes. Then the tomato, onion, garlic, chilli and ginger are removed, blended and returned, and the soup cooks on until the chicken is tender. A small handful of torn basil goes in at the end, off the boil, which no other version includes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Slotted spoon

  • 1 whole soup chicken, chopped
  • 4 large tomatoes
  • 2 medium onions
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 2 chillies
  • 1 root ginger
  • 2 tsp chicken stock, or fish, beef or vegetable
  • 1.5 litres water
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • A small handful of torn basil

Optional

  • Vegetables, other meats, fresh or smoked fish

Method

  1. Combine everything except the basil in a pot.
  2. Boil on high for 20 minutes.
  3. Remove the tomato, onion, garlic, chilli and ginger.
  4. Blend them smooth and return them to the pot.
  5. Cook until the chicken is tender.
  6. Lower the heat, stir in the torn basil, and simmer a few minutes.
  7. Season and serve.

Chef Tips

  • A soup chicken, not a roasting bird — an older, tougher hen with enough collagen to give the broth body. It is the single biggest difference between a good light soup and a thin one.
  • Everything in at once, twenty minutes on high. The blending-and-returning happens afterwards, which is simpler than the marinate-first method and gives a very similar soup.
  • Torn basil at the end, off the boil. It is unique to this version among the documented light soups and it lifts the whole pot.
  • Blend the aromatics smooth and sieve them if you want it genuinely clear. This version does not sieve, so it is slightly thicker than a market light soup.
  • Until the chicken is tender, which for a real soup chicken can be a long time. The twenty minutes is only the first stage.
  • The stock is given as two teaspoons of powder or cube. With a proper soup chicken you can leave it out entirely.
  • Fresh or smoked fish is offered as an addition. Smoked fish turns this into a different and very good soup, but add it late — it falls apart.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a young roasting chicken.
  • Boiling the basil rather than stirring it in off the heat.
  • Skipping the remove-blend-return step and just simmering everything.
  • Stopping at twenty minutes, before the chicken is tender.
  • Adding smoked fish at the start, so it disintegrates.

Health Benefits

  • No added oil.
  • A long-simmered whole chicken gives collagen and minerals that a breast-only broth does not.
  • High in protein and very low in carbohydrate.
  • Basil contributes eugenol and other aromatic antioxidants.

Cultural Note

That this recipe comes with a narrator as well as a cook is worth noticing — it was recorded as someone describing how they make it, which is why it reads as a sequence of plain actions rather than a tested formula. The basil is the kind of detail that only survives when a recipe is taken down from a person rather than compiled from other recipes.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Simmer gently. Add fresh basil rather than relying on the cooked-in leaves.

Serve with: Fufu, Omo Tuo, Rice
