Shaah Cadays

Somalia's beloved spiced milk tea, black tea simmered with cardamom, cinnamon, and cloves until deeply fragrant, then sweetened generously and served throughout the day as a gesture of hospitality.

Shaah cadays is woven into the rhythm of daily Somali life, brewed fresh multiple times a day and always offered to guests as a first gesture of welcome, its warm spice blend reflecting the same Indian Ocean trade routes that shaped so much of Somali cuisine.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Fine strainer

  • 750 ml (3 cups) water
  • 3 black tea bags or 3 tbsp loose black tea
  • 4 cardamom pods, crushed
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3 whole cloves
  • 250 ml (1 cup) whole milk
  • 3-4 tbsp sugar, or to taste

Method

  1. Bring the water to a boil in a saucepan with the cardamom, cinnamon, and cloves.
  2. Reduce to a simmer and let the spices infuse for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the tea and simmer for another 3-4 minutes until deeply colored.
  4. Add the milk and sugar, and bring back to a gentle simmer, stirring.
  5. Simmer for another 2-3 minutes until well combined and fragrant.
  6. Strain into cups and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Simmering the whole spices in water first, before adding the tea, allows their flavor to fully infuse.
  • Adjust the sugar to taste - Somali tea is traditionally served quite sweet.
  • This tea is a genuine gesture of hospitality - always offered warmly to guests.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the milk too early, before the spices have had a chance to properly infuse the water.
  • Boiling too vigorously once the milk is added, which can cause it to curdle or scorch.
  • Using pre-ground spices instead of whole ones, which provide a less aromatic result.

Health Benefits

  • Cardamom and cinnamon offer some antioxidant properties; best enjoyed with moderate sugar.

Cultural Note

Shaah cadays is a fundamental part of Somali hospitality, traditionally the first thing offered to any guest entering a home, brewed fresh multiple times throughout the day.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best made fresh; does not keep well once prepared.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, taking care not to boil.

Serve with: Sabaayad, Xalwo, Kac Kac
