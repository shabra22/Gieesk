🦐 Thiebou Diola, the Casamance ‘C’est Bon’

Thiebou diola

Grilled sea bream and prawns on rice fried with dried toufa, yokhos and shrimp, topped with a pepper-onion marinade and a crushed okra-and-bissap-leaf beugueuthie.

2h 30mTotal time
30mPrep
1hCook
4Servings
620Calories
HardDifficulty

The page attributes this dish explicitly to the Diola community of Casamance. A sea bream marinates in lemon, garlic, green chile, pepper and mustard and is grilled, pan-cooked or oven-cooked until nicely browned. Cooked rice is fried with 50 g each of toufa and yokhos (dried seafood) and small shrimp. An onion sauce cooks a few minutes with lime; a raw pepper-and-onion marinade rests 30 minutes; okra boils 10 minutes and green bissap leaves 5, crushed together into the beugueuthie. Prawns are pan-cooked. Everything is stacked on the rice and served with lime and, optionally, palm oil.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grill, pan or oven, Frying pan, Pot for the okra and bissap leaves, Large serving dish

    Fish

    • 1 sea bream
    • Juice of 1 lemon
    • 1 garlic clove, grated
    • 1 green chile, chopped
    • 1/2 tsp black pepper
    • 1/2 tsp mustard
    • A drizzle of peanut oil

    Rice

    • 500 g cooked white rice
    • 50 g toufa (dried seafood)
    • 50 g yokhos (dried oysters)
    • 50 g small dried or fresh shrimp
    • Salt — quantity not printed

    Onion sauce

    • 1 medium onion, chopped
    • 1 small green bell pepper, diced
    • 1 fresh chile, diced
    • 1 tsp chopped parsley
    • Juice of 1 lime
    • 1 garlic clove, grated
    • Salt, pepper, peanut oil — quantities not printed

    Onion and pepper marinade

    • 1 green pepper, brunoise
    • 1 red pepper, cubed
    • 1 yellow pepper, brunoise
    • 2 small onions, brunoise
    • 1 pinch salt
    • 1 tbsp mustard
    • Juice of 1/2 lime

    Beugueuthie

    • 100 g green bissap leaves
    • 3 okra
    • 1 pinch salt

    To serve

    • 200 g prawns
    • Lime — quantity not printed
    • Palm oil (optional) — quantity not printed

    Method

    1. Mix the lemon juice, garlic, green chile, pepper and mustard for the fish marinade.
    2. Brush the sea bream with the marinade, cover and rest 30 minutes (the page header says 1 hour of marinating; the method says 30 — both are recorded).
    3. Cook the fish over coals, in a pan or in the oven with a drizzle of peanut oil, until nicely browned. No time or temperature is printed.
    4. Fry the cooked rice with the toufa, yokhos and small shrimp. Add salt if needed and keep warm.
    5. Sauté the onion in a little oil, add the green pepper, chile, parsley, garlic, salt and pepper. Cook a few minutes, add the lime juice and let it reduce slightly. Set aside.
    6. Mix the peppers, onions, salt, mustard and lime juice and marinate 30 minutes.
    7. Boil the okra in salted water 10 minutes and drain.
    8. Boil the green bissap 5 minutes, drain and salt. Chop or coarsely crush it with the okra to make the beugueuthie.
    9. Cook the prawns in a pan with a little peanut oil, salt and pepper.
    10. Spread the rice on a large dish. Top with the onion and pepper marinade, the beugueuthie, the okra, the prawns and the grilled fish.
    11. Serve with lime and, if wanted, palm oil.

    Chef Tips

    • ‘Nicely browned’ is the one fish cue; no time is printed for the fish or the prawns.
    • Toufa and yokhos are bought, dried and called ‘lightly fermented’ by the page, which gives no process for them; small dried shrimp, smoked fish or shrimp powder are the printed substitutes.
    • Fry the dried seafood with the rice, not in the sauce.
    • Beugueuthie is the bissap-leaf and okra mash; the page also gives ‘beugueudj’ as a Casamance name for bissap sauce elsewhere.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cooking the prawns without a cue.
    • Skipping the raw pepper-and-onion marinade.

    Cultural Note

    ‘Thiebou diola is specific to the Diola community of Casamance, which is one of the Senegalese ethnic groups,’ says the page — the only entry in this collection in which a source explicitly attributes a dish to the Jola/Diola. The attribution is the source’s own. The page adds that the dish is less known than ceebu jen.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 1 day refrigerated.

    Reheating: Reheat the rice in a pan; serve the marinade and beugueuthie cold.

    Serve with: Lime wedges, Palm oil
