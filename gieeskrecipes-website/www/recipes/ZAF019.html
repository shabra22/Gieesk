🍩 Koe'sisters

Koe'sister — koesister

Yeasted dough heavy with aniseed, cinnamon, ginger, cardamom and naartjie zest, fried into soft oblongs, soaked in boiling spiced syrup and rolled in coconut — Sunday morning in the Cape, and not the plaited koeksister.

This is the koe'sister, and it is a different dish from the Afrikaans koeksister despite the near-identical name. The koeksister is a plait of unspiced dough, fried and plunged into ice-cold syrup, and it is crisp. The koe'sister is a spiced yeast bun, fried and soaked in syrup while both are hot, then rolled in desiccated coconut, and it is soft. Conflating them is a standing irritation in Cape Town, and the two have separate histories.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Deep heavy pot, for frying, Large deep pan, for the syrup, Slotted spoon, Kitchen scales

    The dough

    • 625 g cake flour
    • 3 tbsp aniseed
    • 2 1/2 tbsp ground cinnamon
    • 2 tbsp instant yeast
    • 1 tbsp ground ginger
    • 1 tbsp ground cardamom
    • 1 tbsp naartjie (tangerine) zest
    • 1 tsp baking powder
    • 1 tsp salt
    • 300 ml (1 1/4 cups) water
    • 240 ml (1 cup) milk
    • 3 tbsp butter
    • 4 tbsp sugar
    • 1 large free-range egg

    To fry and finish

    • 2 L oil, for deep-frying
    • 300 g desiccated coconut

    The syrup

    • 710 ml (3 cups) water
    • 600 g sugar
    • 3 cinnamon sticks
    • 3 cardamom pods
    • 3 pieces naartjie peel, fresh or dried

    Method

    1. Whisk all the dry dough ingredients together in a large bowl — flour, aniseed, cinnamon, yeast, ginger, cardamom, zest, baking powder and salt.
    2. Warm the water, milk and butter together for 35 to 40 seconds in a microwave, then stir in the sugar until it dissolves. It should be lukewarm, not hot — hot liquid kills the yeast.
    3. Whisk the egg. Add half the wet mixture to the dry ingredients, add the egg, and stir. Add the rest of the wet mixture and mix by hand to a sticky dough. Oil your hands and work the dough with them to make it manageable.
    4. Cover with clingfilm and leave to rise for 1 hour 30 minutes, or until doubled.
    5. Flour a surface, cut the dough into 40 g pieces and shape each into an oblong with greased hands. Rest them 10 minutes.
    6. Meanwhile make the syrup: put the water, sugar, cinnamon sticks, cardamom and naartjie peel in a large deep pan over high heat and boil for 20 to 25 minutes, until syrupy. Set aside.
    7. Heat the oil in a deep pot and fry the koe'sisters in batches until golden brown, 2 to 3 minutes a side. Drain on kitchen paper.
    8. Bring the syrup back to a hard boil and soak the fried koe'sisters in it for about a minute a side.
    9. Scatter coconut on a plate, lay the syrupy koe'sisters on it and sprinkle more coconut over the top. Serve with coffee or tea.

    Chef Tips

    • The dough is meant to be sticky, not smooth. A dough kneaded until smooth gives dense koe'sisters rather than light, airy ones. Oil your hands instead of adding flour.
    • The syrup is ready when it drips slowly off a spoon and bubbles thickly. Add 4 tablespoons of water after each batch of soaking to keep it at that consistency.
    • Both the syrup and the koe'sisters need to be hot when they meet. This is the opposite of the koeksister, where hot dough goes into ice-cold syrup.
    • For darker koe'sisters, increase the sugar and the cinnamon in the dough.
    • They freeze well fried and un-soaked. Thaw, then soak in fresh hot syrup to serve.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Kneading the dough smooth, which is the single commonest reason they come out heavy.
    • Using hot rather than lukewarm liquid, which kills the yeast before the dough ever rises.
    • Soaking them in syrup that has cooled, so it sits on the surface instead of soaking in.
    • Letting the syrup reduce too far between batches without adding water back, after which it candies rather than soaks.
    • Calling them koeksisters. Different dough, different syrup temperature, different dish.

    Health Benefits

    • Portioned at 40 g each, so the batch of fifty-five is built for sharing rather than sitting.
    • The aniseed, cardamom and ginger in the dough are traditionally used to aid digestion.
    • Egg and milk in the dough give a little protein.

    Cultural Note

    Koe'sisters are Sunday-morning food in the Cape, bought or made early and eaten with coffee, and they are a fixture of Ramadan and Eid tables. The name probably shares a root with the Malay kuih, meaning cake or sweet — another word that travelled to the Cape with the people who were taken there.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Airtight at room temperature 2 days. Freeze them fried but un-soaked for a month.

    Reheating: Warm briefly and re-soak in hot syrup, then re-coat in coconut.

    Serve with: Strong coffee, Tea, Boeber, at Ramadan
