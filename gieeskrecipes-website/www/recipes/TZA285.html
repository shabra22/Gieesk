🍳 Tanzanian Café Shakshuka

Shakshuka ya Tanzania

Tanzanian Shakshuka — eggs poached in a rich Tanzanian spiced tomato sauce with coconut milk, pili pili, and fresh coriander. The North African classic completely reinvented through the Zanzibar spice palette — deeper, richer, and more fragrant than any other version.

Tanzanian shakshuka takes the Middle Eastern-North African classic and passes it through the Zanzibar spice kitchen — the addition of coconut milk, cardamom, and tamarind creates a sauce of unexpected depth and complexity. Served in the same pan it was cooked in, with warm chapati for dipping, it is one of the finest brunch dishes in modern Tanzania.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide oven-safe frying pan or cast iron skillet

  • 6 large eggs

Tanzanian Shakshuka Sauce

  • 600g ripe tomatoes — diced (or 1 tin)
  • 1 red and 1 green pepper — diced
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — sliced
  • 100ml coconut milk
  • 1 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tsp pili pili flakes
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1.5 tsp salt

Finish: ¼ cup coriander, lime zest, crumbled feta (optional)

    Method

    1. Heat olive oil. Cook onions and peppers until softened — 8 minutes.
    2. Add garlic, cumin, paprika, cardamom, and pili pili. Stir 1 minute.
    3. Add tomatoes, tamarind, and sugar. Simmer 10 minutes until thick and rich.
    4. Stir in coconut milk. Simmer 3 more minutes.
    5. Season. Taste — the sauce should be rich, slightly tart, and fragrant.
    6. Make 6 wells in the sauce. Crack one egg into each well.
    7. Cover and cook on low heat 6—8 minutes until whites are set but yolks still runny.
    8. Scatter coriander and lime zest. Crumble feta if using.
    9. Serve immediately in the pan.

    Chef Tips

    • The sauce must be thick before adding eggs — thin sauce produces watery whites.
    • Cover the pan — the trapped steam sets the whites from the top.
    • Watch carefully at the end — the difference between perfect and over-cooked eggs is 1 minute.
    • Serve in the pan — it retains heat and makes the best presentation.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Thin sauce — watery whites.
    • Over-cooking eggs — rubbery yolks.
    • Not covering during egg cooking.

    The Science

    The coconut milk addition creates a sauce that is simultaneously acidic (from tomatoes and tamarind) and rich in fat — this combination causes partial emulsification, producing a sauce with a smoother, more velvety texture than a plain tomato shakshuka. The fat also carries the fat-soluble aromatic compounds from the spices more effectively.

    Cultural Note

    Tanzanian shakshuka is the dish that represents the modern Dar es Salaam café scene at its best — globally aware, locally rooted, and executing at a level that any international café would be proud of.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Sauce keeps 4 days. Add eggs to order.

    Reheating: Reheat sauce. Add fresh eggs.

    Serve with: Warm chapati (TZA004), Sourdough bread, Chai ya Tangawizi (TZA221)
