🥟 Beef Samosas

Samosa — Sambusa

A slack near-batter pastry cooked into sheets and dried, filled with minced beef and four onions seasoned with beef masala, folded into triangles and deep-fried.

The samosa came to Uganda with the Gujarati trading community and is now completely naturalised — sold on every street, at every function, and called sambusa as often as samosa. The pastry here is not a short pastry: 600 ml of water to 500 g of flour makes a slack, almost pourable dough that is cooked into thin sheets and then dried before filling, which is the East African wrapper method. The beef masala as a single pre-blended ingredient is characteristically East African rather than Indian.

Ingredients

Equipment: Flat pan or griddle, Deep pan for frying, Slotted spoon, Small bowl, for the sealing paste

    The pastry

    • 500 g wheat flour
    • 5 ml (1 tsp) salt
    • 600 ml water
    • Oil and flour, for brushing

    The filling

    • 500 g minced beef, washed
    • 4 large onions, finely chopped
    • 2 cloves garlic, finely grated
    • 5 ml (1 tsp) ginger, finely grated
    • 60 ml (4 tbsp) coriander, chopped
    • 5 ml (1 tsp) beef masala
    • 60 ml (4 tbsp) water
    • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) salt
    • 1 to 2 green chillies, finely chopped (optional)

    The sealing paste

    • 60 ml (4 tbsp) wheat flour
    • 45 to 60 ml (3 to 4 tbsp) warm water

    To fry

    • Oil, for deep frying

    Method

      The filling

      1. Cook the minced beef with the garlic, ginger, beef masala, salt and the 4 tbsp of water until the meat is done and the liquid has gone.
      2. Stir in the chopped onions, coriander and chilli, and let the onions soften slightly. Set aside to cool completely.

      The pastry

      1. Mix the flour and salt with the 600 ml of water into a slack, smooth batter-like dough and knead it as well as you can.
      2. Cook it in thin sheets on a flat pan, brushing with oil and dusting with flour.
      3. Cut the sheets into strips or circles and let them dry — this is what stops them tearing when you fold.

      Assembly

      1. Mix the 4 tbsp of flour with warm water into a thick sealing paste.
      2. Fill each pastry piece with the cooled filling and fold into a triangle, sealing the last edge with the paste.

      Frying

      1. Deep-fry in batches until golden and crisp.
      2. Drain and serve hot.

      Chef Tips

      • Dry the pastry sheets before you fill them. This is the step people skip and it is why their samosas burst in the oil.
      • Cool the filling completely. Warm filling steams inside the pastry and weakens the seal.
      • The sealing paste is flour and water and it is not optional. A samosa that opens in the fryer empties into the oil.
      • Four onions to 500 g of beef looks like a lot and is correct — the onion is half the filling.
      • Beef masala as a single pre-blended spice is the East African way and it is what makes this taste Ugandan rather than Indian.
      • Wash the minced beef before cooking, as the recipe specifies. It is standard practice in Ugandan kitchens.
      • No frying temperature is given in the source. Medium-hot oil, and fry in small batches so it stays there.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Filling undried pastry sheets.
      • Using hot filling.
      • Skipping the sealing paste.
      • Overfilling, so the triangle will not close.
      • Crowding the fryer, which drops the oil temperature and gives you greasy samosas.

      Health Benefits

      • Minced beef supplies protein, iron and vitamin B12.
      • Four onions bring a genuine amount of quercetin and fibre.
      • Ginger, garlic and coriander all go in raw to the filling.
      • They are deep-fried, which is the honest limit on how healthy they get.

      Cultural Note

      The samosa is the most completely naturalised of the Indian contributions to Ugandan food — so much so that most people would not think of it as Indian at all. It arrived with the Gujarati trading community whose shops, the dukas, were the commercial spine of colonial-era Uganda, and it stayed on every street after that community was expelled in 1972.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Refrigerate cooked samosas up to 2 days. Uncooked, they freeze well for a month.

      Reheating: A hot oven for a few minutes. Never a microwave — the pastry goes limp.

      Serve with: Ugandan tea, Chilli sauce, Kachumbari
