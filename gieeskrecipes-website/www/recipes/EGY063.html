🍪 Kahk (Eid Cookies with Pistachio and Honey Filling)

Kahk al-eid — كحك العيد

Ghee-rich cookies with toasted sesame, filled with a pistachio and honey paste, baked until golden on the bottom and dusted with powdered sugar.

2h 5mTotal time
40mPrep
25mCook
20Servings
300Calories
MediumDifficulty

Flour, salt, sugar, cinnamon, sesame and yeast are rubbed with room-temperature ghee and warm water into a dough that rests an hour. A filling of flour browned in ghee with sesame, honey and ground pistachios is cooled and rolled into 20 small balls. Dough balls of about 40 g are filled, pressed in a mamoul mold or marked with a fork, baked at 350°F for 20 to 25 minutes or until the bottom is golden brown, and dusted with powdered sugar.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Mamoul mold or fork, Baking tray

    Dough

    • 3 cups flour.
    • a dash of salt - approximately ⅛ teaspoon.
    • 1 Tablespoon granulated sugar.
    • ½ teaspoon cinnamon.
    • 3 Tablespoons toasted sesame seeds- optional but highly recommended.
    • ½ teaspoon dry yeast.
    • 1 cup ghee- room temperature and not melted.
    • ⅓ cup warm water

    Filling

    • 1 Tablespoon ghee or butter.
    • 1 Tablespoon flour.
    • 1 teaspoon sesame seeds.
    • ¼ cup honey.
    • ½ cup pistachios coarsely ground.

    To finish

    • Powdered sugar.

    Method

    1. Mix the dough ingredients to a nice consistent dough, cover and rest for an hour.
    2. Filling: cook the ghee and flour on medium, stirring until the flour turns golden brown. Add the sesame and honey and return to low heat until it thickens. Stir in the pistachios, cool 10 minutes and form 20 small balls.
    3. Divide the dough into 20 balls of about 40 g each. Fill, press into a mamoul mold or mark with a fork.
    4. Bake in a 350°F oven for 20 to 25 minutes, or until the bottom is golden brown.
    5. Dust with powdered sugar just before serving.

    Chef Tips

    • Cues: flour turns golden brown; filling thickens; bottom golden. The dough rest is NO CUE, TIME ONLY.
    • Ghee gives the distinctive aroma, says the author.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using melted ghee in the dough; the page says room temperature, not melted.

    Cultural Note

    Amira Ibrahim says families shape kahk together before Eid, and Christians also make it after Lent and at Easter; it is served with tea or coffee.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Airtight at room temperature up to a week (our estimate).

    Reheating: Not needed.

    Serve with: Tea or coffee
