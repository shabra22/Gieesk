🦪 Casamance Mangrove Oysters, Grilled and Sun-Dried

Huîtres de palétuviers / yokhos

Mangrove oysters piled with wood in alternate layers 1.5 to 2 m high and burned, removed as they open, then sun-dried two days — the Diola women’s preservation of the Casamance oyster, documented without quantities.

The Casamance mangrove oyster is gathered by Diola women on the falling tide. ORSTOM’s 1987 study records the preservation routes: oysters detached from burned branches or roasted or boiled in a large basin, shells opened and the flesh removed (the text notes fingers get burned), then sun-dried 1 to 2 days, or smoked 1 to 2 days on a round tray over the fire, or grilled. For the grill, oysters and wood are stacked in alternate layers until the pile is 1.5 to 2 metres high, the fire is lit and the oysters are removed as they open — undercooked ones go back — and the grilled oysters are sun-dried for two days. Dried flesh is one tenth of the initial weight. The Slow Food Ark of Taste records that in high season the oysters are boiled or grilled and served with pepper sauce and rice, in low season sun-dried, smoked or eaten dried. THE SOURCES PRINT NO QUANTITIES, NO TEMPERATURES AND NO SAFETY FIGURES. This is a documented traditional practice, not a home recipe.

Ingredients

Equipment: Open fire, Round tray (a ‘van’) for smoking, Mat or rack for sun-drying, Baskets or jute sacks for storing

  • Mangrove oysters — gathered at minimum size over 7 cm (ORSTOM); no quantity
  • Wood for the grilling pile — no quantity
  • Pepper sauce and rice to serve (Slow Food Ark of Taste) — no quantities

Method

    Prepare

    1. Gather the oysters on the falling tide and keep only those over about 7 cm.

    Grill

    1. Stack the oysters and wood in alternate layers until the pile is 1.5 to 2 metres high. Light the fire. Remove the oysters from the fire as they open; put the ones that have not opened back.

    Dry

    1. Open the shells and take out the flesh. Sun-dry the grilled oysters for 2 days (smoked oysters are dried first, then smoked 1 to 2 days; plain sun-drying is 1 to 2 days).

    Store

    1. Store in baskets or jute sacks hung above the fire or on rice-granary shelves.

    Chef Tips

    • The one doneness cue is that oysters are taken off the fire ‘as they open’ (« au fur et à mesure qu’ils s’ouvrent »); undercooked ones are put back.
    • Dried flesh weighs about a tenth of the starting weight (attributed to DOPM).
    • Women ‘know how to recognise the state of maturity of the oysters’; no criteria are printed.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Eating oysters that have not opened on the fire.
    • Taking undersized oysters (below about 7 cm).
    • Treating the process as a home protocol: no temperature, time-at-temperature or safety limit is published.

    Cultural Note

    The practice is the Diola women’s: ORSTOM’s own title assigns it to the ‘système d’exploitation diola’ and the Slow Food Ark of Taste puts some 4,000 Diola women in the annual harvest. The sources describe a harvest that damages mangrove reproduction when branches are cut and a sustainable alternative of wooden frames and strings (strings removed after 5 months), a divergence from the FAO / Slow Food Tobor project (garlands moved at 1.5 months, ready in 4 to 5 months) that is recorded and not merged.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: In baskets or jute sacks, above the fire or on rice-granary shelves; no storage time is printed.

    Reheating: Not applicable.

    Serve with: Pepper sauce and rice (Slow Food Ark of Taste), Soupou kandia
