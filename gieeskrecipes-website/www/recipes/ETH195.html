🥛 Ethiopian Milk Pudding

ወተት ፑዲንግ — Wetet Puding

Ethiopian Milk Pudding — whole milk thickened with cornstarch and sweetened with honey, fragrant with cardamom and rose water, set in individual cups and chilled. The Ethiopian panna cotta — smooth, barely sweet, perfumed with Ethiopian honey.

Ethiopian milk pudding is the modern dessert restaurant classic — a set milk pudding that combines the European panna cotta format with the Ethiopian spice tradition. The cardamom and rose water make it distinctly Ethiopian while the set texture makes it elegant.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, 6 small cups or ramekins

  • 600ml whole milk
  • 3 tbsp honey
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 3 tbsp cornstarch
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tbsp rose water

Topping: drizzle of honey + pinch of ground cinnamon

    Method

    1. Whisk cornstarch with ½ cup cold milk until smooth.
    2. Heat remaining milk with honey, sugar, and cardamom until warm.
    3. Add cornstarch mixture, whisking continuously.
    4. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until thick and smooth — 8—10 minutes.
    5. Add rose water. Stir.
    6. Pour into individual cups.
    7. Chill minimum 2 hours until set.
    8. Top with a drizzle of honey and pinch of cinnamon before serving.

    Chef Tips

    • Whisk the cornstarch into cold milk first — prevents lumps.
    • Stir constantly once thickening begins.
    • The pudding must be smooth — any lumps come from insufficient stirring.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding cornstarch directly to hot milk — lumps.
    • Not stirring once it starts to thicken.

    The Science

    Cornstarch gelatinises at 62—72°C — forming a smooth gel matrix when the granules absorb water and swell. The continuous stirring during heating prevents the granules from settling and cooking unevenly, which would create lumps. The resulting gel is thermoreversible — it liquefies when reheated.

    Cultural Note

    Ethiopian milk pudding is the modern Ethiopian restaurant's elegant dessert offering — using ingredients as ancient as Ethiopian honey and cardamom in the contemporary panna cotta format, it demonstrates that Ethiopian dessert culture can meet international dining expectations.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

    Reheating: Serve chilled — do not reheat.

    Serve with: As a restaurant dessert, At celebrations, Chilled on warm highland evenings
