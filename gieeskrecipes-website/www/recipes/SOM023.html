🫘 Fuul

Fuul Mudammas

Small fava beans cooked down for an hour with tomato, onion and xawaash, then mashed in the pot, dressed with olive oil and chilli oil and eaten with bread — the Somali version, spicier than its Egyptian and Sudanese cousins.

Fuul travelled the length of the Red Sea and every country along it claims a version. What makes the Somali one Somali is the xawaash stirred in with the beans, which gives it a warmth and heat the others do not have. It is a breakfast dish by tradition and a Ramadan dish by necessity — filling enough at suhoor to carry a person through the day, and rich enough at iftar to be worth waiting for.

Ingredients

Equipment: 5-litre pot with a lid, Ladle or sturdy wooden spoon for mashing, Blender or small food processor

    For the beans

    • 450 g (1 lb) small dry fava beans
    • 1.5 L (6 cups) water, for soaking
    • 1.5 L (6 cups) water, for cooking
    • 2 medium tomatoes (275 g), chopped
    • 1 medium onion (130 g), chopped
    • 4 large cloves garlic, minced
    • 1 large clove garlic, minced — added at the end
    • 60 g (1/4 cup) fresh cilantro, chopped
    • 1 tsp xawaash spice blend
    • 2 tsp salt, to taste
    • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
    • 2 lemon slices

    For the tomato topping

    • 2 medium tomatoes (275 g), blended smooth
    • 1/4 tsp xawaash spice blend
    • 1/4 tsp salt
    • Pinch of ground black pepper
    • 1 tsp olive oil

    For the chilli oil

    • 120 ml (1/2 cup) olive oil
    • 2 jalapeno peppers, chopped

    Method

    1. Wash the beans, cover them with water and boil for 10 minutes.
    2. Turn off the heat, cover the pot and leave the beans to soak for one hour.
    3. Drain and rinse the beans, then put them in a clean 5-litre pot with the cooking water, tomatoes, onion, the 4 cloves of garlic, cilantro, xawaash, salt, black pepper and lemon slices. Hold back the single large clove of garlic.
    4. Cover and cook over medium heat for one hour, stirring every 15 minutes.
    5. Mash the beans roughly in the pot with a ladle or wooden spoon, leaving plenty of them whole.
    6. Stir in the reserved garlic and cook for another 10 minutes — added this late, it stays sharp and fresh.
    7. For the topping, blend the tomatoes with the xawaash, salt, pepper and olive oil.
    8. For the chilli oil, warm the olive oil gently with the chopped jalapenos and take it off the heat before the chilli darkens.
    9. Serve the beans in a wide bowl, pour olive oil generously over the top, then spoon over the blended tomato and as much chilli oil as you want.

    Chef Tips

    • Bean size decides whether you have to peel. Small fava beans do not need peeling; large broad beans always do, even in the Middle East.
    • If you are using large dry broad beans, soak them overnight, boil for half an hour in fresh water, then peel them individually — most cooks decide once is enough and buy the small ones after that.
    • An overnight soak replaces the 10-minute boil and hour's rest entirely. Rinse the soaked beans, then cook as written.
    • Fuul always benefits from longer cooking. If it tastes flat, it usually needs another twenty minutes rather than more salt.
    • Tinned fava beans work. One cup of dry beans gives about three cups cooked, so this recipe's 450 g is roughly six cups of cooked beans.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using large broad beans without peeling them, which leaves tough skins through the whole dish.
    • Adding all the garlic at the start, so none of it keeps its edge against the long cooking.
    • Mashing the beans smooth — fuul should be part purée and part whole bean, not a paste.
    • Frying the beans in ghee or butter. There is no frying in fuul: the olive oil goes over the top at the table, not into the pot.

    Health Benefits

    • Fava beans are high in plant protein and dietary fibre.
    • A good source of folate, iron and manganese.
    • Naturally low in fat before the olive oil is added, and entirely plant-based.

    Cultural Note

    Fuul is eaten at breakfast across Somalia and all day in Mogadishu's cafes, traditionally with tamees bread. In Ramadan it appears at both ends of the fast — at suhoor before dawn because it lasts, and at iftar because a bowl of it with bread feeds a whole table cheaply.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days. The flavour improves on the second day.

    Reheating: Reheat on the hob with a splash of water, stirring, until loose again. Add fresh oil and tomato at serving.

    Serve with: Canjeero, Sabaayad, Shaah Cadays
