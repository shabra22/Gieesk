🧄 Yaji with Onion and Garlic

Yaji — suya spice mix

Equal volumes of kuli kuli and ginger powder, with onion, garlic and paprika — combine thoroughly and that is the entire method.

This is yaji at its simplest to make and its most different in composition: half a cup each of ground kuli kuli and ginger powder, a quarter cup each of chilli, paprika, onion powder and garlic powder, and a tablespoon of sea salt. No grinding, no sieving, no toasting — combine all the ingredients thoroughly and store in an airtight container. The ratio of kuli kuli to ginger is one to one by volume, which is a markedly gingerier blend than the weighed version, and the onion, garlic and paprika appear in no other documented yaji.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Whisk or spoon, Airtight container

  • 1/2 cup ground kuli kuli — or roasted groundnut powder as a substitute
  • 1/2 cup ginger powder
  • 1/4 cup chilli powder
  • 1/4 cup paprika powder
  • 1/4 cup onion powder
  • 1/4 cup garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp sea salt

Method

  1. Combine all of the ingredients thoroughly in a bowl.
  2. Store in an airtight container for future use.

Chef Tips

  • Kuli kuli over roasted groundnut powder, and the reason is stated: the lower oil content is what the author calls the secret to professional-quality suya spice. Peanut powder will clump.
  • One to one by volume, kuli kuli to ginger. That is far gingerier than the weighed blend at Yaji, where it is 110 g to 75 g. Taste both and pick.
  • The onion, garlic and paprika are unique to this blend. They make it rounder and less sharp, and more immediately recognisable to a palate used to barbecue rubs.
  • No sieving needed because there is no fibrous African negro pepper in it. That also means it is missing a flavour the other blends have.
  • Paprika contributes colour rather than heat. If your suya looks pale, this is the blend that fixes it.
  • An airtight container is specified rather than the freezer. Ginger powder keeps better cold, so the freezer is still worth it.
  • No cloves here either. The academic description of yaji treats clove as canonical and three of the four blog blends omit it — see Yaji.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Substituting peanut powder and getting a paste.
  • Assuming this blend is interchangeable with the weighed one.
  • Leaving it in a warm cupboard for months.
  • Adding salt again when seasoning the meat.
  • Expecting the fibrous depth that negro pepper gives the other blends.

Health Benefits

  • Defatted groundnut cake is high in protein and low in fat.
  • Garlic and onion powder carry organosulfur compounds.
  • Paprika supplies carotenoids.

Cultural Note

The difference between this blend and the weighed one is a good illustration of how a trade spice becomes a home spice. Onion powder, garlic powder and paprika are pantry items rather than northern market items, and their presence here marks a blend assembled by a cook working from what is available rather than from what a Mai Suya would reach for.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 6 months in an airtight container, longer in the freezer.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Suya, Dambu Nama, Asun
