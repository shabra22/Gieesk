🌽 Empengyere

Empengyere

Whole maize boiled about eight hours in a clay pot until it finally goes tender, then beans added, with stinging nettle and pumpkin leaves — served with pumpkin and obushera.

Empengyere is whole maize and beans cooked in a clay pot, and the eight hours are not an exaggeration: whole dried maize kernels take that long to become tender, and nothing shortens it. The beans go in afterwards, and the greens are stinging nettle and pumpkin leaves. It belongs to the Bakiga of Kabale, Rukiga, Kanungu and Rukungiri, and it is served with pumpkin and a cup of obushera — which makes it one of the most complete single meals in the country.

Ingredients

Equipment: Clay pot, or a heavy pot, Long spoon, Gloves, for the nettle

  • Whole dried maize kernels
  • Beans
  • Stinging nettle
  • Pumpkin leaves
  • Water
  • Salt

To serve

  • Pumpkin
  • Obushera

No quantities are documented

    Method

    1. Soak the maize and the beans separately overnight.
    2. Boil the whole maize in a clay pot for about eight hours, until tender. Top up the water as it reduces.
    3. Add the beans and continue cooking until they are soft.
    4. Add the stinging nettle and pumpkin leaves.
    5. Season with salt.
    6. Serve with pumpkin and obushera.

    Chef Tips

    • About eight hours for the maize, and there is no way round it. Whole dried kernels are the hardest thing to cook in a Ugandan kitchen and this dish is defined by the patience it takes.
    • Beans after the maize, not with it. Beans cooked for eight hours disintegrate entirely.
    • Top the water up throughout. Eight hours will empty any pot.
    • A clay pot is specified and it does hold a low even heat better than metal over a fire. A heavy pot is the substitute.
    • Handle the stinging nettle with gloves until it is in the pot. Cooking destroys the sting completely.
    • Nettle is a real vegetable here rather than a foraged curiosity — high in iron and protein and eaten deliberately.
    • Served with pumpkin and obushera, it is a full meal: grain, pulse, greens, a vegetable and a fermented drink.
    • No quantities are documented. The one source is a named Ugandan journalist and it gives none, so nothing here is estimated.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cutting the eight hours.
    • Adding the beans at the start.
    • Letting the pot boil dry.
    • Handling nettle with bare hands.
    • Adding the greens hours before serving.

    Health Benefits

    • Maize and beans together form a complete protein.
    • Stinging nettle is exceptionally high in iron, calcium and protein for a leaf.
    • Pumpkin leaves add vitamin A, iron and folate.
    • Very high in fibre, and cooked with no added fat at all.

    Cultural Note

    An eight-hour dish tells you about a household as much as a recipe: a fire that stays lit, someone at home, and a pot that can be left. Kigezi is cold, hilly and hungry country, and empengyere is the answer to all three at once.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days. It is better the next day.

    Reheating: Warm gently with a little water.

    Serve with: Obushera, Pumpkin, Karo
