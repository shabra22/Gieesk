Federation

Rice on one half of a great oval plate, spaghetti on the other, stewed meat and vegetables layered over both, salad and a whole banana on the side — the Somali restaurant order that refuses to choose.

Federation is not a recipe so much as a decision: whole portions of both starches, side by side on one plate, layered with assorted stewed meats and vegetables. The name is usually explained by the union of two dishes into one, and by the sheer quantity. It is ordered in traditional Somali restaurants and rarely made at home, because at home nobody needs the plate to prove anything.

Ingredients

Equipment: The largest oval platter you own, Two pots, for the rice and the pasta, Pan, for the suugo

    The rice half

    • 1 batch bariis iskukaris or bariis maraq, about 4 cups cooked

    The pasta half

    • 400 g spaghetti, cooked and drained

    The topping

    • 1 batch suugo suqaar, or any mixture of stewed meats
    • 1 batch korkasaarka bariiska, the fried potato and raisin topping

    To finish the plate

    • 2 tomatoes and 1/2 cucumber, sliced, with a squeeze of lemon
    • 1/2 onion, thinly sliced
    • Basbaas, to taste
    • 2 bananas, halved — one half per person
    • 1 lemon, cut into wedges

    Method

    1. Make the rice and keep it hot and covered.
    2. Cook the spaghetti until just done, drain it, and toss it with a spoonful of the suugo so it does not set into a block.
    3. Have the suugo suqaar hot, and the fried potato topping ready.
    4. Mound the rice across one half of the platter and the spaghetti across the other, so they meet down the middle. Keep the line visible — that division is the dish.
    5. Ladle the suugo over both halves, working from the centre out.
    6. Scatter the fried potato and raisin topping across the top.
    7. Arrange the sliced tomato, cucumber and onion down one side and squeeze lemon over them.
    8. Set the banana halves and the lemon wedges on the plate and bring the whole thing to the table with basbaas alongside. It is eaten from the shared platter.

    Chef Tips

    • Keep the rice and pasta visibly separate. A federation that has been stirred together is just a plate of food.
    • Toss the drained spaghetti with a little of the sauce immediately. Bare pasta sitting in a pile welds itself together.
    • The banana is not a joke and not a garnish. Somalis eat it with the savoury food, and its sweetness against the salt and the tomato is the reason it is there.
    • Make the suugo the day before if you can — it improves overnight, and it is the only long job on the list.
    • Scale the platter to the table. This is a dish designed to be eaten by several people from one plate.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Serving half portions of each starch. Federation means whole portions of both, and halving them misses the entire point.
    • Mixing the rice and pasta together before the sauce goes on.
    • Leaving off the salad, the banana and the basbaas, which are what make it a complete plate rather than a pile.
    • Draining the pasta and letting it stand dry while the rest is assembled.

    Health Benefits

    • The stewed meat topping supplies protein, iron and zinc.
    • Raw salad and banana add vitamin C and potassium alongside the cooked food.
    • Very large by design — it is built as a shared platter, not a single serving.

    Cultural Note

    Federation is ordered mainly in traditional Somali restaurants and is rare at home. The name is read as the union of two dishes in one country, and the dish carries a certain humour about its own size: a plate that answers the question of rice or pasta by declining to answer it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate the components separately, up to 3 days. Assembled, it does not keep.

    Reheating: Reheat each component on its own and assemble fresh.

    Serve with: Basbaas, Fresh banana, Casiir Liin Dhanaan
