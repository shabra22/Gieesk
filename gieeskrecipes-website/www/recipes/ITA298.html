🌽 Polenta Concia Valdostana

Polenta Concia

Valle d'Aosta's extravagantly rich polenta, cooked cornmeal folded generously with melting Fontina and butter until it becomes almost molten, a warming centerpiece of Alpine winter meals.

Polenta concia takes the region's already-rich polenta taragna concept even further, layering the cooked cornmeal with a truly generous quantity of Fontina and butter, resulting in a dish substantial enough to serve as a complete meal on its own.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed pot, Wooden spoon

  • 250 g (1⅔ cups) coarse cornmeal
  • 1.2 L (5 cups) water
  • 10 g (2 tsp) salt
  • 400 g (14 oz) Fontina Valle d'Aosta, cubed
  • 150 g (⅔ cup) unsalted butter
  • Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for serving

Method

  1. Bring the water to a boil in a heavy-bottomed pot and add the salt.
  2. Gradually whisk in the cornmeal to avoid lumps.
  3. Reduce the heat to low and cook, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon, for about 40–45 minutes until thick and no longer grainy.
  4. In the final 10 minutes of cooking, fold in the cubed Fontina, letting it melt into the polenta.
  5. Stir in the butter until fully incorporated and the polenta becomes rich, glossy, and almost stretchy from the melted cheese.
  6. Spoon into bowls and finish with a little grated Parmigiano.
  7. Serve immediately while hot and the cheese is still molten.

Chef Tips

  • Stir frequently throughout cooking to prevent the polenta from sticking and scorching.
  • Add the cheese near the end so it melts in properly rather than overcooking and separating.
  • This dish is deliberately extravagant — don't hold back on the Fontina and butter.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the cornmeal too quickly, causing lumps to form.
  • Not stirring often enough, resulting in scorched polenta on the bottom of the pot.
  • Being too restrained with the cheese, missing the dish's intended rich, molten character.

Cultural Note

Polenta concia is a warming centerpiece of Valle d'Aosta's Alpine winter cooking, its extravagant use of Fontina cheese reflecting the region's rich dairy tradition.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate leftovers for up to 2 days; it firms up considerably once cooled.

Reheating: Reheat gently with a splash of milk, stirring to loosen and re-melt the cheese.

Serve with: Nothing more — this is a complete, rich meal on its own
