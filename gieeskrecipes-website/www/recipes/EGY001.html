🍝 Koshari (One-Pan Egyptian Koshari)

Koshari — كشري

Rice, lentils and three shapes of pasta cooked in one pan, topped with a garlic-tomato sauce, a cumin-vinegar sauce, a chili 'shattah', chickpeas and crisp fried onions.

1h 55mTotal time
15mPrep
40mCook
4Servings
640Calories
MediumDifficulty

Thin onion slices are fried in oil until brown and then drained; the leftover onion oil fries vermicelli, rice, lentils and macaroni, and boiling stock is added with broken spaghetti. Once the water can no longer be seen on the surface the pan goes on the lowest heat for 11 to 13 minutes. A tomato sauce, a thin cumin-vinegar sauce and a chili shattah are made separately and spooned over the rice with chickpeas and the fried onions.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide deep pan with a lid, Small saucepans for the sauces, Paper towels

    Onions

    • 1 large onion thinly sliced
    • ½ to ¾ cup cooking oil

    Rice and pasta

    • 1 cup short-grain rice or Egyptian rice (Read the notes)
    • ¼ cup green lentils. Wash and soak in very hot water for one hour before using in this recipe
    • ½ cup small elbow macaroni uncooked
    • ¼ cup vermicelli uncooked
    • 60 gm spaghetti break in half or to three
    • 3 tablespoon onion oil leftover oil from frying the onion
    • 1 cube chicken or vegetable bouillon
    • 3 ½ cups boiling water

    Tomato sauce

    • 1 large tomato pureed or two medium tomatoes
    • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
    • 1 ½ teaspoon minced garlic
    • ¼ teaspoon black pepper powder
    • ¼ teaspoon cumin powder
    • ¼ teaspoon coriander powder
    • ¼ teaspoon salt
    • ¼ teaspoon sugar
    • 2 tablespoon onion oil
    • 1 cup water

    Cumin sauce

    • 1 cup water
    • 1 teaspoon garlic minced
    • ½ teaspoon cumin powder
    • ½ teaspoon coriander powder
    • ½ teaspoon salt
    • 1 tablespoon white vinegar

    Shattah (chili sauce)

    • 2 ½ tablespoon onion oil
    • 1 teaspoon chili powder
    • 1 to 2 tablespoon tomato sauce previously prepared
    • 2 tablespoon cumin sauce prepared previously

    To top

    • 1 cup cooked chickpeas use canned

    Method

    1. Fry the onion slices in the oil, stirring, until they turn brown, then switch the heat off at once. Lift them onto paper towels; they crisp as they cool.
    2. In 3 tablespoons of the onion oil, fry the vermicelli until brown, add the washed rice and fry for 1 to 2 minutes, then stir in the lentils and the elbow macaroni for a few seconds each. Add the bouillon and boiling water.
    3. When the pan is about to boil, lay in the broken spaghetti and cover. When you can no longer see water on the surface, turn to the lowest heat and cook for 11 to 13 minutes, then fluff with a fork.
    4. Tomato sauce: fry the garlic for a few seconds, add the tomato puree, then the paste, spices, salt and sugar for about a minute, then the water. Simmer until the oil comes to the surface and the sauce thickens a little.
    5. Cumin sauce: boil the water with the garlic and spices, add the vinegar, and turn the heat off when it returns to a boil.
    6. Shattah: fry the chili powder in the onion oil for a few seconds, add 1 to 2 tablespoons of tomato sauce and 2 tablespoons of cumin sauce, and switch off.
    7. Serve the rice with tomato sauce, shattah to taste, chickpeas and the fried onions.

    Chef Tips

    • Cues: onions brown, then heat off; water no longer visible on the surface; tomato sauce done when oil rises. The lentil soak is NO CUE, TIME ONLY (one hour in very hot water).
    • The page gives no temperatures beyond medium, lowest and increased heat.
    • Chickpeas are canned on this page; the other sources cook dried ones.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Letting the onions keep cooking after the heat is off, so they darken and turn bitter (our caution).
    • Skipping the sauces; the dish relies on the sharp cumin-vinegar sauce and the chili shattah.

    Cultural Note

    Muna Kenny calls it a famous street food and the national dish of Egypt. Killebrew's page says it is believed to have come from India (khichri) and arrived with the British in the late 1800s, citing others; that origin is her report, not settled fact.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Keep the components in separate containers in the fridge; Guidry's page says leftovers keep up to 5 days.

    Reheating: Reheat rice and pasta together with a splash of water; warm the sauces separately. The fried onions are best added fresh.

    Serve with: Salata baladi (tomato-cucumber salad)
