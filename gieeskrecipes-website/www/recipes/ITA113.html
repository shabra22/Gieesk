🥟 Canederli allo Speck

Canederli allo Speck

Hearty bread dumplings studded with smoky speck, a specialty of Italy's Alpine north showing clear Austro-Hungarian influence, served either in a light broth or alongside melted butter.

Canederli reflect the distinctly Germanic culinary heritage of Trentino-Alto Adige, using stale bread and cured meat in a dumpling that's closer to a Central European knödel than to anything found further south in Italy.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Large pot

  • 300 g (10.5 oz) stale bread, cut into small cubes
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) warm milk
  • 150 g (5.3 oz) speck, finely diced
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 eggs
  • 30 g (¼ cup) flour, plus more as needed
  • Fresh chives or parsley, chopped
  • 1 tbsp butter, for sautéing
  • 1.2 L (5 cups) beef or vegetable broth
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Place the bread cubes in a large bowl and pour the warm milk over them, letting them soak for 15 minutes.
  2. Meanwhile, sauté the onion in butter until softened, then add the speck and cook briefly.
  3. Add the onion and speck mixture to the soaked bread along with the eggs, flour, chives, salt, and pepper.
  4. Mix well until everything holds together — the mixture should be moist but formable; add a little more flour if too wet.
  5. Let the mixture rest for 15 minutes to firm up.
  6. With wet hands, shape the mixture into balls about the size of a golf ball.
  7. Bring the broth to a gentle simmer and carefully add the dumplings.
  8. Simmer gently for 15–18 minutes until the dumplings float and are cooked through.
  9. Serve hot in the broth, or drain and serve with melted butter and a scattering of chives.

Chef Tips

  • Let the bread soak thoroughly before mixing — dry patches make it harder to bind the dumplings together.
  • Test one dumpling in the simmering broth first; if it falls apart, mix in a little more flour before shaping the rest.
  • Simmer gently rather than at a rolling boil, which can cause the dumplings to break apart.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using fresh, soft bread instead of properly stale bread, which absorbs liquid differently and can result in a mushy texture.
  • Overworking the mixture, which can make the dumplings dense rather than light.
  • Boiling too vigorously, which risks breaking the dumplings apart in the pot.

Cultural Note

Canederli reflect the distinctly Alpine, Austro-Hungarian-influenced cuisine of Trentino-Alto Adige, a region where German remains widely spoken and the food culture differs markedly from the rest of Italy.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate cooked dumplings for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in broth or a pan with a little butter.

Serve with: Sauerkraut, A simple green salad
