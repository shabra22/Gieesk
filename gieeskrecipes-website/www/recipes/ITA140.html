Seadas

Large, golden Sardinian pastry parcels filled with tangy fresh cheese, fried until crisp, and drizzled generously with warm honey — a striking sweet-savory finish to a Sardinian meal.

The sharp, slightly sour young pecorino used to fill seadas is essential to balancing the dish's generous honey drizzle, and the pastry's thin, crisp shell shatters to reveal a molten, stretchy cheese center that catches diners by surprise every time.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin or pasta machine, Deep pot for frying

  • 400 g (3 cups) 00 flour
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) lard or butter, softened
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) warm water
  • Pinch of salt
  • 400 g (14 oz) young, tangy pecorino or fresh cheese, coarsely grated
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • 150 g (½ cup) honey, warmed

Method

  1. Combine the flour, lard or butter, water, and salt into a smooth, elastic dough. Rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
  2. Combine the grated cheese with the lemon zest.
  3. Roll the dough very thin, using a pasta machine if available, and cut into rounds about 12cm across.
  4. Place a portion of the cheese filling in the center of half the rounds, leaving a border.
  5. Top each with a second round of dough, pressing the edges firmly to seal, crimping if needed to ensure a tight closure.
  6. Heat vegetable oil to 180°C (350°F) and fry the parcels until deeply golden and puffed, about 2–3 minutes per side.
  7. Drain briefly on paper towels.
  8. Serve immediately while hot, drizzled generously with warm honey.

Chef Tips

  • Seal the edges of each parcel very thoroughly — any gap will let the cheese leak out during frying.
  • A young, tangy pecorino gives the best contrast against the sweet honey; overly aged, hard cheese won't melt properly.
  • Serve these immediately after frying, while the cheese center is still molten and stretchy.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not sealing the edges well enough, causing the filling to escape into the frying oil.
  • Using aged, hard cheese instead of a young, fresh variety, which won't achieve the right melting texture.
  • Letting the fried seadas sit too long before serving, when the cheese center is at its best straight from the fryer.

Cultural Note

Seadas are a beloved Sardinian specialty, their striking sweet-and-savory combination of tangy fried cheese and honey a distinctive marker of the island's unique culinary identity.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately; not suited to storage or reheating given the molten cheese center.

Reheating: Not recommended; best enjoyed fresh and hot.

Serve with: Warm honey, A glass of sweet Sardinian wine
