🟤 Dzowoe

Dzowoe — adaakwa, spicy peanut balls

Equal weights of groundnut paste and toasted corn flour with cayenne, ginger and clove — warrior food, by her account, needing no cooking at all.

Dzowoe is a spiced peanut ball and it is one of the oldest-feeling foods in this collection: a hundred and fifty grams of groundnut paste to a hundred and fifty of toasted corn flour, with a tablespoon of cayenne, a tablespoon of ginger powder, half a teaspoon of clove powder, a tablespoon of sugar and optional salt. Nothing is cooked — the corn flour is already toasted and the paste binds it. Her note is that dzowoe was one of the few preparations prepared as seasoned food for warriors in battle, which makes sense: it keeps, it travels and it is dense with calories.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Mortar, or strong hands, Airtight tin

  • 150 g groundnut paste
  • 150 g toasted corn flour (tom brown flour)
  • 1 tbsp cayenne pepper, or 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 1 tbsp ginger powder
  • 1/2 tsp clove powder
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1/2 tsp salt (optional)

Method

    The published method for this recipe is video-only. No written steps exist in the source.

    1. Combine the toasted corn flour with the cayenne, ginger powder, clove powder, sugar and optional salt.
    2. Work in the groundnut paste until the mixture comes together.
    3. Press and roll into firm balls.
    4. Store in an airtight tin.

    The order above follows the dry-then-wet logic of the ingredients, not this source's own steps.

      Chef Tips

      • Equal weights of paste and toasted corn flour, a hundred and fifty grams each. That one-to-one ratio is the whole recipe.
      • No cooking at all. The corn flour is already toasted — this is the same ablemanu or tom brown that binds aprapransa and ayikple.
      • A tablespoon of cayenne for eight servings is substantial, and she notes this makes for a mildly spiced dzowoe. The traditional version is hotter.
      • Press firmly. Groundnut paste and dry flour will not hold unless you compact them.
      • Keeps well in a tin, which is why she records it as food for warriors on campaign.
      • The same snack is dakuwa among the Hausa of northern Nigeria, by her own note — a genuine cross-border food.
      • Be aware the method is reconstructed. She publishes a complete ingredient list and the steps are in her video.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Using untoasted corn flour.
      • Not compacting the balls firmly enough.
      • Treating the reconstructed step order as the author's own.
      • Adding liquid, which it does not need.
      • Expecting it to be fiery — this version is deliberately mild.

      Health Benefits

      • Groundnuts supply protein, niacin, folate, magnesium and vitamin E.
      • Toasted maize adds fibre and B vitamins.
      • Ginger and clove both carry documented antimicrobial compounds.
      • Very calorie-dense, which is why it travels well.

      Cultural Note

      A dense, spiced, keeping food made of ground nuts and toasted grain is the kind of thing every travelling culture invents, and dzowoe being remembered as warriors' rations fits it exactly. That the Hausa make the same thing under the name dakuwa, hundreds of miles north, says how well the idea worked.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Several weeks in an airtight tin.

      Reheating: Not applicable.

      Serve with: Eaten on its own, Hausa Koko, Tea
