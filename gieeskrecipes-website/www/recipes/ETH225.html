🟡 Ethiopian Passion Fruit Juice

ፓሽን ፍሩት ጭማቂ — Passion Fruit Chumaki

Ethiopian Passion Fruit Juice — fresh passion fruits halved, pulp strained and diluted with water, sweetened with honey. Ethiopia grows exceptional passion fruit in its highland areas — the concentrated aroma and intense flavour produce one of the world's finest fresh juices.

Ethiopian passion fruit grows in the highland areas around Jimma and in the Rift Valley gardens — the fruit is smaller than commercial varieties but more intensely flavoured. The fresh juice, strained and diluted, is one of the most aromatic drinks imaginable.

Ingredients

Equipment: Sieve, Bowl, Spoon

  • 8 ripe passion fruits
  • 2 cups cold water
  • 2 tbsp honey
  • Ice

Method

  1. Halve passion fruits. Scoop pulp and seeds into a sieve over a bowl.
  2. Press through the sieve — extracting as much juice as possible.
  3. Discard seeds (or reserve for garnish).
  4. Mix passion fruit juice with cold water and honey.
  5. Taste — adjust honey.
  6. Serve over ice.

Chef Tips

  • Press firmly through the sieve — much of the juice is in the sacs around the seeds.
  • Ethiopian passion fruit is very concentrated — needs more dilution than supermarket varieties.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-diluting — too intense to drink.

The Science

Passion fruit's distinctive aroma comes from over 100 volatile compounds including ethyl butanoate and ethyl hexanoate — these are present in extraordinary concentrations in Ethiopian highland varieties. The tartaric and citric acids produce the characteristic tartness.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian passion fruit juice is the drink that stops international visitors — the concentrated fragrance of the Ethiopian highland passion fruit, diluted in cold water and sweetened with mountain honey, is one of the most extraordinary juice experiences in Africa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Drink immediately or refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Not applicable — serve cold.

Serve with: At juice bars, With Honey Cake (ETH199), As a post-meal digestif
