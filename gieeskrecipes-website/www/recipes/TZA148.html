🍚 Tanzanian Coconut Milk Rice

Wali wa Tui

Tanzanian Wali wa Tui — long-grain rice cooked entirely in fresh coconut milk. Even simpler than Wali wa Nazi, this version forgoes all additions — pure rice, pure coconut milk, pure Zanzibar. Every grain is infused with coconut.

Wali wa Tui is the purest expression of the Swahili coast's love for coconut — rice cooked in nothing but fresh coconut milk. No spices, no additions, no embellishment. The coconut milk does everything: it flavours, enriches, and colours the rice a beautiful creamy white.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with tight lid

  • 3 cups long-grain rice — washed and soaked 20 minutes
  • 500ml thick fresh coconut milk
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Combine coconut milk, water, and salt. Bring to a boil.
  2. Drain soaked rice. Add to boiling coconut milk.
  3. Stir once. Bring back to a boil.
  4. Cover tightly. Reduce to lowest heat.
  5. Cook 18—20 minutes without lifting the lid.
  6. Rest 10 minutes. Fluff gently with a fork.

Chef Tips

  • Fresh coconut milk only — this dish reveals the difference immediately.
  • Do not lift the lid.
  • The rice will have a slightly cream colour and a rich fragrance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Lifting the lid.
  • Using canned coconut milk without adjustment.

Cultural Note

Wali wa Tui is the rice that makes everything else taste better — served alongside the coast's finest fish curries and meat stews, its gentle coconut fragrance is the thread that ties the whole coastal meal together.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days.

Reheating: Steam or microwave covered.

Serve with: Any Tanzanian stew or curry, Samaki wa Kupaka (TZA061), Mchuzi wa Kamba (TZA066)
