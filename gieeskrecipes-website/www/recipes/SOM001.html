🫓 Canjeero

Canjeero

Somalia's beloved spongy, fermented flatbread, its surface dotted with tiny bubbles from an overnight ferment, eaten at nearly every breakfast table across the country.

Canjeero shares a fermentation lineage with Ethiopia's injera, but its batter is thinner, its ferment shorter, and it's cooked on only one side, resulting in a softer, more tender bread that Somali families fold and dip into stews, drizzle with ghee and sugar, or simply eat plain alongside milky tea.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Non-stick crepe pan or flat griddle

  • 500 g (4 cups) all-purpose flour
  • 150 g (1 cup) fine cornmeal
  • 7 g (2 1/4 tsp) active dry yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 750 ml (3 cups) warm water, approximately

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast and sugar in a little of the warm water and let sit for 10 minutes until foamy.
  2. Combine the flour, cornmeal, and salt in a large bowl.
  3. Add the yeast mixture and remaining water gradually, whisking until you have a smooth, thin batter similar to a crepe batter.
  4. Cover and let ferment at room temperature for at least 8 hours, ideally overnight, until bubbly and slightly sour-smelling.
  5. Heat a non-stick pan or griddle over medium heat, no oil needed once seasoned.
  6. Pour a ladle of batter into the center, swirling gently to spread it into a thin round, or let it spread naturally without swirling for a thicker canjeero.
  7. Cover with a lid and cook for 2-3 minutes, until the surface is covered in small bubbles and set - canjeero is cooked only on one side.
  8. Remove and stack on a plate, covering with a clean cloth to keep soft while cooking the rest.

Chef Tips

  • A longer, overnight ferment develops the characteristic tangy flavor and bubbled surface that defines good canjeero.
  • Cooking with the lid on traps steam, which helps set the top without needing to flip it.
  • The first canjeero often does not turn out perfectly as the pan seasons - do not worry if it takes a round or two to get going.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the fermentation, which results in a flat, less flavorful bread without the characteristic bubbled texture.
  • Making the batter too thick, which prevents the small surface bubbles from forming properly.
  • Flipping the canjeero, which is traditionally cooked and served with only one side browned.

Cultural Note

Canjeero is a cornerstone of Somali daily life, traditionally made fresh each morning and central to breakfast across the country, from Mogadishu households to rural pastoral communities.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate stacked and wrapped for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a dry pan or microwave, covered with a damp cloth to keep soft.

Serve with: Suugo (tomato meat sauce), Ghee and sugar, Somali tea
