🥫 Pot Luck Gari Foto

Gari foto

Built on half a tin of corned beef and scrambled egg — keep turning it until there are no dry white or yellow lumps of gari left.

The store-cupboard version, and the cue at the end of it is the clearest statement anywhere of what garifoto should look like: keep turning it until the stew saturates the gari and there are no dry white or yellow lumps. Two cups of gari are dampened with three quarters of a cup of water and a quarter teaspoon of salt, sprinkled on with a fork, and the gari should be dampened but not dripping wet. Eggs are scrambled separately and set aside; onion, corned beef, garlic, tomatoes, ginger and red pepper cook in palm and peanut oil; the eggs go back in, then the gari on low heat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large frying pan, Fork, Mixing bowl, Wooden spoon

  • 2 cups gari
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 large fresh tomatoes, or 2 cups grape tomatoes, chopped — or 3 drained tinned tomatoes, chopped, seeded if preferred
  • 1/2 tin corned beef, about 6 oz — or 1 cup of any leftover fish, meat or beans, in chunks
  • 1 tbsp tomato sauce (optional)
  • 6 tbsp peanut or other vegetable oil
  • 2 tbsp red palm oil, if available
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 1 tsp fresh grated ginger, or 1/2 tsp dried ground ginger
  • 1/4 tsp ground red pepper
  • 1 or 2 cloves garlic (optional)

Garnish

  • Parsley (optional)

Method

  1. Sprinkle the water and salt over the gari with a fork. The gari should be dampened, but not dripping wet.
  2. Scramble the eggs and set them aside.
  3. Heat the palm and peanut oils.
  4. Sauté the onion until clear.
  5. Add the corned beef, garlic, tomatoes, ginger and red pepper.
  6. Add the scrambled eggs back in.
  7. Turn the heat to low and mix in the gari mixture, using a fork to break up any lumps of gari.
  8. Keep turning it until the stew saturates the gari and there are no dry white or yellow lumps.
  9. Garnish with parsley and serve.

Chef Tips

  • The finishing cue is the best description of correctly made garifoto anywhere: keep turning until the stew saturates the gari and there are no dry white or yellow lumps. Dry lumps are the fault to look for.
  • Dampened but not dripping wet, sprinkled on with a FORK. A fork distributes the water far more evenly than pouring.
  • Scramble the eggs separately and add them back. Cracking eggs into the pan gives you a different dish.
  • Both oils — six tablespoons of peanut and two of palm. The palm oil is for colour and flavour rather than volume.
  • Half a tin of corned beef is what makes this a pot-luck dish, and she offers leftover fish, meat or beans as alternatives. It is a use-what-you-have recipe.
  • A fork again for breaking up lumps as the gari goes in. Gari clumps and a spoon will not separate it.
  • A fancier egg option she mentions is omelette strips rather than scrambled.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Pouring the water on rather than sprinkling with a fork.
  • Over-wetting the gari.
  • Cooking the eggs in the stew.
  • Leaving dry white lumps of gari in the finished dish.
  • Using only one of the two oils.

Health Benefits

  • Gari is gluten-free and supplies resistant starch.
  • Eggs and corned beef together make it high in complete protein.
  • Red palm oil provides provitamin-A carotenoids.
  • Uses up leftovers, which is the point of the dish.

Cultural Note

Pot luck is exactly right as a name — gari foto is a dish for whatever is in the cupboard, and half a tin of corned beef is as authentic an ingredient in modern Ghanaian cooking as anything grown locally. The instruction to hunt down dry white lumps is the voice of someone who has eaten a badly made one.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. It firms up as the gari keeps absorbing.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of water.

Serve with: Fried egg, Shito, Sliced avocado
