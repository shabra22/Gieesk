🐟 Tanzanian Coconut Basted Fish

Samaki wa Kupaka

Samaki wa Kupaka — Zanzibar's most iconic fish dish. Whole fish marinated in Zanzibar spices, grilled over charcoal, and repeatedly basted with a rich coconut-tamarind sauce until deeply lacquered, smoky, and fragrant. The Swahili coast at its absolute finest.

Samaki wa Kupaka is what happens when the Indian Ocean's finest fish meets Zanzibar's world-class spices and the ancient coastal technique of layered coconut-tamarind basting. At Zanzibar's Forodhani night market, the smell of samaki wa kupaka grilling over charcoal fills the warm night air.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Zanzibar and the Swahili coast

Samaki wa Kupaka is the fish equivalent of Kuku wa Kupaka — a whole snapper or kingfish marinated in Zanzibar spices, grilled over charcoal, and repeatedly basted with coconut-tamarind sauce until magnificently lacquered. It is the signature fish dish of Zanzibar and one of the most celebrated seafood preparations in East Africa. The Forodhani Gardens night market is famous for it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill, Basting brush

  • 1 large whole fish (1—1.5 kg) — snapper or kingfish, scored

Spice Marinade

  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp coriander
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 2 tsp salt

Coconut-Tamarind Basting Sauce

  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 3 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Score fish deeply on both sides. Rub marinade all over and into scores. Marinate 2 hours.
  2. Make basting sauce: simmer all sauce ingredients 8 minutes. Divide in two.
  3. Grill fish skin-side down without moving for 8—10 minutes.
  4. Flip. Begin basting every 3—4 minutes.
  5. Continue grilling and basting 15—20 more minutes, building layer upon layer of glaze.
  6. Serve with reserved sauce.

Chef Tips

  • Score deeply to the bone.
  • Medium coals only — coconut milk sugars burn at high heat.
  • Multiple thin baste layers create the lacquer.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Shallow scoring.
  • High heat.
  • Single basting.

The Science

Each baste layer caramelises on the fish surface through Maillard reaction. Multiple thin layers build a complex, multi-layered glaze — far more intense than a single thick application.

Cultural Note

Samaki wa Kupaka is the food memory every visitor to Zanzibar carries home — the fish grilled at Forodhani since the Sultanate era. To eat it at the water's edge as dhows pass in the evening is to understand why Zanzibar is called the Spice Island.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Warm briefly on grill.

Serve with: Wali wa Nazi (TZA002), Kachumbari, Chapati (TZA004)
