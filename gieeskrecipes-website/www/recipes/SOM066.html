🎂 Doolsho Soomaali

Doolsho Soomaali

The Somali sponge — whites whipped with the sugar first, then the yolks, then the flour folded in by hand, with cardamom for flavour and no raising agent at all beyond the air you beat in.

This is the cake Somalis mean by doolsho: six eggs, a cup of sugar, a cup of flour, and the whites beaten before the yolks go anywhere near them. It rises on beaten air alone — there is no baking powder in it — which is why every instruction about folding gently is doing real work. The two modern concessions are melted butter for moistness and cornstarch standing in for cake flour, which was never available.

Ingredients

Equipment: Stand mixer or electric beaters, A bowl large enough to sit the mixing bowl inside, 24 cm (9 1/2 inch) cake tin, Sieve, Large metal spoon or spatula

    The dry mixture

    • 140 g (1 cup) plain flour
    • 65 g (1/2 cup) cornstarch
    • 1/4 tsp salt

    The flavouring

    • 60 ml (4 tbsp) unsalted butter, melted
    • 1 tsp ground cardamom

    The eggs

    • 6 large eggs, separated
    • 200 g (1 cup) granulated sugar

    For the tin

    • 1 tbsp soft butter and 1 tbsp flour

    Method

    1. Sift the flour, cornstarch and salt together twice. Sifting twice is not fussiness — it is how a batter with no raising agent stays light.
    2. Mix the melted butter with the ground cardamom and set it aside.
    3. Butter and flour the cake tin, and heat the oven to 177C / 350F.
    4. Separate the eggs. Stand your mixing bowl inside a larger bowl of hot tap water to warm it.
    5. Put the whites in the warm bowl and beat, adding the sugar gradually as you go.
    6. Keep beating for 8 to 10 minutes, until the mixture is thick, foamy and holds its shape.
    7. Add the yolks and beat for exactly one minute.
    8. Fold in the sifted flour mixture gently, by hand. Vigorous mixing knocks out the air, and the air is the only thing making this rise.
    9. Fold in the cardamom butter until just combined, and no further.
    10. Pour into the prepared tin and bake for 35 minutes. Do not go over — a sponge is dry by nature and an extra five minutes makes it very dry indeed.

    Chef Tips

    • Warm the bowl in hot water before the whites go in. Warm whites whip higher and hold better.
    • Eight to ten minutes on the whites, and not less. This is where the entire rise comes from.
    • Fold the flour in by hand even if you have a stand mixer. A machine will deflate the batter in seconds.
    • One cup of plain flour to half a cup of cornstarch is the substitute for cake flour, arrived at by trial because cake flour was not available.
    • A burnt bottom and a pale top means the oven was too hot and the shelf too low. Bake in the middle of the oven at a slightly lower heat.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding the sugar to the whites all at once, which collapses them before they have whipped.
    • Folding the flour in vigorously, or with a machine, which deflates the batter.
    • Overbaking. A sponge with no fat in the batter dries out fast, and 35 minutes is the number.
    • Skipping the double sift, which leaves lumps of cornstarch that show up as pale streaks.

    Health Benefits

    • Six eggs across ten servings makes this a relatively high-protein cake.
    • Very little fat in the batter — four tablespoons of butter in the whole cake.
    • No raising agents, so nothing beyond eggs, flour, sugar and butter.

    Cultural Note

    The difficulty in Somalia was never the recipe, it was the equipment. Few households had an electric mixer and most had no oven at all, so cakes were baked in a pot on a charcoal stove with embers laid on the lid and warm coals underneath — and the right number of coals and the right length of time were things you learned by doing. Those who knew it, knew it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Covered, at room temperature 2 days. It stales faster than a butter cake.

    Reheating: Serve at room temperature.

    Serve with: Shaah Cadays, Xalwo, Somali coffee
