🍞 Makonde Cassava Bread

Mkate wa Muhogo — Wamakonde

Makonde Cassava Bread — cassava flour mixed with water and a little coconut milk, formed into flat rounds and pan-fried in the Makonde tradition. Naturally gluten-free, slightly chewy, and deeply satisfying — the daily bread of southern Tanzania's cassava country.

Makonde cassava bread is the staple bread of Mtwara — where cassava is the dominant crop, cassava flour is also the primary flour. This simple pan-fried bread is made daily from the same cassava that goes into the stew, reflecting the Makonde's complete dependency on and mastery of this single crop.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Wide pan

  • 2 cups cassava flour
  • 1 cup warm water
  • 3 tbsp coconut milk
  • ½ tsp salt

Method

  1. Mix cassava flour, coconut milk, and salt.
  2. Add warm water gradually, mixing until a soft but not sticky dough forms.
  3. Divide into 8 balls. Flatten each into a round 1cm thick.
  4. Heat a dry pan over medium heat.
  5. Cook each round 4—5 minutes per side until golden and cooked through.
  6. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Cassava dough is naturally gluten-free — do not expect it to behave like wheat dough.
  • It will not puff up like chapati — it stays flat and dense.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expecting it to rise or puff — it will not.

The Science

Cassava flour contains no gluten — when formed into dough, the binding comes from cassava starch gelatinisation (heated cassava starch absorbs water and becomes tacky). The coconut milk fat helps produce a more tender, less brittle result.

Cultural Note

Makonde cassava bread is the daily bread of southern Tanzania — made every morning from the cassava flour ground the day before, eaten with the cassava stew, lived entirely within the world of one remarkable crop.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best fresh. Store 2 days.

Reheating: Warm in dry pan.

Serve with: Makonde cassava stew (TZA248), Any stew or sauce
