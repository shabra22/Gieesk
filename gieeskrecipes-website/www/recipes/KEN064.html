🍗 Kenyan Chicken Wet Fry

Kuku ya Kukaanga wa Mchuzi

Kenyan Chicken Wet Fry — chicken pieces fried until golden then finished in a thick, concentrated tomato, onion, and bell pepper sauce with Kenyan spices. Bold, saucy, and deeply satisfying — the chicken version of Kenya's most popular restaurant preparation.

Chicken wet fry applies the same two-stage technique as beef wet fry — high-heat searing followed by finishing in a concentrated sauce. Because chicken cooks faster than beef, this is a quicker dish while delivering all the same bold character. The sauce should be thick enough to coat every piece generously.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large frying pan, Wooden spoon

  • 1 kg chicken pieces — bone-in, cut into serving pieces
  • 2 large onions — sliced
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 1 green bell pepper — sliced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Season chicken with salt, pepper, and paprika.
  2. Heat oil in a large pan over high heat. Brown chicken pieces for 4—5 minutes per side until golden. Remove.
  3. In the same pan, cook onions until golden — 6 minutes.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, and bell pepper. Cook 2 minutes.
  5. Add tomatoes, curry powder, and cumin. Cook for 5—7 minutes until thick.
  6. Return chicken. Add ¼ cup water. Cover and cook for 15—20 minutes until chicken is cooked through.
  7. Uncover and reduce sauce for 3—4 minutes until it clings to the chicken.
  8. Add lemon juice and coriander. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Brown the chicken in a very hot pan — this crust is the soul of wet fry.
  • Reduce the sauce at the end until it just coats the chicken — not swimming in liquid.
  • Bell pepper adds authentic wet fry character — do not skip it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Low heat for browning — produces pale, steamed chicken.
  • Too watery sauce — reduce until thick and clinging.

The Science

Two-stage cooking: high heat creates Maillard browning on the chicken surface; gentle covered simmering then finishes cooking the interior without drying it out.

Cultural Note

Chicken wet fry is one of the most ordered dishes in Kenyan restaurants — beloved for its bold sauce, caramelised chicken, and the way it pairs perfectly with ugali or chapati.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat quickly in a pan with a splash of water.

Serve with: Ugali, Chapati, Steamed Rice, Sukuma Wiki, Kachumbari
