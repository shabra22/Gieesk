🦐 Ghanaian Okro Soup

Fetri detchi — okro soup

Finely diced rather than puréed, dry-fried three minutes, then stock and half a teaspoon of edible potash — and seven kinds of seafood.

The other way to make Ewe okra soup, and it is the opposite of the dough method: five hundred grams of okra finely diced and dry-fried for three minutes, then stock for two minutes, then half a teaspoon of edible potash or a teaspoon of bicarbonate of soda for one minute more. The seafood load is extraordinary — grilled mackerel, smoked haddock, cubed salmon, cod, crabs, smoked tilapia and prawns, plus shiitake mushrooms. Three tablespoons of zomi, a litre of fish stock and three hundred grams of kale or spinach. She calls it homage to her Ewe dish of banku and okra soup.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Blender, Knife, Wooden spoon

  • 500 g okra, finely diced
  • 2 large onions, finely diced
  • 1 large onion, roughly chopped
  • 50 g blended ginger
  • 3 tbsp zomi (spiced palm oil), or groundnut oil
  • 2 habanero chillies
  • 1/2 tsp edible potash, or 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda
  • 1 litre fish or vegetable stock
  • 1 shrimp stock cube
  • 300 g kale or spinach
  • 3 large blended tomatoes
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Seafood and mushrooms

  • 2 large grilled mackerel
  • 150 g smoked haddock
  • 150 g cubed salmon
  • 150 g cubed cod
  • 200 g shelled medium crabs
  • 150 g smoked tilapia
  • 150 g prawns
  • 150 g shiitake mushrooms

Method

  1. Soften the onion in the zomi, about 2 minutes.
  2. Add the chilli and cook 2 minutes.
  3. Add the blended ginger and cook 3 minutes.
  4. Add the blended tomatoes and cook 3 minutes.
  5. Add the smoked tilapia and cook 5 minutes.
  6. Separately dry-fry the finely diced okra 3 minutes.
  7. Add stock to the okra and cook 2 minutes.
  8. Add the edible potash or bicarbonate of soda and cook for another minute.
  9. Combine with the soup base and cook 2 minutes.
  10. Add the remaining seafood, the mushrooms and the greens, and let it steam 5 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • Dry-frying the diced okra for three minutes before any liquid is the move that distinguishes this version. It drives off some water and concentrates the flavour before the mucilage develops.
  • Half a teaspoon of edible potash OR a teaspoon of bicarbonate of soda — note the alkali is optional in effect but doubles in quantity if you switch. Potash is stronger.
  • The alkali goes in for ONE minute only, after the stock. Any longer and it will taste soapy.
  • Finely diced, not puréed. This gives a soup with recognisable okra in it rather than the self-bound dough of the other documented version.
  • Zomi is the deeper, spiced palm oil and only three tablespoons of it for eight servings — a lean soup by Ghanaian standards.
  • Seven kinds of seafood is a diaspora abundance rather than a village pot, and she is open that this is a UK adaptation. Use three or four and it will still work.
  • Kale as an alternative to spinach is her substitution and it holds its texture better in a long soup.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the dry-fry and adding the okra to liquid.
  • Leaving the alkali in for more than a minute.
  • Swapping potash for soda at the same quantity.
  • Puréeing the okra, which belongs to the other version.
  • Adding the delicate seafood early.

Health Benefits

  • Okra supplies soluble fibre, folate and vitamins C and K.
  • A very high and varied seafood load gives omega-3 fats, iodine, selenium and complete protein.

Cultural Note

Two Ewe-connected cooks publishing fundamentally different okra techniques under the same dish name is a good reminder that regional cuisines are not single recipes. The dicing-and-alkali route gives a soup you can see the okra in; the dough route gives something smoother and stranger. Both go with banku.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. The seafood does not keep longer.

Reheating: Gentle heat. Do not boil.

Serve with: Banku, Akple, Kenkey

  • ON THE ALKALI: this is the only documented Ghanaian okra soup to use one. Edible potash in Ghana is the same family of mineral alkali as Nigeria's kaun, which is chemically trona rather than true potash.
  • ON ZOMI: palm oil processed further than ordinary red palm oil, darker and more strongly flavoured. See Abomu and Mpotompoto, where it is also specified by name.
