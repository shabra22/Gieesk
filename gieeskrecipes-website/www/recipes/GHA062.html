🥩 Chichinga with Suya Spice Blend

Chichinga

A chef's two-blend system — the suya is toasted first until it darkens slightly and smells nutty and toasty, three to five minutes.

This is the most elaborate documented chichinga, built on two separate spice blends the chef keeps on hand. The suya blend alone runs to ten bouillon cubes, anise seeds, cloves, five grains of selim, a cup of peanut butter powder, a quarter cup of cayenne and three tablespoons each of ginger, garlic, paprika and onion powder — and crucially it is toasted in a dry pot before use until it darkens slightly and smells nutty and toasty. Two pounds of sirloin pounded to a quarter inch, cut into inch squares, three to a skewer with red and yellow peppers and red onion.

Ingredients

Equipment: 8 to 10 metal or wooden skewers, Dry pot, for toasting the suya, Meat mallet, Grill, Basting brush

  • 2 lb sirloin steak
  • 3 tbsp ginger garlic puree
  • 2 tbsp all-day seasoning blend
  • 1 1/2 tbsp salt
  • 2 tsp tomato paste
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • 3/4 cup suya spice blend, plus more for garnish
  • 1/3 cup neutral oil
  • 2 medium red onions, 1/2-inch dice
  • 2 red bell peppers
  • 2 yellow bell peppers
  • Sea salt, to garnish

Suya spice blend

  • 10 bouillon cubes
  • 1 1/2 tsp anise seeds
  • 1/2 tsp whole cloves
  • 5 grains of selim
  • 1 cup unsweetened peanut butter powder
  • 1/4 cup cayenne pepper
  • 3 tbsp ground ginger
  • 3 tbsp garlic powder
  • 3 tbsp sweet paprika
  • 3 tbsp onion powder

All-day seasoning blend, makes a scant 3/4 cup

  • 1 tbsp dried parsley
  • 1 tbsp dried oregano
  • 1 tbsp salt
  • 2 tsp dried thyme
  • 2 tsp dried rosemary
  • 2 tsp whole black peppercorns
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1/4 cup sweet paprika
  • 3 tbsp granulated garlic
  • 2 tbsp granulated onion
  • 2 tsp Worcestershire powder (optional)

Method

  1. Combine the all-day seasoning blend ingredients.
  2. Combine the suya spice blend ingredients.
  3. Toast the suya in a pot over medium-low heat until it has darkened slightly and smells nutty and toasty, 3 to 5 minutes.
  4. Pound the steak to a quarter inch thick and cut into 1-inch squares.
  5. Coat the meat with the ginger garlic puree, all-day seasoning, salt, tomato paste, paprika and half the suya.
  6. Skewer about 3 pieces of sirloin per skewer with the onion and peppers.
  7. Mix the remaining suya with the oil and brush over the skewers.
  8. Refrigerate 1 hour, then bring to room temperature.
  9. Grill hot, turning and basting, until the vegetables and meat are slightly charred and the vegetables are tender, 5 to 8 minutes total.
  10. Garnish with more suya and sea salt.

Chef Tips

  • Toast the suya blend first. Until it darkens slightly and smells nutty and toasty, three to five minutes — this is the step that separates a chef's suya from a shop-bought one, and it transforms the raw peanut powder.
  • Pound the sirloin to a quarter inch before cutting. Thin squares cook through in the five to eight minutes the vegetables need.
  • Half the suya into the meat, half mixed with oil as a baste. Two applications, and the second one is what crusts.
  • Refrigerate an hour, then bring back to room temperature before grilling. Cold meat on a hot grill chars outside and stays raw inside.
  • The doneness cue covers both components: slightly charred meat AND tender vegetables. The vegetables are the slower of the two.
  • Ten bouillon cubes in the blend is a lot of salt, so go carefully with the extra tablespoon and a half.
  • Both blends keep, which is the point of making them — the all-day seasoning makes a scant three quarters of a cup and is useful far beyond this dish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using the suya blend untoasted.
  • Grilling straight from the fridge.
  • Meat thicker than a quarter inch.
  • All the suya into the marinade, leaving nothing to baste with.
  • Over-salting on top of ten bouillon cubes.

Health Benefits

  • Sirloin is high in protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
  • Peanut butter powder gives the peanut flavour with far less fat than paste.
  • Red and yellow peppers are very high in vitamin C.
  • Grilled rather than fried, with the oil used only as a baste.

Cultural Note

Keeping two standing spice blends and toasting one before use is a restaurant approach to a street food, and it is how a chef makes a charcoal-grill flavour reproducible in a home kitchen. The bouillon cubes in the suya are not a shortcut but how the blend is actually made across West Africa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best at once. The blends keep for months in sealed jars.

Reheating: Briefly on a hot grill or under a broiler.

Serve with: Shito, Kelewele, Jollof Rice
