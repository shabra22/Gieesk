🍃 Ekwang (Cocoyam Leaf Rolls in Palm-Oil Stew)

Ekwang / ekpang nkukwo

Grated cocoyam wrapped in cocoyam or other leaves into finger-shaped rolls, arranged in a palm-oil-coated pot over beef, dried fish and crayfish and simmered about an hour.

The page frames ekwang as a traditional Nigerian dish native to the Efik and Ibibio that is widely enjoyed in Cameroon and very popular with the Bakweri tribe in the southwest. Beef is cooked with salt, Maggi and onions until tender and 2–3 cups of stock reserved. Cocoyam is peeled, cut, washed and pulsed or grated and lightly salted; the leaves are washed and torn. The pot is coated with about 1/2 cup of palm oil. About a tablespoon of grated cocoyam goes on a leaf and is wrapped tightly into a finger shape; the rolls are arranged in a circle leaving a hole in the middle. The onion, crayfish, fish, meat, seasonings and palm oil go in, and the pot simmers about 10 minutes without stirring until the ekwang firms up. Then the stock and about 3 cups of water (or 4–6 cups hot water) are added and it cooks on medium heat about an hour, stirring as little as possible.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wide pot, Food processor or grater

  • 4 pounds cocoyam
  • 4–5 bunches leafy greens (cocoyam leaves, collard greens, spinach or romaine lettuce)
  • 1/2 pound boiled beef
  • 1–2 cups dried fish
  • 1/2 cup ground crayfish
  • 1/2 medium onion, chopped
  • 1 1/2–2 cups palm oil (red oil)
  • 1 tablespoon bouillon granules or Maggi
  • 1 tablespoon ground country onions (optional)
  • 1 teaspoon white pepper
  • 1 Scotch bonnet pepper or any hot pepper (optional)
  • 1 teaspoon bitter leaf (optional; printed ‘teapoons’)
  • Salt and pepper to taste

Method

  1. Cook the beef with salt, Maggi and onions until tender. Reserve 2–3 cups of stock.
  2. Peel, cut and wash the cocoyam. Pulse or grate it and salt it lightly.
  3. Wash and tear the leaves; discard any that are torn through.
  4. Coat the pot with about 1/2 cup palm oil. Place about a tablespoon of grated cocoyam on a leaf and wrap it tightly into a finger shape. Arrange the rolls in a circle, leaving a hole in the middle.
  5. Add the onion, crayfish, fish, meat, salt, pepper, bouillon, palm oil and country onions. (The page is ambiguous about which pot gets these.)
  6. Simmer about 10 minutes without stirring, until the ekwang firms up. Add about 1/2 cup of the beef stock.
  7. Add the remaining stock and about 3 cups of water (or 4–6 cups hot water). Cook on medium heat about an hour until fully cooked, stirring as little as possible.

Chef Tips

  • The printed cue for the first stage is that the ekwang ‘firms up’ after about 10 minutes.
  • Stir as little as possible so the rolls hold together.
  • A reader comment says some people react to cocoyam leaves; the author suggests collard greens.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring the pot and breaking the rolls.
  • Overfilling the leaves.
  • Treating the 1 1/2–2 cups of palm oil and the 1/2 cup for coating as the same quantity — the page is unclear.
