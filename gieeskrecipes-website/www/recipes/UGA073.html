🍗 Dressed Chicken Luwombo

Oluwombo lw'enkoko

Two whole chickens grilled golden over embers strewn with banana peels, stuffed with curried potatoes, trussed, wrapped in banana leaf with a third of the sauce each, and steamed three to four hours.

This is luwombo built like a banquet dish rather than a household one, from the Daily Monitor's long-running food columnist. Three details make it: banana peels spread over the barely glowing embers, so the chickens take up banana smoke before they ever see a leaf; the chickens grilled whole and then stuffed with potatoes cooked in their own separate tomato sauce; and a steaming time of three to four hours for village chickens, or under an hour for broilers. It is a real distinction and worth respecting.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal stove or barbecue grill, Two saucepans, Large saucepan with a lid, Matooke stems, or a perforated metal colander, String

  • 2 whole chickens
  • Banana leaves
  • Tomatoes
  • Shallots
  • Garlic — reserve 10 ml (2 tsp)
  • 60 ml (4 tbsp) tomato paste
  • Salt and pepper
  • Butter — or margarine
  • Bay leaf
  • Chicken stock
  • Irish potatoes
  • Onions
  • Kayi — string, for trussing and tying

Used in the method but absent from the printed list

  • Cooking oil

For the fire

  • Charcoal and banana peels

Method

    The grill

    1. Get a charcoal stove or grill alight and fiercely hot, then let the embers die down until they are barely glowing.
    2. Spread some banana peels over the embers.
    3. Grill the chickens whole until they are a good golden brown. Remove and set aside.

    The sauce

    1. Drop the tomatoes into boiling water for a few minutes, remove, peel off the skins and mash them coarsely with your fingers.
    2. Heat cooking oil and fry the chopped shallots until translucent.
    3. Add the garlic — holding back 2 teaspoons — and fry a few minutes.
    4. Add two-thirds of the tomatoes, the grilled chickens and the tomato paste.
    5. Simmer over low heat. Add salt, pepper, the butter, the bay leaf and some chicken stock.

    The stuffing

    1. Peel and quarter the Irish potatoes.
    2. In a second pan, heat oil and fry the chopped onions until translucent, then add the reserved garlic and fry a few minutes.
    3. Add the remaining tomatoes, half the tomato paste, salt and pepper.
    4. Add the potatoes, bring to the boil, and simmer covered about 10 minutes.

    Assembly

    1. Stuff the two chickens with the potatoes and truss them with string.
    2. Lay a luwombo leaf in a shallow bowl and set a chicken in it with the drumsticks facing up.
    3. Spoon about a third of the sauce into the leaf.
    4. Fold the ends of the banana leaf together and tie carefully with string, without piercing the leaf.

    Steaming

    1. Line a large saucepan with matooke stems, or use a perforated metal colander.
    2. Set the luwombos inside, tuck banana leaves over the top and tie with string or banana fibre.
    3. Steam over low heat until the chicken is tender — allow at least 3 to 4 hours for village chickens, or under an hour for broilers.
    4. Serve the parcels closed.

    Chef Tips

    • Banana peels on the embers. That is the detail to steal from this recipe — the chickens pick up banana smoke before they are ever wrapped, and it is why this tastes different from a luwombo that was only steamed.
    • Embers barely glowing, not fiery. Get the fire hot and then wait.
    • Three to four hours for a village chicken, under an hour for a broiler. That is the columnist's own distinction and it matters more than any other timing in the recipe.
    • Tie the leaf without piercing it. A punctured parcel leaks its sauce into the steaming water and you lose the point of the dish.
    • Reserve two teaspoons of garlic for the potato pan. The recipe splits the garlic between the two sauces and the printed list does not say so.
    • Cooking oil is used twice in the method and does not appear in the ingredient list at all. You need it; the amount is your judgement.
    • The same columnist published this recipe again the following year specifying margarine where this version says butter. Same writer, same dish, different fat, unreconciled — use either.
    • Matooke stems line the pan in place of banana leaf spines. A metal colander does the same job and the recipe says so.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Grilling over flames instead of dying embers.
    • Skipping the banana peels.
    • Steaming a village chicken for an hour.
    • Piercing the parcel when tying it.
    • Using all the garlic in the first sauce.

    Health Benefits

    • Chicken supplies complete protein, niacin, selenium and vitamin B6.
    • Steamed in leaf rather than fried, so no additional fat after the sauce.
    • Tomatoes, onions and potatoes all go into the same dish.
    • Contains dairy in the butter or margarine.

    Cultural Note

    Luwombo was created in 1887 for Kabaka Mwanga II by his chef Kawuta, and this version is closer to that court register than a household one: two whole birds, a stuffing, a separate sauce, and half a day of steaming. Its author wrote it for the sixtieth anniversary of independence, recalling a Kampala where restaurants were scarce and the local woteli establishments in the peri-urban areas served Ugandan food.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate in the parcels up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Steam the parcels again for 15 minutes.

    Serve with: Matooke, Posho, Firinda
