🥜 Spicy Coated Groundnuts

Spicy coated groundnuts

A pound of raw peanuts in a spiced batter fried at around 360 degrees — the browner they are, the crispier, but not past burnt.

Coated groundnuts are sold in bags all over Ghana and this is the documented version: eight ounces of flour with three teaspoons of ground cayenne, a teaspoon of salt, a quarter teaspoon of white pepper and a teaspoon of baking powder, bound with a quarter cup of evaporated milk, about four tablespoons of water and two eggs, coating a pound of shelled raw unsalted peanuts with their skins on. The oil goes to around 360 degrees. Her doneness cue is a judgement rather than a point: the browner they are, the crispier they'll be, but being careful they do not cross the line to burned.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Deep pot or fryer, Thermometer, Slotted spoon

  • 1 lb shelled raw unsalted peanuts with skins — about 3 cups Spanish peanuts
  • 8 oz flour — 1 1/2 to 2 cups unsifted
  • 3 tsp ground dried red cayenne pepper
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp white pepper
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1/4 cup evaporated milk, plus about 4 tbsp water
  • 2 eggs
  • Oil, for deep frying

Method

  1. Combine the flour, cayenne, salt, white pepper and baking powder.
  2. Beat the evaporated milk, water and eggs together.
  3. Mix the wet into the dry to make a thick batter.
  4. Stir in the raw peanuts with their skins on, so each is coated.
  5. Heat the oil to around 360 degrees Fahrenheit.
  6. Fry in batches, separating the clusters.
  7. The browner they are, the crispier they will be — but be careful they do not cross the line to burned.
  8. Drain and cool completely before storing.

Chef Tips

  • Her cue is a judgement and it is honest: the browner they are, the crispier they will be, but do not let them cross the line to burned. There is no fixed point.
  • Raw peanuts, not roasted. They cook in the batter, and pre-roasted nuts will be overdone by the time the coating crisps.
  • Skins on. They add colour, a little bitterness and most of the antioxidants.
  • Three hundred and sixty degrees is on the hot side for a battered item, which is what gets the coating crisp before the nut overcooks.
  • A thick batter, not a thin one. Four tablespoons of water is the adjustment — add it gradually.
  • Cool completely before storing or the steam will soften the coating.
  • Spanish peanuts are specified as the three-cup equivalent. They are small, round and skin-on, which is exactly what this wants.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using roasted peanuts.
  • Removing the skins.
  • A thin batter, which slides off.
  • Frying too cool, so the coating absorbs oil.
  • Bagging them while still warm.

Health Benefits

  • Peanuts supply protein, niacin, folate, magnesium and vitamin E.
  • The skins carry most of the nut's antioxidant polyphenols.
  • Eggs and evaporated milk add complete protein.
  • Capsaicin from three teaspoons of cayenne.

Cultural Note

Coated groundnuts are sold in twisted paper and plastic bags at every Ghanaian lorry park and junction, and they are the snack that goes with a bottle of something cold. The batter is what makes them Ghanaian rather than merely roasted nuts — spiced, crisp and faintly sweet from the milk.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 weeks in an airtight tin once completely cool.

Reheating: A low oven briefly to re-crisp.

Serve with: Kelewele, Sobolo, Pito
