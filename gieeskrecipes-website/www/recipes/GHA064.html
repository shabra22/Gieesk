🥜 Tankora Powder

Tankora — yaji, suya spice

Half a cup of powdered peanuts from kulikuli with a quarter cup of toasted corn flour — the de-oiled peanut is what makes it a powder rather than a paste.

Tankora is the dry coating for chichinga and the reason it works is the kulikuli: peanuts that have had their oil pressed out, so the powder stays dry instead of clumping into butter. Half a cup of that powder goes with a quarter cup of toasted corn flour, made from a quarter cup of toasted ground white popcorn, plus dried ground red pepper, dried ginger and salt to taste. Optional additions are dried powdered green bell pepper, mace and ground white pepper. The instruction is one line: mix all of the ingredients together.

Ingredients

Equipment: Spice grinder or mortar, Mixing bowl, Sealed jar

  • 1/2 cup powdered peanuts from kulikuli
  • 1/4 cup toasted corn flour — from 1/4 cup toasted, ground white popcorn
  • Dried ground red pepper, to taste — about 1/4-1/2 tsp
  • Dried ground ginger, to taste — about 1/2 tsp
  • Salt, to taste — maybe 1/4 tsp if the peanuts are salted

Optional

  • 1/2 tsp dried powdered green bell pepper
  • 1/4 tsp mace
  • 1/2 tsp ground white pepper

Method

  1. Press the oil from roasted peanuts to make kulikuli, then powder it.
  2. Toast a quarter cup of white popcorn and grind it to a flour.
  3. Combine the peanut powder and the toasted corn flour.
  4. Add the dried red pepper, dried ginger and salt to taste.
  5. Add any of the optional green bell pepper powder, mace or white pepper.
  6. Mix all of the ingredients together.
  7. Store in a sealed jar.

Chef Tips

  • The peanuts must be de-oiled. Ordinary ground peanuts turn to butter under pressure and will clump — kulikuli, with the oil pressed out, stays a dry powder.
  • Toasted ground white popcorn is her corn flour, which is an unusual and clever route to it. Toasting is what gives the nutty depth.
  • Salt only if the peanuts were unsalted. Her note is about a quarter teaspoon if they were salted — taste first.
  • Mace is an unusual inclusion and it is optional. It gives a warm, faintly floral note under the pepper.
  • Others use seasoning cubes in place of some of the salt and savoury depth, by her own note. That is what the elaborate chef version at Chichinga with Suya Spice Blend does, with ten of them.
  • Keep it dry and sealed. Any moisture and the peanut powder cakes.
  • Equal parts by feel is roughly two of peanut powder to one of corn flour, which is the backbone of the blend.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using ordinary ground peanuts rather than de-oiled kulikuli.
  • Untoasted corn flour.
  • Salting before tasting.
  • Storing it somewhere humid.
  • Grinding the corn so fine it becomes starch rather than flour.

Health Benefits

  • De-oiled peanut powder is high in protein with much of the fat removed.
  • Toasted maize adds fibre and B vitamins.
  • Ginger and pepper both carry documented bioactive compounds.
  • Vegan and gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Tankora is sold in little newspaper twists beside the chichinga grill so you can dust on more as you eat, and the de-oiled peanut at its heart is a genuinely clever piece of food technology — a way to get peanut flavour as a dry powder centuries before anyone sold defatted peanut flour in a packet.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Months in a sealed jar kept dry.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Chichinga, Grilled chicken, Dusted over kelewele
