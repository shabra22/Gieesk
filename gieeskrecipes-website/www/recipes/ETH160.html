🫙 Ethiopian Condiment Platter

ቅምስ ፍሬ — Qims Fre

Ethiopian Condiment Platter — the complete set of Ethiopian table condiments assembled: Awaze (ETH144), Silsi (ETH146), Ayib with Herbs (ETH145), Honey Butter (ETH152), Timatim Selata (ETH147), Berbere Oil (ETH155), and Green Chilli Paste (ETH149). The full Ethiopian condiment experience.

The Ethiopian condiment platter is brought to the celebration table alongside the mesob — each condiment providing a different flavour dimension: fierce heat (awaze, mitmita), fresh acid (silsi, timatim), cooling dairy (ayib), sweet richness (honey butter), and oil richness (berbere oil). Together they complete the Ethiopian flavour universe.

Ingredients

Equipment: Multiple small ramekins and bowls, Serving board

    Assemble from individual recipes:

    • Awaze — hot spiced paste
    • Silsi — fresh tomato sauce
    • Ayib with Herbs — cool cheese
    • Honey Butter — sweet spread
    • Timatim Selata — tomato salad
    • Berbere Oil — fasting oil
    • Green Chilli Paste — fresh heat

    Method

    1. Make berbere oil (ETH155) first — it needs time to infuse.
    2. Make awaze (ETH144) — improves overnight.
    3. Make silsi (ETH146) — 30 minutes.
    4. Make ayib with herbs (ETH145) — 2 hours for draining.
    5. Make green chilli paste (ETH149) — 15 minutes.
    6. Make timatim selata (ETH147) — serve fresh.
    7. Make honey butter (ETH152) — 5 minutes.
    8. Arrange all in small ramekins on a wooden board.

    Chef Tips

    • Awaze and berbere oil can be made days ahead.
    • Silsi and timatim salad must be made the day of.
    • Ayib needs 2 hours of draining — plan ahead.
    • Arrange by colour for maximum visual impact.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Last-minute preparation of everything — most condiments improve with time.

    Cultural Note

    The Ethiopian condiment platter is the flavour toolkit of the great Ethiopian meal — every possible flavour dimension available at once. The Ethiopian tradition of placing multiple condiments on the table simultaneously reflects the philosophy of abundance: that the well-prepared host ensures that every guest can adjust the meal to their precise preference.
