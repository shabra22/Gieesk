🍖 Pork Joint Pork

Pork — Nsambya style

Four kilos of ribs marinated three hours in ground garlic, ginger and chilli, then deep-fried in batches until very dark and crisp — with the potatoes and matooke cooked afterwards in the same oil.

A Ugandan pork joint sells pork by the kilo and offers it three ways: deep-fried, pan-fried, or roasted on skewers over coals. This is the deep-fried version, recorded at a pork centre in Kabale, and the detail worth stealing is what happens after: the potatoes and the matooke go into the reserved frying oil, which is where all the flavour ended up. It is eaten with a sharp cabbage slaw dressed with lemon, which is the only thing cutting through it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle or grinder, Large saucepan for deep frying, Slotted spoon, Skewers, for the roasted version

    The pork

    • 4 kg pork ribs
    • 1 garlic bulb
    • A palm-sized chunk of root ginger
    • 5 chillies
    • Salt
    • Cooking oil, for the marinade and for frying

    For the pan-fried version instead

    • 8 tomatoes, chopped
    • 2 onions, chopped
    • 1 stock cube

    The sides

    • 1 green cabbage, shredded
    • 2 tomatoes, chopped
    • 2 red onions, sliced
    • 4 medium potatoes
    • 4 matooke
    • 1 lemon
    • Pork fat or cooking oil

    Method

    1. Grind the garlic, ginger and chillies together and mix with salt and oil into a marinade.
    2. Coat the ribs thoroughly and marinate for at least 3 hours.

    Deep-fried

    1. Heat oil in a large saucepan and fry the ribs in batches until very dark brown and crispy.
    2. Lift out and drain, reserving the oil.

    Or pan-fried

    1. Fry the pork briefly, then add the chopped tomatoes, onions, stock cube and salt, and cook for 10 minutes, breaking the tomatoes down into a paste.

    Or roasted

    1. Season the ribs, thread them onto skewers and cook over coals or in an oven.

    The sides

    1. Cook the potatoes in the reserved frying oil for about 10 minutes.
    2. Cook the matooke in the same oil, for longer.
    3. Make the slaw with the cabbage, tomatoes, onions, salt and the juice of the lemon.
    4. Serve everything together.

    Chef Tips

    • Cook the potatoes and matooke in the pork oil. That is the whole reason a pork joint plate tastes like it does, and throwing the oil away is throwing away the best of it.
    • Very dark brown and crispy, not golden. Undercooked pork ribs at a joint would be sent back.
    • Fry in batches. Crowding the pan drops the temperature and you get greasy pork instead of crisp.
    • Three hours of marinating minimum. The ground garlic, ginger and chilli needs time to get into a rib.
    • The lemon in the slaw is not a garnish — it is the only acid on the plate and it is doing real work.
    • Four kilos sounds like a lot and is a normal order for six people at a joint. Scale down freely.
    • Some joints season with vinegar rather than the garlic-ginger-chilli marinade, and some roast rather than fry. All three are standard.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Discarding the frying oil.
    • Pulling the ribs out at golden rather than dark.
    • Frying it all at once.
    • Cutting the marinating time.
    • Serving it without something sharp alongside.

    Health Benefits

    • Pork ribs are high in protein, thiamine, selenium and zinc.
    • The cabbage slaw brings raw vegetables and vitamin C to a very rich plate.
    • Garlic, ginger and chilli are all in the marinade rather than a sauce.
    • Deep-fried pork with oil-cooked potatoes is celebration eating, not everyday food.

    Cultural Note

    The pork joint is one of Kampala's real social institutions — Wandegeya, Nakulabye, Ntinda, Kyebando — and prices per skewer or per kilo are common knowledge. The trade has its own vocabulary too: kikalayi for the pan-fried pork, kibaafu for another pan-fried category, and the joint itself as a place people go to sit rather than to eat quickly.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Refresh in a hot dry pan or a hot oven to bring the crispness back.

    Serve with: Gonja, Kikalayi, Cabbage slaw with lemon
