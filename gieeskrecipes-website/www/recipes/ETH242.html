🍲 Harari Maraq

ማርቅ — Maraq

Harari Maraq — the aromatic meat broth of the ancient walled city of Harar. Beef or lamb simmered for 90 minutes with berbere, cardamom, fenugreek, and the distinctive Harari spice blend. Served with unleavened Harari bread for dipping.

Harari Maraq is the food of the ancient Jugol walls — the slow-simmered broth that has been cooked in Harar's narrow streets for centuries, reflecting the city's position at the crossroads of Arab, Somali, Oromo, and Amhara food cultures.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Harar — the walled city, the fourth holiest city of Islam

Harar is one of the most extraordinary cities in Africa — a medieval walled city (the Jugol) that is the fourth holiest city of Islam after Mecca, Medina, and Jerusalem. It has 82 mosques within its walls, a tradition of coffee (Harari coffee is among Ethiopia's finest), and a distinctive food culture that blends Arab, Somali, Oromo, and Amhara influences. Harari Maraq is the city's signature meat broth — a slow-cooked beef or lamb broth with aromatic spices including berbere, cardamom, and fenugreek, served with the distinctively Harari unleavened bread. Harar also has the famous tradition of feeding wild hyenas by hand.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 1kg bone-in beef or lamb
  • 2 onions — halved
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 1 tsp berbere
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 2 litres water
  • 2 tsp salt
  • Fresh coriander and lime for serving

Method

  1. Combine all ingredients except coriander and lime in a large pot.
  2. Bring to a boil. Skim foam 5 minutes.
  3. Reduce heat. Simmer covered 80—90 minutes.
  4. Remove bones. Shred meat. Return to broth.
  5. Season. Garnish with coriander.
  6. Serve with lime and Harari bread.

Chef Tips

  • Bone-in meat is essential — the marrow enriches the broth.
  • The Harari spice blend is subtler than standard berbere.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boneless meat — thin broth.

Cultural Note

Harari Maraq is the food of the Jugol — cooked in the narrow streets of the ancient walled city, eaten by families who have lived in the same house for generations, spiced with a blend that reflects 1,000 years of trade through one of Africa's most cosmopolitan cities.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Harari unleavened bread, With lime wedges, At Harari celebrations
