🦀 Tanzanian Crab Curry

Mchuzi wa Kaa

Tanzanian Mchuzi wa Kaa — fresh Zanzibar crabs in a rich coconut milk and tamarind curry. The most communal seafood dish of the coast — messy, fun, and extraordinarily flavourful.

Crab curry is the most communal seafood dish of the Tanzanian coast — eaten with hands, shells cracked at the table, sauce mopped with chapati. The mess is half the point.

Ingredients

Equipment: Very large pot

  • 4 fresh whole crabs (500g each) — cleaned and quartered
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 4 tomatoes — diced
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 300ml coconut milk
  • 2 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup coriander

Method

  1. Clean and quarter crabs. Crack claws.
  2. Cook onions until golden. Add garlic, ginger, spices.
  3. Add tomatoes. Cook 8 minutes.
  4. Add crab pieces. Stir to coat.
  5. Add coconut milk and tamarind. Simmer covered 20—25 minutes.
  6. Crab is ready when bright red throughout.

Chef Tips

  • Crack claws before cooking.
  • Crab turns bright red when cooked.
  • Provide finger bowls.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Uncrushed claws.
  • Undercooked crab — grey not red.

The Science

Crab shell contains astaxanthin bound to protein — heat denatures the protein, releasing astaxanthin which turns the shell bright red. This colour change is the cooking indicator.

Cultural Note

Crab curry is the most social seafood dish of coastal Tanzania — the meal eaten together, shells cracked, sauce shared, voices raised.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: Chapati (TZA004), Wali wa Nazi (TZA002), Cold Drinks
