🌽 Roasted Corn with Ube

Agbado yinyan — roast corn and African pear

Fresh corn roasted on hot coals and African pear either roasted alongside or softened in freshly boiled water — starch and fat, the same logic as tortilla and avocado.

This is the rainy season on a Nigerian roadside: a cob of corn roasted over coals until the kernels are toasty and fragrant, and ube — the African pear — either roasted beside it or dropped into freshly boiled water to soften. Ube cooked tastes, by one good description, like avocado cut through with green olives and a touch of cream, and the pairing is not arbitrary. It is starch plus a fatty fruit, which is the same structural logic as corn tortillas and guacamole. No quantities are published and none are needed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill or hot coals, Tongs, A bowl, if softening the ube in water, Kettle

  • Fresh corn on the cob
  • Ube (African pear), whole

Optional third element

  • Fresh coconut, in pieces
  • Salt

Method

  1. Strip the husks from the corn.
  2. Roast the cobs over hot coals, turning, until the kernels are golden and toasty and smell fragrant — around 15 to 20 minutes.
  3. For the ube, either roast it on the coals alongside the corn, or put it in a bowl and cover with freshly boiled water until it softens.
  4. Serve the hot corn with the softened ube, pulling the flesh off the pear's stone.
  5. Add a piece of fresh coconut if you have it, and salt to taste.

Chef Tips

  • Ube must be roasted on hot coals or soaked in freshly boiled water before eating. Raw it is unpleasant and almost inedible.
  • Freshly boiled water, not hot tap water. It needs real heat to soften.
  • Turn the corn constantly. Roast corn burns in patches if you leave it, and the burnt patches are bitter rather than smoky.
  • Fresh corn, in season. This is a rainy-season food in Nigeria and out of season both components are poor.
  • The pairing works because it is starch plus a fatty fruit — the same reason tortillas go with avocado. Knowing that is what stops it seeming like an odd combination.
  • Fresh coconut makes it a trio, which is how it is often sold.
  • No quantities exist for this in any source and none are needed — one cob and one ube per person is the street-side unit.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Eating ube raw.
  • Using lukewarm water to soften it.
  • Leaving the corn still on the coals until it chars.
  • Trying it out of season with old corn.
  • Treating the ube as a garnish rather than half the dish.

Health Benefits

  • Ube is high in monounsaturated fat, similar in profile to avocado.
  • Fresh corn supplies fibre, folate and lutein.
  • Nothing is added — no oil, no sugar.
  • Vegan and gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Roast corn and ube is the most seasonal food in Nigeria — it appears with the rains, is sold from charcoal braziers at the roadside for a few months, and then goes. Ube is Dacryodes edulis, the African pear, and it has no real substitute: the combination of a starchy cob and a soft, faintly resinous, oily fruit is particular to this part of the world.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Eat at once. Roast corn goes tough within the hour.

Reheating: Back on the coals briefly. Never the microwave.

Serve with: Fresh coconut, Zobo, Kunun Aya
