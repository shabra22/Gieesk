🍹 Tamarind Tonic

Tamarind tonic

Tamarind, ginger and honey boiled together with constant stirring until the flesh dissolves off the seeds, then cooled, sieved and lengthened with lemon and tonic water.

This started as a morning drink — tamarind, ginger, honey, lemon and warm water — and became a long cold one with tonic water added. The technique is the stirring: you keep working it so the tamarind flesh dissolves into the water rather than clinging to the seeds, and you stop when the water has changed colour and the flesh is off. The honey goes in during the boil, which is unusual and is how it is written.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Wooden spoon, Sieve, Jug

  • 240 ml (1 cup) tamarind, peeled
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) ginger, grated
  • 480 ml (2 cups) water
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) honey
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • Tonic water, to top up

Method

  1. Put the water, tamarind, ginger and honey in a clean pan and bring to the boil.
  2. Keep stirring so the tamarind flesh dissolves into the water.
  3. Once the water has changed colour and the flesh is off the seeds, take it off the heat.
  4. Let the juice cool.
  5. Sieve it.
  6. Add the lemon juice and top up with tonic water.
  7. Serve chilled.

Chef Tips

  • Keep stirring throughout the boil. That is what gets the flesh off the seeds and into the water, and a pan left alone gives you weak tonic and wasted tamarind.
  • The colour change and the bare seeds are the cue to stop.
  • The honey goes in at the start, in the boil, which is how the recipe is written. It behaves differently from honey stirred in cold.
  • Cool it before sieving. Hot, the pulp goes straight through.
  • Lemon and tonic water go in after cooling. Boiled lemon juice tastes cooked.
  • Without the tonic water and served warm, this is the morning drink it came from — tamarind, ginger, honey, lemon and hot water.
  • Peel the tamarind properly. Shell fragments in the pan end up in the sieve at best.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not stirring during the boil.
  • Sieving it hot.
  • Adding the lemon juice to the boil.
  • Under-boiling, so the flesh stays on the seeds.
  • Serving it at room temperature.

Health Benefits

  • Tamarind supplies vitamin C, potassium, magnesium and organic acids.
  • Ginger is anti-inflammatory and settles the stomach.
  • Honey rather than refined sugar.
  • The tonic water brings the only added sugar of any consequence.

Cultural Note

Tamarind grows widely in Uganda, particularly in the north and east, and turns up as much in savoury cooking and porridge as in drinks. Tamarind, ginger, honey and lemon in hot water is a very common household morning routine, and this is that drink dressed up.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate the tamarind base up to 4 days. Add the tonic water when serving.

Reheating: Warm the base without the tonic water for a hot drink.

Serve with: Muchomo, Samosas, Bagiya
