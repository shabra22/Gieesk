🥜 Poulet Mafé

Poulet mafé — à la sauce d’arachide

Chicken browned with onions and chile, simmered in a peanut-paste sauce seasoned with nététou — the only mafé in this collection that uses a fermented seasoning.

A short, home-kitchen mafé from the Afrik cooking pages. Chicken pieces are browned in oil, onions and chile are added until the onions turn golden, then garlic for two minutes and tomato concentrate for two more. Peanut paste (the page names Dakatine as one brand), a teaspoon of nététou, salt, pepper and a litre of water go in, and everything simmers gently for 30 to 45 minutes with a kilo of mixed vegetables — carrot, potato, pumpkin, aubergine, sweet potato, cabbage. There is no acid and no okra, and the nététou — pre-fermented locust-bean seasoning — is what gives it a savoury edge.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid, Mortar or bowl for crushing garlic

  • 1 chicken, cut up — weight not printed
  • 200 g peanut paste (the page says ‘Dakatine or other’)
  • 1 kg vegetables of your choice, alone or mixed: carrots, potatoes, pumpkin, aubergines, sweet potatoes, cabbage, etc.
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 small tins of tomato concentrate — tin size not printed
  • 2 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 2 red chiles
  • 1 teaspoon nététou (fermented locust-bean seasoning, bought ready-made)
  • Oil, salt, pepper — quantities not printed
  • 1 litre water

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a large pot and brown the chicken pieces.
  2. Add the sliced onions and the chile and cook until the onions are golden.
  3. Add the crushed garlic and cook 2 minutes so the garlic takes colour, then the tomato concentrate and cook 2 minutes more.
  4. Add the peanut paste, nététou, salt, pepper and 1 litre of water. Cook everything over low heat for 30 to 45 minutes.
  5. Add the vegetables according to how long they take — firm ones (carrot, cassava, pumpkin) with the liquid, soft ones (aubergine, cabbage) later — and cook until tender.
  6. Serve with rice.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Until the onions are golden’ and ‘so that the garlic browns’ are the two colour cues in the original — both better guides than the stopwatch.
  • Nététou is added in small quantity, one teaspoon here. It is pungent and salty; taste before adding more salt.
  • Keep the heat low once the peanut paste goes in, or the paste can scorch on the bottom of the pot.
  • Add the vegetable pieces in stages; the printed method does not stage them, so this is a cook’s adjustment, not a printed step.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • High heat after the peanut paste is in.
  • Adding all vegetables at once so the soft ones disintegrate.
  • Salting before tasting the nététou.

Health Benefits

  • Peanut paste is a good source of protein and healthy fats.
  • A kilo of mixed vegetables gives fibre and a spread of vitamins.
  • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

Cultural Note

One of the claims in the sources is that mafé arrived in Senegal from Mali; Fine Dining Lovers (Mathilde Bourge, 2018) puts it in a single sentence — ‘a dish from Mali adapted to Senegalese cuisine’ — with no citation. No other source in this collection makes or contradicts that claim.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of water.

Serve with: Steamed white rice
