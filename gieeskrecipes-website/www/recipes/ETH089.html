🧄 Ethiopian Garlic Fish

ቀጭን ዋ አሣ — Nech Shinkurt Asa

Ethiopian Garlic Fish — fish fillets pan-fried in kibbeh with abundant garlic, lemon, fresh jalapeño, and coriander. The mild, garlic-forward fish dish that contrasts with the intensely spiced berbere preparations — light, fresh, and completely accessible.

Garlic fish is the refreshing counterpoint to Ethiopia's spiced fish dishes — the kibbeh and abundant garlic produce a golden, aromatic sauce that is significantly milder than berbere-based preparations. Ideal for those who prefer the flavour of kibbeh without the heat of berbere.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 600g fish fillets
  • 3 tbsp kibbeh
  • 8 garlic cloves — sliced
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 jalapeño — sliced
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper

Method

  1. Season fish with salt and pepper.
  2. Heat kibbeh in a wide pan over medium-high heat.
  3. Sear fish 3 minutes per side. Remove. Set aside.
  4. In the same pan: add sliced garlic. Cook 2 minutes until golden.
  5. Add jalapeño and lemon juice.
  6. Return fish. Coat in garlic kibbeh. Cook 1 more minute.
  7. Scatter coriander.

Chef Tips

  • The garlic must be sliced, not minced — it needs to caramelise into golden slices.
  • Remove fish before cooking garlic — garlic burns faster than fish cooks.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-cooking garlic — burned garlic is bitter.
  • Minced garlic — burns too fast.

The Science

Sliced garlic caramelises more evenly than minced — the larger pieces have more surface area for Maillard browning while the interior remains sweet and mild. Minced garlic, with its much smaller pieces, burns on the exterior before the interior has cooked, producing acrid compounds.

Health Benefits

  • Garlic is powerfully antimicrobial and antioxidant.
  • Very lean protein.
  • Kibbeh carries fat-soluble vitamins.

Cultural Note

Garlic fish is the gentle face of Ethiopian fish cooking — for guests who want the kibbeh richness without the berbere heat. It is the dish that makes Ethiopian food accessible to those who are new to it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Very briefly.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Fresh salad, Ayib (ETH096)
