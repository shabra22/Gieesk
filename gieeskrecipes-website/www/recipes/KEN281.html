🍮 Kenyan Crème Caramel

Kremi ya Karamel

Kenyan Crème Caramel — silky, wobbly custard baked in a caramel-lined mould and unmoulded to reveal a gleaming caramel topping. The hotel buffet dessert that never loses its elegance — simple ingredients, classical technique, extraordinary result.

Crème caramel has been on the menu of every Nairobi hotel since the first French-trained chefs arrived. It never goes out of style because it is genuinely perfect — the wobbling custard, the caramel that flows over the plate, the contrast of sweet and slightly bitter caramel with the creamy egg custard.

Ingredients

Equipment: 6 ramekins, Large roasting tin for bain-marie, Small saucepan for caramel, Oven (160°C)

    Caramel

    • 150g sugar
    • 3 tbsp water

    Custard

    • 4 whole eggs
    • 2 egg yolks
    • 80g sugar
    • 500ml whole milk
    • 100ml cream
    • 1 tsp vanilla extract

    Method

    1. Make caramel: heat sugar and water without stirring until deep amber. Pour quickly into ramekins, tilting to coat base. Work fast.
    2. Make custard: whisk eggs, yolks, and sugar. Heat milk and cream until just steaming. Pour slowly over egg mixture, whisking. Add vanilla. Strain.
    3. Pour custard into caramel-lined ramekins.
    4. Place ramekins in a roasting tin. Fill with boiling water halfway up the ramekins.
    5. Bake at 160°C for 35—40 minutes until set with a slight wobble in the centre.
    6. Cool completely. Refrigerate at least 4 hours.
    7. To serve: run a knife around the edge. Place a plate on top. Invert firmly.

    Chef Tips

    • The caramel must be deep amber — pale caramel is too sweet; very dark adds desirable bitterness.
    • The bain-marie keeps oven temperature gentle — direct heat produces curdled custard.
    • It must wobble when baked — a fully set crème caramel is over-baked.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Light caramel — too sweet.
    • No bain-marie — curdled.
    • Over-baking — rubbery texture.

    The Science

    Egg proteins coagulate between 60—85°C. The bain-marie limits the maximum temperature the custard reaches to just under 100°C — far enough above 85°C to set the custard but gentle enough to prevent the rapid coagulation that creates a rubbery, curdled texture. The caramel provides invertase-converted sugars that flow when the dessert is inverted, creating the sauce.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days before unmoulding.

    Reheating: Serve cold or at room temperature.
