🍲 Kenyan Chicken Soup

Supu ya Kuku

Supu ya Kuku is Kenya's most comforting soup — a whole chicken or chicken pieces simmered with onions, garlic, ginger, carrots, tomatoes, and gentle spices until the broth is golden, rich, and deeply nourishing. The Kenyan chicken soup is the ultimate remedy for cold days, illness, and the need for something warm and restorative.

Every culture has its chicken soup, and Kenya is no different. Supu ya kuku is warmth in a bowl — a clear, golden broth rich with the essence of slowly simmered chicken, fragrant with ginger and garlic, and filled with tender vegetables. It is served to the sick, the cold, and the tired across Kenya, and its restorative quality is universally acknowledged.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large stockpot, Ladle, Strainer

  • 1 whole chicken (about 1.5 kg) or 1.2 kg bone-in pieces
  • 2 large onions — roughly chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — halved
  • 4 garlic cloves — crushed
  • 2 tbsp fresh ginger — sliced
  • 3 medium carrots — cut into chunks
  • 2 celery stalks — chopped
  • 2 medium potatoes — quartered
  • 2 litres water
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 2 bay leaves
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice — for serving

Method

  1. Place chicken in a large pot. Cover with cold water. Bring to a boil, cook 5 minutes, then discard this water — removes impurities.
  2. Add 2 litres fresh cold water. Add onions, tomatoes, garlic, ginger, peppercorns, bay leaves, curry powder, and salt.
  3. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to a steady simmer.
  4. Cook for 30—35 minutes until the chicken is completely cooked through.
  5. Remove the chicken. Allow to cool briefly, then shred or cut into pieces — discard the skin and bones or return bones to the pot.
  6. Add carrots, potatoes, and celery to the broth. Simmer for 20 minutes until the vegetables are tender.
  7. Return the chicken pieces to the soup.
  8. Taste and adjust seasoning. Serve hot with fresh coriander and lemon juice.

Chef Tips

  • The first boil-and-discard is essential for a clean, clear golden broth.
  • Never boil vigorously — a gentle simmer produces a clearer, more flavourful broth.
  • Using a whole chicken gives a richer broth than pieces alone — the carcass releases more gelatin.
  • A squeeze of lemon juice just before serving brightens the entire soup.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the first boil — produces a cloudy, slightly gamey broth.
  • Boiling too hard — makes the broth cloudy and the chicken dry.
  • Not seasoning adequately — chicken soup needs generous salt to taste its best.

The Science

Simmering a whole chicken extracts gelatin from the skin, cartilage, and bones. This gelatin dissolves into the broth, giving it body and the characteristic silky mouthfeel that separates a great chicken soup from a thin, watery one. The initial boil-and-discard removes soluble proteins that create foam and a cloudy, slightly bitter flavour.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in gelatin and collagen — supports gut lining, joint health, and skin.
  • Provides complete protein from the chicken.
  • Carrots and tomatoes add vitamins A and C.
  • Ginger has natural anti-inflammatory and digestive properties.
  • One of the most easily digestible, nourishing meals available.

Cultural Note

Supu ya kuku is Kenya's universal comfort food — served to the sick, the cold, the tired, and the grieving across every community and every region. The warmth of chicken soup crosses all boundaries of language, religion, and culture, making it one of the most deeply human foods in the Kenyan repertoire.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on stovetop until hot throughout.

Serve with: Fresh Bread, Chapati, White Rice, Lemon Wedges, Pili Pili Sauce
