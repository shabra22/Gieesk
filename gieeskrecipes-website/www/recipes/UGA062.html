🐜 Nswaa

Nswaa — nsswa, winged termites

Winged termites stirred in a dry pan five minutes until the wings drop off and can be winnowed out, then washed and returned to a clean pan with salt and onion for ten minutes more.

Nswaa are winged termites, and the Baganda have three ways of doing them: roasted, boiled, or pounded into flour and prepared as luwombo — the last being the most popular. The roasting has a doneness cue built into it that nothing else in Ugandan insect cookery has: five minutes of stirring and the wings come off by themselves, and then you winnow them out. Five named types come out of different anthills at different times, and nsejjere, the biggest and fattest, is considered the most delicious.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Wooden spoon, Winnowing tray or basket, Mortar and pestle, for the pounded version, Drying tray

  • Winged termites (nswaa) — no quantity is given in the source
  • Salt
  • Onions, chopped

For the boiled method

  • Salted water

Method

    Method one — roasted

    1. Heat a saucepan and drop in the winged ants.
    2. Stir with a wooden spoon for about 5 minutes. The insects lose their wings during this step, and the wings must be winnowed out.
    3. Wash them lightly.
    4. Return them to a clean pan over medium heat, sprinkle with salt and add the chopped onions.
    5. Stir constantly for about 10 minutes.

    Method two — boiled

    1. Add salted water before putting them on the fire.
    2. Boil about 10 to 15 minutes.
    3. Sun-dry, then remove the wings.

    Method three — pounded, the most popular among the Baganda

    1. Clean by removing the wings, then roast.
    2. Sun-dry.
    3. Pound into a flour.
    4. Prepare as luwombo.

    Chef Tips

    • The wings coming off during the five-minute roast is the cue. You do not pull them; you stir until they release and then winnow them out.
    • Winnowing means tossing them so the light wings blow away. A tray and a breeze, or a basket, does it.
    • The salt and onion go into a clean pan, not the one you roasted in. Wing fragments would otherwise end up in the finished dish.
    • The boiled method salts the water before the pan goes on the heat, which is the reverse of most Ugandan practice and is how it is printed.
    • The pounded version is the interesting one — roasted, sun-dried, pounded to flour and then cooked as a luwombo. That is the most popular treatment among the Baganda and it turns an insect into a sauce base.
    • Five named types, all worth recognising: ntunda are small and black with delicate wings from knee-height anthills in the evening; embobya have a stronger flavour and come out after rain stops; ennaka are very small and black, mostly in Bulemeezi County; nsejjere are biggest and fattest, reddish brown and black on top, and considered the most delicious; mpawu are second biggest, build large sharp-pointed anthills, and have their main season in April.
    • This is the one recipe in its source with no numeric quantities at all — only timings. Nothing here is estimated to cover that.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Trying to remove wings by hand before roasting.
    • Not winnowing, so wings end up in the dish.
    • Using the roasting pan for the second stage.
    • Over-roasting past five minutes before the wash.
    • Skipping the sun-drying in the boiled and pounded versions.

    Health Benefits

    • Very high in protein and in fat, which is why they are prized.
    • A good source of iron, zinc and essential fatty acids.
    • Roasted dry with no added oil.
    • Seasonal and wild-harvested, with no farming input at all.

    Cultural Note

    Termites come out of the ground after rain in swarms, which makes harvesting them a communal and time-limited event rather than a purchase. That the Baganda's favourite treatment is to pound them into flour for luwombo says something about how completely integrated insects are into the region's cooking — not a snack on the side but a sauce base for a ceremonial dish.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Sun-dried, they keep for months. Fresh-roasted, 3 days refrigerated.

    Reheating: A few minutes in a dry pan.

    Serve with: Matooke, Luwombo, Posho
