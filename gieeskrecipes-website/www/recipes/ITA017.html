🥔 Italian Mashed Potatoes

Purè di Patate

Purè di Patate is the Italian answer to mashed potatoes — silky and rich with butter and Parmigiano-Reggiano, worked through a ricer for an exceptionally smooth, lump-free finish.

Where many mashed potato traditions favor a rustic, chunky style, the Italian version leans toward luxuriously smooth — a legacy of French culinary influence in Northern Italy, refined with the addition of nutty Parmigiano-Reggiano.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Potato ricer or food mill, Saucepan for warming milk

  • 1 kg (2.2 lb) starchy potatoes, peeled and cut into even chunks
  • 240 ml (1 cup) warm whole milk
  • 100 g (7 tbsp) butter
  • 50 g (⅔ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1½ tsp fine sea salt, plus more to taste
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a pinch
  • White pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Place the potatoes in a large pot, cover with cold salted water, and bring to a boil. Cook until fork-tender, about 15–20 minutes.
  2. Meanwhile, warm the milk gently in a small saucepan — do not let it boil.
  3. Drain the potatoes well and pass through a ricer or food mill back into the warm pot while still hot.
  4. Over low heat, beat in the butter a little at a time until melted and incorporated.
  5. Gradually stir in the warm milk until the purée reaches a smooth, spoonable consistency.
  6. Fold in the Parmigiano-Reggiano, nutmeg, salt, and white pepper.
  7. Taste and adjust seasoning, then serve immediately while hot and silky.

Chef Tips

  • A ricer or food mill is essential for the characteristic silky-smooth Italian texture — a hand masher leaves the puree noticeably coarser.
  • Keep the milk warm before adding — cold milk shocks the potato starch and can make the purée gluey.
  • Add the butter before the milk for the richest, most stable emulsion.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a food processor, which overworks the starch and turns the potatoes gummy and paste-like.
  • Adding cold milk or butter, which can cause the texture to seize.
  • Under-salting the cooking water, resulting in bland potatoes no amount of seasoning afterward can fully fix.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of potassium and vitamin C from the potatoes.
  • Calcium-rich from the milk and cheese.

Cultural Note

Purè di Patate is a beloved comfort side across Northern Italy, especially popular alongside slow-braised dishes like osso buco and brasato during the colder months.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently over low heat with a splash of warm milk, stirring until smooth again.

Serve with: Braised meats, Osso Buco, Roasted sausages, Meatballs in sauce
