🍃 Afang Soup

Afang — okazi, ukazi

Afang leaves soaked in hot water two minutes then blended, with four bunches of waterleaf wilted first — and no water added to the soup at all, the meat broth is the liquid.

Afang is the other great Efik soup and its two greens behave very differently from edikang ikong's. Okazi, the afang leaf, is tough and fibrous: it gets soaked in hot water for two minutes and then blended to whatever consistency you like. Waterleaf goes in first and wilts in two or three minutes, releasing the moisture the soup needs, which is why no water is added at all — the meat broth is the entire liquid. The blended afang then takes five minutes or until tender enough. Note that the two greens are measured in different units, so the ratio cannot be worked out.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Bowl for soaking, Colander

  • 1 kg assorted meats
  • 4 bunches waterleaf
  • 3 cups okazi or afang leaves
  • 2 cooking spoons palm oil
  • 1-2 pieces smoked fish
  • 3 pieces stockfish
  • 2 cups periwinkles
  • 1-2 cooking spoons ground scotch bonnet
  • 1 cooking spoon crayfish powder
  • 2 bouillon cubes
  • 1 crayfish seasoning cube
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Boil the assorted meats and stockfish until very soft, keeping the broth. Add no water beyond what the meats need.
  2. Soak the afang leaves in hot water 2 minutes, then blend to your preferred consistency.
  3. Add the smoked fish and periwinkles to the seasoned broth and cook 3 to 5 minutes.
  4. Add the sliced waterleaf and cook 2 to 3 minutes, until wilted.
  5. Add the palm oil and cook 3 minutes.
  6. Add the blended afang and cook 5 minutes, or until tender enough.
  7. Add the crayfish powder and the ground scotch bonnet. Taste and adjust the salt.
  8. Take it off the heat and let it stand 1 to 2 minutes on the residual heat.

Chef Tips

  • Two minutes in hot water and no more for the afang. It is a soak to soften, not a cook — longer and the leaf loses its character.
  • Blend to your preferred consistency. Some cooks like it almost smooth, others leave it coarse, and both are legitimate.
  • Waterleaf before afang. The waterleaf supplies the moisture that lets the soup take no added water at all.
  • No water in the soup. The meat broth is the liquid, which is why the meats are boiled with only what they need.
  • Three minutes on the palm oil before the afang goes in. It needs to be incorporated first.
  • Okazi, ukazi and afang are the same leaf — Gnetum africanum. It is a different plant from utazi and from uziza despite the similar names.
  • The ratio of the two greens cannot be worked out from this recipe, because waterleaf is given in bunches and okazi in cups. Those units are incommensurable and no source resolves them.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling the afang leaves rather than soaking them two minutes.
  • Adding water to the soup.
  • Putting the afang in before the waterleaf.
  • Adding the palm oil last.
  • Treating okazi and utazi as the same leaf.

Health Benefits

  • Afang leaf is high in protein for a vegetable, and rich in iron.
  • Periwinkle is very lean and high in iron and magnesium.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.
  • Waterleaf contributes vitamin C and minerals.

Cultural Note

Afang and edikang ikong are the two soups Calabar is judged by, and they are built on opposite leaf logic: edikang ikong pairs a tender leaf with a watery one, afang pairs a tough fibrous one with the same watery one. In both cases the waterleaf is doing the same job, which is why it turns up in nearly every Efik soup and almost never on its own.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat, briefly.

Serve with: Iyan, Eba or garri, Akpu or fufu
