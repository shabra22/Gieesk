🍛 Nkatenkwan with Okra and Eggplant

Hkatenkwan — groundnut stew

The version with vegetables in it — two cups of okra and a whole eggplant added five minutes after the main pot, cooked until chicken and vegetables are tender.

Most documented groundnut soups are meat and peanut alone. This one puts the vegetables in the pot: two cups of chopped okra and a medium eggplant go in five minutes after the main pot is assembled, and everything cooks until both the chicken and the vegetables are tender. Two thirds of a cup of peanut butter to one chicken is the leanest peanut ratio of any documented version, which makes this the thinnest and most broth-like of the family — closer to light soup with groundnut in it than to the thick diaspora versions.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Knife, Wooden spoon

  • 1 chicken, cut up
  • 1 inch piece ginger
  • 1/2 onion
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tbsp peanut oil
  • 1 cup chopped onion
  • 1 cup chopped tomatoes
  • 2/3 cup peanut butter
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 2 hot chiles, or 1 tsp cayenne
  • 1 medium eggplant
  • 2 cups okra

Method

  1. Assemble the chicken, ginger, half onion, tomato paste, peanut oil, chopped onion, chopped tomatoes, peanut butter, salt and chiles in the pot.
  2. Cook 5 minutes.
  3. Add the eggplant, cut into chunks, and the chopped okra.
  4. Cook until the chicken and vegetables are tender.
  5. Serve with fufu or rice.

Chef Tips

  • Two thirds of a cup of peanut butter to a whole chicken is the leanest documented ratio. Expect a broth rather than a thick soup — that is this version, not a mistake.
  • Vegetables in after five minutes. They are part of the soup here rather than a side, which no other documented groundnut soup does.
  • Okra will thicken the soup slightly as it cooks and releases its mucilage. Two cups is a lot, so the body comes partly from the okra rather than the peanut.
  • Until chicken and vegetables are tender is the only cue. Unusually, this recipe does NOT mention the oil separating, which every other documented version treats as essential.
  • Eggplant in chunks, not slices. It will collapse if cut too small.
  • Two hot chiles or a teaspoon of cayenne — the source offers either, and they are not equivalent in flavour.
  • If you want the standard thick nkatenkwan, this is not the recipe. Start at Groundnut Soup with Chicken.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expecting a thick soup from this peanut ratio.
  • Cutting the eggplant too small.
  • Adding the vegetables at the start.
  • Cooking it so long the okra disintegrates.
  • Assuming the oil will separate as in the other versions — this recipe does not call for it.

Health Benefits

  • Okra supplies soluble fibre, folate and vitamin K.
  • Eggplant adds fibre and anthocyanins in the skin.
  • The leanest peanut ratio of the documented versions, so lower in fat.
  • High in protein from the whole chicken.

Cultural Note

Putting okra and garden egg into groundnut soup is closer to how it is actually eaten in many Ghanaian households than the meat-and-peanut versions that get published — the vegetables stretch the pot and the okra does some of the thickening work the peanut would otherwise have to do alone.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated. The okra softens further.

Reheating: Gentle heat. Do not boil hard or the okra breaks down.

Serve with: Fufu, Omo Tuo, Rice
