Olive all'Ascolana

Ascoli Piceno's celebrated stuffed and fried olives, large green olives pitted whole and wrapped around a rich meat filling before being breaded and fried until golden.

The painstaking technique of pitting each olive in one continuous spiral, keeping it intact enough to wrap around a spoonful of filling, has been passed down through generations in Ascoli Piceno, where these olives remain a point of fierce local pride.

Ingredients

Equipment: Olive pitter or small paring knife, Shallow dishes for breading, Deep pot for frying

  • 40 large green olives (Ascolana Tenera if available), pitted whole
  • 150 g (5.3 oz) ground pork
  • 100 g (3.5 oz) ground beef
  • 50 g (1.8 oz) mortadella, finely chopped
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 60 g (¾ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1 egg, plus 2 eggs for breading
  • Pinch of nutmeg
  • 150 g (1⅛ cups) all-purpose flour
  • 150 g (1½ cups) breadcrumbs
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Pit the olives carefully, working in a continuous spiral cut so each olive stays intact and can be opened flat, then wrapped closed again around the filling.
  2. Warm a little oil and sauté the onion until softened, then add the ground pork and beef, browning well.
  3. Let the meat cool slightly, then finely chop or process it with the mortadella until well combined.
  4. Mix in the Parmigiano, 1 egg, and nutmeg, seasoning well.
  5. Wrap each pitted olive around a small amount of the meat filling, pressing gently to seal it closed.
  6. Set up a breading station with flour, beaten eggs, and breadcrumbs.
  7. Roll each stuffed olive in flour, then egg, then breadcrumbs, coating thoroughly.
  8. Heat vegetable oil to 180°C (350°F) and fry the olives in batches until deeply golden, about 3–4 minutes.
  9. Drain on paper towels and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Pitting the olives in one continuous spiral takes practice but is what allows them to wrap neatly around the filling without falling apart.
  • Processing the cooked meat filling until fine makes it much easier to pack neatly into each small olive.
  • These are traditionally served as part of a larger fritto misto (mixed fried) platter alongside other fried bites.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the pitting process, which can break the olives and make them impossible to stuff neatly.
  • Leaving the meat filling too coarse, making it difficult to pack tightly into each olive.
  • Frying at too low a temperature, resulting in greasy rather than crisp olives.

Cultural Note

Olive all'Ascolana is a protected specialty of Ascoli Piceno in the Marche, with a designated PGI status protecting the tradition, and remains one of the region's proudest culinary exports.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate uncooked, breaded olives for up to 1 day, or freeze for up to 1 month.

Reheating: Reheat cooked olives in a hot oven to re-crisp; avoid the microwave.

Serve with: As part of a fritto misto platter, A chilled white wine from the Marche
