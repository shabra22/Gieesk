🍳 Kenyan Spanish Omelette

Omeleti ya Kiispania

Kenyan Spanish Omelette — a thick, hearty frittata of eggs, potatoes, onions, tomatoes, and capsicum cooked slowly until set through. The Kenyan adaptation adds local spices and coriander to the classic Spanish tortilla concept — more substantial and deeply flavoured.

The Kenyan spin on a Spanish omelette keeps the essence — thick egg and potato cake — but adds the characteristic Kenyan flavour profile: tomatoes, coriander, paprika, and curry powder. The result is something between a frittata and a Kenyan omelette, sliceable like a pie and satisfying enough to be a complete meal. It is ideal for meal prep — it keeps well and tastes even better the next day.

Ingredients

Equipment: Oven-safe frying pan or non-stick pan, Bowl, Spatula

  • 6 large eggs
  • 2 medium potatoes — peeled, thinly sliced and par-cooked
  • 1 large onion — thinly sliced
  • 1 medium tomato — diced
  • ½ red bell pepper — sliced
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp curry powder
  • 1½ tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 2 tbsp milk

Method

  1. Par-cook the potato slices: boil for 5—7 minutes until just tender but still holding shape. Drain and cool.
  2. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a pan over medium heat. Cook onions for 5 minutes until soft.
  3. Add tomatoes and bell pepper. Cook for 3 minutes.
  4. Beat eggs with milk, paprika, curry powder, salt, and pepper. Stir in coriander.
  5. Add par-cooked potato slices to the pan with the onion and tomato mixture.
  6. Pour the beaten egg mixture over everything. Shake the pan gently to distribute evenly.
  7. Cook over low heat for 8—10 minutes until the edges and bottom are set but the top is still slightly liquid.
  8. Finish option 1 (grill): place the pan under a grill for 3—4 minutes until the top is set and golden.
  9. Finish option 2 (flip): place a plate over the pan and flip the omelette onto it, then slide back into the pan to cook the other side for 3—4 minutes.
  10. Cut into wedges and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Par-cooking the potatoes first ensures they cook through in the brief egg-cooking time.
  • Very low heat is essential — this must cook slowly to set through without burning.
  • The omelette is ready on the bottom when a spatula slid underneath meets no resistance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Raw potatoes — they will not cook through in the egg-cooking time.
  • Too high heat — burns the bottom before the top sets.
  • Rushing — this omelette takes 10 minutes minimum on low heat.

The Science

The thick egg and potato cake is set by gentle, prolonged heat — the proteins need time to coagulate throughout the mass without the exterior burning. This is why a Spanish omelette is cooked on very low heat for a longer time than a regular omelette.

Cultural Note

The Kenyan Spanish omelette has become popular in urban kitchens as a meal-prep-friendly, budget-conscious option that is equally good for breakfast, lunch, or a light dinner.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 2 days. Eat cold or reheat briefly.

Reheating: Microwave briefly or warm in a pan over low heat.
