🐟 Mbongo Tchobi (African Spicy Black Stew)

Mbongo tchobi

White fish steaks simmered in a blended tomato, herb and toasted-njansa sauce with mbongo spice mix — the spicy black stew the author says the Bassa of the Littoral claim as theirs.

55 minTotal time
15 minPrep
40 minCook
6Servings
380Calories
MediumDifficulty

Adamu’s version uses a ready-made mbongo spice mix. Tomatoes, onion, green onions, toasted njansa, garlic, basil and parsley are blended and pureed into hot oil with very little water, brought to a boil and simmered about 10 minutes. Mbongo spice, fish steaks, bouillon and salt go in and simmer 10 minutes or more until the fish is cooked through, with water added as necessary to prevent burning. Catfish gets a 1-minute hot-water dip and rinse to remove sliminess. Served warm with boiled plantains. The page explains that the black colour traditionally comes from burning hiomi bark (Afrostyrax lepidophyllus); this recipe relies on the spice mix instead.

Ingredients

Equipment: Skillet, Blender, Pot

  • 3 pounds (1.5 kg) white fish, cut into steaks
  • 10 njansa
  • 1/2 large onion
  • 4 large tomatoes
  • 1/4 cup (15–16 g) parsley
  • 1/2 cup (12–15 g) basil
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 2 green onions
  • 1/4–1/2 cup (60–118 ml) vegetable oil
  • 4–6 tablespoons (48–72 g) mbongo spice (about half a 4-ounce jar of the ready-made mix)
  • 1 tablespoon (10–12 g) bouillon powder or 2 Maggi cubes
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. If using catfish, pour hot water over it for a minute and rinse to remove the sliminess.
  2. Lightly toast the njansa in a hot skillet for about 5 minutes.
  3. Blend the tomatoes, onion, green onions, njansa, garlic, basil and parsley.
  4. Puree the blend into hot oil with very little water, bring to a boil and simmer about 10 minutes.
  5. Add the mbongo spice, fish, bouillon and salt and simmer another 10 minutes or more, until the fish is cooked through. Add water as necessary to prevent burning.
  6. Serve warm with boiled plantains.

Chef Tips

  • The printed doneness cue is ‘until the fish is cooked through’.
  • Add water as needed so the sauce does not burn.
  • The page calls the spice mix a convenience; the traditional black colour comes from burnt hiomi bark.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the toasting of the njansa.
  • Skipping the hot-water rinse for catfish.
  • Letting the sauce dry out instead of adding water.

Cultural Note

Adamu writes that the Bassa tribe, specifically in the Littoral Region, calls the dish theirs. The Afrik printing makes the same attribution but with a different formula.
