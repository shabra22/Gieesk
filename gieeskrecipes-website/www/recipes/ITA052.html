Agnolotti del Plin

Agnolotti del Plin are tiny, hand-pinched pasta parcels from Piedmont, filled with leftover roasted meats and traditionally sealed with a distinctive pinch (plin) between finger and thumb.

Born from the Piedmontese tradition of using leftovers from a Sunday roast, these small pasta pillows are famous for their precise, tight pinch-sealed shape, which distinguishes them from larger ravioli and requires real practice to master.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor, Pasta machine, Piping bag, Bench scraper

  • 1 batch Fresh Egg Pasta Dough

Filling

  • 300 g (10 oz) mixed roasted or braised beef and pork (leftover roast works beautifully)
  • 100 g (3.5 oz) cooked spinach or Swiss chard, squeezed dry
  • 1 egg
  • 60 g (¾ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a pinch
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

To serve

  • 80 g (⅓ cup) butter
  • A few fresh sage leaves

Method

  1. Finely chop or pulse the roasted meats and spinach in a food processor until well combined but not pureed to a paste.
  2. Mix in the egg, Parmigiano, nutmeg, salt, and pepper until smooth. Chill for at least 30 minutes.
  3. Roll the pasta dough into long, thin strips using a pasta machine.
  4. Pipe a thin, continuous line of filling along one half of each strip, close to the edge.
  5. Fold the dough over the filling lengthwise, pressing gently along the length to seal, avoiding air pockets.
  6. Using your thumb and forefinger, pinch the dough at small, even intervals along the sealed edge, then cut between each pinch with a knife or pasta wheel to separate the individual agnolotti.
  7. Bring a large pot of salted water to a gentle boil and cook the agnolotti for 2–3 minutes until they float.
  8. Meanwhile, melt the butter with sage until golden and fragrant.
  9. Lift the agnolotti directly into the sage butter, tossing gently, and serve immediately with extra Parmigiano.

Chef Tips

  • The pinch technique takes practice — work slowly at first to get a feel for the even spacing and tight seal.
  • Using leftover roasted meat gives the most authentic, deep flavor — this dish was born from resourcefulness.
  • Keep the filling on the drier side; too much moisture makes sealing difficult and can cause the pasta to tear.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using too much filling per agnolotto, making them difficult to seal and prone to bursting.
  • Not pinching tightly enough, causing the filling to leak during cooking.
  • Overworking the meat filling into a smooth paste, losing the traditional slightly textured bite.

Cultural Note

Agnolotti del Plin holds Protected Geographical Indication status, and the specific pinching technique is considered a defining mark of skilled Piedmontese home cooks and pasta makers.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Uncooked agnolotti freeze well in a single layer for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Cook frozen agnolotti directly from frozen in boiling water, adding 1 extra minute.

Serve with: Sage brown butter, A light meat jus as an alternative, A Barbera or Dolcetto wine
