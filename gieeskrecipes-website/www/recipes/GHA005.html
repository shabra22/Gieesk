🥟 Omo Tuo, Absorption Method

Omo tuo — rice balls

Lid on and left alone until every drop is absorbed, then mashed against the side of the pot and rolled by shaking the bowl in a circle.

The third documented omo tuo sits between the other two on water — two cups of jasmine rice to three and a half of water — and it is the only one that treats the cooking as a strict absorption method. Do not remove the lid until the water has been absorbed, which takes about fifteen to twenty minutes. The rice is then mashed against the side of the pot with a wooden spoon rather than in a separate bowl, and the balls are formed by putting a portion in a slightly wet bowl and shaking it in a circular motion — a shaping technique no other source describes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot with a tight lid, Colander, Wooden spoon, Small bowl, for shaping

  • 2 cups long grain jasmine rice
  • 3 1/2 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Rinse the rice in a colander.
  2. Add the rice, water and salt to the pot, cover, and cook on low.
  3. Do not remove the lid until the water has been absorbed — about 15 to 20 minutes.
  4. Mash the rice against the side of the pot with a wooden spoon.
  5. Put a portion into a slightly wet bowl.
  6. Shake the bowl in a circular motion until the rice forms a ball.
  7. Turn out and repeat.

Chef Tips

  • The shaking technique is the thing to take from this recipe. A portion in a slightly damp bowl, shaken in a circle, rounds itself without being handled — far neater than moulding by hand and it does not compress the rice.
  • Lid stays on. The source is explicit: do not remove it until the water has been absorbed. Lifting it releases the steam that is doing the cooking.
  • Two cups to three and a half is the middle of the three documented ratios. A reasonable place to start if you have never made it.
  • Mash against the side of the pot, not in a bowl. Less transferring, and the residual heat of the pot keeps it workable.
  • Slightly wet, not wet. A soaking bowl makes the surface slimy.
  • Jasmine rice is specified here, as in one of the other two versions. It collapses more readily than a firmer long grain.
  • No sensory doneness cue is given beyond the absorption of the water, so the lid rule is doing double duty as the timer.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Lifting the lid to check.
  • Mashing in a cold bowl.
  • A bowl too wet for shaping.
  • Handling the balls after shaking them, which flattens them.
  • Cooking on high, which absorbs the water before the grain has collapsed.

Cultural Note

Shaking a ball of rice round a damp bowl rather than patting it between the palms is the kind of detail that only gets written down by someone who learned the dish at home. It is also the one omo tuo source in print with both a personal byline and a Ghanaian label, which is why the technique is worth preserving.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated, wrapped.

Reheating: Steam, or drop into hot soup.

Serve with: Groundnut Soup, Palm Nut Soup, Light Soup
