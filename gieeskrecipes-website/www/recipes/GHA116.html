🥜 Nkate Cake

Nkate cake — nkatie cake

Two hundred grams of roasted peanuts into caramelised sugar — stir quickly, because the mixture will begin to stiffen.

Peanut brittle, Ghanaian style, sold in slabs and triangles at every lorry park. Two hundred grams of roasted unsalted peanuts are chopped with a large knife or a food processor. A hundred and fifty grams of granulated sugar melts in a steel pan over low heat, stirring occasionally, which may take a few minutes. Once the sugar has turned golden brown and has no more lumps, the chopped peanuts go in and you stir quickly to coat them, because the mixture will begin to stiffen. It is poured onto greaseproof paper, flattened under a second sheet, and cut while hot — watch your fingers, because it will still be very hot.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium or large steel pan, Large knife or food processor, Baking tray, Greaseproof paper, two sheets, Rolling pin or flat-based pan, to flatten

  • 200 g (1 1/3 cups) roasted unsalted peanuts, without shells
  • 150 g (3/4 cup) granulated sugar

Method

  1. Chop the peanuts, using a large knife or a food processor.
  2. Put the sugar in a medium or large steel pan over low heat.
  3. Allow the sugar to melt, stirring occasionally. This may take a few minutes.
  4. Once the sugar has turned golden brown and has no more lumps, pour the chopped peanuts into the pan.
  5. Stir quickly to coat the peanuts with the caramel, as the mixture will begin to stiffen.
  6. Pour it onto a baking tray lined with greaseproof paper.
  7. Top with a second sheet and flatten. Watch your fingers, as the mixture will still be VERY hot.
  8. Cut it while hot.
  9. Allow to cool completely.

Chef Tips

  • Golden brown AND no more lumps. Both conditions, and the lumps are the one people miss — undissolved sugar gives a grainy brittle.
  • Low heat, not medium. Sugar alone with no water scorches fast and there is no recovering it.
  • Stir quickly once the nuts go in, because it begins to stiffen immediately. Have the tray and paper ready before you start.
  • Cut it while hot. Cold brittle shatters where it wants to, not where you score it.
  • The second sheet of greaseproof is what lets you press it flat without burning yourself. Use a rolling pin on top of it.
  • Double the quantities for a bigger batch, by the author's own note. The ratio holds.
  • Best fresh, though it keeps a week airtight. Humidity is what makes brittle go sticky.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Medium or high heat, which burns the sugar.
  • Adding the nuts before the lumps have gone.
  • Stirring slowly once the nuts are in.
  • Cutting it cold.
  • Pressing it flat with a bare hand.

Health Benefits

  • Peanuts supply protein, niacin, folate, magnesium and vitamin E.
  • Just two ingredients, with no additives or preservatives.
  • Vegan, dairy-free and gluten-free.
  • The nuts outweigh the sugar, which is unusual for a brittle.

Cultural Note

Nkate cake is sold in plastic-wrapped slabs through car windows at every Ghanaian traffic junction, and it is the one Ghanaian sweet most visitors come home talking about. That the only retrievable recipe with real quantities is by a British home baker rather than a Ghanaian cook says something about whose food gets written down.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Up to a week in an airtight container, per the author. Best fresh.

Reheating: Not applicable. Do not reheat.

Serve with: Sobolo, Bissap, Kofi Brokeman
