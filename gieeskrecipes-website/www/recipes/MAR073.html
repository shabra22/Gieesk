🍪 Ghriba Cookies (Sesame)

Ghriba / ghoriba — tea cookies (غريبة)

Crumbly flour-and-oil cookies with toasted sesame and orange blossom water, baked until just a very light brown and cracked on top.

1hTotal time
25mPrep
20mCook
25Servings
170Calories
EasyDifficulty

A kneaded dough of flour, powdered sugar, toasted sesame, salt, baking soda, orange blossom water and oil is rested 10 to 15 minutes, shaped into ping-pong-ball-size pieces and baked at 325°F (160°C) for about 20 minutes. They should be cooked through but only a very light brown, and the tops crack.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Baking sheets, Cooling rack

  • 8 cups all purpose flour (1 kg)
  • 2 cups powdered sugar (250g)
  • 1 cup toasted sesame seeds (150 g)
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 Tbsp orange blossom water
  • 1 Tbsp baking soda
  • 1 cup unflavored oil (like vegetable oil) - you may need more.

Method

  1. Mix the flour, powdered sugar, sesame, salt and baking soda in a large bowl.
  2. Pour in the orange blossom water and 1/2 cup of the oil and begin mixing, adding more oil as needed. Knead by hand about 10 minutes.
  3. The dough should feel slightly wet and oily to the touch but still soft. Rest it 10 to 15 minutes.
  4. Preheat the oven to 325°F (160°C). Shape the dough into pieces about the size of a ping pong ball (about 2 inches or 5 cm across), keeping the edges from cracking.
  5. Bake about 20 minutes, watching closely: they should be cooked through but only a very light brown. The tops will crack.
  6. Cool 5 minutes on the tray, then move to a rack.

Chef Tips

  • Cue: cooked through, only a very light brown, with cracked tops.
  • The page notes they should be watched, since they colour quickly.
  • The dough rest is NO CUE, TIME ONLY.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-baking until brown.
  • Making the dough dry rather than slightly oily.

Health Benefits

  • Sesame adds some minerals; the cookies are a sugar-and-oil treat.

Cultural Note

The author says ghriba are a simple cookie often served with tea in Morocco, traditionally made on an inverted half-circle pan, and that people either like them or do not.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The page says they last several weeks.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Mint tea
