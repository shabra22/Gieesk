🫘 Traditional Kenyan Githeri

Githeri

Githeri is one of Kenya's most iconic traditional dishes — maize and beans slow-cooked until tender, then sautéed with onions, tomatoes, and simple seasonings into a deeply nourishing, affordable, and satisfying meal enjoyed across the entire country.

Githeri has deep roots in central Kenya, where maize and beans have long been staple crops. Traditionally cooked in large batches over wood fires for farming communities and family gatherings, it remains today a symbol of nourishment, simplicity, and resilience. From school canteens to home kitchens to roadside stalls, githeri feeds Kenya. Add a ripe avocado on the side and it becomes something quietly extraordinary.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large cooking pot or pressure cooker, Frying pan, Wooden spoon, Knife, Chopping board, Measuring cups

  • 2 cups (350g) dried white maize
  • 2 cups (350g) dried red beans — rosecoco or kidney beans
  • 2 medium onions — finely chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable or sunflower oil
  • 2 tsp salt — adjust to taste
  • ½ tsp black pepper — optional
  • 1 tsp curry powder — optional
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Wash the maize and beans thoroughly under cold running water.
  2. Soak them separately overnight (8–12 hours) to reduce cooking time and improve digestibility. Drain and rinse well.
  3. Combine the soaked maize and beans in a large pot and cover generously with fresh water.
  4. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer until both are completely tender — about 90 minutes. Add water as needed to keep them submerged. (Pressure cooker: 35 minutes.)
  5. Drain any excess water and set aside.
  6. Heat the oil in a large frying pan over medium heat.
  7. Add the onions and cook, stirring, until soft and lightly golden — about 6–8 minutes.
  8. Add the garlic and cook for 30 seconds until fragrant.
  9. Stir in the tomatoes and cook until they break down into a thick sauce — about 6 minutes.
  10. Add the cooked maize and beans to the pan. Season with salt, black pepper, and curry powder if using.
  11. Mix thoroughly and cook for 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, allowing all the flavours to come together beautifully.
  12. Taste and adjust seasoning. Scatter with chopped fresh coriander and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Soaking overnight is the single most important step — it dramatically cuts cooking time and makes the beans easier to digest.
  • Cook until the beans are just tender with a slight bite — not mushy. Texture is everything in githeri.
  • Add diced carrots or bell peppers when sautéing for extra colour, nutrition, and sweetness.
  • A ripe avocado served alongside is one of the great simple pleasures in Kenyan food.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the overnight soak — dramatically increases cooking time and affects texture.
  • Overcooking the maize and beans until they become mushy.
  • Under-seasoning — githeri needs generous salt to bring out its earthy depth.
  • Adding tomatoes before the onions have softened — results in a raw, harsh flavour.

Cultural Note

Githeri has deep roots in central Kenya, where maize and beans have been staple crops for generations. Traditionally cooked in large batches over wood fires for farming communities, it is a symbol of nourishment, simplicity, and Kenyan resilience. Today it is enjoyed by people from every region and community across the country.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 4 days. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop with a splash of water, or microwave until piping hot, stirring halfway through.

Serve with: Fresh avocado, Sukuma Wiki, Kachumbari, Beef Stew, Grilled sausages, Fried eggs, Chai ya Maziwa
