🍲 Morseddu Calabrese

Morseddu

A rustic Calabrian stew of mixed offal simmered slowly in a spicy tomato sauce with chili and herbs, traditionally scooped up with pitta, the region's own flatbread.

Morseddu reflects Calabria's nose-to-tail cooking tradition, historically built from whatever cuts a family could afford, slow-simmered with the region's characteristic heat until deeply savory and thick enough to be eaten by the spoonful with bread.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed pot with lid

  • 800 g (1.75 lb) mixed offal (such as liver, heart, and lung), cleaned and cubed
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 dried chilies, crumbled
  • 500 g (1 lb) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry red wine
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Pitta calabrese or crusty bread, for serving

Method

  1. Warm the olive oil in a heavy pot and sauté the onion until softened.
  2. Add the garlic and chilies and cook briefly.
  3. Add the offal and brown well on all sides.
  4. Pour in the wine and let it reduce by half.
  5. Add the crushed tomatoes, bay leaf, and rosemary, and season.
  6. Cover and simmer gently for 1.5 hours, stirring occasionally, until the meat is very tender and the sauce has thickened.
  7. Uncover for the final 10–15 minutes if the sauce needs to reduce further.
  8. Serve hot with pitta or crusty bread for scooping.

Chef Tips

  • Browning the offal thoroughly before braising builds essential flavor for the finished stew.
  • Low, slow cooking is essential for tenderizing the offal properly.
  • Adjust the chili to taste — Calabrian cooking tends toward real heat, but the level is a matter of personal preference.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the braise, which leaves the offal tough rather than properly tender.
  • Skipping the browning step, missing an important layer of flavor.
  • Being too shy with the chili, which is a defining character of authentic Calabrian cooking.

Cultural Note

Morseddu is closely associated with the city of Catanzaro in Calabria, a hearty, resourceful dish reflecting the region's long tradition of nose-to-tail cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; flavor deepens the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop.

Serve with: Pitta calabrese, A robust Calabrian red wine
