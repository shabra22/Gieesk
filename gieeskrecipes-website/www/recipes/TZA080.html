🍜 Tanzanian Fish Soup

Supu ya Samaki

Tanzanian Supu ya Samaki — fragrant fish soup simmered with vegetables, ginger, lemon grass, and coriander in a light tomato broth. The coastal Tanzanian healing soup — light, restorative, and deeply nourishing.

Fish soup is the coastal Tanzanian healing food — lighter than meat soup, quicker to make, and made with the freshest fish from that morning's catch. Lemon grass and ginger make it particularly restorative.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 800g fish — bone-in pieces and fish heads
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 cup tomatoes — diced
  • 1 onion — roughly chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger — sliced
  • 1 stalk lemon grass — bruised
  • 1 carrot — sliced
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander
  • Lime wedges

Method

  1. Combine fish, water, onion, garlic, ginger, lemon grass, and turmeric.
  2. Bring to a boil. Skim foam.
  3. Add tomatoes and carrot. Simmer gently 25—30 minutes.
  4. Remove fish. Flake meat from bones. Discard bones and lemon grass.
  5. Return flaked fish. Season. Garnish with coriander. Serve with lime.

Chef Tips

  • Fish heads and frames make the richest broth.
  • Lowest simmer — hard boiling clouds broth.
  • Lime at the table brightens every bowl.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Hard boiling.
  • Discarding fish frames.

The Science

Fish bones contain collagen and omega-3 fatty acids that leach into the broth during simmering, contributing to nutritional value.

Cultural Note

Fish soup is what fishermen eat for breakfast before heading out to sea — nourishing, warming, and made from fish that did not sell. Nothing is wasted.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Chapati (TZA004), With Rice, As a standalone
