🍖 Ethiopian Lamb Shank Stew

የበግ ቁስ ወጥ — Ye-Beg Qus Wat

Ethiopian Lamb Shank Wat — whole lamb shanks braised in a deeply flavoured berbere-kibbeh sauce for 2 hours until the meat falls off the bone. The restaurant showpiece — a whole lamb shank half-submerged in deep red berbere sauce, served on injera.

Lamb shank wat is the restaurant presentation showpiece — a whole lamb shank, its bone standing proud above the sauce, served on a large injera. The shank is braised in the Key Wat sauce until completely tender, absorbing the berbere deeply throughout.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot or Dutch oven

  • 4 lamb shanks
  • 3 onions — finely diced
  • 4 tbsp kibbeh
  • 5 tbsp berbere
  • 6 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1.5 cups water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Brown shanks in kibbeh — 4 minutes per side. Remove.
  2. Dry caramelise onions — 15 minutes.
  3. Add kibbeh and berbere. Toast 3 minutes.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, tomato paste.
  5. Return shanks. Add water.
  6. Simmer covered 100—120 minutes until falling tender.
  7. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Brown the shanks first — adds depth.
  • The shank bone is the presentation element — do not remove it.
  • Sauce should be thick enough to coat the shank.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Short cooking — shank must fall off the bone.

Cultural Note

Lamb shank wat is the dish that Ethiopian restaurant chefs use to demonstrate their mastery — a single shank on a large injera, surrounded by berbere sauce, is one of the most visually impressive dishes in Ethiopian cuisine.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days.

Reheating: Gently — keep shanks submerged in sauce.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Served tableside on a large injera for maximum presentation
