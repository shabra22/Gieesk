🍃 Kwacoco Bible (Cocoyam Parcels with Fish, Turkey and Spinach)

Kwacoco bible

Grated cocoyam mixed with spinach, red oil, crayfish, smoked fish and smoked turkey, wrapped in banana leaves and steamed about an hour.

100 minTotal time
40 minPrep
60 minCook
5Servings
460Calories
MediumDifficulty

Adamu says kwacoco bible is a major staple of the Bakweri tribe in the South West province and is known by that name across Cameroon. The smoked turkey, fish, crayfish, pepper and seasoning cube are boiled lightly 3–5 minutes and the stock reserved. The cocoyam is peeled, bruised bits cut out, cut into chunks, washed at once and grated by hand or pulsed with water, ‘pureed, not too fine’. It is combined with the fish, spinach, red oil, crayfish, turkey, stock, salt and country onion. Banana leaves are cut, washed, patted dry and wilted over an open flame; about 1/2–1 cup of mixture goes on each leaf, folded and sealed. The parcels are steamed on a rack in a pot lined with banana leaves about an hour, adding water as needed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot with rack, Food processor or grater

  • 3 pounds cocoyam, peeled and cleaned
  • 4–5 cups fresh spinach, chopped
  • 1 cup red oil, or more to preference
  • 1/2 cup crayfish
  • 1–1 1/2 cups smoked fish
  • 1–1 1/2 cups smoked turkey, cut in bite-size pieces
  • 2 teaspoons salt, adjust to taste
  • 1 Scotch bonnet, pierced
  • 1 teaspoon bouillon powder or Maggi
  • 1/2 teaspoon country onion spice (optional)
  • Banana leaves (or foil)

Method

  1. Lightly boil the smoked turkey, fish, crayfish, pepper and seasoning cube for 3–5 minutes. Reserve the stock and remove the pepper.
  2. Peel the cocoyam, removing as little flesh as possible. Cut out bruised spots, cut into large chunks and wash right away.
  3. Grate by hand or pulse with water in a food processor until pureed, not too fine.
  4. Combine the fish, spinach, red oil, crayfish, turkey, stock, salt and country onion with the grated cocoyam.
  5. Cut the banana leaves into rectangles, trim, wash and pat dry, and wilt them over an open high flame (or use foil).
  6. Put about 1/2–1 cup of the mixture on each leaf. Fold, press into a rectangle and fold the sides twice to seal.
  7. Steam on a rack in a large pot lined with banana leaves, about an hour, adding water as needed, until cooked through.

Chef Tips

  • The only printed cue is ‘cooked through’ after about an hour of steaming.
  • Peel away from your body and take as little flesh as possible.
  • The author notes cocoyam can leave a slight itch in the throat, so cook it through.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the cut cocoyam unwashed so it discolours.
  • Forgetting to add water to the steaming pot.
  • Skipping the wilting of the banana leaves, which makes them tear.
