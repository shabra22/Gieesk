🥬 Sukuma Peanut Greens

Mboga za Karanga — Wasukuma

Sukuma Peanut Greens — amaranth or sweet potato leaves sautéed with onion and stirred with groundnut paste at the end. The Sukuma people's distinctive green vegetable preparation — the groundnut paste enriches the greens into something more substantial than plain sautéed vegetables.

Among the Sukuma people of Lake Victoria, greens are routinely cooked with groundnut paste — a tradition that dramatically increases both the protein content and the flavour of everyday vegetables. This practice also increases the bioavailability of the greens' fat-soluble vitamins, as the groundnut fat carries them into absorption.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 400g mchicha (amaranth) or sweet potato leaves
  • 2 tbsp groundnut paste
  • 1 onion — diced
  • 1 tomato — diced
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Heat oil. Cook onion until golden.
  2. Add tomato. Cook 3 minutes.
  3. Add greens. Stir until beginning to wilt.
  4. Mix groundnut paste with 2 tbsp warm water. Stir into the greens.
  5. Cook 3—4 more minutes until greens are wilted and sauce coats them.
  6. Season.

Chef Tips

  • The groundnut paste must be mixed with warm water first — prevents lumping.
  • Add at the end — prolonged cooking makes the peanut taste bitter.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding dry peanut paste directly — lumps.
  • Adding too early.

The Science

Adding groundnut paste to greens increases the bioavailability of fat-soluble vitamins (A, K, E) dramatically — these vitamins are absorbed only in the presence of dietary fat. The greens' carotenoids (which produce vitamin A) are converted to active vitamin A 3—5 times more efficiently when eaten with fat.

Health Benefits

  • Greens provide iron, calcium, vitamins A and C.
  • Groundnut paste adds protein and fat for vitamin absorption.
  • Together: significantly more nutritious than either alone.

Cultural Note

Sukuma peanut greens represent a nutritional wisdom that the Sukuma arrived at through observation over generations — that groundnuts make greens both tastier and more nourishing. Modern nutrition science confirms what Sukuma cooks have known for centuries.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Briefly.

Serve with: Ugali wa Mtama (TZA244), Dried fish stew (TZA243)
