Chapman

Equal parts orange and lemon-lime soda over ice in a dimpled beer mug, with a couple of capfuls of blackcurrant cordial and a few drops of Angostura.

Chapman is a Nigerian invention with an unusually well-documented origin: it was crafted in the 1960s by Sam Alamutu, a hotelier and manager at Nigerian Hotels, for his wife, who preferred soft drinks to alcohol — and it was first served publicly at the Ikoyi Hotel and the Ikoyi Club on Lagos Island before spreading across the country. The recipe is simple and, honestly, unquantified: equal amounts of orange and lemon-lime soda over ice, a couple of capfuls of cordial, a few drops of Angostura bitters, and cucumber and citrus to garnish. The dimpled beer mug is not optional.

Ingredients

Equipment: Dimpled beer mug, Long spoon, Knife

  • Ice, to fill a dimpled beer mug
  • Orange soda and lemon-lime soda, in equal amounts
  • A couple of capfuls of cordial or squash — blackcurrant or grenadine
  • A few drops of Angostura bitters

Garnish

  • Cucumber slices
  • Lemon and/or lime slices

Method

  1. Fill a dimpled beer mug with ice.
  2. Pour in equal amounts of orange soda and lemon-lime soda.
  3. Add a couple of capfuls of cordial or squash — blackcurrant or grenadine.
  4. Add a few drops of Angostura bitters.
  5. Garnish with cucumber, and lemon or lime slices.
  6. Stir once and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The dimpled beer mug is the documented serving vessel and it genuinely matters to how a chapman reads — it is a tall drink served in heavy glass.
  • Equal parts orange and lemon-lime is the only stated ratio. Everything else is capfuls and drops.
  • Cucumber is not a garnish you can skip. It is what makes a chapman taste of itself rather than of mixed fizzy drinks.
  • A few drops of Angostura, and no more. It is there for the bitter edge and it dominates quickly.
  • The drink was made for a non-drinker from the start, which is worth knowing for how you frame it — it is not a mocktail version of something alcoholic.
  • Build it in the glass over ice rather than mixing in a jug. The layers sort themselves out as you pour.
  • Blackcurrant cordial is the usual choice in Nigeria; grenadine gives a redder, sweeter drink.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving out the cucumber.
  • Too much bitters.
  • Using only one soda rather than both.
  • Mixing it in a jug and losing the fizz.
  • Serving it without enough ice, so it goes flat and warm.

Health Benefits

  • Alcohol-free by design.
  • Cucumber and citrus add some vitamin C.
  • Angostura bitters contain gentian, traditionally used as a digestive.
  • Lower in alcohol-related harm than any cocktail, which was the point of its invention.

Cultural Note

Chapman is one of very few Nigerian dishes or drinks with a named inventor, a date and a venue — Sam Alamutu, the 1960s, the Ikoyi Hotel — and a daughter on the record confirming it. It is the default party drink for anyone not drinking alcohol, served tall over ice with cucumber floating in it, and it is Lagos in a glass.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Make to order. It does not keep.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Suya, Small chops and puff puff, Asun
