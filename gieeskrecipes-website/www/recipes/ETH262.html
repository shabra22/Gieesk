💒 Ethiopian Wedding Feast

ሠርግ ምግብ — Serg Mgib

Ethiopian Wedding Feast — the complete traditional Ethiopian wedding meal for 50 guests: Doro Wat (ETH061), Key Wat (ETH021), Tibs (ETH022), Kitfo (ETH029), Ayib (ETH096), Gomen (ETH106), Beyaynetu (ETH121), Tej (ETH215), and Difo Dabo (ETH004). The grandest meal in Ethiopian food culture.

The Ethiopian wedding feast is the maximum expression of Ethiopian hospitality — a multi-day celebration of cooking that feeds an entire community. The scale requires community effort: neighbours cook together for days.

Ingredients

Equipment: Commercial-scale pots, Large mitads, Multiple clay jebenas

    All at wedding scale (50+ portions):

    • Doro Wat — 10kg chicken
    • Key Wat — 5kg beef
    • Tibs — 4kg beef
    • Kitfo — 2kg tenderloin
    • Gomen
    • Ayib
    • Beyaynetu
    • Tej — multiple fermentation vessels
    • Difo Dabo — 10+ loaves

    Method

    1. 3 days before: begin Tej fermentation.
    2. 2 days before: cook Key Wat (huge pot). Prepare Doro Wat stock.
    3. 1 day before: complete Doro Wat. Prepare all fasting dishes.
    4. Wedding day morning: fresh Tibs, Kitfo, fresh Ayib.
    5. Wedding day: assemble the grand mesob. Coffee ceremony throughout.

    Chef Tips

    • Ethiopian wedding cooking is community cooking — it cannot be done alone.
    • Scale every recipe: wedding quantities require large clay or metal pots.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Starting too late.
    • Trying to cook alone.

    Cultural Note

    The Ethiopian wedding feast is the highest expression of hospitality — 'ye-serg mgib' (wedding food) is the standard by which all other Ethiopian food is measured. When an Ethiopian says something is 'as good as wedding food', they mean it cannot be better.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Consume over wedding days.

    Reheating: In large pots.

    Serve with: At the Ethiopian wedding table — guests eat communally in groups
