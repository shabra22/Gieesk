🥘 Egusi Soup, Yoruba Lumpy Style

Egusi elewe — lumpy egusi

The egusi mixed to a stiff paste with a fork, scooped into small balls and cooked fifteen to twenty minutes on low without scattering them — stirred only gently, if at all.

This is the third egusi technique: the balls. Ground seed is worked with blended onion and pepper using a fork until it holds as a stiff paste, then spooned into simmering stock in small mounds and left to set. Fifteen to twenty minutes on low heat, with gentle stirring only once they can take it. The oddity in this recipe — and it is worth knowing it is an oddity — is two tablespoons of ground cashew nut added at the end as a further thickener. No other egusi recipe consulted uses it and the author does not flag it as her own.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Fork, Tablespoon, for scooping the balls

  • 8 pieces beef
  • 8-10 pieces pomo (cow skin)
  • Handful of stockfish pieces
  • 2 medium smoked fish
  • 2 cups egusi, ground
  • 2 tbsp cashew nut, ground
  • 1 cup spinach, blanched and squeezed
  • 1 big onion
  • 2 cooking spoons blended tomato, pepper and onion, previously boiled
  • 2 tbsp washed bitter leaf
  • 2 tbsp ground crayfish
  • Scotch bonnet pepper, to taste
  • 3 cooking spoons palm oil
  • 1 cup meat stock
  • 2 cups water
  • Seasoning cube, to taste
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Season the beef and pomo with a seasoning cube, pepper, a little onion and salt, and marinate 30 minutes.
  2. Boil until cooked, adding the stockfish when they are nearly done, then the smoked fish for 5 to 10 minutes. Set aside.
  3. Blend the remaining onion with pepper using very little water, to a paste.
  4. Mix the blended onion with the ground egusi using a fork, to form a stiff paste.
  5. Heat the oil and fry the tomato, onion and pepper mixture 10 minutes.
  6. Add the seasoning cube, crayfish, 1 cup of meat stock and 2 cups of water. Boil 10 minutes.
  7. Lower the heat and correct the seasoning.
  8. Scoop the egusi paste into small balls and add them to the stock. Cook on low 15 to 20 minutes.
  9. Stir gently to avoid scattering the balls, and add the ground cashew nut to thicken further.
  10. Return all the meats and cook 5 minutes.
  11. Add the bitter leaf and spinach and cook 3 to 5 minutes.
  12. Serve with eba or a swallow of choice.

Chef Tips

  • A fork, not a spoon, and not a blender. You are compacting the paste so it holds its shape, and a fork gives you the control.
  • Very little water in the onion-and-pepper blend. Any excess and the paste will not stiffen.
  • Leave the balls alone for the first ten minutes. They need to set on the outside before anything touches them.
  • Stir gently once, late, and only to move them. Scattered balls make this Efo Elegusi by accident.
  • The ground cashew is anomalous. It appears in no other egusi recipe consulted and is not flagged as the author's invention — treat it as optional and know that it is unusual.
  • Low heat for the balls. A rolling boil breaks them up.
  • Bitter leaf and spinach together at the end — the bitter leaf is already washed, so it needs no more than the spinach does.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Blending the egusi paste instead of working it with a fork.
  • Too much water in the pepper blend, so the paste never stiffens.
  • Stirring as soon as the balls go in.
  • Boiling hard instead of simmering low.
  • Treating the cashew nut as standard when it is unique to this recipe.

Health Benefits

  • Melon seed and cashew together give a high protein and mineral load.
  • Bitter leaf is traditionally used for digestion and carries iron.
  • Low in carbohydrate.
  • Collagen from pomo and stockfish.

Cultural Note

The balls are the point. Where the Igbo and Ijebu styles disperse the seed through the liquid, the Yoruba and Edo styles keep it in discrete, set mounds that you break into with the swallow. One named Edo source describes them as knots of cream in the soup, which is as good a description of the effect as anyone has managed.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated. Freeze without the greens if you can.

Reheating: Low heat, no stirring, so the balls survive.

Serve with: Eba or garri, Iyan, Amala
