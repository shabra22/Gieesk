🌿 Ethiopian Mustard Greens

የአበሻ ጎመን — Ye'abesha Gomen

Ethiopian Ye'abesha Gomen — the traditional Ethiopian mustard greens (Ethiopian kale, Brassica carinata), more peppery and bitter than collard greens, cooked with onion, garlic, and green jalapeños. The indigenous Ethiopian green — more intensely flavoured than ordinary collard greens.

Ye'abesha Gomen ('Ethiopian greens') refers specifically to the Ethiopian kale (Brassica carinata) — an indigenous Ethiopian plant with a more complex, slightly peppery flavour than the collard greens used in standard Gomen. When available, it is preferred by Ethiopian cooks for its more authentic and complex flavour.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wide pan

  • 500g Ethiopian kale or mustard greens (or collard greens)
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 green jalapeños — sliced
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Strip stems. Roughly chop greens.
  2. Blanch in boiling water 3 minutes. Drain.
  3. Heat oil. Cook onion until golden.
  4. Add garlic and jalapeño. Cook 2 minutes.
  5. Add blanched greens. Toss.
  6. Cook 10 minutes, stirring, until wilted and fragrant.
  7. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Ethiopian kale (Brassica carinata) is more bitter than collard greens — blanching is more important.
  • The green jalapeño (not berbere) provides the heat for greens — this is correct.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not blanching — the peppery bitterness is too intense raw.

The Science

Ethiopian kale (Brassica carinata) contains higher concentrations of glucosinolates than collard greens — these compounds are both more pungent and more nutritionally valuable. The blanching step converts some of these glucosinolates to isothiocyanates (anti-cancer compounds) via myrosinase enzyme activity.

Health Benefits

  • Highest glucosinolate content of the Ethiopian greens.
  • Very high vitamin K.
  • More nutritionally dense than collard greens.

Cultural Note

Ye'abesha Gomen is the authentic Ethiopian green — the kale that has been grown in Ethiopian highlands for thousands of years, the green that appears in the oldest Ethiopian cooking traditions. Finding it outside Ethiopia requires Ethiopian specialty markets; when found, its superior flavour is immediately evident.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Briefly.

Serve with: With Doro Wat and Kitfo — the most traditional pairing, On the fasting platter
