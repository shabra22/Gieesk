🧅 Chicken Yassa, One-Pot

Yassa poulet

A spicy marinade that pulls double duty as the sauce — cider vinegar and lime, four hours minimum, then everything in one pot.

330 minTotal time
20 minPrep
70 minCook
5Servings
540Calories
MediumDifficulty

The cleanest architecture of the documented yassas: the marinade becomes the sauce, strained so the solids and liquid can be used at different stages. Two large yellow onions thinly sliced, six garlic cloves, a carrot, a third of a cup each of cider vinegar and fresh lime juice, two tablespoons of ginger, a tablespoon of lime zest, a scotch bonnet, two teaspoons of cracked peppercorns, a teaspoon of cayenne and a bay leaf, over two pounds of bone-in chicken thighs and drumsticks for at least four hours. The chicken browns eight minutes a side, whole allspice toasts two minutes, and the sauce reduces twenty minutes until the liquid has almost completely evaporated and the onions are lightly caramelised.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot or Dutch oven, Strainer, Tongs

  • 2 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 6 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 medium carrot, thinly sliced
  • 1/3 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1/3 cup fresh lime juice
  • 2 tbsp peeled and finely chopped fresh ginger
  • 1 tbsp finely grated lime zest
  • 1 scotch bonnet chile, stemmed, seeded and finely chopped
  • 2 tsp coarsely cracked black peppercorns
  • 1 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1 bay leaf
  • Kosher salt
  • 2 lb bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs and drumsticks
  • 1 tsp whole allspice berries
  • 2 tbsp peanut or vegetable oil, plus more for brushing

To serve

  • Cooked white rice

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken with the onions, garlic, carrot, vinegar, lime juice, ginger, lime zest, scotch bonnet, peppercorns, cayenne, bay leaf and salt for at least 4 hours, tossing occasionally.
  2. Strain the marinade, keeping the solids and the liquid separate.
  3. Brown the chicken in batches over medium-high, until golden brown on both sides, about 8 minutes per side.
  4. Toast the allspice berries until fragrant, about 2 minutes.
  5. Add the marinade solids and cook until the onions have softened, about 5 minutes.
  6. Return the chicken, add the reserved liquid, cover, and simmer until the chicken is tender and cooked through, about 30 minutes.
  7. Remove the chicken.
  8. Reduce the sauce uncovered over medium-high until the liquid has almost completely evaporated and the onions are lightly caramelised, about 20 minutes.
  9. Season, pour the sauce over the chicken, and serve warm with rice.

Chef Tips

  • Straining the marinade is the move that makes this work — the solids go in to soften and the liquid goes in to braise, so neither job is compromised.
  • Four hours is a minimum and the recipe's total time is nearly five, so plan the day around it rather than the hour.
  • Eight minutes a side is longer than most browning instructions and it is deliberate: skin-on chicken needs that long to render properly.
  • The allspice goes in dry, straight after the chicken comes out, and toasts for two minutes in the rendered fat. That is a distinctive step no other documented yassa has.
  • Until the liquid has almost completely evaporated — that is the endpoint of the sauce, and twenty minutes uncovered on medium-high is what it takes.
  • Carrot in the marinade is unusual among the yassas and it sweetens the finished sauce. Keep it thinly sliced so it melts.
  • A note on the source's own title: it calls the dish grilled, but the method as printed is pan-browned. Follow the method.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Tipping the whole marinade in at once instead of straining it.
  • Cutting the marination short.
  • Browning for four minutes a side instead of eight.
  • Leaving the chicken in while the sauce reduces, which overcooks it.
  • Stopping the reduction while liquid remains.

Health Benefits

  • Onions, garlic and ginger together supply a broad range of anti-inflammatory compounds.
  • Lime juice and zest are a good source of vitamin C and limonene.
  • Cider vinegar adds acidity without sodium.
  • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

Cultural Note

Published under a Senegalese cook's own name in an American food magazine in 2012, this is one of the earliest well-specified yassas in English, and its double-duty marinade is the neatest statement of what the dish actually is — an acid-and-onion braise where nothing is wasted.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat, covered.

Serve with: White rice, Kaani, Attaya
