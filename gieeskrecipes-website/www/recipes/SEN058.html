🌾 Spring Vegetable Fonio Pilaf

Pilaf de fonio aux légumes

Shallot, garlic and carrot stewed in a half cup of broth, peas and corn folded in, then two cups of cooked fonio and scallions — the pilaf Thiam serves with his lamb shank mafé.

The grain on the side of the plate in Thiam’s book. A shallot cooks about a minute until soft but not brown, garlic and a diced carrot cook three minutes more, a half cup of broth simmers the carrot about ten minutes under a lid, peas and corn go in for a minute, and two cups of already-cooked fonio and thin-sliced scallions are folded through. It is served hot or at room temperature, and it is the accompaniment named in the mafé and yassa entries (Lamb Shank Mafé, Chicken Yassa).

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan with lid

  • 2 tbsp peanut or vegetable oil
  • 1 shallot, thinly sliced
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • 1 large carrot, diced
  • 1/2 cup vegetable or chicken broth
  • 1/4 cup fresh or frozen green peas
  • 1/4 cup fresh or frozen corn kernels
  • 2 cups cooked fonio
  • 2 scallions, thinly sliced
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper — quantities not printed

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a saucepan over medium-high. Cook the shallot about 1 minute, until soft but not brown.
  2. Add the garlic and carrot and cook 3 more minutes, until the garlic is fragrant.
  3. Add the broth, cover and simmer about 10 minutes, until the carrot is tender.
  4. Add the peas and corn and cook about 1 minute.
  5. Fold in the cooked fonio and the scallions. Season to taste. Serve hot or at room temperature.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Soft but not brown’ for the shallot, ‘fragrant’ for the garlic and ‘tender’ for the carrot are the three printed cues.
  • Cook the fonio first and let it cool slightly; it folds in more cleanly and doesn’t clump.
  • It is equally good at room temperature, as the page says.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Browning the shallot.
  • Stirring hard after the fonio goes in and breaking the grains.

Health Benefits

  • Fonio is gluten-free and rich in amino acids methionine and cysteine.
  • Carrots, peas and corn give fibre and carotenoids.

Cultural Note

Fonio is the ancient West African millet that Thiam has spent years bringing to markets; Saveur’s John O’Connor calls it ‘an ancient species of millet that was a staple before the French arrived’, making a comeback in the Casamance.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: A splash of water in a covered pan, or serve cool.

Serve with: Lamb shank mafé, Chicken yassa
