🔥 Beer iyo Kilyo la Solay

Beer iyo Kilyo la Solay

Liver, heart and kidney laid straight onto red-hot charcoal with nothing but coarse salt and black pepper, turned twice and eaten standing up — what a Somali household cooks first for a guest who has just arrived.

When an animal is slaughtered the offal cooks first, and it is offered to the guest of honour while the rest of the meat is still on the fire. There is no marinade and no pan: the pieces go onto the coals themselves. Somalis send greetings from the liver and the heart, and the dish carries some of that weight — it is the fastest, plainest and most generous thing the kitchen can produce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Natural hardwood charcoal, Barbecue or fire pit, Long tongs

  • 1 lamb or goat liver
  • 1 lamb or goat heart
  • 2 lamb or goat kidneys
  • 2 tsp coarse sea salt
  • 2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

Method

  1. Cut the liver into even pieces about 2.5 cm thick, quarter the heart, and halve the kidneys lengthwise.
  2. Wash everything well in cold water and pat it dry. Do not soak it in brine or milk — that takes away the flavour the dish is about.
  3. Season one side with half the salt and pepper. Use no oil at all; nothing is needed when the meat goes onto the coals.
  4. Light good natural hardwood charcoal with no lighter fluid — the fuel is an ingredient here and paraffin tastes of paraffin.
  5. When the coals are red hot, blow the top layer of ash away.
  6. With tongs, lay the liver, heart and kidneys directly on the coals. Cook for 2 minutes, then turn.
  7. Season the second side with the remaining salt and pepper, and turn again after another 2 minutes.
  8. Give it a final minute or two to your liking and serve straight away, off the fire.

Chef Tips

  • Natural lump charcoal, lit without accelerant. Briquettes and lighter fluid both leave a taste, and there is nothing in this recipe to hide behind.
  • Blow the ash off before the meat goes on, or it sticks to the surface.
  • No brine, no milk, no marinade. The whole point is the flavour of very fresh offal and smoke.
  • Kidneys halved lengthwise cook in the same time as 2.5 cm pieces of liver, which is why they are cut that way.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking. Liver and kidney both go from tender to grainy within a minute, and on open coals that minute passes fast.
  • Using lighter fluid or self-igniting briquettes, which taints everything on the fire.
  • Cooking on a grate over the coals instead of on them — this dish specifically goes on the embers.
  • Cutting the pieces unevenly, so some are raw while others are ruined.

Health Benefits

  • Offal is among the richest natural sources of iron, zinc, vitamin A and B12.
  • Cooked with no added fat at all.
  • Very high in protein and essentially free of carbohydrate.

Cultural Note

Somalis regard liver as precious and give it to the guest of honour. When a household slaughters an animal, the offal is cooked and offered first while the main cuts are still cooking — a way of feeding someone within minutes of their arrival, which matters in a culture where a guest may have travelled a long way.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Not a keeping dish — it is made and eaten within minutes.

Reheating: Not recommended; reheating overcooks it.

Serve with: Cad iyo Caano, Basbaas, Canjeero
