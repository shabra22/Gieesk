🍅 Red Red Stew, the Long Cook

Red red stew

Vegan, with the beans squeezed between thumb and forefinger to test them and the tomatoes cooked forty-five minutes to an hour before they go in.

The vegan red red, and the one with the best bean doneness test in print: simmer at least an hour, or until the beans are tender enough to be squeezed easily between thumb and forefinger. The tomato base is cooked much longer than in the other documented versions — forty-five to sixty minutes, or until the tomatoes start to break down — before the beans join for a further thirty minutes until the beans are tender and the tartness of the tomatoes has dissipated. Seventy-five millilitres of palm oil, and a warning that palm oil has a low smoke point.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, for the beans, Heavy pan, Stick blender (optional), Wooden spoon

  • 200 g dried black-eyed beans, or a 400 g tin
  • 75 ml (5 tbsp) palm oil or carotene oil
  • 1 red onion, finely diced
  • 2.5 cm piece fresh root ginger, finely grated — unpeeled if organic
  • 1/2 tbsp dried chilli flakes
  • 1/2 red scotch bonnet chilli, deseeded and diced
  • 1/2 tsp curry powder
  • 1/2 tbsp chilli powder
  • 1 x 400 g tin chopped or whole plum tomatoes
  • 200 g plum tomatoes, roughly chopped
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

To serve

  • Gari, for sprinkling
  • Fried plantain

Method

  1. For dried beans: rinse, cover with water, bring to the boil, then simmer at least 1 hour, or until the beans are tender enough to be squeezed easily between thumb and forefinger. For tinned: drain, rinse, drain again.
  2. Heat the oil on low to medium until it melts — palm oil has a low smoke point, so be careful not to let it burn.
  3. Add the onion, ginger, chilli flakes and scotch bonnet and sauté a few minutes, until the onion is translucent.
  4. Add the curry and chilli powders.
  5. Add all the tomatoes, the purée, salt and pepper.
  6. Cook on medium 45 to 60 minutes, or until the tomatoes start to break down.
  7. Blend with a stick blender if you prefer it smooth.
  8. Add the beans, reduce to medium-low, and cook a further 30 minutes, until the beans are tender and the tartness of the tomatoes has dissipated.
  9. Check the seasoning and serve with gari on top and fried plantain.

Chef Tips

  • The bean test is the best in print: tender enough to be squeezed easily between thumb and forefinger. Far more reliable than a clock.
  • Forty-five to sixty minutes on the tomatoes before the beans go in, which is three to six times longer than the other documented versions. It is what makes this stew taste deep rather than sharp.
  • Until the tartness of the tomatoes has dissipated is the second cue, after the beans go in. Two separate endpoints and both are worth waiting for.
  • Palm oil has a low smoke point — her own warning. Melt it on low to medium and do not let it get near smoking.
  • Vegan as written, which none of the other documented red reds are. The smoked fish is what they use for depth and the long tomato cook is what replaces it here.
  • Her tip for tinned chopped tomatoes: add them twenty minutes into the cooking time, or stir in a tablespoon of sugar to counter the tartness.
  • Gari sprinkled on top is not optional in Ghana — it adds the sour, grainy note the dish is built to carry.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Testing the beans by time rather than by squeezing one.
  • Short-cutting the forty-five-minute tomato cook.
  • Letting the palm oil smoke.
  • Serving while the tomato still tastes sharp.
  • Leaving off the gari.

Health Benefits

  • Vegan, and a complete protein when served with gari or rice.
  • Black-eyed beans are high in folate, iron and soluble fibre.
  • Red palm oil supplies provitamin-A carotenoids and tocotrienols.
  • The long tomato cook maximises lycopene availability.

Cultural Note

A vegan red red is not a compromise — the dish is beans, tomato and palm oil at heart, and the smoked fish in the other versions is seasoning rather than structure. The thumb-and-forefinger bean test is the kind of instruction that comes from someone who has cooked a lot of beans and knows a clock will lie to you.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat. Better the next day.

Serve with: Fried plantain, Gari, Kelewele
