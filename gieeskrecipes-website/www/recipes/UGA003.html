🥘 Katogo Byenda

Katogo Byenda

Tripe and sweetbreads soaked an hour, simmered an hour until tender, then lifted out while the sauce is built in their own cooking liquor and poured back over matooke.

This is the katogo people will cross Kampala for, and the technique that makes it is the one most home cooks skip: the byenda is boiled in salted water for an hour, then lifted out, and the tomato sauce is built separately in a second pan before the reserved cooking liquor goes back in. That liquor is where all the flavour went, and the dish is thin without it. The soak beforehand — an hour in cold water, changed often — is not optional with offal.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, for the soak, Saucepan with a lid, A second frying pan, Grater

    The byenda

    • 1 kg tripe and sweetbreads (byenda)
    • 500 ml water, for boiling
    • 1.5 tsp salt

    The matooke

    • 2 kg matooke, peeled and washed

    The sauce

    • 5 big ripe tomatoes, grated
    • 2 large onions, chopped
    • 2 medium green peppers, chopped
    • 2 large carrots, chopped
    • 30 ml (2 tbsp) tomato paste
    • 30 ml (2 tbsp) Royco, or 1 beef stock cube
    • 1 to 2 chillies, finely chopped (optional)
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) oil

    To serve

    • Greens
    • Fruit juice

    Method

    1. Rinse the byenda and soak it in cold water for 1 hour, changing the water frequently.
    2. Remove the tough outer skin from the sweetbreads and any excess fat and veins from the tripe.
    3. Wash thoroughly and cut into bite-sized pieces.
    4. Boil in the 500 ml of water with the salt for 20 minutes.
    5. Reduce the heat and simmer for about 40 minutes more, until tender.
    6. Lift the byenda out of the soup and keep the soup.

    The sauce

    1. Heat the oil in a separate pan and fry the onions, green pepper, carrots and byenda together, stirring frequently.
    2. Add the tomato paste and keep stirring until the colour darkens.
    3. Dissolve the Royco in cold water, or crumble the stock cube into some of the hot soup, and pour it in with the grated tomatoes and the chilli.
    4. Cover and simmer until the tomatoes have cooked down.
    5. Gradually add the reserved soup and bring to the boil. Check the seasoning.

    Finish

    1. Pour it over the peeled matooke, cover, and cook on high heat for 10 minutes.
    2. Reduce to low and simmer until the matooke is done.
    3. Serve with greens and fruit juice.

    Chef Tips

    • Keep the cooking liquor. Lifting the byenda out and building the sauce separately, then returning the soup, is the whole technique — throw it away and you have thrown away the dish.
    • Soak it an hour in cold water and change the water often. Offal that has not been soaked and trimmed will taste of exactly what you did not remove.
    • Trim the tough outer skin off the sweetbreads and the fat and veins off the tripe before it ever meets heat.
    • Darken the tomato paste before the liquid goes in. Raw paste tastes metallic and never quite cooks out later.
    • The matooke goes in last and cooks in the sauce, which is what separates this from serving a stew alongside matooke.
    • An hour of simmering is the minimum for tender tripe. If it is still chewy, keep going — nothing else will fix it.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Discarding the cooking soup.
    • Skipping the soak.
    • Leaving the fat and veins on.
    • Adding the liquid before the tomato paste has darkened.
    • Serving it before the tripe is genuinely tender.

    Health Benefits

    • Tripe is high in protein and low in fat once trimmed, and a good source of B12, selenium and zinc.
    • Carrots, peppers and five tomatoes make this close to a complete plate.
    • One tablespoon of oil for six servings.
    • Offal makes full use of the animal, which is both economical and the older way of eating.

    Cultural Note

    Katogo byenda is a weekend and a payday breakfast, and the stall that does it well has a reputation that travels by word of mouth. Like mogodu in South Africa and skopo across the region, it belongs to a family of offal dishes that were dismissed by people who did not eat them and are now on menus at prices that would surprise the women who perfected them.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Better on the second day.

    Reheating: Warm gently on the hob with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Dodo, Fresh fruit juice, Ugandan tea
