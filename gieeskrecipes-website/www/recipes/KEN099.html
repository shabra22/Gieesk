🥥 Kenyan Coconut Pilau

Pilau ya Nazi

Kenyan Coconut Pilau — the most fragrant of all Kenyan pilau variations. Basmati rice and spiced chicken or goat cooked in a blend of coconut milk and stock with whole coastal spices and deeply caramelised onions. Sweet, creamy, and aromatic — a coastal celebration in every grain.

Coconut pilau takes the revered Mombasa pilau technique and adds the coast's other great ingredient — thick coconut milk — to the cooking liquid. The result is a rice dish with a subtly creamy sweetness underlying all the aromatic complexity of the pilau spices. It is particularly popular for Eid celebrations along the coast, where the richness of coconut milk makes the pilau more indulgent.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot with tight lid, Wooden spoon

  • 2 cups (400g) basmati rice — rinsed and soaked 30 minutes
  • 700g chicken or goat — bone-in pieces
  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 3 cups chicken or goat stock
  • 3 large onions — thinly sliced
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 5 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil or ghee
  • 4 cardamom pods — crushed
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 4 cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Caramelise onions in oil over medium heat for 18—20 minutes until deeply golden.
  2. Add whole spices. Fry 1 minute.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes, turmeric, and curry powder. Cook for 8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add meat. Brown 5 minutes.
  6. Add 1 cup stock. Cover and simmer for 25—30 minutes (chicken) or 45 minutes (goat) until tender.
  7. Add the drained rice, coconut milk, and remaining stock. Stir once gently.
  8. Bring to a gentle boil. Reduce to lowest heat. Cover tightly.
  9. Cook undisturbed for 20 minutes until liquid is absorbed.
  10. Rest covered for 10 minutes. Garnish and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Never let the coconut milk boil vigorously after adding — it can split and make the rice oily.
  • Use freshly extracted coconut milk for the most fragrant result.
  • The caramelised onion base is just as critical here as in classic pilau.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling hard after adding coconut milk — it splits.
  • Under-caramelising the onions.
  • Too much liquid — coconut milk is richer than stock so slightly less total liquid is needed.

The Science

Coconut milk contains both water and fat. The fat phase carries fat-soluble aromatic compounds from the spices and distributes them through the rice during cooking. The natural sugars (sucrose, glucose) in coconut milk also caramelise slightly during cooking, adding a subtle sweetness to the finished pilau.

Cultural Note

Coconut pilau is a celebrated variation of Swahili coastal cooking — the fusion of the pilau tradition with the coast's beloved coconut milk creates one of the most indulgent and aromatic rice dishes in the entire Kenyan culinary repertoire.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days. Freeze for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Sprinkle with water and reheat gently — the coconut fat can separate slightly but recombines with stirring.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Natural Yogurt, Kuku wa Nazi, Samaki wa Kupaka, Coconut Chutney
