🌱 Kontomire Stew with Plantain

Nkontomire — kontomire stew

Cocoyam leaves with goat, crab and smoked fish — and the cue that matters is the agushi beginning to clump in the sauce after a few minutes.

Kontomire stew is the other half of ampesi and it is one of the great dishes of the Akan kitchen. A cup of palm oil softens the onions; blended tomatoes and pepper go in and simmer until they start to fry, three to five minutes; then marinated goat, smoked fish and crabs. The kontomire follows, and then the soaked agushi — ground melon seeds — stirred in very gently. The cue is exact and worth knowing: the agushi begins to clump in the sauce after a few minutes. Ten to fifteen minutes on low and it is done. Turkey berries and calabash nutmeg are in the list.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot, Blender, Separate pot, for the plantain, Wooden spoon

  • 1 lb kontomire (cocoyam) leaves, washed and chopped — spinach works
  • 2 lb goat meat — chicken or beef acceptable
  • 2 large crabs
  • 1 smoked fish
  • 1/2 cup ground agushi (melon seeds)
  • 1 cup palm oil
  • 2 medium onions and a handful of shallots
  • 1 blended onion
  • 7-10 medium tomatoes
  • A handful of turkey berries
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1-2 garlic cloves
  • 1-2 scotch bonnet peppers
  • A dash of alligator pepper
  • 1-2 grated calabash nutmegs
  • Salt, to taste

To serve

  • 5 fingers of plantain

Method

  1. Boil the crabs about 5 minutes.
  2. Soften the onions in the palm oil.
  3. Add the blended tomatoes and pepper and leave to simmer until it starts to fry, stirring about 3 to 5 minutes.
  4. Add the marinated goat, the smoked fish and the crabs.
  5. Add the kontomire leaves.
  6. Add the soaked agushi while stirring very gently, and leave to simmer — the agushi begins to clump in the sauce after a few minutes.
  7. Leave to simmer on low heat about 10 to 15 minutes.
  8. Meanwhile boil the plantains about 15 minutes, and serve alongside.

Chef Tips

  • The agushi clumping is the cue and it is the whole character of the dish — you want soft lumps of melon seed suspended in the sauce, not a smooth thickened gravy.
  • Stir very gently once the agushi is in. Vigorous stirring breaks the clumps up and you lose the texture entirely.
  • Until it starts to fry is the tomato cue — the moment the water has cooked off and the oil comes back out. Three to five minutes of stirring.
  • A cup of palm oil is a lot and it is correct. Kontomire stew is a rich dish and the oil carries the leaf.
  • Turkey berries, alligator pepper and calabash nutmeg are all in the list and all are genuinely Ghanaian — the turkey berries are bitter little green globes and they are not a garnish.
  • Crabs boiled five minutes separately, then added. They are in for flavour as much as for eating.
  • Grate the calabash nutmeg fresh. One to two whole nutmegs is a strong aromatic hit and it is one of the markers of a properly made kontomire stew.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring hard after the agushi goes in, destroying the clumps.
  • Adding the tomatoes and moving on before they start to fry.
  • Reducing the palm oil.
  • Substituting true nutmeg for calabash nutmeg.
  • Cooking the leaves so long they lose their colour.

Health Benefits

  • Cocoyam leaves are exceptionally high in vitamin A, vitamin C, iron and calcium.
  • Melon seeds supply plant protein, magnesium and zinc.
  • Red palm oil contributes provitamin-A carotenoids and tocotrienols.
  • Crab and smoked fish both add lean protein, iodine and selenium.

Cultural Note

Kontomire stew with ampesi is the everyday Akan plate, and the clumps of agushi suspended in dark green leaf and red oil are what it should look like. Turkey berries, alligator pepper and calabash nutmeg in one ingredient list is a good sign that a recipe was written by someone cooking it rather than describing it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat. Do not stir hard or the agushi clumps break down.

Serve with: Ampesi, Boiled plantain, Rice
