🥣 Lakh, Millet and Soured Milk

Lakh / lahk

Two to three cups of cooked millet, warm or cooled, mixed with a litre of lait caillé, chopped coconut, golden raisins, fruit, vanilla sugar and a few drops of orange blossom water — the source’s whole method is ‘Mix. Eat.’

10 minTotal time
10 minPrep
4Servings
310Calories
EasyDifficulty

Published on the ‘Bon Appétit!’ blog on Thursday 29 October 2009 under the persona ‘Senegal Daily’, this is the plainest lakh in the research. 2 to 3 cups of cooked millet, warm or cooled, are mixed with 1 litre of lait caillé (soured milk), chopped fresh coconut, a handful of golden raisins, an apple, peach or mango, 2 bananas, 1 packet of vanilla sugar, a few drops of fleur d’oranger and sugar to taste. The method, in its entirety as printed, is ‘Mix. Eat. Simple enough, right?’ There are no times and no temperatures. The author says her husband made it every night in Senegal and calls it ‘the Senegalese answer to Swiss muesli’ — millet for the oats, soured milk for the yoghurt. THE LAIT CAILLÉ IS BOUGHT: the recipe contains no souring step, so there is no fermentation to cue. Cooking the millet is not described on the page.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, Spoon

  • 1 liter lait caillé (soured milk), bought
  • Fresh coconut, chopped — quantity not printed
  • Handful golden raisins
  • 1 apple, peach or mango, chopped
  • 2 bananas, chopped
  • 1 packet vanilla-flavoured sugar
  • A few drops of fleur d’oranger (orange blossom water)
  • 2–3 cups cooked millet, warm or cooled
  • Sugar, to taste

Method

  1. Mix all the ingredients together.
  2. Eat.

Chef Tips

  • The method as printed is ‘Mix. Eat. Simple enough, right?’
  • The page gives no way to cook the millet; use millet you have already cooked.
  • Lait caillé is described as available in French and Middle Eastern grocery stores.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Looking for a method the page does not have.
  • Souring milk yourself without a safe source of guidance.
  • Using sweetened yoghurt without lowering the sugar.

‘The Senegalese answer to Swiss muesli.’ A 2010 reader comment: ‘I left senegal in March 2010 and this is one of the many of the foods I miss.’ The post spells the dish ‘lahk’ in the body and ‘Lakh’ in the title; the post does not name a language.
