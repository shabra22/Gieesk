Umbricelli alla Norcina

Thick, hand-rolled eggless noodles from Norcia dressed in a creamy sauce of crumbled sausage, cream, and a shaving of the region's famous black truffle.

Norcia has been synonymous with pork butchery for centuries, and the sauce named for it — 'alla norcina' — traditionally combines the town's excellent sausage with cream and truffle, tossed here with thick, chewy umbricelli, a close cousin of strangozzi and pici.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Large pot, Sauté pan

  • 400 g (3 cups) 00 flour
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) water, approximately
  • Pinch of salt
  • 400 g (14 oz) Italian sausage, casings removed and crumbled
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry white wine
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) heavy cream
  • 40 g (1.4 oz) fresh black truffle, plus more for shaving, or truffle paste
  • 40 g (½ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the flour, water, and salt into a firm dough, kneading for 10 minutes. Wrap and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Roll the dough into thick ropes and hand-roll into long, slightly irregular noodles, similar to a thick spaghetti.
  3. Warm the olive oil in a large pan and cook the crumbled sausage over medium heat until browned, breaking it into small pieces.
  4. Add the garlic and cook briefly, then pour in the wine and let it reduce by half.
  5. Stir in the cream and simmer gently for 5 minutes until slightly thickened.
  6. Grate in most of the truffle and stir through, keeping some back for garnish.
  7. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the umbricelli for 4–5 minutes until tender.
  8. Drain the pasta, reserving a little cooking water, and toss into the sauce with the Parmigiano, loosening with pasta water as needed.
  9. Plate and finish with a final shaving of truffle over the top.

Chef Tips

  • Rolling the dough into thick, rustic strands by hand is traditional and part of the dish's character.
  • Brown the sausage well before adding liquid — this builds the base flavor of the whole sauce.
  • If fresh truffle isn't available, a good-quality truffle paste stirred in at the end is a reasonable substitute.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the cream sauce until it becomes too thick and heavy rather than lightly coating the pasta.
  • Skipping the wine reduction, which balances the richness of the cream and sausage.
  • Using bland sausage — a well-seasoned, fennel-forward Italian sausage makes a real difference here.

Cultural Note

Alla norcina sauce takes its name directly from Norcia, a town so associated with pork butchery that 'norcino' became the Italian word for a pork butcher throughout much of the country.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The sauce keeps refrigerated for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop with a splash of cream or milk to loosen.

Serve with: A medium-bodied red wine from Umbria
