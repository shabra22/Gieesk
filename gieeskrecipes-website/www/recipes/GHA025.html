🍳 Ghanaian Egg Stew

Egg stew — egg sauce

Beaten egg poured into thickened tomato purée in sections and left alone until the surface turns white and the eggs almost set.

Egg stew is breakfast and supper across much of West Africa, and the Ghanaian version is distinguished by using tomato alone where Nigeria adds bell pepper. Five plum tomatoes, garlic, onion and an optional habanero are blended and simmered covered ten to twelve minutes until thickened. Five eggs are beaten with bouillon and poured into the stew in sections without stirring, then covered on medium-low until the surface turns white and the eggs are almost set — about five minutes. Only then are they stirred into clusters and cooked uncovered another five.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Frying pan or saucepan with a lid, Bowl, for beating the eggs

  • 5 plum tomatoes, roughly chopped
  • 4 cloves garlic, roughly chopped
  • 1 small onion, roughly chopped
  • 1 habanero pepper, stemmed (optional)
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 tsp bouillon powder, preferably chicken
  • 5 large eggs
  • Kosher salt, if needed

To serve

  • Cooked yam, plantains or rice

Method

  1. Blend the tomatoes, garlic, onion and habanero.
  2. Heat the oil and add the purée.
  3. Simmer covered until thickened, 10 to 12 minutes.
  4. Stir in 1 teaspoon of the bouillon powder.
  5. Beat the eggs with the remaining bouillon.
  6. Pour the eggs into the stew in sections, without stirring.
  7. Cover on medium-low until the surface turns white and the eggs almost set, about 5 minutes.
  8. Uncover, stir into clusters, and cook uncovered about 5 minutes more, until the liquid reduces.

Chef Tips

  • Pour the egg in sections and do not stir. That is the whole technique — it gives you clusters of set egg in sauce rather than a scramble.
  • Surface turning white and the eggs almost set is the cue, at about five minutes. Stirring before that point destroys the clusters.
  • Ten to twelve minutes covered on the tomato purée first. The stew must be thick before the egg goes in, or you get egg soup.
  • Tomato only, no bell pepper. The author is explicit that this is what separates the Ghanaian version from the Nigerian one.
  • Vegetable oil rather than palm oil, by the author's choice — she says palm oil can dominate. If you want it redder and richer, palm oil is the swap.
  • Bouillon is split between the stew and the beaten egg. Seasoning the egg itself is what stops the clusters tasting bland.
  • Salt only if needed, after the bouillon. Bouillon powder is already salty.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring the eggs in as soon as they are poured.
  • Adding the eggs before the purée has thickened.
  • Adding bell pepper, which makes it the Nigerian version.
  • All the bouillon into the stew, leaving the egg unseasoned.
  • Salting before tasting.

Cultural Note

Egg stew with boiled yam or fried plantain is one of the most common meals in Ghana — cheap, fast and made from things that keep without refrigeration. That the Ghanaian and Nigerian versions differ by exactly one ingredient, the bell pepper, is the kind of detail that only a cook who has made both would bother writing down.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. Egg stew does not keep well beyond that.

Reheating: Low heat, briefly. Overheating makes the egg rubbery.

Serve with: Boiled yam, Fried plantain, Sugar Bread
