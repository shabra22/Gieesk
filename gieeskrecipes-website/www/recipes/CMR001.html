🥜 Ndolé (Spinach or Bitterleaves and Peanut Soup)

Ndolé

Stew beef, stockfish and blended skinless peanuts simmered with ground crayfish and washed bitterleaves, finished with shrimp fried in hot oil with onion — the dish its author calls often referred to as the national dish.

Imma Adamu’s ndolé builds the stew in stages. Beef is seasoned with salt, bouillon and onion and boiled until tender; stockfish is boiled separately and added, aiming for about 3 cups of stock. Skinless peanuts are boiled about 10 minutes, cooled and blended fine, then stirred in with a blended onion and garlic. Ground crayfish goes in and the pot simmers 10 minutes, stirring often so it does not burn. Washed bitterleaves (or frozen spinach) are added for a few more minutes, and the stew is finished with shrimp stirred in hot oil until just pink with sliced onion. The page says it is traditionally served with miondo or bobolo (fermented cassava sticks) or boiled or fried plantains.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Frying pan

  • 2 tablespoons Maggi (bouillon)
  • 1 large onion, sliced (a second onion is blended with the garlic in the method — not listed separately)
  • 1 pound (about 2 cups) skinless groundnuts/peanuts
  • 1/2 pound shrimp
  • 1/2 pound stockfish
  • 1/2–1 pound stew beef, cut in chunks
  • 4–5 garlic cloves
  • 3/4 cup ground crayfish
  • 3/4–1 pound washed bitterleaves (or frozen spinach)
  • 2–3 cups oil
  • Salt — quantity not printed

Method

    Meat and fish

    1. Season the beef with salt, bouillon and onion and boil until tender. The page says only that the time depends on the meat chosen.
    2. Boil the stockfish separately in salted water and add it to the beef. Aim for about 3 cups of stock. (The page notes the stockfish is soaked overnight.)

    Peanut base

    1. Boil the peanuts about 10 minutes, cool, and blend fine. Stir the blended peanuts into the meat and stockfish.
    2. Blend one onion with the garlic and add it. Add the crayfish and simmer 10 minutes, stirring often to prevent burning. Season with salt and bouillon.

    Greens and shrimp

    1. Add the washed bitterleaves (or spinach) and simmer several more minutes.
    2. Stir the shrimp in hot oil until they just turn pink, add the sliced onion for a few minutes, then stir the mixture into the pot. Serve hot.

    Chef Tips

    • The printed cues are ‘until tender’ for the meat and ‘just turn pink’ for the shrimp.
    • Stir often once the peanuts and crayfish are in — the page warns the pot can burn.
    • Fresh spinach is blanched about 2 minutes; dried bitterleaves are soaked overnight and cooked 15 minutes with 1 teaspoon bicarbonate of soda (page notes).

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Leaving the peanuts unblended or only coarsely blended.
    • Walking away from the pot after the peanuts and crayfish go in.
    • Adding the bitterleaves without washing them first.

    Cultural Note

    Adamu calls ndolé ‘often referred to as the national dish’ of Cameroon. Other printings call it ‘the national dish of the south of Cameroon’ and tie it to the Douala of the Littoral. The attributions are the authors’ own and are not reconciled.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: No storage time is printed.

    Reheating: Reheat gently, stirring, as the peanut base can catch.

    Serve with: Miondo or bobolo (Miondo (Fermented Cassava Sticks — Research-Scale Process), Bobolo (Fermented Cassava Sticks — Pilot-Scale Process)), Boiled or fried plantains, Corn fufu
