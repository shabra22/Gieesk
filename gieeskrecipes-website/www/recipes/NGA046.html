🦐 Itsekiri Banga Soup

Obe-ekpo — banga

The cold-water extraction — palm fruit boiled thirty minutes, pounded, then squeezed with cold water — and catfish set in undisturbed for exactly seven minutes.

This is banga as the Itsekiri make it, and it differs from the Urhobo version in one step that the source itself calls diagnostic: the cream is extracted with cold water rather than just-boiled. Palm fruit boils thirty minutes until the flesh softens, is pounded to separate flesh from seeds, then squeezed by hand with cold water and strained to give about three cups of thick orange cream. The fish goes in at the end on a strict schedule — catfish undisturbed for seven minutes, shrimp for the final three — and the finished soup should coat the back of a spoon.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Mortar and pestle, Sieve, Ladle

  • 2 lbs fresh palm fruit, or 3 cups tinned palm fruit concentrate
  • 1.5 lbs fresh catfish, cleaned and cut into medium pieces
  • 1/2 lb fresh shrimp, deveined and rinsed
  • 1/2 lb smoked fish, deboned and broken into chunks
  • 3 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 1/4 cup whole dried crayfish
  • 2 scotch bonnet peppers
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 2 seasoning cubes
  • Salt to taste

Banga spice blend — quantities not specified in the source

  • Beletete
  • Oburunbebe sticks
  • Ataiko
  • Irugeje seeds

To serve

  • Starch (usi)

Method

  1. If using fresh palm fruits, boil them in water for 30 minutes until the flesh softens. Pound in a mortar to separate the flesh from the seeds. Add cold water and squeeze the flesh by hand to extract the cream. Strain through a sieve — you should get about 3 cups of thick, orange cream.
  2. Bring the cream to the boil, stirring occasionally to prevent separation, and simmer 15 minutes until it thickens slightly.
  3. Add the diced onion and scotch bonnets and simmer 10 minutes.
  4. Drop in the oburunbebe sticks and irugeje seeds. Sprinkle in the ground beletete and ataiko.
  5. Add the ground and whole dried crayfish and cook 10 minutes.
  6. Add the smoked fish and seasoning cubes and cook 5 minutes.
  7. Place the catfish pieces into the soup gently. Do not stir — let the fish sit in the simmering cream and cook undisturbed for 7 minutes.
  8. Add the shrimp for the final 3 minutes. Shrimp overcook quickly and turn rubbery if left too long.
  9. The soup should be rich, creamy and aromatic, and it should coat the back of a spoon. If it is too thick, add a splash of warm water.
  10. Serve with starch. Tear off a piece, dip it generously into the soup, and swallow.

Chef Tips

  • Cold water for the extraction, and the source says this is what distinguishes the Itsekiri method from the Igbo one. The Urhobo version pours just-boiled water over the bruised nuts instead.
  • Thirty minutes on the palm fruit boil, against fifteen to twenty in the Urhobo account. Both are stated by named sources and neither is adjusted here.
  • Seven minutes undisturbed for the catfish. Do not stir, do not turn, do not look at it. Catfish falls apart if handled.
  • Three minutes for the shrimp and no more. The source's reason is exact: shrimp overcook quickly and turn rubbery.
  • Coats the back of a spoon is the consistency cue, and it is a good one for a soup this rich.
  • Stir occasionally while the cream first boils, to prevent it separating. After that, as little stirring as possible.
  • Total time is about eighty minutes including the extraction. If you start from the tin, take an hour off.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using hot water for the extraction, which is the Urhobo rather than the Itsekiri method.
  • Stirring after the catfish goes in.
  • Leaving the shrimp in longer than three minutes.
  • Letting the cream separate at the first boil.
  • Serving it thin enough to run off the spoon.

Cultural Note

Banga with starch is, by this source's account, what announces an Itsekiri table — served at Temotsi weddings, at funeral rites, at naming ceremonies and chieftaincy installations. The word banga itself comes from the Itsekiri and Urhobo languages and refers to the palm fruit, which is why the soup carries different names everywhere else and that one everywhere the fruit is called that.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated. Remove the shrimp before freezing.

Reheating: Gentle heat, lid off, no stirring once warm.

Serve with: Starch, Iyan, Eba or garri
