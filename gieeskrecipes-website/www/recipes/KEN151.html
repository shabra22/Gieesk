🥜 Kenyan Roasted Groundnuts with Salt

Karanga za Chumvi

Karanga za Chumvi — the most universal Kenyan snack. Raw groundnuts dry-roasted until perfectly toasted, then immediately salted while hot. The simplest, most satisfying, and most portable food in Kenya — sold at every roadside, market, and gathering.

The salted roasted groundnut is Kenya's original fast food — sold in paper cones at every bus stop, market stall, and street corner since before anyone can remember. The trick is in the salting: fine salt goes on immediately after roasting, while the nuts are still hot enough that the salt adheres to the surface. Every Kenyan has their own preferred level of doneness — from pale and mild to deeply toasted and nutty.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pan or wok, Wooden spoon or spatula, Paper towels

  • 500g raw groundnuts — in their skins
  • 1—1½ tsp fine salt
  • 1 tbsp fine sand — optional (traditional roasting method)

Method

  1. Place raw groundnuts in a large dry heavy pan over medium heat.
  2. Stir continuously and constantly for 15—20 minutes until the skins begin to split and peel, the colour darkens to a deep golden, and the aroma turns deeply nutty.
  3. Remove from heat immediately — they continue cooking from residual heat.
  4. Immediately sprinkle fine salt over the hot nuts. Toss to coat.
  5. Spread on paper towels to cool.
  6. Once cool, rub between palms to remove loose skins if desired.
  7. Serve in paper cones or small bowls.

Chef Tips

  • Constant stirring is non-negotiable — groundnuts burn in seconds once hot.
  • Salt immediately while still hot — this is when the salt adheres and absorbs best.
  • Remove from heat slightly before they look done — residual heat finishes them.
  • The traditional sand-roasting method (roasting in hot sand in a clay pot) gives incomparably even results.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Unattended pan — burns irreversibly in seconds.
  • Salting cold nuts — salt does not adhere and the flavour is flat.
  • High heat — burns the outside before the inside is cooked.

The Science

Roasting creates the Maillard reaction between groundnut amino acids and sugars, producing hundreds of new flavour compounds. Fine salt on hot nuts partially dissolves in the surface oils, carrying flavour into the nut rather than just sitting on the skin.

Cultural Note

Salted roasted groundnuts are woven into the fabric of Kenyan social life — shared at gatherings, sold on every corner, and eaten during conversations, football matches, and long journeys. They are the snack that requires no ceremony.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight container for up to 2 weeks at room temperature.

Reheating: Not required.

Serve with: Cold Soda, Kenyan Masala Chai, Roasted Maize, As a standalone snack
