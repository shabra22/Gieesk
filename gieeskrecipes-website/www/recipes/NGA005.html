🍲 Gbegiri with Assorted Beef

Gbegiri (abula)

A blended-and-sieved gbegiri built on 800 g of assorted beef, with the palm oil heated three minutes and deliberately not bleached.

This is the buka version — gbegiri with turkey neck, shaki and smoked snout in it, rather than the plain bean soup. Two things set it apart. The beans are blended and then sieved every time, not only when hand-mashed, so the chaff always comes out. And the palm oil is heated for about three minutes without bleaching, which the author states explicitly — a useful counterweight to the ayamase recipes where bleaching is mandatory. She also says unpeeled beans will do, which no other source consulted is willing to concede.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot or pressure cooker, Blender, Fine-hole sieve, Saucepan

  • 1 cup brown beans, peeled
  • 1/4 cup palm oil
  • 2 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 2 beef stock cubes
  • Cayenne pepper, to taste
  • Salt to taste

Assorted meat (optional)

  • 800 g assorted beef, about 3 cups — turkey neck, shaki and smoked snout
  • 1 onion
  • Salt

Method

  1. Soak the beans in water 3 to 5 minutes and peel to remove the skin, or use the blender to peel them.
  2. Boil the peeled beans until soft, or pressure-cook them.
  3. Separately, boil the assorted meat with the stock cubes, onions and salt until tender. Keep the stock and set the meat aside.
  4. Blend the cooked beans smooth, then pass through a fine-hole sieve and discard the chaff.
  5. Heat the palm oil approximately 3 minutes — without bleaching it.
  6. Stir the bean puree into the oil. Aim for a consistency somewhere in the middle of loose and thick.
  7. Add the beef and cayenne, reduce the heat and cook about 10 minutes.
  8. Add the crayfish, stock cube and salt, and cook 3 minutes more.

Chef Tips

  • 'Without bleaching oil' is the author's own phrase and it matters. Three minutes is to warm and loosen the oil, not to strip its colour.
  • Sieve every time, not just when you mash by hand. A fine-hole sieve and the chaff goes in the bin.
  • Target consistency is 'somewhere in the middle of loose and thick' — loose enough to pour over amala, thick enough to sit on it.
  • The 3-to-5-minute soak is much shorter than it sounds like it should be. You only want the bean hydrated enough to split.
  • She permits unpeeled beans. If you do that, the sieve stops being optional and becomes the only thing standing between you and grit.
  • The ijabe — 'the small broom used for cooking' — is offered as the alternative to the blender for getting the texture right.
  • The meat is marked optional in the original. Without it this becomes Gbegiri with a quarter cup of oil instead of three tablespoons.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Bleaching the palm oil out of habit.
  • Skipping the sieve after blending.
  • Soaking the beans for hours instead of minutes.
  • Making it too thick to pour.
  • Adding the crayfish at the start, where it loses its aroma over the full cook.

Health Benefits

  • Cowpeas supply protein, folate and soluble fibre.
  • Collagen-rich cuts like shaki and snout add gelatine.
  • Red palm oil provides vitamin E and carotenoids.
  • Gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Ajoke places this squarely in the buka — 'commonly served at restaurants also known as Buka' — and describes the target as 'delicious, velvety and mildly flavoured'. Mildly flavoured is the point: gbegiri is the quiet element on the plate, and the heat arrives with the stew poured over it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated. Keep the meat in the soup.

Reheating: Low heat, loosen with water or stock.

Serve with: Amala, Ewedu, Abula
