🫔 Kenyan Chapati Roll

Chapati Roll

Kenyan Chapati Roll — a freshly made chapati spread with a thin egg omelette, filled with kachumbari and pili pili sauce, then rolled into a tight log and eaten on the go. The quintessential Nairobi street breakfast and lunch — fast, filling, and completely Kenyan.

The chapati roll is one of Nairobi's most beloved street foods — a hot chapati spread with spiced egg, filled with fresh kachumbari, rolled and eaten on the way to work or school. At every school gate, bus stop, and market entrance, the chapati roll vendor is a fixture of Nairobi morning life.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pan, Spatula

  • 2 chapatis
  • 2 large eggs — beaten
  • 1 tbsp milk
  • 1 tbsp kachumbari per roll
  • Pili pili sauce — to taste
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Beat eggs with milk, salt, and pepper.
  2. Heat oil in a pan over medium heat.
  3. Place a chapati in the pan.
  4. Pour half the egg mixture over the chapati. Let it spread naturally.
  5. Allow the egg to set partially — 1—2 minutes.
  6. Flip the chapati-egg together. Cook for 30 seconds.
  7. Remove from heat. Add kachumbari and pili pili along one edge.
  8. Roll firmly into a tight log.
  9. Wrap in paper for street food style or serve on a plate.

Chef Tips

  • The egg should be just set — not dry — when you roll.
  • Roll immediately while hot and flexible.
  • Street style: wrap tightly in newsprint or paper for structural support.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooked egg — dry and cracks when rolling.
  • Waiting too long to roll — the chapati stiffens.

The Science

Pouring the egg directly onto the chapati in the pan creates a bonded egg-chapati layer — the egg adheres to the chapati surface as it sets, creating a unified sheet that rolls without separating.

Cultural Note

The chapati roll is Nairobi's most beloved on-the-go breakfast — the snack that fuels the city's early mornings. Every school child knows it; every office worker has eaten it standing at a street stall before catching a matatu.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a pan.

Serve with: Fresh Juice, Kenyan Masala Chai, Pili Pili Sauce
