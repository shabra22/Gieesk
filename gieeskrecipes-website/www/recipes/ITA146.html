Pallotte Cacio e Ova

Rustic Abruzzese fried balls of cheese, egg, and breadcrumbs, simmered in a light tomato sauce — historically a meatless stand-in for meatballs during periods of fasting.

Born as an inventive meat substitute for Abruzzo's poorer rural families, especially during Lent and other fasting periods, pallotte cacio e ova have since become beloved in their own right, their savory, cheesy interior a satisfying surprise inside a light tomato sauce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Deep pot for frying, Saucepan for sauce

  • 250 g (2½ cups) stale bread, crusts removed, torn into pieces
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) milk
  • 200 g (2 cups) grated pecorino cheese
  • 4 eggs
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
  • 1 clove garlic, minced
  • Breadcrumbs, as needed
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • 600 g (1.3 lb) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Soak the bread in the milk until softened, then squeeze out excess liquid.
  2. Combine the soaked bread with the pecorino, eggs, parsley, garlic, salt, and pepper into a thick, moldable mixture, adding a little breadcrumb if too wet.
  3. Rest the mixture for 15 minutes, then shape into small balls, about the size of a walnut.
  4. Heat vegetable oil to 175°C (345°F) and fry the balls in batches until golden all over.
  5. Meanwhile, warm the olive oil in a saucepan and sauté the onion until softened.
  6. Add the crushed tomatoes and simmer for 15–20 minutes to make a light sauce.
  7. Add the fried balls to the simmering tomato sauce and cook gently for 10 minutes, allowing them to absorb some of the sauce.
  8. Serve hot, with plenty of sauce spooned over.

Chef Tips

  • Squeeze the soaked bread thoroughly to avoid a mixture that's too wet to shape properly.
  • Fry the balls until well-browned before adding to the sauce, which helps them hold their shape through the final simmer.
  • These freeze well before the final tomato-sauce simmer, making them a good make-ahead option.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a mixture that's too wet, causing the balls to fall apart during frying.
  • Skipping the initial fry and simmering raw balls directly in the sauce, which won't hold together as well.
  • Oversalting, given how much salt the pecorino already contributes.

Cultural Note

Pallotte cacio e ova originated as a resourceful meat substitute in rural Abruzzo, particularly valued during Lent when meat was traditionally avoided.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop in the sauce.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A simple green salad
