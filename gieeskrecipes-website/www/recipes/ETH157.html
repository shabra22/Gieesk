🍯 Ethiopian Infused Honey

ቅጠላ ቅጠል ማር — Qitela Mar

Ethiopian Herb-Infused Honey — Ethiopian mountain honey gently warmed with dried sacred basil (besobela), rue (tena adam), and a cinnamon stick. The aromatic honey used in coffee, on breads, and as a medicine. Ethiopia's herb-infused honey tradition.

Herb-infused honey is part of Ethiopia's ancient tradition of medicinal foods — the sacred basil and rue infused into honey produce a condiment with both extraordinary flavour and significant antimicrobial and digestive properties.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small saucepan, Clean jar

  • 250ml raw Ethiopian mountain honey
  • 2 sprigs dried sacred basil (besobela) — or regular dried basil
  • 1 sprig dried rue (tena adam)
  • 1 small cinnamon stick

Method

  1. Warm honey very gently over lowest heat — it must not boil (above 40°C destroys enzymes).
  2. Add herbs and cinnamon.
  3. Keep below 40°C for 8—10 minutes — the honey should feel warm, not hot.
  4. Remove from heat. Allow to cool with herbs still in.
  5. Transfer to a jar. Leave herbs in for 24 hours.
  6. Strain out herbs or leave them in (leaves are edible).

Chef Tips

  • Never boil honey — it destroys the enzymes and kills the flavour.
  • Ethiopian raw honey is already complex — the herbs add another layer, not a replacement.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overheating — damages the honey's active compounds.

The Science

Raw honey contains glucose oxidase (produces hydrogen peroxide — antimicrobial), invertase (digests sucrose), and numerous volatile aromatic compounds. All are deactivated above 40—45°C. The gentle infusion carries the herb volatile compounds (linalool from basil, rutin from rue) into the honey without destroying its active properties.

Health Benefits

  • Raw honey's antimicrobial and antioxidant properties.
  • Sacred basil (tulsi) adaptogenic compounds.
  • Rue supports digestive health.
  • One of Ethiopia's most important traditional medicines.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian herb-infused honey sits at the intersection of food and medicine — it is given to those with coughs and sore throats, drizzled into coffee as a tonic, spread on celebration breads as a blessing. The besobela (sacred basil) that is infused into it is the same plant used in Ethiopian Orthodox church ceremonies.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Indefinitely at room temperature — honey does not spoil.

Reheating: Warm very gently if crystallised.

Serve with: In Ethiopian coffee, On breads (ETH004, ETH005), As a throat medicine, In tej (ETH095)
