🔥 Chicken Yassa, Oven-Roasted

Yassa poulet

The chicken roasts at 200C for forty-five minutes while the onions cook down separately for thirty-five to forty — deep golden, then melting and caramelised.

3h 55mTotal time
25mPrep
1h 30mCook
4Servings
580Calories
MediumDifficulty

A chef's version that splits the work in two: four whole chicken legs, slashed and marinated, roast at 200C fan 180C for forty-five minutes until deep golden and cooked through, while four onions — two roughly chopped into the marinade and two thinly sliced — cook down for thirty-five to forty minutes until melting and caramelised. The marinade is blitzed garlic, spring onion, half a green pepper, ginger, scotch bonnet and parsley with a good pinch of salt, the juice of two lemons and a tablespoon of Dijon. The two halves come together with green olives for a final five minutes on low heat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or food processor, Roasting tray, Large heavy pan, Oven

  • 4 medium onions — 2 roughly chopped, 2 thinly sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic, peeled
  • 1 spring onion, roughly chopped
  • 1/2 green pepper, roughly chopped
  • 15 g ginger, roughly chopped
  • 1 scotch bonnet chilli, deseeded and chopped
  • A handful flat-leaf parsley
  • 2 lemons, juiced
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 4 whole chicken legs, skin on, slashed
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 bay leaf
  • Green olives (optional)

To serve

  • Plain rice, couscous or vermicelli

Method

  1. Blitz the roughly chopped onions, garlic, spring onion, green pepper, ginger, scotch bonnet and parsley with a good pinch of salt.
  2. Add the lemon juice and the Dijon mustard.
  3. Marinate the chicken with the sliced onion for at least a couple of hours, or overnight.
  4. Roast the chicken at 200C, fan 180C, gas 6, for 45 minutes, until deep golden and cooked through.
  5. Meanwhile cook the marinade and onions down — starting on high heat, then medium-high — for 35 to 40 minutes, until cooked through, melting and caramelised.
  6. Add the roasted chicken and the olives, cover, and finish for 5 minutes on a low heat.
  7. Serve with plain rice, couscous or vermicelli.

Chef Tips

  • Melting and caramelised is the onion endpoint, and thirty-five to forty minutes is what it takes. This is the longest onion cook of any documented yassa and the sauce shows it.
  • Roasting the chicken separately means you get crisp skin, which neither the one-pot nor the grilled version delivers. That is the trade this recipe makes.
  • Start the onions high, then drop to medium-high. The initial heat drives off the marinade's liquid so the onions can actually colour.
  • Two onions into the marinade and two sliced for the pan. Keep the split — the blitzed ones become sauce body, the sliced ones keep some texture.
  • Only five minutes together at the end. Any longer and the roast skin softens, which defeats the method.
  • Red wine vinegar here against cider vinegar or white vinegar elsewhere. It is a rounder, less sharp acid and it suits the long onion cook.
  • Vermicelli as a serving option is worth knowing — it is genuinely eaten with yassa in Senegal, not a Western substitution.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the onions for fifteen minutes and calling them caramelised.
  • Roasting the chicken and the onions in the same dish.
  • Blitzing all four onions.
  • Leaving it together too long at the end.
  • Skipping the slashes in the chicken, so the marinade stays on the surface.

Health Benefits

  • Four onions across four servings is a substantial source of quercetin and prebiotic fibre.
  • Lemon juice and parsley together supply vitamin C.
  • Roasting rather than frying keeps the added fat to three tablespoons.
  • Gluten-free and dairy-free with rice.

Cultural Note

Roasting the chicken apart from the sauce is a restaurant solution to a home-cooking problem — you cannot get crisp skin and a long onion braise out of one pot — and it is the kind of adjustment a chef makes without changing what the dish is.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. The skin will soften.

Reheating: Oven, to recover some of the skin. Covered reheating will not.

Serve with: Plain rice, couscous or vermicelli, Green olives, Attaya
