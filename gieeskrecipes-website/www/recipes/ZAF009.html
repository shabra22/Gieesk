🍚 Uphuthu

Uphuthu namasi

Maize meal added in two stages to a small amount of boiling water and worked with the back of a wooden spoon into crumbs — eaten cold with amasi and a spoonful of sugar, or hot with beans.

Uphuthu is the Zulu crumbly pap, known as krummelpap in Afrikaans, and the trick is the water: barely more than a cup of it to three hundred grams of meal, which is why it crumbles instead of flowing. Adding the meal in two stages rather than all at once is what keeps the crumbs distinct. Cold with amasi it is summer food; hot with sugar beans or a cabbage stew it is dinner.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan with a lid, Wooden spoon

  • 300 ml (1 1/4 cups) water
  • 1 tsp salt — or half that, to taste
  • 300 g maize meal

To serve, cold

  • 710 ml (3 cups) amasi
  • A splash of cream
  • 1 tbsp sugar per bowl

Method

  1. Bring the water to the boil, add the salt, and add half the maize meal. Turn the heat to low.
  2. Work the mixture with the back of a wooden spoon, adding the remaining maize meal gradually, until it resembles coarse crumbs.
  3. Cook for 20 minutes, stirring every 5 minutes so the bottom does not catch and the crumbs stay separate.
  4. Let it cool, then serve with cold amasi, a splash of cream and a spoonful of sugar.

Chef Tips

  • The back of the spoon, not the bowl of it. You are pressing the meal into crumbs rather than stirring it into a paste.
  • In two stages. All the meal at once and the water is absorbed before you can break up the lumps.
  • A teaspoon of salt in 300 g is on the salty side for a dish eaten with sweetened amasi — half a teaspoon suits most people.
  • Hot, with sugar beans or a cabbage-and-tomato stew, it is a completely different meal from the same pot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using too much water, which gives smooth pap rather than crumbs.
  • Stirring it as though it were porridge, which compacts the crumbs.
  • Leaving it more than five minutes unstirred, when the base catches.
  • Serving it hot with amasi, which warms the milk and loses the contrast.

Health Benefits

  • Amasi supplies protein, calcium and live lactic-acid cultures.
  • Maize meal is a slow-release carbohydrate and naturally gluten-free.
  • Low in fat before the cream goes on.

Cultural Note

Uphuthu with amasi is the dish South Africans of a certain generation associate with summer evenings and with grandmothers, and it is eaten across language groups under different names — uphuthu in isiZulu, umphokoqo in isiXhosa, krummelpap in Afrikaans. The maize is the same; the name tells you whose kitchen you are in.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days, covered.

Reheating: Warm in a pan, breaking the crumbs up with a fork as it heats.

Serve with: Amasi, Iimbotyi, Chakalaka
