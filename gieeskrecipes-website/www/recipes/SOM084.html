🥬 Mbogga

Mbogga — Isbinaasho

Potato and carrot boiled soft and mashed chunky with a fork, then folded into onion, tomato and spinach cooked down in olive oil — the vegetable side that goes with everything.

Two names, two histories: mbogga is the coastal, Chimwiini-influenced word, and isbinaasho is the Italian spinaci worn down into Somali. The dish is the same either way, and what distinguishes it is the mashing — chunky, with a fork, so you get a rough texture rather than a purée. It is the most substantial vegetable side in the repertoire and it gets eaten with bread, with muufo, with canjeero, with rice, or with fresh pasta, which tells you how flexible it is.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Frying pan, Fork, for mashing

  • 2 potatoes
  • 2 carrots
  • 2 litres water
  • 59 ml (1/4 cup) olive oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) vegetable seasoning
  • 2 tomatoes, peeled and diced
  • 454 g (1 lb) spinach

Method

  1. In a large pot over medium heat, boil the potatoes and carrots in the 2 litres of water for 30 to 45 minutes, until the carrots are fork tender. Lift the carrots out.
  2. Keep boiling the potatoes another 15 to 20 minutes, until fork tender. Cool them, peel them, and mash them chunky with a fork.
  3. Mash the cooled carrots the same way.
  4. In a pan over high heat, sauté the diced onion in the olive oil for 3 minutes.
  5. Add the vegetable seasoning and cook for 3 minutes, then add the tomatoes and cook for 5 minutes.
  6. Add the spinach, cover, and cook for 10 minutes.
  7. Add the mashed potato and carrot, stir well, cover and cook for 5 more minutes.

Chef Tips

  • Mash it chunky with a fork. A masher or a processor turns it into baby food and loses the whole character of the dish.
  • The carrots come out before the potatoes are done. They cook faster, and leaving them in means carrot mush.
  • Boil the potatoes in their skins and peel them after — they hold together better and the skin slips off easily once cooked.
  • Cool them before mashing. Hot potato mashed hard goes gluey.
  • A pound of spinach looks like an absurd amount and cooks down to almost nothing. Trust it.
  • Peel the tomatoes. In a dish this soft, tomato skins are the one thing that stands out unpleasantly.
  • It is a good use for the vegetables at the back of the fridge, and it reheats better than most vegetable sides.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mashing it smooth.
  • Leaving the carrots in with the potatoes for the whole boil.
  • Mashing the potato while it is still hot.
  • Not peeling the tomatoes.
  • Under-cooking the spinach so it stays raw and squeaky in the mash.

Health Benefits

  • Spinach is high in iron, folate and vitamins A and K.
  • Carrots add beta-carotene; potatoes add potassium and vitamin C.
  • Entirely plant-based, with olive oil as the only fat.
  • High in fibre and genuinely filling as a side.

Cultural Note

The two names are the dish's own history written down: mbogga from the Chimwiini-speaking Bravanese coast, isbinaasho from Italian, sitting side by side in one Somali kitchen. Somali food vocabulary is full of this — Arabic, Italian, Swahili and Chimwiini words all naturalised, and none of them feeling foreign to the people using them.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Warm in a covered pan with a splash of water.

Serve with: Canjeero, Muufo, Bariis Iskukaris
