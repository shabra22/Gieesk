🥬 Kenyan Ethiopian Kale

Sukuma Wiki ya Kiethiopia

Kenyan Ethiopian Kale — lacinato kale (also called Ethiopian kale or dinosaur kale) prepared the Kenyan way with onions, tomatoes, garlic, and a simple spice blend. Tender, slightly nutty, and more flavourful than regular sukuma wiki.

Ethiopian kale (Brassica carinata) is a robustly flavoured variety of kale that grows very well in East African conditions and has become increasingly popular in Kenyan urban gardens and markets. Its darker, more intensely flavoured leaves have a slight nuttiness that regular sukuma wiki lacks, and it responds beautifully to the classic Kenyan preparation of onions, tomatoes, and garlic.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pan, Wooden spoon

  • 500g Ethiopian kale (lacinato kale) — stems removed, leaves roughly chopped
  • 2 medium onions — finely chopped
  • 2 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger — optional
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • ¼ cup water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp curry powder — optional

Method

  1. Remove the tough central stems from the kale leaves. Roughly chop the leaves.
  2. Heat oil over medium heat. Cook onions until golden — 6 minutes.
  3. Add garlic (and ginger if using). Cook 30 seconds.
  4. Add tomatoes and curry powder if using. Cook for 5—6 minutes until thick.
  5. Add the kale and water.
  6. Stir to coat with the sauce. Cover and cook for 6—8 minutes until the kale is tender.
  7. Season with salt and pepper. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Ethiopian kale holds its texture better than regular sukuma wiki — it benefits from slightly longer cooking.
  • Massaging the raw leaves with a little salt before cooking breaks down the cell walls and reduces bitterness.
  • Adding lemon juice just before serving brightens the earthy flavour.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Undercooking — Ethiopian kale needs 6—8 minutes minimum to become tender.
  • Not removing the tough stems — they take much longer to cook than the leaves.

The Science

Kale contains tough cell walls reinforced with cellulose. Cooking breaks down these walls progressively — adequate cooking time (6—8 minutes minimum) is needed to achieve a tender result. The glucosinolates that give kale its slight bitterness also partially break down during cooking.

Health Benefits

  • One of the most nutrient-dense vegetables — exceptional levels of vitamins K, C, and A.
  • Rich in calcium and iron.
  • High in antioxidants including lutein and zeaxanthin for eye health.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian kale represents the expanding diversity of traditional Kenyan vegetable cultivation — a robust, highly nutritious variety that is perfectly suited to the Kenyan climate and is increasingly finding its place in Kenyan kitchens alongside sukuma wiki.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh. Refrigerate for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm gently over low heat.

Serve with: Ugali, Beef Stew, Grilled Chicken, Rice, Chapati
