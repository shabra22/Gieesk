Confetti di Sulmona

Sulmona's celebrated sugared almonds, whole almonds coated in layer upon layer of hardened sugar syrup until smooth and glossy, traditionally given at weddings, baptisms, and other major life celebrations.

Achieving confetti's signature smooth, hard sugar shell at home requires patience and repeated thin coatings rather than one thick layer, a simplified version of the specialized rotating-drum technique used by Sulmona's professional confetti makers for centuries.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Wide tray, Candy thermometer

  • 200 g (1½ cups) whole almonds, toasted
  • 400 g (2 cups) granulated sugar
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) water
  • 1 tsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Spread the toasted almonds on a tray and keep warm in a low oven.
  2. Combine the sugar, water, and lemon juice in a heavy saucepan and heat gently, without stirring, until it reaches about 115°C (240°F) on a candy thermometer.
  3. Working quickly, add the warm almonds to the syrup and stir gently to coat.
  4. As the mixture cools and the sugar begins to crystallize and turn cloudy, keep stirring continuously until each almond is coated in a rough sugar layer.
  5. Spread the coated almonds on a tray to cool and harden slightly.
  6. For a smoother finish, repeat the process with a fresh, thin batch of syrup, coating the almonds again for a second, more even layer.
  7. Let dry completely before serving or storing.

Chef Tips

  • Keeping the almonds warm before adding them to the syrup helps the sugar adhere more evenly.
  • Continuous stirring during the crystallization stage is what develops the classic even, opaque sugar coating.
  • True confetti-makers apply many thin coats over hours in a rotating drum — a couple of home-applied layers gets reasonably close without the specialized equipment.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting the sugar syrup go past the right temperature, which can cause it to caramelize rather than crystallize into a proper coating.
  • Not stirring continuously during the coating stage, resulting in an uneven, clumpy result.
  • Rushing the drying time, leaving the almonds sticky rather than properly hardened.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Confetti di Sulmona are a centuries-old specialty of the Abruzzo town of Sulmona, traditionally given as favors at weddings, baptisms, and other major Italian life celebrations.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container at room temperature for several months.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Traditionally given as wedding or celebration favors, not typically eaten as a dessert course
