🍺 Pito

Pito — northern grain beer

Sorghum malted five days, soured to pH 4.25, then fermented with the dambile yeast head at ambient twenty-eight to thirty-two degrees.

Pito is the sorghum beer of northern Ghana and it is a two-stage fermentation: a spontaneous lactic souring followed by an alcoholic fermentation using a yeast head saved from the previous brew. Published here as a documented process because no household recipe with quantities exists. The souring endpoint is specific — the typical sour point of the traditional pito process is pH 4.25 with 0.33 per cent lactic acid. The alcoholic stage runs at ambient twenty-eight to thirty-two degrees from an original extract of thirteen degrees Plato to 4.2 per cent alcohol and a final pH of 3.5. It is drunk while still fermenting.

Ingredients

Equipment: Steeping vessel, Jute sacks, for germination, Drying trays, Mill, Large brewing pot, Straining cloth

  • Red sorghum grain, for malting
  • Water
  • Dambile — the yeast head saved from a previous brew

No household quantities are published. See the note.

    Method

    1. Steep the sorghum 12 to 12.5 hours for the optimised process, or overnight traditionally.
    2. Germinate 5 days at 30 degrees — traditionally 4 to 6 days under a cover of jute sacks, sprinkled with water daily except on the last day.
    3. Dry at 40 degrees for the optimised process, or sun-dry two or more days traditionally.
    4. Grind the malt and mix with water.
    5. Boil 3 to 4 hours.
    6. Leave to undergo spontaneous lactic acid fermentation at ambient temperature, 28 to 32 degrees — about 12 hours.

    SOURING ENDPOINT: pH 4.25 with about 0.33 per cent lactic acid. That is the typical sour point of the traditional process.

    1. Dilute, then cook about 3 hours.
    2. Cool.
    3. Pitch the dambile yeast head from a previous brew and ferment 12 to 24 hours at ambient temperature.

    ALCOHOLIC ENDPOINT: from an original extract of about 13 degrees Plato to about 4.2 per cent alcohol and a final pH of about 3.5.

    1. Drink while still fermenting — the shelf life is 2 to 3 days.

    Chef Tips

    • The souring endpoint is the number to work to: pH 4.25 with about a third of a per cent lactic acid. That is the one hard figure in the traditional process and it comes from a study that measured real brews.
    • Dambile is the yeast head skimmed from the previous brew and carried forward. It is what makes pito a continuous tradition rather than a recipe.
    • Five days' germination at thirty degrees is the optimised figure, against four to six days traditionally under jute sacks. Sprinkle water daily except the last day.
    • Two separate fermentations, and they do different things — the first sours, the second makes alcohol. Skipping the souring gives you something that is not pito.
    • It is drunk while still fermenting and keeps two to three days. There is no finished, stable pito.
    • Finished-product pH across studies runs 3.2 to 3.6 or 3.5 to 5, and alcohol 1.4 to 4 per cent. Those are ranges of what is sold, not targets to brew to.
    • No published Ghanaian source states how brewers judge completion — one review says so explicitly. The pH figures above are the nearest thing to an endpoint anyone has measured.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Skipping the lactic souring stage.
    • Pitching a commercial yeast instead of dambile and expecting the same result.
    • Treating the finished-product pH ranges as brewing targets.
    • Storing it — it does not keep.
    • Watering the malt on the last day of germination.

    Health Benefits

    • A significant source of B vitamins from the yeast and lactic bacteria.
    • Sorghum is gluten-free.
    • Low in alcohol by beer standards at around 2 to 4 per cent.
    • The lactic souring makes the grain's minerals more available.

    Cultural Note

    Pito is drunk from calabashes at pito bars across northern Ghana, lukewarm and still working, and the dambile yeast head carried from brew to brew means a given bar's pito has a continuous microbial lineage. That nobody has published how a brewer knows when it is done — while four separate studies have measured its pH — is a good summary of the gap between research and practice.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 to 3 days, and it is drunk while still fermenting.

    Reheating: Not applicable. Drunk lukewarm.

    Serve with: Drunk on its own, lukewarm, Tuo Zaafi, Grilled Guinea Fowl
