🥣 Lakh-Thiahane au Poisson Sec

Lakh-thiahane — savoury lakh

A very thick savoury millet porridge: an onion browned with dried fish and tomato paste, pounded onions and chilli, then 3 litres of boiling water, ground raw peanuts for a quarter of an hour, and sankhal for another quarter of an hour.

The savoury register of lakh, which the dessert-leaning sources miss. An onion is browned in peanut oil with the dried fish; tomato paste is dissolved in a little water; the two remaining onions are pounded with the chilli and added. Raw peanuts are pounded and sieved into flour. Three litres of boiling water are added, and as soon as it boils again the peanut flour goes in with stirring; cover and cook a quarter of an hour; add the sankhal (millet semolina) and cook another quarter of an hour. Serve lukewarm. It should be very thick, ‘like a sort of soup’ — as printed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Mortar and pestle, Sieve

  • 1 piece of dried fish — quantity not printed
  • 500 g raw peanuts
  • 70 g tomato paste
  • 3 large onions
  • 1 chilli
  • 500 g sankhal or gné-gné (millet semolina)
  • Peanut oil — quantity not printed
  • 3 litres boiling water
  • Salt, pepper

Method

  1. In a pot, brown one sliced onion in peanut oil with the dried fish. Add the tomato paste and dilute with a little water.
  2. In a mortar, pound the other two onions with the chilli to form a paste and add it to the pot.
  3. Pound the raw peanuts and sieve them to get a flour.
  4. Pour 3 litres of boiling water into the pot and salt. As soon as it boils again, add the peanut flour, stirring. Cover and cook for a quarter of an hour.
  5. Add the sankhal and continue cooking another quarter of an hour.
  6. Serve lukewarm, peppered. It should be very thick, like a sort of soup.

Chef Tips

  • The printed cue is ‘dès qu’elle bout de nouveau’ — as soon as it boils again — before adding the peanut flour.
  • Stir continuously when the flour goes in to avoid lumps.
  • The page’s ‘Courir’ in the method is a near-certain misprint for ‘couvrir’ (cover); reported in the sources.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the flour to water that is not boiling.
  • Letting it set cold and thick before serving.
  • Skipping the pounding of the onions into a paste.

Cultural Note

Savoura’s page sits beside her dessert lakh and shows the dish in its savoury form — a thick peanut-millet meal built on dried fish, tomato and chilli.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated; it thickens.

Reheating: Add water and warm gently.

Serve with: Hot pepper, Lemon
