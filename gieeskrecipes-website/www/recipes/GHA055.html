🥘 Garifoto

Garifoto — gari foto, gari jollof

A chef's boarding-school dish — gari dampened with salted water until damp but not soaking wet, then folded through a tomato stew off the heat.

Garifoto is gari turned into a meal, and this version comes from a Ghanaian chef who frames it as made with the provisions boarding-school pupils typically took with them, a comforting dish for difficult times. A quarter teaspoon of salt is dissolved in half a cup of water and sprinkled over a cup and a half of gari until it is damp but not soaking wet. Carrots, green beans and green pepper are blanched. Onion, garlic, ginger, tomatoes, prekese and chilli cook until the stew is developed, the vegetables go in for five minutes, then the heat drops and the gari is folded through.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large frying pan or wok, Small pot, for blanching, Fork, Mixing bowl

  • 1 1/2 cups gari (dried grated cassava) — or cooked couscous or fonio
  • 1/4 tsp salt, plus more for blanching
  • 1/2 cup water, for dampening the gari
  • 60 g carrots, medium dice
  • 60 g green beans, medium dice
  • 60 g green pepper, medium dice
  • 6 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 4 tbsp palm oil, or olive oil
  • 125 g onion, medium dice
  • 1 tsp garlic, minced
  • 2 tsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 340 g tomatoes, seeded and diced
  • 1 tsp dried mushroom, fish or shrimp powder (optional)
  • 1/2 tsp prekese, or allspice
  • 2 tsp minced chilli, or to taste
  • 8 basil leaves, cut into ribbons
  • 4 eggs, cooked as desired

Method

  1. Dissolve the 1/4 teaspoon of salt in 1/2 cup of water and add it to the gari until it is damp but not soaking wet.
  2. Blanch the carrots, green beans and green pepper in salted water.
  3. Sauté the onion in the palm and vegetable oils until softened.
  4. Add the garlic, ginger, tomatoes, powder if using, chilli, salt and prekese, and cook until the stew is developed.
  5. Add the blanched vegetables and cook approximately 5 minutes.
  6. Reduce to low.
  7. Mix in the moistened gari until it is damp but not soaked — it will continue to absorb the sauce.
  8. Turn off the heat.
  9. Serve about one cup of garifoto per portion, topped with the basil ribbons and an egg.

Chef Tips

  • Damp but not soaking wet, twice — once when you dampen the gari and again when you fold it through. Her phrase, and it is the whole technique: gari keeps absorbing, so stop before it looks right.
  • It will continue to absorb the sauce after the heat is off. That is why you turn the heat off rather than cooking it on.
  • Half a cup of salted water to a cup and a half of gari for the initial dampening. Precise, and worth measuring.
  • Blanch the vegetables separately. They keep their colour and bite rather than going soft in the stew.
  • Prekese is the Ghanaian aromatic — Tetrapleura tetraptera, the four-winged fruit. She offers allspice as the substitute, which is not the same but is in the right direction.
  • Couscous or fonio will stand in for gari, by her own note. Fonio in particular behaves similarly.
  • Basil ribbons and an egg on top are not a garnish — they are what turns it from a side into a meal.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-wetting the gari at either stage.
  • Cooking it on after the gari goes in.
  • Boiling the vegetables in the stew.
  • Leaving out the egg.
  • Using plain nutmeg or no aromatic in place of prekese.

Health Benefits

  • Gari is gluten-free and supplies resistant starch.
  • A generous blanched-vegetable load keeps most of its vitamin C.
  • Eggs make it a complete protein.
  • Red palm oil contributes provitamin-A carotenoids.

Cultural Note

Gari keeps without refrigeration, which is why it went to boarding school in a tin and why garifoto exists at all — a hot meal from dry store cupboard goods. That one of Ghana's best-known chefs published it during a lockdown, framed as comfort food for difficult times, is a neat closing of that circle.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. The gari continues to absorb and firms up.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of water, or eat at room temperature.

Serve with: Fried egg, Shito, Red Red
