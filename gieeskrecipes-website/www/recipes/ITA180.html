🍫 Salame di Cioccolato

Salame di Cioccolato

A playful no-bake Italian sweet shaped and sliced to resemble a real salami, combining crushed biscuits, cocoa, and butter, chilled until firm — a beloved family kitchen classic across the country.

Every Italian family seems to have their own version of salame di cioccolato, a no-cook, no-fuss dessert traditionally made by children helping in the kitchen, its rolled log shape and speckled interior deliberately mimicking a cured salami when sliced.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Plastic wrap or parchment paper

  • 250 g (8.8 oz) plain tea biscuits or digestive biscuits, roughly broken
  • 150 g (⅔ cup) unsalted butter, softened
  • 100 g (½ cup) granulated sugar
  • 50 g (⅓ cup) cocoa powder
  • 1 egg
  • 50 g (⅓ cup) chopped walnuts or hazelnuts (optional)
  • 2 tbsp rum or coffee liqueur (optional)
  • Powdered sugar, for dusting

Method

  1. Cream the butter and sugar together until smooth.
  2. Beat in the cocoa powder and egg until well combined.
  3. Stir in the rum or liqueur if using.
  4. Fold in the broken biscuits and nuts, mixing until everything is evenly coated in the chocolate mixture.
  5. Turn the mixture out onto a sheet of plastic wrap or parchment paper.
  6. Shape into a log about 5–6cm in diameter, rolling the wrap tightly around it to compact and smooth the shape.
  7. Twist the ends of the wrap to secure and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight, until firm.
  8. Unwrap, dust generously with powdered sugar to mimic salami's characteristic white coating, and slice into rounds to serve.

Chef Tips

  • Break the biscuits into irregular, varied-sized pieces rather than crumbs, which creates the characteristic marbled cross-section when sliced.
  • Rolling the log tightly in plastic wrap while shaping helps it hold a neat, compact form.
  • This is a wonderful no-bake project to make with children, given how simple and forgiving the process is.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Crushing the biscuits too finely, losing the distinctive marbled texture that makes this dessert visually resemble salami.
  • Not chilling long enough, resulting in a log too soft to slice cleanly.
  • Skipping the powdered sugar dusting, which is what completes the playful salami illusion.

Cultural Note

Salame di cioccolato is a beloved no-bake family tradition found throughout Italy, often one of the first desserts children learn to help make in the kitchen.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, wrapped, for up to 1 week.

Reheating: Not applicable; served chilled.

Serve with: Espresso
