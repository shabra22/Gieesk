🌿 Thiéré Mboum, Millet Couscous with Moringa-Leaf Sauce

Thiéré mboum

Dried millet couscous rehydrated and steamed, topped with a sauce of dried moringa leaves (nébéday), cabbage, three dried-fish seasonings, mutton tripe, tomato paste and ground peanut, finished with nokkos and nététou mix.

A millet-and-leaf-sauce dish of the kind Mbaye lists among the many thiéré variants. Dried moringa leaves are steamed to rehydrate in the top of the couscoussier. In a pot of water go the cabbage, the steamed moringa and three dried-fish items (a guedj toumboulane, a guedj kong and a kéthiath); the sauce is built with tomato paste, then mutton tripe and a piece of mutton fat, thickened with 150 g of ground peanut (guerté noflay) and cooked on low heat. Nokkos and nététou mix go in at the end. Meanwhile the dry thiéré is sprinkled with water, covered with a cloth, rested 10 minutes and given 3 minutes in the microwave; it is spread on a large dish and topped with the mboum sauce, cooked until the sauce turns ‘onctueuse’.

Ingredients

Equipment: Couscoussier or steamer, Large pot, Large serving dish

  • 500 g thiéré (dry millet couscous)
  • 50 g nébéday (dried moringa leaves)
  • 1/4 cabbage
  • 1 guedj toumboulane
  • 1 guedj kong
  • 1 kéthiath
  • 300 g mutton tripe
  • 1 piece of mutton fat
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 150 g ground peanut (guerté noflay)
  • 1 tbsp nokkos
  • 1 tbsp nététou mix
  • Salt — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Cut the cabbage. Steam the dried moringa leaves in the top of the couscoussier to rehydrate them.
  2. Into a pot of water put the cabbage, the steamed moringa and the three dried-fish pieces (guedj toumboulane, guedj kong, kéthiath).
  3. Build the sauce with the tomato paste. Add the tripe and the mutton fat.
  4. Add the ground peanut to thicken and bind. Simmer on low heat until the sauce becomes onctueuse (smooth and rich). Add the nokkos, then the nététou mix. Salt.
  5. Meanwhile sprinkle the dry thiéré with a little water, cover with a cloth and rest 10 minutes; give it 3 minutes in the microwave.
  6. Spread the thiéré on a large dish and top with the mboum sauce.

Chef Tips

  • The sauce’s printed cue is ‘until the sauce becomes onctueuse’ — smooth and rich.
  • Add the nététou last; it is a seasoning, not a bulk ingredient.
  • Dried moringa must be rehydrated first by steaming.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the dried-fish seasonings that give the sauce depth.
  • Cooking at high heat — the page says ‘feu doux’.
  • Adding the nététou early, which loses its character.

Health Benefits

  • Moringa leaves are rich in iron, calcium and vitamin A.

Cultural Note

The source assigns this dish to no region and no ethnic group, so none is claimed here. Mbaye’s site lists thiéré mboum, made with moringa leaves, among the regional thiéré variants.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Sauce 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Reheat the sauce gently; re-steam the couscous.

Serve with: Hot pepper
