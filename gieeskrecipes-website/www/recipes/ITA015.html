🧀 Ricotta Gnocchi

Gnocchi di Ricotta

Ricotta Gnocchi — sometimes called gnudi — are far lighter and quicker to make than their potato cousins, using well-drained ricotta and Parmigiano to create delicate, cloud-like dumplings.

Because there's no need to cook and rice potatoes, ricotta gnocchi comes together in a fraction of the time, and their signature lightness comes down to one crucial step: draining as much moisture from the ricotta as possible before mixing the dough.

Ingredients

Equipment: Fine mesh sieve or cheesecloth, Mixing bowl, Bench scraper

  • 500 g (2 cups) full-fat ricotta, well-drained overnight
  • 100 g (1¼ cups) finely grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1 large egg
  • 120–150 g (1–1¼ cups) all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • ½ tsp fine sea salt
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a pinch

Method

  1. Drain the ricotta in a fine mesh sieve lined with cheesecloth, refrigerated, for at least 4 hours or overnight — this step is essential.
  2. In a bowl, combine the well-drained ricotta, Parmigiano, egg, salt, and nutmeg, mixing gently.
  3. Add the flour a little at a time, folding just until a soft, barely-sticky dough comes together — stop as soon as it holds.
  4. On a floured surface, roll the dough into ropes about 2cm thick.
  5. Cut into pillows about 2cm long.
  6. Bring a large pot of salted water to a gentle boil. Cook the gnocchi in batches until they float to the surface, about 2–3 minutes.
  7. Lift out gently with a slotted spoon — they're more delicate than potato gnocchi — and toss immediately with sauce.

Chef Tips

  • Draining the ricotta thoroughly is the single most important step — skipping it leads to a dough that needs far too much flour and turns dense.
  • Handle the dough as little as possible for the lightest texture.
  • Test-cook one gnocco first; if it falls apart, gently work in a touch more flour before shaping the rest.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping or rushing the ricotta-draining step.
  • Overmixing the dough, which develops gluten and toughens the gnocchi.
  • Boiling too vigorously — a gentle simmer prevents these delicate dumplings from breaking apart.

Health Benefits

  • Higher in protein than potato gnocchi thanks to the ricotta and Parmigiano.
  • A good source of calcium.

Cultural Note

In Tuscany, this style of gnocchi is often called 'gnudi' — meaning 'nude' — a playful nod to the fact that they're essentially the filling of ravioli served without its pasta wrapper.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best cooked fresh the day they're made; uncooked gnocchi can be frozen for up to 1 month.

Reheating: Cook from frozen directly in boiling water — do not thaw first.

Serve with: Sage brown butter, Simple tomato sauce, Pesto alla Genovese
