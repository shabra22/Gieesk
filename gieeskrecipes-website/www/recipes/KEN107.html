🌾 Kenyan Sorghum Ugali

Ugali wa Mtama

Kenyan Sorghum Ugali — made from ground sorghum (mtama) flour, with a distinctive reddish-brown colour, slightly smoky flavour, and excellent nutritional profile. A traditional staple in parts of Western and Eastern Kenya.

Sorghum ugali (ugali wa mtama) is darker and more intensely flavoured than white maize ugali, with a slight smokiness that many who grew up eating it consider deeply comforting. Sorghum has been cultivated in Kenya for thousands of years and remains a vital crop in drier regions where maize does not grow as well. The ugali made from it is denser and more sustaining.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Wooden ugali stick or heavy spoon

  • 2 cups sorghum flour (mtama flour)
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt — optional

Method

  1. Bring 3 cups water to a boil.
  2. Mix sorghum flour with remaining 1 cup cold water to a smooth slurry.
  3. Pour slurry into boiling water while stirring constantly.
  4. Stir vigorously over medium-low heat for 12—15 minutes until thick and firm.
  5. Add more flour if needed to achieve the right consistency.
  6. Shape into a mound. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Sorghum ugali tends to be slightly stickier than maize ugali — a lightly oiled spoon helps with shaping.
  • Sorghum takes slightly longer than maize to fully cook through — allow the full 12—15 minutes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not cooking long enough — the ugali will taste raw and starchy.
  • Too thin — should be firm enough to hold its shape.

The Science

Sorghum contains more tannins than maize, which contribute to its distinctive slightly astringent flavour and reddish colour. These tannins also have antioxidant properties.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in antioxidants from sorghum tannins.
  • Good source of iron and B vitamins.
  • High in dietary fibre.
  • Gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Sorghum has been a vital crop in Kenya for thousands of years, particularly in drier regions. Sorghum ugali remains a proud staple in communities where it has been grown and eaten for generations.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended — make fresh.

Serve with: Sukuma Wiki, Beef Stew, Omena, Managu, Traditional Greens
