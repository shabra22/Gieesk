🍖 Kenyan Roasted Goat Ribs

Mbavu za Mbuzi

Kenyan Roasted Goat Ribs — mbavu za mbuzi rubbed with a simple blend of garlic, ginger, salt, and pepper, then slow-roasted over charcoal until the fat renders, the skin crisps, and the meat falls tenderly from the bone. Pure, honest Kenyan barbecue.

Goat ribs are one of the great Kenyan outdoor cooking experiences. The fat that runs through the ribs bastes the meat as it cooks over charcoal, the edges caramelise and char lightly, and the meat — when given enough time — becomes impossibly tender. Eaten directly from the bone with kachumbari and ugali, it is Kenyan barbecue at its most elemental.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill or jiko, Tongs, Mixing bowl

  • 1.5 kg goat ribs — cut into sections
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Mix garlic, ginger, salt, pepper, paprika, cumin, oil, and lemon juice into a paste.
  2. Rub the paste thoroughly over all surfaces of the goat ribs. Marinate for 1 hour at room temperature or overnight in the refrigerator.
  3. Light the charcoal. Allow to burn to glowing coals with a light layer of white ash — not open flames.
  4. Place the ribs on the grill over medium-low charcoal heat.
  5. Roast for 35—45 minutes, turning every 8—10 minutes, until the outside is browned and slightly charred and the meat is tender.
  6. The ribs are ready when the meat pulls easily from the bone.
  7. Rest for 5 minutes and serve with kachumbari and ugali.

Chef Tips

  • Low and slow is the rule for ribs — cook over medium heat, not high, to render the fat without burning.
  • The ribs are done when the meat begins to pull back from the bone tips.
  • A baste of the leftover marinade (boiled first for safety) applied during the last 10 minutes adds a beautiful caramelised gloss.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking over high heat — burns the outside before the inside is tender.
  • Turning too frequently — prevents proper crust formation.

The Science

Goat ribs contain significant collagen in the connective tissue between and around the bones. Slow roasting at moderate heat converts this collagen to gelatin over time, producing the characteristic fall-off-the-bone tenderness. High heat hardens the protein too quickly for collagen breakdown to occur.

Health Benefits

  • High in protein and rich in collagen from the bone-in preparation.
  • Goat fat has a healthier fatty acid profile than beef or lamb fat.

Cultural Note

Roasted goat ribs are a centrepiece of Kenyan outdoor gatherings, community events, and family celebrations. The smell of goat ribs over charcoal is one of the most evocative aromas in Kenyan outdoor food culture.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat in oven at 180°C for 10 minutes.

Serve with: Kachumbari, White Ugali, Chapati, Pili Pili Sauce, Roasted Sweet Potatoes
