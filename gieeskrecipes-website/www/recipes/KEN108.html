🌾 Kenyan Cassava Ugali

Ugali wa Muhogo

Kenyan Cassava Ugali — ugali made from dried, ground cassava flour. Whiter and slightly softer than maize ugali, with a mild, neutral flavour that pairs beautifully with rich stews and sauces. Popular along the Kenyan coast and in parts of Western Kenya.

Cassava ugali (ugali wa muhogo) is made from dried cassava that has been ground into flour. It is whiter, slightly softer, and milder in flavour than maize ugali, making it an excellent neutral base for strongly flavoured stews and curries. Along Kenya's coast, it is sometimes combined with maize flour for a hybrid ugali that benefits from both.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Wooden spoon

  • 2 cups cassava flour (muhogo flour)
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt — optional

Method

  1. Bring 3 cups of water to a boil.
  2. Mix cassava flour with remaining 1 cup cold water to a smooth slurry.
  3. Stir slurry into boiling water continuously.
  4. Stir over medium-low heat for 10—12 minutes until thick and smooth.
  5. Cassava ugali cooks faster than maize — check consistency after 8 minutes.
  6. Shape and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Cassava ugali is naturally smoother and whiter than maize ugali.
  • It cooks faster — begin checking consistency after 8 minutes.
  • For a hybrid: combine 1 cup cassava flour with 1 cup maize flour for a popular coastal variation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking — cassava ugali can become too sticky if cooked too long.

The Science

Cassava starch granules are very fine and gelatinise quickly, which is why cassava ugali cooks faster than maize ugali. The lack of gluten and the fine starch particles also produce a smoother texture.

Cultural Note

Cassava ugali is a traditional staple along the Kenyan coast where cassava grows abundantly, and in parts of Western Kenya. Its mild flavour makes it an ideal companion for the bold, spiced sauces of coastal cuisine.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: Coconut Fish Curry, Samaki wa Kupaka, Maharagwe ya Nazi, Omena Stew
