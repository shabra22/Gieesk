🍫 Gianduiotto

Gianduiotto

Turin's iconic hazelnut chocolates, their distinctive upside-down boat shape formed by hand or mold while the ganache is still pliable, made from a smooth blend of chocolate and finely ground toasted hazelnuts.

Gianduiotto was born in 19th-century Turin, reportedly created to stretch scarce cacao supplies by blending in the region's abundant hazelnuts, a happy substitution that became the foundation for what is now one of Italy's most beloved chocolate traditions, including the spread it later inspired.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor, Double boiler or heatproof bowl, Parchment paper, Small knife or mold

  • 200 g (7 oz) milk chocolate, chopped
  • 150 g (1 cup) hazelnuts, toasted and skins removed
  • 100 g (1/2 cup) powdered sugar
  • 50 g (3 tbsp) unsalted butter, softened

Method

  1. Grind the toasted hazelnuts in a food processor until they form a smooth, oily paste, scraping down as needed.
  2. Melt the milk chocolate gently over a double boiler.
  3. Combine the melted chocolate with the hazelnut paste, powdered sugar, and softened butter, mixing until smooth and glossy.
  4. Pour the mixture onto a sheet of parchment paper and spread to an even thickness of about 1cm.
  5. Chill in the refrigerator until firm enough to hold a shape but still pliable, about 1-2 hours.
  6. Cut into small triangular or rectangular pieces and shape each by hand into the traditional upside-down boat form, or press into a gianduiotto mold.
  7. Chill again until fully set before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Grinding the hazelnuts thoroughly into a smooth paste, rather than leaving them coarse, is essential for the classic silky gianduiotto texture.
  • Work with the mixture while it is still slightly pliable but firm enough to hold a shape - too cold and it will crack rather than shape neatly.
  • A gianduiotto-shaped mold gives the most authentic look, though hand-shaping works well too.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-grinding the hazelnuts, leaving a gritty rather than silky-smooth texture.
  • Letting the mixture get too cold before shaping, causing it to crack rather than form neatly.
  • Using dark chocolate instead of milk chocolate, which changes the traditional flavor balance.

Cultural Note

Gianduiotto originated in 19th-century Turin and remains one of Piedmont's most cherished chocolate traditions, later inspiring the famous hazelnut-chocolate spread that shares its name.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container in a cool place for up to 2 weeks.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: Espresso
