🥣 Lakh à la Sauce Neuteri

Lakh — neuteuri

Millet semolina rolled into small balls with sprinkled water, cooked in boiling water about a quarter of an hour, spread against the sides of a big dish and filled with dakh or soured milk and the neuteri sauce of pounded baobab and roasted peanut paste.

A festive millet porridge-with-sauce. The ‘neuteri’ sauce is made by pounding the baobab pulp with roasted peanut paste, adding a little hot water to get a thick cream, sieving it, and sweetening it to taste with vanilla sugar and orange blossom. The millet semolina (2 kg) is sprinkled with water and worked into little beads, poured into 4 litres of boiling water and cooked gently about a quarter of an hour. The porridge is then spread against the walls of a large container, leaving a big hollow in the middle, into which the dakh or soured milk is poured, then the neuteri sauce. The quantities are those of a family-festival batch and the page prints no yield.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle, Fine sieve, Large pot, Large dish

  • 2 kg millet semolina (or sankhal)
  • 1 kg roasted peanut paste
  • 1 kg baobab fruit (‘pain de singe’)
  • Sugar — quantity not printed
  • 2 sachets vanilla sugar
  • A few drops of orange blossom water
  • 4 tbsp dakh or soured milk (lait caillé)
  • 4 litres boiling water, for cooking the beads

Method

    Neuteri sauce

    1. Pound the baobab and add the peanut paste while continuing to mix and pound. Add a little hot water to obtain a thick cream and pass it through a sieve. This is the neuteri sauce.
    2. Sweeten the sauce to taste, then add the vanilla sugar and the orange blossom.

    Lakh

    1. Make small beads with the millet semolina sprinkled with water.
    2. Pour the beads into 4 litres of boiling water and cook gently for about a quarter of an hour.
    3. Pour the porridge into a large dish and spread it against the sides, leaving a big hollow in the centre. Pour the dakh or soured milk into the hollow, then the neuteri sauce.

    Chef Tips

    • The only texture target printed is the sauce: ‘une crème épaisse’, a thick cream.
    • The page gives time only for the millet: about a quarter of an hour, with no cue for when the beads are done.
    • Soured milk is a bought ingredient here; its souring is not documented on this page (see Dakh, Soured-Milk Butter for dakh).

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding too much water to the baobab-peanut paste and losing the cream.
    • Not sieving the sauce.
    • Cooking the beads at a hard boil rather than ‘doucement’.

    Health Benefits

    • Baobab pulp is high in vitamin C and fibre.
    • Millet is gluten-free.

    Cultural Note

    Spelling note, as printed: the title says ‘neuteri’ and the method says ‘neuteuri’. The lakh/fondé/araw taxonomy comes from a Gret/Enda Graf paper (Broutin, François, Sokona, Tandia, Touré, 2005): fondé is a thinner araw-granule porridge served with lait caillé, lakh a more substantial one.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best served fresh; sauce 2 days refrigerated.

    Reheating: Serve cool or at room temperature.

    Serve with: Dakh, Sweet tea
