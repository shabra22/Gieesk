🥥 Tanzanian Coconut Rice

Wali wa Nazi

Tanzanian Wali wa Nazi — long-grain rice cooked in rich coconut milk until every grain is fragrant, creamy, and perfectly separate. The defining rice dish of the Tanzanian Swahili coast and Zanzibar — the foundation of every coastal celebration table.

Wali wa Nazi is the rice that defines coastal Tanzanian cooking — every grain perfumed with coconut milk, slightly glossy, and incomparably fragrant. In Zanzibar, no feast is complete without it. The secret is the ratio of coconut milk to water and the absorption method that seals all the fragrance inside.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan with tight lid

  • 3 cups long-grain rice — washed until water runs clear
  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Combine coconut milk, water, salt, and oil in a heavy saucepan. Bring to a boil.
  2. Add washed rice. Stir once. Return to a boil.
  3. Reduce to the lowest possible heat. Cover tightly.
  4. Cook for 18—20 minutes without lifting the lid.
  5. Remove from heat. Rest covered for 10 minutes.
  6. Fluff gently with a fork. Serve.

Chef Tips

  • Never lift the lid during cooking — the steam is what cooks the rice evenly.
  • Thick coconut milk gives a richer result than coconut milk from a carton.
  • The resting period is essential — it allows the steam to finish cooking the top grains.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Lifting the lid — steam escapes and rice is undercooked.
  • Too much coconut milk — makes the rice sticky and dense.

The Science

The absorption method traps steam in the covered pot — the rice cooks through both absorbed liquid and steam condensation. Coconut milk fat coats the starch granules, producing the characteristic separate, glossy grains.

Cultural Note

Wali wa Nazi is the rice of celebration, ceremony, and coastal life in Tanzania. In Zanzibar, it is served at every wedding, Eid feast, and important gathering — its fragrance alone signals that something special is happening.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Steam briefly or microwave with a damp cloth.

Serve with: Mchuzi wa Samaki, Kuku wa Kupaka, Maharage ya Nazi, Pilau ya Kienyeji
