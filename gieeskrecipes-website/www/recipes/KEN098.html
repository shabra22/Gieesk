🍛 Kenyan Vegetable Pilau

Pilau ya Mboga

Kenyan Vegetable Pilau — fragrant basmati rice cooked with deeply caramelised onions, whole pilau spices, and a colourful mix of carrots, peas, and potatoes. All the aromatic celebration character of classic pilau, completely plant-based.

Vegetable pilau proves that the magic of Kenyan pilau lies entirely in the technique — the deeply caramelised onions, the whole spices, the stock — not in the meat. Replace the meat with hearty vegetables and you get something equally aromatic, equally satisfying, and accessible to everyone at the table. It has become a staple in urban Kenyan homes where vegetarian cooking is increasingly popular.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot with lid, Wooden spoon

  • 2 cups (400g) basmati rice — rinsed and soaked 30 minutes
  • 2 medium carrots — diced
  • 1 cup frozen peas
  • 2 medium potatoes — cubed
  • 1 red bell pepper — diced
  • 3 large onions — thinly sliced
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 4 cups vegetable stock
  • 4 cardamom pods — crushed
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 4 cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 6 peppercorns
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Heat oil in a large pot. Cook onions for 18—20 minutes until deeply golden — the same caramelisation as meat pilau.
  2. Add whole spices. Fry 1 minute.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes and turmeric. Cook for 8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add carrots and potatoes. Stir and cook for 3 minutes.
  6. Add drained rice, peas, and bell pepper. Stir once to combine.
  7. Add vegetable stock. Bring to a boil.
  8. Reduce to lowest heat. Cover tightly and cook undisturbed for 20 minutes.
  9. Rest covered for 10 minutes. Fluff and garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Deeply caramelised onions are as important here as in meat pilau — 18—20 minutes minimum.
  • Add peas and bell pepper with the rice — they are delicate and need only the residual heat.
  • Use a good vegetable stock rather than plain water — the flavour difference is significant.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-caramelising the onions — the pilau will taste flat.
  • Overcooking the vegetables — they should have some texture.

The Science

Vegetables release steam during the covered cooking phase that contributes to cooking the rice from above. Root vegetables like carrots and potatoes are added earlier because their denser cell structure requires more heat to soften than the rice or delicate peas.

Health Benefits

  • High in dietary fibre from the variety of vegetables.
  • Complex carbohydrates from rice and potatoes.
  • Rich in vitamins A and C from carrots and peppers.
  • Completely plant-based and cholesterol-free.

Cultural Note

Vegetable pilau has emerged as an important dish in modern Kenyan cooking — honouring the celebrated technique of the Swahili pilau tradition while making it accessible to vegetarians and those seeking lighter celebration food.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days. Freeze for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Sprinkle with water and reheat gently.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Natural Yogurt, Coconut Chutney, Fried Plantains, Kuku wa Nazi
