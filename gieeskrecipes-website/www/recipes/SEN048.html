🐟 Thieboudienne, Stuffed Fish with Basmati

Thiéboudienne — Saveur version

Eight 4-oz grouper or snapper fillets, each slit and stuffed with a parsley-garlic-chile mix, simmered in a tomato-paste broth and then lifted out while 4 cups of basmati cook in the sauce.

The Saveur May 2012 rendering fills fillets rather than steaks and gets its depth from fish sauce and tamarind paste rather than from guedj and yeet. Onions and green pepper cook about ten minutes in oil, a 12-oz can of tomato paste another ten, then six cups of stock; the stuffed fillets poach about 18 minutes and are lifted out. Carrots, aubergine, turnip and cassava cook about 40 minutes and are lifted out; dried white hibiscus (optional), tamarind paste and fish sauce go in for five. Four cups of basmati rice are stirred into the broth, covered on low about 45 minutes, and the plates are assembled with lime.

Ingredients

Equipment: 8-quart Dutch oven, Paring knife, Slotted spoon

    Fish and stuffing

    • 1/4 cup finely chopped parsley
    • 2 tsp crushed red chile flakes
    • 6 cloves garlic, minced
    • 2 scallions, minced
    • 1/4 small yellow onion, minced
    • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper — quantities not printed
    • 8 (4-oz) fillets grouper or red snapper

    Thiéboudienne

    • 1/2 cup canola or palm oil
    • 2 medium yellow onions, roughly chopped
    • 1 medium green bell pepper, stemmed, seeded, roughly chopped
    • 1 (12-oz) can tomato paste
    • 6 cups fish or vegetable stock
    • 6 small carrots, halved crosswise
    • 1 large eggplant, cut into large chunks, or 4 small Thai eggplants
    • 1 medium turnip, peeled and cut into 12 wedges
    • 1/2 cassava root, peeled and cut into 1 1/2-inch chunks
    • 1/3 cup dried white hibiscus flowers (optional)
    • 2 tbsp tamarind paste
    • 2 tbsp fish sauce
    • 4 cups basmati rice
    • Lime wedges — quantity not printed

    Method

    1. Mix the parsley, chile flakes, garlic, scallions, onion, salt and pepper. Cut a 2-inch lengthwise slit in each fillet with a paring knife, stuff with the mixture and set aside.
    2. Heat the oil in an 8-quart Dutch oven over medium. Cook the onions and green pepper, stirring, until softened — about 10 minutes.
    3. Add the tomato paste and cook, stirring occasionally, until the paste is lightly browned — about 10 minutes. Add the stock and bring to a boil.
    4. Reduce to medium-low and add the fillets. Cook until just cooked through — about 18 minutes. Remove with a slotted spoon to a plate and cover to keep warm.
    5. Add the carrots, eggplant, turnip and cassava. Cook, stirring occasionally, until tender and very soft — about 40 minutes. Transfer the vegetables to a bowl with a slotted spoon and keep warm.
    6. Add the hibiscus if using, the tamarind paste and the fish sauce. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the hibiscus flowers soften — about 5 minutes.
    7. Add the rice and stir to combine. Reduce to low and cook covered until tender — about 45 minutes. Remove from the heat and fluff with a fork.
    8. Divide the fish, vegetables and rice among plates. Serve with lime wedges.

    Chef Tips

    • ‘Until the vegetables are very soft and the paste is lightly browned’ is the colour-and-texture cue for the long middle stage.
    • Fish are lifted out once ‘just cooked through’; they would fall apart in the 45-minute rice stage.
    • Fish sauce is the only fermented product — added with the tamarind and hibiscus, 2 tablespoons, with no further handling.
    • Compare Thiebou Jenn: this version uses fillets rather than steaks, no head or tail, fish sauce rather than guedj and yeet, and a 12-oz can of paste rather than 2 cups.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Leaving the fish in for the rice stage.
    • Not stirring the rice in evenly.
    • Using hot water instead of the hot broth.

    Cultural Note

    This magazine rendering replaces guedj and yeet with fish sauce and tamarind paste. No individual cook is credited on the recipe page.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

    Reheating: Steam gently with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Lime wedges
