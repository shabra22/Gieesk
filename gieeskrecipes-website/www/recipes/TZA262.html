🐐 Iraqw Goat and Roots Stew

Mchuzi wa Mbuzi na Mizizi — Waaraqw

Iraqw Goat and Roots Stew — goat slow-cooked with yams, turnips, and local roots in the Iraqw tradition of the Mbulu Highlands. A highland stew of great depth — the goat provides protein and bone broth, the roots provide starch and sweetness, and the altitude provides a natural pressure that makes everything more tender.

Iraqw goat and root stew is a highland dish made for the cool evenings of the Mbulu plateau — the combination of goat (the Iraqw's primary meat animal) with the root crops that grow well at altitude produces a deeply nourishing, warming meal.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot

  • 800g young goat — bone-in
  • 300g yam or sweet potato — cubed
  • 200g turnips — cubed
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 3 cups water
  • Fresh herbs

Method

  1. Brown goat in a dry pot (no oil — the fat from the goat is sufficient).
  2. Add onions. Cook 3 minutes.
  3. Add water, salt, and pepper.
  4. Simmer covered on very low heat for 60 minutes.
  5. Add root vegetables. Simmer 25—30 more minutes until everything is very tender.
  6. Garnish with fresh herbs.

Chef Tips

  • The Iraqw cook without oil — the goat's own fat renders out to cook the onions.
  • The root vegetables should be falling-soft.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding oil — not traditional.
  • Under-cooking roots.

Health Benefits

  • Goat is very lean.
  • Root vegetables provide complex carbohydrate and vitamins.
  • High collagen broth from bone-in goat.

Cultural Note

Iraqw goat stew is the highland evening meal — cooked as the cool air descends from the Mbulu escarpment, eaten as a family around the fire. The Iraqw's Cushitic heritage is reflected in their emphasis on goat over cattle — the same preference found across the Horn of Africa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Ugali wa Mtama (TZA244), With fermented milk (TZA253) as drink
