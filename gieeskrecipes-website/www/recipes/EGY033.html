🍲 Fattah (Egyptian Rice, Crisp Bread and Meat with Garlic-Vinegar and Tomato Sauces)

Fattah — فتة

Layers of baked pita, buttery rice, garlic-vinegar broth and tender mastic-scented beef or lamb, finished with a spiced tomato sauce.

3h 30mTotal time
30mPrep
3hCook
6Servings
780Calories
HardDifficulty

Beef or lamb is browned with melted mastic and simmered with onion, carrot, celery, tomato and spices until done; the card gives a 3-hour cook time. Rice is cooked in ghee and hot broth for 13 to 15 minutes, pita squares are baked at 350°F for 10 to 12 minutes until crisp, a garlic-vinegar sauce is made, and a tomato sauce simmers 10 minutes. It is assembled as bread, white garlic sauce, rice, meat and tomato sauce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Medium pot with lid, Baking sheet, Saucepans

    Meat

    • 1 Tablespoon ghee/unsalted butter.
    • 2-3 mastic pieces.
    • 2 pounds beef/lamb cut into 2-inch squares.
    • 2 bay leaves.
    • ¼ teaspoon ground cardamom.
    • ¼ teaspoon sugar.
    • ½ teaspoon black pepper.
    • 1 medium onion quartered.
    • 1 (100g) Roma tomato quartered.
    • 2 (70g)celery stalks, cubes almost half a cup.
    • 1 garlic clove.
    • 1 large (60g) carrot cut into chunks.
    • Boiling water to cover.
    • 1 teaspoon salt I've used pink salt.

    Rice

    • 1 Tablespoon ghee or unsalted butter.
    • 1 Tablespoon olive oil.
    • 2 cups (400g) short grain or jasmine rice.
    • 2 ¼ cup hot water/beef broth or a mixture.
    • Salt to taste.

    Sauces

    • 1 Tablespoon ghee/unsalted butter.
    • 1 Tablespoon olive oil.
    • 5 large garlic cloves minced.
    • ¼ cup white vinegar.
    • 2 cups tomato sauce.
    • 2 Tablespoons tomato paste.
    • 1 teaspoon 7-spice blend.
    • Salt and pepper to taste.
    • 3 cups beef/lamb broth divided.
    • 6 Pita bread loaves. more or less depending on your liking. cut into 1 inch squares.

    Method

    1. Melt the ghee, stir in the mastic until it melts, and brown the meat. Add the bay, sugar, pepper, cardamom and vegetables, cover with boiling water and skim. Simmer loosely covered until done; add the salt in the last 5 minutes.
    2. Rice: stir the rice in the fat for 2 to 3 minutes, add the hot liquid and cook for 13 to 15 minutes.
    3. Pita: cut into 1-inch squares and bake at 350°F for 10 to 12 minutes, until the edges and bottoms are crisp and golden brown.
    4. Garlic sauce: cook the garlic in the fat for 30 seconds to 1 minute until fragrant and lightly browned, add the vinegar for 3 to 4 minutes, and reserve about half the garlic mix. For the tomato sauce add the tomato sauce, 7-spice, paste and 1 cup of broth to the pan and simmer 10 minutes to thicken.
    5. White sauce: boil the rest of the broth with the reserved garlic-vinegar mix.
    6. Optional: brown the cooked meat in a covered skillet. Assemble: pita, about 1 cup of white sauce, rice, meat and tomato sauce.

    Chef Tips

    • Cues: pita edges and bottoms crisp and golden; garlic lightly browned; sauce thickened. The meat time is only 'until done'; the card says 3 hours total cook time.
    • The body says the sauce can be made 2 days ahead; the card says to reserve half the garlic while the body says half the garlic-vinegar mixture.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Letting the garlic burn.
    • Assembling too early so the bread goes fully soft if you want crispy fattah (the author says her family prefers crispy; some say it should be soggy).

    Cultural Note

    Amira Ibrahim calls fattah a traditional Egyptian food to celebrate almost any occasion, especially Eid al-Adha, and says molokhia is commonly served with it. Nermine Mansour adds that Egyptian fatta, unlike the Levantine one, has no yogurt.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Page: the sauce can be made 2 days ahead; assemble just before serving.

    Reheating: Reheat the meat and sauces separately, then assemble.

    Serve with: Molokhia
