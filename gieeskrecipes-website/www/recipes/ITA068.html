Cavatelli con Cime di Rapa

Small, ridged shell-shaped pasta from Puglia, tossed with bitter cime di rapa (broccoli rabe), garlic, anchovy, and chili in olive oil for one of the region's most defining rustic dishes.

Cavatelli, hand-formed by dragging small pieces of dough with the fingers to curl them into little shells, are a staple of Puglian home cooking, and their pairing with peppery, slightly bitter cime di rapa is one of the most iconic combinations in all of southern Italian cuisine.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Small knife for shaping, Large pot, Large sauté pan

  • 400 g (3 cups) semolina flour
  • 180 ml (¾ cup) warm water, plus more as needed
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 large bunch cime di rapa (broccoli rabe), tough stems trimmed
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 4 anchovy fillets (optional for a vegan version)
  • 1 dried chili, crumbled
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Mix the semolina, water, and salt into a firm dough, kneading for 10 minutes until smooth. Rest, covered, for 20 minutes.
  2. Pinch off small pieces of dough and drag each across a work surface with a fingertip to curl it into a small shell shape; set the cavatelli on a floured tray.
  3. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil, add the cime di rapa, and cook for 2 minutes, then scoop out with a slotted spoon, leaving the water boiling.
  4. Add the cavatelli to the same water and cook until they float and are tender, about 4–5 minutes.
  5. Meanwhile, warm the olive oil in a large pan and gently sauté the garlic, anchovy, and chili until the anchovy dissolves into the oil.
  6. Add the blanched cime di rapa to the pan and toss to coat, breaking it up slightly with a spoon.
  7. Drain the cavatelli, reserving some cooking water, and add to the pan with a splash of the water, tossing everything together over heat for a minute to combine.
  8. Season to taste and serve immediately, with a drizzle of good olive oil.

Chef Tips

  • Semolina dough is stiffer than egg pasta dough — knead persistently until it truly comes together.
  • Do not discard the vegetable cooking water — using the same pot for the pasta infuses a little of its bitterness and flavor.
  • Anchovy should melt into the oil rather than remain in visible pieces, seasoning the dish rather than announcing itself.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the cime di rapa, which loses its pleasant bitterness and turns dull and mushy.
  • Making the dough too soft, leaving cavatelli that lose their shape or turn gummy when boiled.
  • Skipping the chili, which is what balances the bitter greens and salty anchovy.

Health Benefits

  • Cime di rapa is rich in vitamins A, C, and K, along with fiber.
  • A largely plant-based dish that can be made fully vegan by omitting the anchovy.

Cultural Note

This dish is considered one of the truest expressions of Puglian cucina povera — humble, resourceful cooking built from durum wheat and whatever bitter greens grew abundantly in the region.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Dried, uncooked cavatelli keep for a week; the finished dish is best eaten fresh.

Reheating: Gently in a pan with a splash of olive oil or water.

Serve with: Grated pecorino, though traditionally this dish is served without cheese
