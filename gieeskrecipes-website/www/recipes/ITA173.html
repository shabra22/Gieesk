🫓 Farinata di Ceci

Farinata

A thin, baked Genoese flatbread made simply from chickpea flour, water, and olive oil, cooked in a wide copper pan until the edges turn crisp and blistered.

Farinata's simplicity is deceptive — just three ingredients, but achieving the right balance of a custardy interior and crisp, blistered edge depends heavily on a traditional wide, shallow copper pan and a very hot oven.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide, shallow baking pan (traditionally copper), Whisk

  • 250 g (2 cups) chickpea flour
  • 750 ml (3 cups) water
  • 10 g (1¾ tsp) salt
  • 100 ml (scant ½ cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus more for the pan
  • Freshly ground black pepper

Method

  1. Whisk the chickpea flour into the water gradually, working out any lumps.
  2. Whisk in the salt and let the batter rest, uncovered, at room temperature for at least 2 hours, skimming off any foam that rises to the surface.
  3. Preheat the oven to its highest setting, ideally with a pizza stone if available.
  4. Generously oil a wide, shallow baking pan and pour in a portion of the batter to a thickness of about 5mm.
  5. Drizzle a little more olive oil over the surface.
  6. Bake for 15–20 minutes until the edges turn deeply golden and crisp and the surface is set with some blistering.
  7. Season generously with black pepper.
  8. Cut into wedges and serve immediately while hot.

Chef Tips

  • Letting the batter rest for the full time helps the chickpea flour hydrate properly and improves the final texture.
  • A very hot oven is essential for achieving the characteristic crisp, blistered edges.
  • A wide, shallow pan produces a thin, properly textured farinata — a deep pan will give an entirely different, less traditional result.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the resting time, which can leave a grainy rather than smooth texture.
  • Making the layer too thick, resulting in a dense, custardy result rather than the traditional thin, crisp-edged farinata.
  • Using an oven that isn't hot enough, leaving the farinata pale and soft rather than properly golden and crisp.

Health Benefits

  • Chickpea flour provides plant-based protein and fiber, and the dish is naturally vegan and gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Farinata is a beloved Genoese street food, sold in dedicated farinata shops throughout the city, often eaten as a quick snack folded into a piece of focaccia.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh and hot; does not hold or reheat particularly well.

Reheating: Reheat briefly in a hot oven to help re-crisp.

Serve with: A glass of Ligurian white wine, As a snack on its own
