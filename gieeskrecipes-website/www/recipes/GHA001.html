🥣 Fufu

Fufu — pounded cassava and plantain

Cassava and green plantain boiled to fork-tender and pounded in a mortar — the plantain first, then the cassava, then the two together.

Fufu is the swallow the forest zone is built on, and it is pounded rather than stirred. Cassava and green plantain are peeled, the cassava core and the plantain seeds removed, boiled about thirty minutes until fork-tender, then pounded — plantain to a smooth dough first, cassava after, the two brought together and moulded into a ball. The technical literature is clearer than the recipes about what you are aiming for: the boiled starch must be pulverised into a wet powdery form before it binds itself into fufu. No named source publishes weights, pounding times or a pounding cue, and that gap is stated rather than filled.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Wooden mortar (waduro) and pestle (woma), Bowl of water, for wetting the hands, Knife

  • 1 medium cassava tuber
  • 2 fingers green plantain
  • Water, enough to just cover the cassava and plantain
  • 1 tsp salt (optional)

Method

  1. Peel and slice the cassava and plantain, removing the cassava core and the plantain seeds.
  2. Cover with water and cook over medium heat 30 minutes, until fork-tender.
  3. Pound the plantain first, to a smooth dough.
  4. Pound the cassava separately.
  5. Combine the two and pound together until the mass binds into itself and pulls away clean.
  6. Wet your hands and mould into a smooth ball.
  7. Serve in a bowl with soup poured around it.

Chef Tips

  • Fork-tender is the boiling cue, and it is the only one published. Undercooked cassava will not pulverise and you will pound forever.
  • Plantain first, cassava second, then together. Pounding them from the start gives an uneven mass.
  • The technique cue worth knowing, from a KNUST engineering paper on mechanising the job: the boiled starch must be pulverised into a wet powdery form BEFORE it binds itself into fufu. If you are pounding a lump rather than breaking it down, you are too early.
  • Wet hands, not oiled. Water stops it sticking without changing the surface.
  • Raw cassava contains toxins, so it is never tasted raw and never undercooked — the source says so plainly.
  • Fufu is not chewed. It is pinched off, dipped in soup and swallowed, which is why the texture matters more than the flavour.
  • No source gives weights, a pounding time or a pounding cue. The ratio above is one tuber to two plantain fingers as printed; adjust toward more plantain for a softer ball.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling until soft rather than fork-tender, which gives a wet, slack fufu.
  • Leaving the woody cassava core in.
  • Pounding both starches together from the start.
  • Dry hands when moulding.
  • Serving it in pieces to be chewed rather than as a ball to be pinched and swallowed.

Health Benefits

  • Gluten-free and fat-free as made.
  • Green plantain is high in resistant starch, which ferments in the colon rather than spiking blood sugar.
  • Cassava supplies vitamin C and manganese.
  • No oil, salt or sugar beyond the optional teaspoon.

Cultural Note

The sound of fufu being pounded at dusk is one of the recognisable sounds of a Ghanaian town, and Osseo-Asare's line about the pestle thumping into the mortar is the best description of it in print. It is eaten with the right hand, pinched off, dipped and swallowed without chewing — a texture-led food in which a lump or a sour note is immediately obvious.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten at once. It stiffens and cracks within a few hours and does not refrigerate well.

Reheating: Not recommended. Fufu is made to order.

Serve with: Light Soup, Groundnut Soup, Palm Nut Soup
