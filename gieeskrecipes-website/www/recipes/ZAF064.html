🎃 Pampoenkoekies

Pampoenkoekies

Pumpkin steamed until soft and drained properly, folded into no more than a cup of flour per two cups of pumpkin, fried in a centimetre of oil until bubbles show on top, then buried in cinnamon sugar.

Pampoenkoekies sit in the odd place between a side dish and a pudding, which is exactly where South Africans eat them — alongside a roast, or after it, or both. The ratio is the whole argument: published recipes run from seven parts pumpkin to one of flour all the way down to one to one, and the useful rule is a ceiling rather than a quantity, no more than one cup of flour per two cups of pumpkin. Draining the steamed pumpkin properly is the other thing nobody mentions and everybody needs.

Ingredients

Equipment: Steamer, Colander, Mixing bowl, Heavy frying pan, Paper towel

    The fritters

    • 450 g pumpkin, steamed, drained and cooled
    • 2 eggs
    • 60 g cake flour
    • 5 ml (1 heaped tsp) baking powder
    • A pinch of salt
    • A pinch of nutmeg, freshly grated
    • A pinch of naartjie peel, grated (optional)
    • Oil, for shallow frying
    • A knob of butter

    The cinnamon sugar

    • 125 ml (1/2 cup) sugar
    • 5 ml (1 tsp) ground cinnamon

    Method

    1. Steam the pumpkin until soft, 40 to 50 minutes. Drain it thoroughly in a colander and let it cool.
    2. Combine the cake flour, baking powder, salt and eggs in a bowl.
    3. Add the drained pumpkin, the nutmeg and the naartjie peel if you are using it.
    4. Mix gently — do not overmix.
    5. Mix the sugar and cinnamon together for the topping.
    6. Heat about 1 cm of oil in the pan with a knob of butter, over medium heat.
    7. Fry spoonfuls of the batter until bubbles form on the top, then flip and fry the other side until cooked through — about 1 to 2 minutes a side.
    8. Drain on paper towel and sprinkle generously with cinnamon sugar while still hot.

    Chef Tips

    • Drain the pumpkin properly. Wet pumpkin makes a batter that will not hold together and tempts you into adding flour, which is how pampoenkoekies turn into pancakes.
    • The ratio rule worth keeping: no more than one cup of flour per two cups of pumpkin. Less flour gives a softer, more custardy fritter, which is the point of them.
    • Wait for the bubbles. It is a more reliable cue than a timer, since the batter thickness varies every time.
    • No published source gives an oil temperature for these — the best available guidance is medium heat, about a centimetre of oil, one to two minutes a side.
    • Cinnamon sugar while hot, not after they have cooled. It needs the steam to stick.
    • A caramel sauce instead of cinnamon sugar is equally traditional — melt 100 g sugar, stir in 125 g butter, then 250 ml cream, and bring to the boil with a pinch of salt.
    • Some recipes use raw finely grated pumpkin instead of cooked and mashed. It gives a firmer, more textured fritter and is a real alternative, not a mistake.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not draining the steamed pumpkin.
    • Adding flour until the batter feels manageable, which makes them heavy.
    • Overmixing.
    • Frying them too hot, so they colour before the centre sets.
    • Sugaring them once they have cooled, when it just falls off.

    Health Benefits

    • Pumpkin is very high in beta-carotene and vitamin A.
    • Eggs add protein and the flour content is genuinely low.
    • Fried and sugared, so treat them as what they are.

    Cultural Note

    That pampoenkoekies get served both with the roast and after it is one of the more charming confusions in South African eating, and it goes back to a farm table where the pumpkin was what there was a lot of. Boerpampoen, the big grey farm pumpkin, is the right one — its flesh is drier and sweeter than the watery orange varieties.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately. Refrigerate up to 2 days.

    Reheating: Refresh in a hot dry pan or a moderate oven — never a microwave, which makes them soggy.

    Serve with: A Sunday roast, as a side, Caramel sauce, as a pudding, Boereboontjies
