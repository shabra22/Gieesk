🦐 Cappon Magro

Cappon Magro

An elaborate Genoese layered salad tower of hardtack, vegetables, fish, and shellfish, bound with a green herb sauce, historically a lavish showpiece dish for wealthy shipping families.

Cappon magro was born as an impressive display dish for Genoa's affluent maritime merchant families, layering the bounty of both sea and land into a towering, architectural salad meant as much to impress visually as to feed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large serving platter, Blender or mortar and pestle

  • 6 slices stale hardtack or crusty bread
  • 1 clove garlic
  • Red wine vinegar, for moistening
  • 400 g (14 oz) firm white fish, poached and flaked
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) mixed shellfish (shrimp, mussels), cooked
  • 2 potatoes, boiled and sliced
  • 2 carrots, boiled and sliced
  • 200 g (7 oz) green beans, blanched
  • 1 beet, boiled and sliced
  • 80 g (3 cups) fresh parsley
  • 2 anchovy fillets
  • 1 clove garlic, for the sauce
  • 2 tbsp capers
  • 1 hard-boiled egg yolk
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • Hard-boiled eggs and olives, for garnish

Method

  1. Rub the bread slices with the garlic clove and lightly moisten with vinegar diluted in water.
  2. Arrange the bread slices as a base layer on a large serving platter.
  3. For the green sauce, blend the parsley, anchovy, garlic, capers, hard-boiled egg yolk, olive oil, and vinegar until smooth.
  4. Spread a layer of the green sauce over the bread base.
  5. Layer the boiled vegetables — potato, carrot, green beans, beet — each lightly dressed with a little of the sauce, building upward.
  6. Add a layer of the flaked fish and shellfish.
  7. Continue layering vegetables and seafood, using the green sauce between layers to bind everything together.
  8. Finish the tower with a final drizzle of sauce and garnish generously with hard-boiled egg slices and olives.
  9. Chill briefly before serving, sliced into wedges.

Chef Tips

  • This dish is meant to be visually impressive — take care building neat, distinct layers as you assemble it.
  • The green sauce should be used generously between layers, as it's what holds the whole assembly together and unifies the flavors.
  • This is traditionally a special-occasion dish, well worth the effort for a genuine celebration.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the assembly, resulting in a messy rather than clean, layered presentation.
  • Being too sparing with the green sauce, leaving the layers under-seasoned and poorly bound.
  • Overcooking the seafood, which can make it tough within the finished dish.

Cultural Note

Cappon magro was historically a lavish display dish among Genoa's wealthy maritime merchant families, its architectural presentation meant to showcase both culinary skill and abundance.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, assembled, for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Not applicable; served cold.

Serve with: A crisp Ligurian white wine
