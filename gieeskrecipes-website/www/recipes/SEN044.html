🌾 Salted Millet Couscous of Fadiouth

Couscous salé de mil de Fadiouth

Ready-to-steam salted millet couscous moistened with warm water, rested 20 minutes, steamed 20, and finished with a spoonful of baobab-leaf powder.

The couscous of the shell island of Fadiouth, in the booklet’s one explicitly Seerer entry. A kilo of salted couscous made from sunnà millet is moistened with warm water, rested 20 minutes, steamed 20 minutes, and — off the heat — stirred with a tablespoon of baobab-leaf powder. It is eaten fresh with a sauce of mangrove flowers, peanuts and meat or seafood; the booklet names the sauce but gives no recipe for it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Couscous steamer (couscoussier), Bowl

  • 1 kg ready-to-cook salted millet couscous
  • 1 tbsp baobab leaf powder
  • Warm water — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Moisten the couscous with warm water and let it rest for 20 minutes.
  2. Steam for 20 minutes.
  3. Off the heat, stir in the baobab leaf powder.
  4. Serve with a sauce of your choice — the traditional sauce of mangrove flowers, peanuts and meat or seafood is not given in the source.

Chef Tips

  • Water is ‘tiède’ — warm, not hot.
  • No doneness cue is printed; use the 20-minute steam as the guide and check the grains are tender and separate.
  • Baobab leaf powder goes in off the heat so it keeps its colour and flavour.
  • The booklet says Fadiouth couscous is sold and eaten locally and most often fresh.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using hot water to moisten.
  • Adding the baobab powder while steaming.

Health Benefits

  • Millet is a gluten-free whole grain rich in magnesium.
  • Baobab leaves add calcium and iron.

Cultural Note

‘The Seerer, the indigenous community who live on the island, have always been the best producers of sunnà millet,’ says the booklet of Fadiouth, an island built of shells. It is the only dish in this round with an explicit Seerer attribution printed by the source.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best fresh; 1 day refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam again for 5–10 minutes.

Serve with: Sauce of mangrove flowers, groundnuts and meat or seafood (no recipe in the source)
