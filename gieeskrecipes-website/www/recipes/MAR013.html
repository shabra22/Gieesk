🏺 Tangia Marrakchia (Pressure-Cooker Version)

Tanjia — Marrakech's urn-cooked lamb (طنجية مراكشية)

Marrakech's famous bachelor stew, cooked in an urn in a hammam's embers. At home: lamb with garlic, cumin, saffron and preserved lemon, pressure-cooked until it falls apart.

Tanjia is the signature dish of Marrakech, traditionally cooked overnight in an earthenware urn buried in the embers that heat the neighbourhood bathhouse. At home, MarocMama's version needs two pounds of lamb or mutton, crushed garlic, salt, saffron, turmeric, white pepper, cumin, half a preserved lemon rind and a little olive oil. Everything goes in a pressure cooker with 1 to 2 cups of water for 45 minutes to 1 hour (closer to 2 hours on the stovetop), until the meat falls apart in a thick sauce. There are no vegetables.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pressure cooker (or heavy lidded pot)

  • 2 lbs. lamb or mutton
  • 2 tbsp garlic crushed
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 pinch of saffron threads crushed
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 3/4 tsp white pepper
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1/2 preserved lemon rind, finely chopped
  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • 1-2 cups water
  • 1 teaspoon smen, optional

Method

  1. Toss the meat with the garlic, salt, saffron, turmeric, white pepper, cumin, preserved lemon rind and smen if using. Marinate at least an hour, ideally overnight.
  2. In the pressure cooker, warm the olive oil and garlic on medium heat for about 3 minutes.
  3. Add the meat and 1-2 cups water (if cooking on the stove top, start with half the water and watch closely as you may need to add more).
  4. Cover and cook on medium-high heat for 45 minutes to 1 hour (closer to 2 hours on the stovetop).
  5. Vent the steam and check that the meat is tender. It should be falling apart with a thick sauce left; if there is a lot of liquid left, continue boiling it down.

Chef Tips

  • Doneness: the meat falls apart and the sauce is thick.
  • The tradition says a tangia uses hardly any water: Taste of Maroc says on average less than a cup per two pounds.
  • Good cuts, per Taste of Maroc: hock, collar, or a mix of pieces.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding too much water; this is a confit-like braise.
  • Opening the pressure cooker before venting the steam.
  • Skipping the marinade time.

Health Benefits

  • High-protein, low-carb meal with no added vegetables.
  • The long cooking breaks down connective tissue for tenderness.

Cultural Note

MarocMama describes tangia as Marrakech's signature dish, cooked in an earthenware urn in the embers of a communal oven overnight, with artisans pooling money on Thursday and eating on Friday afternoon after midday prayer in a park outside the city. Smithsonian Magazine (June 10, 2026) links it to the hammam culture of the medina.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 4 days in the fridge; the confit-like meat reheats well.

Reheating: Warm covered in a low oven or on low heat.

Serve with: Khobz, Mint tea
