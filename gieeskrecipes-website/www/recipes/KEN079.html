🦐 Kenyan Prawn Curry

Mchuzi wa Uduvi

Kenyan Prawn Curry — plump prawns simmered in a fragrant, warmly spiced tomato and onion curry sauce. One of the most luxurious dishes of the Kenyan Swahili coast — ready in under 30 minutes and deeply aromatic.

The Indian Ocean coastline of Kenya has always been prawn territory — fresh prawns from the warm coastal waters have been a prized ingredient in Swahili cooking for centuries. Mombasa's prawn curry, fragrant with garlic, ginger, and coastal spices, is one of the most celebrated dishes of the coast. It cooks astonishingly quickly — prawns need only minutes — making it one of the fastest premium meals in the Kenyan repertoire.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pan, Wooden spoon

  • 500g large prawns — peeled and deveined, tails on
  • 2 medium onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • ½ cup water

Method

  1. Season prawns with salt, turmeric, and paprika.
  2. Cook onions in oil until golden — 8 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook for 6—8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add curry powder, cumin, and remaining spices. Stir 1 minute.
  6. Add water. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  7. Add prawns. Cook for 3—5 minutes only — until prawns are pink and just cooked through.
  8. Stir in garam masala and lemon juice.
  9. Scatter with coriander. Serve immediately with rice.

Chef Tips

  • Prawns cook in 3—5 minutes maximum — the most common mistake is overcooking them to rubbery toughness.
  • Build the curry sauce fully before adding prawns — they will finish cooking in the residual heat.
  • Prawns are done when they have curled into a C-shape and turned fully pink. An O-shape means overcooked.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking prawns — they turn rubbery and tough in minutes.
  • Adding prawns to a cold or barely warm sauce — they stew rather than cook cleanly.

The Science

Prawn proteins (primarily myosin) denature and coagulate at around 60°C — extremely quickly. The characteristic curling from straight to C-shape happens as the muscle proteins contract during cooking. A C-shape means perfectly cooked; an O-shape means the protein has contracted further — the prawn is overcooked and will be rubbery.

Health Benefits

  • Prawns are very high in protein and low in fat.
  • Excellent source of iodine, selenium, and zinc.
  • Low in calories — one of the leanest protein options in the collection.

Cultural Note

Prawn curry is one of the jewels of Kenyan coastal cuisine — the combination of fresh Indian Ocean prawns and the Swahili-Indian spice tradition produces one of East Africa's most celebrated dishes. In Mombasa's restaurants, prawn curry with coconut rice is the quintessential coastal meal.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately. Do not reheat prawns — they overcook easily.

Reheating: Not recommended — serve prawn curry fresh.

Serve with: Wali wa Nazi, Basmati Rice, Chapati, Kachumbari, Naan
