🧄 Garlic Bread (Italian Style)

Pane all'Aglio

Italian-style garlic bread is a crusty loaf split and slathered with a fragrant blend of butter, olive oil, roasted garlic, and parsley, then baked until golden and irresistible.

While garlic bread as a specific dish is more of an Italian-American creation, its roots lie firmly in the Italian tradition of bruschetta and fettunta — bread rubbed with garlic and drizzled with olive oil — dressed up here with butter and a hot oven for a richer, restaurant-style result.

Ingredients

Equipment: Baking sheet, Small bowl, Bread knife

  • 1 loaf ciabatta or Pane Pugliese, split lengthwise
  • 115 g (½ cup) unsalted butter, softened
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) extra virgin olive oil
  • 6 garlic cloves, finely minced or grated
  • 3 tbsp finely chopped fresh parsley
  • 30 g (⅓ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • ½ tsp salt
  • Pinch of red pepper flakes (optional)

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F).
  2. In a small bowl, mash together the softened butter, olive oil, garlic, parsley, half the Parmigiano, salt, and red pepper flakes if using, until well combined.
  3. Spread the garlic butter generously and evenly over the cut sides of the bread.
  4. Sprinkle the remaining Parmigiano over the top.
  5. Place cut-side up on a baking sheet and bake for 10–12 minutes until golden and bubbling at the edges.
  6. For extra color, switch to the broiler for the final 1–2 minutes, watching closely so it doesn't burn.
  7. Slice and serve immediately while hot.

Chef Tips

  • Grating the garlic rather than mincing it gives a more even, milder flavor throughout the butter.
  • Let the butter come fully to room temperature for easy, even spreading.
  • Broiling briefly at the end gives the best golden, slightly crisp top.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using cold, hard butter, which tears the bread when spreading.
  • Burning the garlic under the broiler — watch it closely in the final minute.
  • Using pre-minced jarred garlic, which lacks the fresh, sharp aroma of freshly minced cloves.

Health Benefits

  • Garlic contains allicin, associated with immune and cardiovascular benefits.
  • Parsley adds vitamin K and vitamin C.

Cultural Note

While not a traditional dish found on menus within Italy itself, Italian-style garlic bread has become one of the most beloved Italian-inspired dishes worldwide, especially as a pasta-night side.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh. Unbaked garlic butter can be refrigerated for up to a week or frozen for a month.

Reheating: Reheat in a 180°C (350°F) oven for 5 minutes to re-crisp.

Serve with: Pasta dishes, Minestrone, Italian soups, Antipasto platters
