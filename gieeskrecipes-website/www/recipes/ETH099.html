🥗 Ethiopian Fresh Salad

ሰላጣ — Selata

Ethiopian Selata — a simple fresh salad of tomatoes, cucumber, onion, jalapeño, and coriander dressed with lemon and oil. The universal accompaniment to Ethiopian grilled fish and fried fish — its freshness and acidity cutting through the richness of spiced preparations.

Ethiopian fresh salad is the clean counterpoint to the richly spiced dishes — tomatoes, cucumber, onion, jalapeño, and coriander dressed simply with lemon and a little oil. It appears alongside every grilled and fried fish dish and is the most neutral item on the Ethiopian table.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl

  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 1 cucumber — diced
  • ½ red onion — very thinly sliced
  • 2 jalapeños — finely sliced
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander
  • 3 tbsp lemon juice
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Combine all diced vegetables and jalapeño.
  2. Dress with lemon juice, olive oil, and salt.
  3. Add coriander. Toss gently.
  4. Rest 5 minutes. Toss again. Serve.

Chef Tips

  • Let the salad rest 5 minutes — the salt draws out vegetable juices, creating the dressing.
  • Very fresh vegetables only — this salad lives or dies by the produce.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-dressed — should be light.

The Science

The salt in the dressing draws water from the vegetable cells through osmosis — this water dilutes the lemon juice and oil into a natural dressing that coats each vegetable piece without being heavy.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian selata is the table's breath of fresh air — between the rich kibbeh sauces, the fierce berbere wats, and the complex injera, this simple salad provides the clean, bright counterpoint that makes the overall meal more complete.

Storage: Best eaten immediately.
