⚪ Tanzanian Sesame Balls

Mkate wa Ufuta wa Mviringo

Tanzanian Sesame Balls — toasted sesame seeds bound together with caramelised sugar and honey, rolled into small firm balls with a cardamom note. The sesame sweet of the Tanzanian coast — crunchy, nutty, and deeply satisfying.

Sesame balls are the simplest and most ancient Tanzanian coastal sweet — toasted sesame and caramelised sugar, a touch of cardamom, and the patience to roll them while warm. They are the sweet that has been made the same way on the Swahili coast for centuries.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Parchment paper

  • 1.5 cups sesame seeds — toasted until golden
  • ½ cup sugar
  • 2 tbsp honey
  • 1 tbsp water
  • ¼ tsp ground cardamom
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Toast sesame seeds in a dry pan until golden and fragrant. Cool slightly.
  2. Combine sugar, honey, and water in a saucepan.
  3. Cook over medium heat until the mixture reaches soft-ball stage (115°C).
  4. Add toasted sesame seeds, cardamom, and salt. Stir to combine.
  5. Working quickly with greased hands, roll into small balls (2cm).
  6. Place on parchment. Cool completely.

Chef Tips

  • Work very quickly — the mixture sets fast.
  • Grease hands generously.
  • Cool completely before storing — they stick to each other if warm.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Working too slowly.
  • Storing warm — they stick together.

The Science

See TZA199 for brittle candy sugar science. Honey in the mixture adds invertase and natural inverted sugar — this prevents the characteristic graininess of pure crystallised sugar candy, producing a smoother, less grainy sesame ball.

Cultural Note

Sesame balls are the sweet that has no ceremony around it — they are simply made and simply eaten, one of the oldest confections of the Swahili coast, unchanged across centuries.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight container 2 weeks separated by parchment.

Reheating: Not applicable.
