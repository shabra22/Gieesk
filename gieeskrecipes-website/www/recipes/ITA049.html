🍝 Spinach & Ricotta Cannelloni

Cannelloni Ricotta e Spinaci

This vegetarian take on cannelloni fills pasta tubes with a creamy spinach-ricotta mixture, baked under tomato sauce and béchamel until golden — a comforting meat-free alternative to the classic.

Sharing its filling with ricotta and spinach ravioli, this dish takes the same beloved combination and presents it in generous, rolled tubes, making it a popular choice for vegetarian guests at a Sunday family table.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Piping bag or spoon, Baking dish

  • 12 fresh pasta sheets (or 12 dried cannelloni tubes)

Filling

  • 400 g (14 oz) fresh spinach, wilted, squeezed very dry, and finely chopped
  • 350 g (1½ cups) ricotta, well-drained
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1 egg
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a pinch
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Sauce and topping

  • 500 g (18 oz) tomato passata or crushed tomatoes
  • 1 batch béchamel (as in ITA030, half quantity)
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for topping

Method

  1. Combine the well-squeezed spinach, ricotta, Parmigiano, egg, nutmeg, salt, and pepper in a bowl, mixing until smooth.
  2. If using fresh pasta sheets, cut into rectangles about 10x15cm and blanch briefly in boiling water until just pliable, about 1 minute. If using dried tubes, no pre-cooking is needed.
  3. Fill a piping bag with the spinach-ricotta mixture and pipe into the pasta tubes or roll into the fresh sheets.
  4. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F). Spread a thin layer of tomato sauce on the base of a baking dish.
  5. Arrange the filled cannelloni in a single layer over the sauce.
  6. Pour the remaining tomato sauce over the top, followed by the béchamel, and scatter with the Parmigiano.
  7. Cover with foil and bake for 25 minutes, then uncover and bake a further 15–20 minutes until golden and bubbling.
  8. Rest for 10 minutes before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Squeeze the spinach extremely dry — excess moisture is the most common cause of a watery, loose filling.
  • A piping bag makes filling the tubes far less messy than a spoon.
  • If using dried tubes, ensure they're fully covered by sauce so they soften properly during baking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-draining the spinach and ricotta, leading to a filling that leaks or makes the dish watery.
  • Overfilling the tubes, causing them to split during baking.
  • Skipping the rest time, making the dish difficult to portion cleanly.

Health Benefits

  • Spinach provides iron, vitamin K, and folate.
  • A good source of calcium and protein from the ricotta and Parmigiano.

Cultural Note

Spinach and ricotta cannelloni is a favorite vegetarian option across Italian family tables, especially popular for guests observing meat-free days or personal dietary preferences.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days, or freeze baked or unbaked for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Reheat covered in a 180°C (350°F) oven for 20–25 minutes until warmed through.

Serve with: A simple green salad, Crusty bread, A crisp white wine
