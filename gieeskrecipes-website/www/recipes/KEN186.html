🐟 Swahili Tamarind Fish Stew

Samaki wa Ukwaju wa Kiswahili

Swahili Tamarind Fish Stew — the ancient Swahili coastal preparation of fresh fish in a tangy, sweet-sour tamarind sauce. One of the oldest and most distinctive preparations in Swahili culinary heritage. See KEN128 for the full recipe.

Samaki wa Ukwaju represents centuries of Swahili coastal culinary tradition — fresh Indian Ocean fish combined with tamarind from Arab trade. See KEN128 for the complete recipe and heritage.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pan, Wooden spoon

  • 600g fresh fish fillets — snapper, kingfish, or barracuda
  • 4 tbsp tamarind paste
  • 2 medium onions — chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • ½ cup water
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander

Method

  1. Season fish with salt and turmeric.
  2. Cook onions in oil until golden. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  3. Add tomatoes, curry powder, tamarind paste, brown sugar, and water. Stir well.
  4. Simmer for 6—8 minutes until sauce thickens. Taste and balance sweet-sour.
  5. Add fish. Spoon sauce over gently.
  6. Cover and simmer 8—12 minutes until cooked through.
  7. Garnish with coriander and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Taste and adjust the tamarind-sugar balance before adding fish.
  • See KEN128 for the full technique and cultural notes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking fish.
  • Not balancing sweet-sour flavour.
