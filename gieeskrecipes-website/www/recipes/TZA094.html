🥬 Tanzanian Amaranth Greens

Mchicha

Tanzanian Mchicha — amaranth leaves sautéed with onions, tomatoes, and garlic until wilted and richly flavoured. Tanzania's most nutritious everyday green — more iron and protein than spinach, widely grown and deeply beloved.

Mchicha (amaranth) is Tanzania's most important leafy vegetable — more nutritious than spinach, incredibly fast to cook, and available at every market. It is the green that accompanies ugali at the majority of Tanzanian family meals. Its preparation is simple but the result is deeply satisfying.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wide pan

  • 500g fresh mchicha (amaranth leaves) — washed and roughly chopped
  • 2 medium onions — diced
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper

Method

  1. Heat oil over medium heat.
  2. Cook onions until golden — 6 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and curry powder. Stir 30 seconds.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook 5 minutes until softened.
  5. Add mchicha. Stir to combine.
  6. Cover and cook 4—5 minutes until completely wilted.
  7. Season with salt and pepper. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Do not add water — the leaves release plenty of moisture.
  • Brief cooking preserves the bright green colour and nutrients.
  • Mchicha wilts dramatically — 500g raw produces a modest serving.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding water — makes it soupy.
  • Overcooking — loses colour and nutrients.

The Science

Amaranth leaves contain oxalic acid which dissolves in the water released during cooking — brief cooking minimises oxalic acid buildup in the cooking liquid. The iron in mchicha is non-haem iron; pairing with the tomato's vitamin C significantly increases its absorption.

Health Benefits

  • Higher iron content than spinach.
  • Excellent calcium.
  • Vitamins A and C.
  • Complete plant protein when combined with beans.

Cultural Note

Mchicha is Tanzania's most democratic vegetable — grown in virtually every household garden, bought at every market, and eaten by every family regardless of income. It is the green that has nourished Tanzania for generations.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Briefly in pan.

Serve with: Ugali (TZA001), Beans, Any meat or fish dish
