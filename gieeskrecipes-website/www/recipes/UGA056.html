🥔 Fried Party Irish Potatoes

Irish

Potatoes boiled in generously salted water with onion, garlic-ginger paste and curry powder, cooled completely — even overnight — and then deep-fried to a crust that will not fall apart.

These are the potatoes served at Ugandan gatherings, and the reason they have that famous crisp crust is one instruction: cool them completely before frying. You can boil them one day and fry them the next. Fry them warm and they melt into the oil. The other good idea in here is that the flavour goes in during the boil rather than after — onion, garlic-ginger paste, curry powder and generous salt all go into the water, and that water is worth keeping afterwards.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Deep pan for frying, Slotted spoon, Colander

  • 10 to 12 Irish potatoes
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) garlic-ginger paste
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) curry powder
  • Salt, generously
  • Oil, for deep frying

Optional additions to the boiling water

  • Chopped green pepper, chilli powder, beef or chicken broth

Method

  1. Peel and wash the potatoes, then slice them in half.
  2. Put them in a pan with enough water to boil. Add the onions, garlic-ginger paste, curry powder and salt.
  3. Bring to the boil and boil until cooked through but not falling apart.
  4. Take them off the heat and drain the water into a separate bowl — keep it, it has a lot of flavour and will cook other foods.

The cooling — this is the important part

  1. Let the potatoes cool down completely. You can even fry them the next day.

Frying

  1. Put a pan on the heat with enough oil to deep-fry.
  2. Deep-fry until golden and crispy.
  3. Lift out and let them cool a little.
  4. Serve warm, on their own, with kachumbari, or with your favourite stew.

Chef Tips

  • Cooling them completely is what creates the famous crispy crusty exterior and stops them melting in the oil. This is the difference between these and ordinary chips, and there is no way round it.
  • Boiling them the day before is not a compromise, it is the better version.
  • Season the boiling water generously. That is where the flavour goes in — salt on the outside afterwards never penetrates.
  • Slicing them in half helps them cook fully through. Small potatoes can go in whole.
  • Cooked through but not falling apart. Overcooked potatoes will not survive the fryer.
  • Keep the boiling water. It is full of onion, garlic, ginger and curry and it will cook rice or beans beautifully.
  • Green pepper, chilli powder and a broth cube in the boiling water are all worth adding.
  • These are what turns up at gatherings, which is why the recipe is scaled to ten or twelve potatoes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Frying them warm.
  • Boiling them in plain water and salting after.
  • Overboiling until they crumble.
  • Throwing the boiling water away.
  • Crowding the fryer.

Health Benefits

  • Potatoes are a good source of potassium, vitamin C and vitamin B6.
  • The flavour comes from aromatics in the boiling water rather than added fat.
  • Naturally gluten-free and dairy-free.
  • Deep-fried, so this is party food.

Cultural Note

Boiling before frying is a technique with a long life in a lot of cuisines and here it is doing a specific job: producing a crust sturdy enough to sit on a platter at a function for an hour. The instruction that you can fry them the next day is the most practical thing in the recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Boiled potatoes keep 2 days refrigerated before frying. Fry to order.

Reheating: A hot oven for a few minutes.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Kikalayi, Muchomo
