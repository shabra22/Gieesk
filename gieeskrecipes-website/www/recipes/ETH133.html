🍵 Ethiopian Lentil Soup

ምስር ሾርባ — Misir Shorba

Ethiopian Lentil Soup — a thinner, more soup-like version of Misir Wat, drunk as a warming soup on cold highland evenings and during fasting periods. The berbere lentil soup — same flavours as Misir Wat but in a pourable form.

Ethiopian lentil soup is the pourable version of Misir Wat — the same berbere-onion base with more water added, producing a warming soup rather than a thick stew. Drunk from cups on cold Addis Ababa evenings.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 1.5 cups red lentils
  • 3 onions
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 3 tbsp berbere
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 4 cups water
  • 2 tsp salt
  • Lemon wedges and coriander to serve

Method

  1. Make the base as per Misir Wat — dry-caramelise onions, fry berbere.
  2. Add lentils and water (more water than Misir Wat).
  3. Simmer 25—30 minutes.
  4. For a smooth soup: blend partially or fully.
  5. Season. Serve with lemon and coriander.

Chef Tips

  • More water than Misir Wat — should be pourable.
  • Lemon squeezed at the table brightens the soup significantly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too thick — add more water.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian lentil soup is the warming drink of fasting evenings — when the cold descends on the Ethiopian highlands and the family gathers, a pot of berbere lentil soup heating on the fire is the most comforting thing possible.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: As a standalone soup, With injera for a light meal
