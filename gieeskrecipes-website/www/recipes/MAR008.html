🫒 Chicken, Preserved Lemon and Olives Tagine

Djej mqualli — chicken mkalli (دجاج مقلي)

The signature Moroccan chicken: onions melted down with garlic, turmeric and ginger, chicken gently simmered, then finished with chopped preserved lemon and olives.

Mqualli is a style as much as a dish: garlic, turmeric, ginger and a large amount of onion. Benkabbou softens four large onions, browns the chicken pieces lightly, adds a cup of water and a coriander bouquet and cooks about 50 minutes. The sauce is then reduced for about 20 minutes until most of the liquid has evaporated, chopped preserved lemon and olives are stirred in over medium-low heat for 10 minutes, and the chicken goes back in to warm.

Ingredients

Equipment: Casserole or tagine with lid

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 4 large onions (about 800g), chopped
  • 1,2kg whole chicken cut into pieces or 1,2kg chicken legs and thighs
  • 1 tablespoon finely chopped garlic (about 4 cloves)
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1 teaspoon salt or more to taste
  • ¼ ground black pepper (the source prints no unit)
  • A generous pinch saffron
  • 1 large coriander bouquet, tied
  • 2 medium sized preserved lemons
  • 150g red-brown olives, like Kalamatas

Method

  1. Heat the oil in the casserole, add the onions, cover and leave until soft and translucent, about 10 minutes.
  2. Stir in the garlic and spices, add the chicken and turn it occasionally to lightly brown it, about 10-15 minutes.
  3. Pour in a cup of water (240 ml), set the coriander bouquet on the chicken and cook over medium heat until the chicken is cooked, about 50 minutes.
  4. Lift out the chicken. Reduce the sauce over medium heat for about 20 minutes or until most of the liquid has evaporated; some liquid should remain.
  5. Chop the preserved lemon rind (the author discards the flesh). Add it and the olives to the onions, stir, and leave over medium-low heat for 10 minutes.
  6. Return the chicken to the casserole to warm through and serve.

Chef Tips

  • The author's mother says adding preserved lemon flesh at the start makes the dish too acidic; add the rind at the end.
  • If you have the chicken liver, it can be mashed into the sauce.
  • In Morocco the tagine is sometimes topped with French fries; the author prefers bread for mopping up.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the lemon early, which turns the sauce sharp.
  • Letting the sauce stay watery; reduce it before adding olives.
  • Forgetting that olives and preserved lemon are salty, so taste before adding more salt.

Health Benefits

  • Lean protein with a vegetable-rich onion sauce.
  • Olives add healthy fats; preserved lemon boosts flavour with little salt added at the table.

Cultural Note

In the author's family, her mother objects to preserved lemon flesh at the start of cooking. She notes that many Moroccan homes serve this tagine with French fries on top.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 3 days in the fridge.

Reheating: Warm gently on the stove with a splash of water.

Serve with: Khobz, Preserved lemons
