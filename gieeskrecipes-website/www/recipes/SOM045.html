🍌 Maraq Moos Bukiini

Maraq Moos Bukiini

Beef shoulder stewed with xawaash and turmeric, with thick rounds of semi-ripe plantain dropped in for the last half hour so they hold their shape and turn faintly sweet against the tomato.

Plantain is a staple in southern Somalia, along the Jubba and Shabelle where it grows, and it goes into savoury dishes as readily as sweet ones. The ripeness of the fruit is the whole decision here: green is hard work to peel and slow to cook, over-ripe falls apart and sugars the stew, and semi-ripe sits exactly where it should — holding its shape, absorbing the sauce, and sweet enough to notice.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a lid

  • 120 ml (1/2 cup) canola oil
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 3 large cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp xawaash spice blend
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 680 g (1 1/2 lb) beef shoulder on the bone, cubed
  • 1 tbsp vegetable seasoning, or 2 bouillon cubes, or 2 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
  • 4 hot green peppers (optional)
  • 3 tomatoes, peeled and diced
  • 950 ml (4 cups) hot water
  • 1 semi-ripe plantain, cut into 2.5 cm thick slices
  • 1 green pepper, diced
  • 120 ml (1/2 cup) fresh cilantro, chopped

Method

  1. Fry the sliced onion in the oil over medium heat for 3 minutes, then add the garlic.
  2. Stir in the xawaash and the turmeric.
  3. Add the beef, the vegetable seasoning and the black pepper, and cook for 4 minutes before anything acidic goes in.
  4. Add the hot green peppers if using, then the diced tomatoes.
  5. Pour in the hot water.
  6. Cover and cook over medium heat for half an hour.
  7. Add the sliced plantain and the diced green pepper.
  8. Cover and cook for another half hour, until the beef is tender and the plantain has taken up the sauce without collapsing.
  9. Stir in the chopped cilantro and serve with rice, soor, anjero or sabaayad.

Chef Tips

  • Semi-ripe plantain is the one to buy — yellow with some green left at the ends. Green plantain is a fight to peel and slow to cook; over-ripe turns to mush.
  • Cut the slices thick, a good 2.5 cm. Thin rounds disappear into the stew over half an hour.
  • Four minutes of browning before the tomatoes matters. Acid slows browning to a stop, so anything you want coloured has to be coloured first.
  • Beef shoulder on the bone gives the stew body. Boneless cubes cook faster but the sauce is thinner for it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using over-ripe plantain, which disintegrates and makes the whole stew sweet.
  • Adding the plantain at the start, so it has an hour in the pot and nothing left of its texture.
  • Adding the tomatoes before the beef has browned, which leaves the meat grey and the stew flat.
  • Slicing the plantain thin, after which there is nothing to find in the finished pot.

Health Benefits

  • Plantain is a good source of potassium, vitamin A and resistant starch.
  • Beef provides protein, iron and zinc.
  • A one-pot meal with its starch and vegetables already in it.

Cultural Note

Somalia was once a major banana exporter, and the fertile riverine lands of the south are where plantain and banana became part of everyday cooking rather than a treat. In those regions a plate without some form of banana on it still looks incomplete.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the hob. The plantain softens further on reheating, so stop as soon as it is hot.

Serve with: Soor iyo Maraq, Canjeero, Moos Shiilan
