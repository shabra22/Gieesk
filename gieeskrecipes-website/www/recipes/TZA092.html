🫘 Tanzanian Bean Stew

Maharage ya Mchuzi

Tanzanian Maharage ya Mchuzi — beans simmered in a rich spiced tomato-onion sauce. Tanzania's most essential everyday protein dish — the bean stew that accompanies ugali, rice, and chapati at every meal. Simple, nutritious, and deeply satisfying.

Maharage ya Mchuzi (without coconut milk) is Tanzania's everyday bean stew — the tomato-based version eaten from Dodoma to Mwanza, from Arusha to Dar es Salaam. Cheaper than the coconut version and equally nourishing, it is the protein that sustains most of Tanzania daily.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 400g dried kidney or borlotti beans — soaked overnight
  • 2 large onions — diced
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander

Method

  1. Cook soaked beans in fresh water 60—75 minutes until tender. Reserve 1 cup liquid.
  2. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, tomato paste, spices. Stir 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook 8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add beans and reserved liquid.
  6. Simmer 20 minutes until thick and richly flavoured.
  7. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Completely tender beans before adding to sauce.
  • Tomato paste deepens the sauce colour and flavour.
  • Reserve bean liquid — it thickens naturally.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-cooked beans.
  • Discarding bean liquid.

The Science

See TZA091 for bean cooking science. The tomato paste's concentrated glutamate (umami) deepens the sauce far beyond what fresh tomatoes alone provide.

Cultural Note

Maharage ya Mchuzi is the protein backbone of Tanzania's diet — the bean stew that feeds the nation daily and has sustained Tanzanian communities for generations.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently — improves with reheating.
