Mutere

Cassava or sweet potato cut into pieces and dried in the sun until hard, kept through the dry season, and boiled back to life when needed.

Mutere is sun-dried cassava or sweet potato, and it is a pantry rather than a dish — the way a household holds a harvest through a season when nothing is growing. The pieces are dried until hard, stored, and boiled when wanted. It is the same instinct as the dried cowpea leaf in Ggobe Leaf Powder, the smoked bamboo of maleewa, and the sun-dried mukene: a cuisine without refrigeration preserves by drying, and drying is a technique worth learning as such.

Ingredients

Equipment: Knife, Drying trays or mats, Airtight storage, Saucepan

  • Cassava, or sweet potato
  • Water, for boiling later

No quantities are documented

    Method

    1. Peel the cassava or sweet potato and cut it into pieces.
    2. Spread the pieces out and sun-dry them until hard.
    3. Store them dry.

    To cook

    1. Boil the dried pieces until tender, and serve as a staple.

    Chef Tips

    • Dry it until genuinely hard. Half-dried pieces mould in storage and you lose the batch.
    • Spread the pieces thin and single-layered. Heaped, the middle stays damp while the top dries.
    • Bring them in overnight and when it rains. Rehydrated and re-dried pieces spoil.
    • Cut them small and even. Thick pieces take far longer to dry and longer again to cook back.
    • Store it genuinely airtight and dry. Humidity is what ruins dried staples.
    • Cassava must be properly processed to be safe, and drying is one of the recognised methods — the same principle that governs the climbing yams in Steamed Bitter Yams. Bitter cassava varieties need more than a sun-dry, so use sweet cassava or dry it thoroughly.
    • No quantities and no drying time are documented. Nothing here is estimated to cover that.
    • Dried cassava is ground into flour in much of Uganda too, and that flour is what goes into karo, Karo, alongside millet.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Storing it before it is fully hard.
    • Heaping the pieces on the drying mat.
    • Leaving it out in the rain.
    • Cutting the pieces too thick.
    • Assuming any cassava variety is safe with a short dry.

    Health Benefits

    • Cassava and sweet potato both supply carbohydrate, fibre and potassium.
    • Drying concentrates the calories, which is the point in a lean season.
    • No fat, no sugar and no salt added.
    • Makes a perishable root crop keep for months without refrigeration.

    Cultural Note

    Preservation is the least glamorous and most important part of any traditional cuisine, and Uganda has a whole family of it — smoked bamboo, sun-dried fish pinned on sticks, dried leaf powder, beans preserved in ash and banana leaf, and this. None of them is a recipe and all of them are why there was food in March.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Months, dried and airtight. Once boiled, 2 days refrigerated.

    Reheating: Boil or steam again.

    Serve with: Binyebwa, Dek Ngor, Nzikinziki
