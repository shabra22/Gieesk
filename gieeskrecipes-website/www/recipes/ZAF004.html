🥬 Umfino

Umfino

Stiff maize meal folded through cabbage, spinach, onion and celery leaves caramelised in a hot pan — a complete meal made of two cheap things, and the Eastern Cape's cold-night dinner.

Imifino is the word for wild leafy greens, gathered rather than bought, and umfino is what you make with them: pap and greens cooked separately then brought together at the last moment. Any leaf works — amaranth, blackjack, pumpkin leaves, cabbage, spinach — which is exactly why the dish survived. It is eaten alone as a full meal, not as a side, and the greens go in caramelised rather than boiled.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan with a lid, Wide frying pan, Whisk

    The pap

    • 1.4 L (6 cups) water
    • Salt, to taste
    • 360 g maize meal

    The greens

    • 4 tbsp vegetable oil
    • 1 cup cabbage, thinly sliced
    • 1 onion, halved and sliced
    • A handful of celery leaves
    • 1 cup spinach or wild greens, thinly sliced
    • 1 large potato, peeled, cooked and cubed
    • 2 cloves garlic, chopped
    • 2 tbsp vegetable seasoning

    Method

    1. Bring the water to the boil in a large saucepan and season it with salt.
    2. Whisking, pour in the maize meal and stir until smooth and thick, adding a little more water if it tightens too fast.
    3. Let it come to a properly stiff consistency, stir once more, turn the heat down, cover and cook for 25 minutes. Set aside and keep warm.
    4. Heat the oil in a wide pan until hot. Add the cabbage, onion, celery leaves, cooked potato, garlic and spinach with the vegetable seasoning.
    5. Sauté over medium-high heat until everything has softened and caught a little colour at the edges. Check the seasoning.
    6. Fold the pap through the greens and serve immediately.

    Chef Tips

    • Get the pap properly stiff before you cover it. Loose pap turns the finished dish into a wet mash.
    • Cook the greens hot and fast so they caramelise. Boiled greens make a grey, flat umfino.
    • Any leaf will do, which is the character of the dish — amaranth, pumpkin leaves, blackjack and chard all belong here as much as cabbage.
    • Combine and serve at once. Left standing, the greens steam in the hot pap and lose their edge.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cooking the pap loose, so the two elements never hold together.
    • Softening the greens in a moderate pan instead of colouring them in a hot one.
    • Mixing it together and then letting it sit while you lay the table.
    • Adding raw potato with the greens — it goes in already cooked.

    Health Benefits

    • Leafy greens supply iron, folate, calcium and vitamins A, C and K.
    • Wild greens such as amaranth are typically more nutrient-dense than cultivated cabbage or spinach.
    • Entirely plant-based and high in fibre.

    Cultural Note

    Imifino is foraged food and knowing which leaves to pick is knowledge passed between neighbours rather than written down. The same greens are morogo in Sesotho and Setswana, muroho in Tshivenda, and the practice is common right across the country — a reminder that the cheapest food on the table is often the most nutritious thing on it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Reheat in a pan with a little oil, breaking it up as it warms.

    Serve with: Isibindi, Grilled meat, Amasi
