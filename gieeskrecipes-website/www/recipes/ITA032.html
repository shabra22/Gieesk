🍝 Manicotti

Manicotti

Manicotti are wide pasta tubes filled with a creamy, herbed ricotta filling, baked in tomato sauce until tender and golden — a comforting vegetarian relative of cannelloni.

Where cannelloni is often filled with meat, manicotti's name meaning 'little sleeves' typically refers to a purely cheese-based filling — a simple, satisfying dish popular for family Sunday dinners.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Mixing bowl, Piping bag or spoon, Baking dish

  • 12–14 manicotti tubes (or crepes, as an alternative wrapper)
  • 500 g (2 cups) ricotta
  • 250 g (2 cups) shredded mozzarella, divided
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, divided
  • 1 egg
  • 3 tbsp chopped fresh parsley
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • 600 g (21 oz) tomato passata or marinara sauce

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the manicotti tubes just until pliable, slightly under package directions (they will finish cooking in the oven). Drain and lay flat to cool.
  2. In a bowl, combine the ricotta, half the mozzarella, half the Parmigiano, the egg, parsley, oregano, salt, and pepper, mixing until smooth.
  3. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F). Spread a thin layer of tomato sauce on the base of a baking dish.
  4. Fill a piping bag with the ricotta mixture and pipe into each cooked manicotti tube.
  5. Arrange the filled tubes in the baking dish and pour the remaining sauce over the top.
  6. Scatter with the remaining mozzarella and Parmigiano.
  7. Cover with foil and bake for 25 minutes, then uncover and bake a further 10–15 minutes until bubbling and golden.
  8. Rest for 5–10 minutes before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Undercook the pasta tubes slightly before filling — they finish cooking in the oven and can become too soft if fully cooked beforehand.
  • A piping bag makes filling the delicate tubes far easier and cleaner than a spoon.
  • Fresh herbs make a noticeable difference in the filling compared to dried alone.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Fully cooking the pasta before baking, resulting in overly soft, fragile tubes.
  • Overfilling the tubes, causing them to split.
  • Using low-moisture pre-shredded mozzarella straight from a bag with anti-caking agents, which can affect melt quality — freshly shredded is best.

Cultural Note

Manicotti is more commonly associated with Italian-American home cooking than with a single specific region of Italy, evolving from various stuffed-pasta traditions brought by Southern Italian immigrants.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days, or freeze baked or unbaked for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Reheat covered in a 180°C (350°F) oven for 20 minutes until warmed through.

Serve with: A simple green salad, Garlic bread, A light Chianti
