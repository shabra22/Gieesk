🍪 Daddies

Daddies

Hot sugar syrup poured into the flour to make it flaky, the dough folded three times for layers, chilled overnight and cut into centimetre cubes fried slowly on low heat.

Daddies are small bite-sized sweet fried flour cubes, similar in composition to mandazi but much crunchier because of how much shortening goes in. Nobody seems to know where the name came from. Two things make them work and both are counter-intuitive: the sugar, water, margarine and milk are boiled together and poured into the flour hot, which is what creates the flakiness, and the dough is folded three times like puff pastry, which is what creates the layers. Then it rests overnight.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, Saucepan, Rolling pin, Foil, baking paper or cling film, Frying pan, Sharp knife

  • 1080 ml (4 1/2 cups) flour
  • 240 ml (1 cup) sugar
  • 240 ml (1 cup) water
  • 60 ml (1/4 cup) margarine or butter
  • 60 ml (1/4 cup) powdered milk
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) vanilla essence
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) lemon or orange zest
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) salt
  • 1.25 ml (1/4 tsp) nutmeg, grated
  • Oil, for frying

Method

  1. Mix the flour, salt, nutmeg and zest in a large clean bowl and set aside.

The hot syrup

  1. Put a clean saucepan on medium heat. Pour in the cup of water, then add the sugar, vanilla, margarine and powdered milk.
  2. Mix gradually as it heats, until all the sugar has dissolved.
  3. Let it come to a gentle boil, and after about a minute take it off the heat.
  4. Pour it gradually into the flour mixture, stirring as it goes to avoid lumps. Use a spoon — it will be hot.
  5. Mix until a firm smooth dough forms.

The layers

  1. Roll the dough out to about half a centimetre thick.
  2. Fold it and roll again to about a centimetre thick.
  3. Fold a third time and roll to even it out.
  4. Wrap the dough in foil, baking paper or cling film and refrigerate overnight.

Frying

  1. Take the dough from the fridge and slice it into 1 centimetre squares.
  2. Put a frying pan on medium-low heat with enough oil to submerge them.
  3. Add the daddies without overloading the pan, and fry on low heat until the layers start to show.
  4. Keep frying until they turn a vibrant golden brown.
  5. Drain and serve cool.

Chef Tips

  • Pour the sugar mixture in hot. That is what makes daddies flaky, and it is the whole reason for the boiling step — cooking the sugar also dissolves it properly so you get no granules in the fried dough.
  • Fold three times. Three folds and three rollings is the puff-pastry trick and it is what gives them layers.
  • Use a spoon to mix, because the dough will be too hot for hands.
  • The overnight chill is not optional. It firms the fat back up so the layers survive being cut and fried.
  • Low heat for frying. They need to cook gently all the way through, and the layers showing is the visual cue that it is working.
  • If your butter or margarine is salted, leave the half teaspoon of salt out.
  • They taste better the next day, if you can manage to leave them.
  • An older, simpler version exists with orange juice and zest and no cooked syrup, rolled to half an inch and cut into squares. It is easier and noticeably less flaky.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting the syrup cool before adding it to the flour.
  • Folding once instead of three times.
  • Skipping the overnight rest.
  • Frying on high heat, so the outside colours before the layers open.
  • Overloading the pan.

Health Benefits

  • A dry, shelf-stable snack that needs no refrigeration.
  • The powdered milk adds a little calcium and protein.
  • Small portions by design — they are bite-sized cubes.
  • High in sugar and deep-fried; this is a treat.

Cultural Note

Daddies are one of those snacks that seem to exist only in Uganda under that name, and nobody has established where the word came from. They belong with tea, and their entire appeal is the crunch — which is why the flakiness technique matters more than anything else in the recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: A week in an airtight container. They are better on day two.

Reheating: Not needed — they are served cool and stay crunchy.

Serve with: Ugandan tea, Hot cocoa, Mandazi
