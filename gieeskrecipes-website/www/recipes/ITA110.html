🫑 Peperonata

Peperonata

Sweet bell peppers slowly stewed with onions, tomatoes, and a touch of vinegar until soft and richly flavored — a versatile southern Italian side that improves with a day's rest.

Peperonata's success lies entirely in patience, letting the peppers and onions cook low and slow until they collapse into a soft, sweet, almost jammy stew rather than remaining crisp-tender vegetables.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pan with lid

  • 5 bell peppers, mixed colors, sliced into strips
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh basil, torn
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the olive oil in a large pan and add the onions and peppers with a pinch of salt.
  2. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring occasionally, for 15–20 minutes until softened and beginning to caramelize.
  3. Add the garlic and cook for another minute.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes, vinegar, and sugar, and stir to combine.
  5. Reduce the heat, cover, and simmer gently for 15–20 minutes, until the peppers are very soft and the sauce has thickened.
  6. Uncover for the final few minutes if the mixture needs to reduce further.
  7. Season to taste, finish with torn basil, and serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef Tips

  • Low, slow cooking is essential — rushing over high heat leaves the peppers unevenly cooked rather than meltingly soft.
  • This dish is often even better the next day, once the flavors have had time to meld.
  • A splash of vinegar balances the natural sweetness of the peppers and onions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking over heat that's too high, which can scorch the vegetables before they properly soften.
  • Serving immediately without letting the flavors rest and develop.
  • Using only one color of pepper, missing the natural sweetness variation that mixed colors provide.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Peperonata appears across southern Italy as a versatile side dish, condiment, or sandwich filling, prized for how well it keeps and how its flavor deepens with time.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 5 days; flavor improves after a day.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature or reheat gently on the stovetop.

Serve with: Crusty bread, Grilled meats or sausages, As a topping for bruschetta
