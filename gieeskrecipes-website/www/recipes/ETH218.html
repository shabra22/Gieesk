🥤 Ethiopian Fermented Barley Drink

ሻሚታ — Shamita

Ethiopian Shamita — a light, slightly sour fermented drink made from roasted barley flour and water, fermented for just 1 day. Between a porridge-drink and tella — less alcoholic than tella, more refreshing than borde. The quick fermented grain drink of the highlands.

Shamita is the lightest of the Ethiopian fermented grain drinks — made from roasted barley that is pre-cooked and fermented briefly, it is refreshing, slightly sour, and mildly alcoholic. It is the gateway fermented drink — between the non-alcoholic Atmit and the more alcoholic Tella.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Fermentation container, Straining cloth

  • 1 cup roasted barley flour
  • 4 cups water
  • 2 tbsp previous batch shamita or yogurt (starter)
  • 2 tbsp honey (optional sweetener)

Method

  1. Mix roasted barley flour with 2 cups warm water into a smooth paste.
  2. Cook gently 10 minutes, stirring. Cool to lukewarm.
  3. Add remaining 2 cups cold water and starter.
  4. Ferment covered at room temperature 12—24 hours.
  5. Strain through cloth. Sweeten with honey if desired.
  6. Serve chilled.

Chef Tips

  • Roasted barley is essential — raw barley produces flat flavour.
  • Short ferment — 1 day maximum for the light version.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Raw barley.
  • Over-fermenting — becomes too sour.

The Science

Roasted barley has undergone Maillard reactions — producing malty, complex flavour compounds (primarily pyrazines) that provide the characteristic shamita flavour distinct from tella or borde.

Cultural Note

Shamita is the highland farmer's refresher — the drink that occupies the social space between water and tella, light enough for working hours, flavourful enough for social gatherings. It is the humble daily drink of Ethiopia's grain-farming communities.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Consume within 2 days of straining.

Reheating: Serve cold.

Serve with: As a refreshing highland drink, During harvest season, As a non-coffee morning drink
