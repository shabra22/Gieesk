🥗 Abomu

Abomu — abom, kontomire abom

Steamed cocoyam leaves mashed in the asanka with koobi and kpakposhito, with hot palm oil poured over at the end.

Abomu is defined by its vessel: it is made in and eaten from the asanka, the Ghanaian earthenware grinding bowl, and the mashing is done there rather than in a pan. Four hundred grams of spinach or cocoyam leaves steam with onion, kpakposhito and salted koobi until everything is tender, then the whole lot is mashed in the asanka. Thirty millilitres of zomi — the deeper, unrefined palm oil — is heated and poured over. Tinned sardines in tomato sauce go in. It is served with boiled yam, plantain or cocoyam.

Ingredients

Equipment: Asanka (earthenware grinding bowl) and grinder, Steamer or pot, Small pan, for the oil

  • 400 g organic spinach or cocoyam leaves (kontomire)
  • 1 large onion, sliced
  • 20 g pettie belle chillies (kpakposhito)
  • 185 g salted fish (koobi)
  • 150 g tinned sardines in tomato sauce
  • 30 ml palm oil (zomi)
  • Salt, to taste

Noted in the method but absent from the ingredient list

  • Tomatoes

Method

  1. Steam the kontomire with the tomatoes, onion, pepper and koobi.
  2. Steam until the tomatoes, onions and kontomire leaves are tender.
  3. Transfer to the asanka and mash.
  4. Heat the palm oil in a small pan.
  5. Pour the hot oil over the mashed mixture.
  6. Fold in the tinned sardines.
  7. Serve with boiled yam, plantain or cocoyam.

Chef Tips

  • The asanka is the point. Mashing in earthenware with a wooden grinder gives a coarse, uneven texture that a blender cannot reproduce, and the bowl is also the serving dish.
  • Until the tomatoes, onions and kontomire leaves are tender is the cue, and it covers all three — the onion is usually the slowest.
  • Pour the oil over hot at the end rather than cooking in it. That keeps the zomi's flavour forward instead of cooking it off.
  • Zomi is palm oil that has been further processed to deepen its colour and flavour. Ordinary red palm oil will work and will taste lighter.
  • Thirty millilitres is only two tablespoons, which makes this a notably lean dish for a palm oil preparation.
  • Note that the method calls for tomatoes but the ingredient list has none, so the quantity is genuinely missing. Two medium tomatoes is a sensible reading, offered as such.
  • Koobi is salted dried tilapia and it is very salty indeed. Soak it before use and taste before adding any salt.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a blender instead of an asanka.
  • Cooking the palm oil in with everything else.
  • Adding salt before tasting, when the koobi is already salty.
  • Over-steaming so the leaves go grey.
  • Mashing it to a smooth purée rather than leaving it coarse.

Health Benefits

  • Cocoyam leaves are very high in vitamin A, vitamin C, iron and calcium.
  • Only two tablespoons of oil for four servings.
  • Sardines supply omega-3 fats, calcium and vitamin D.
  • Very low in carbohydrate before the accompaniment.

Cultural Note

An asanka is a shallow ribbed earthenware bowl, and food made in one is both ground and served in the same vessel — which is why abomu is named for the process rather than the ingredients. The ribs do the grinding, and the coarse result is deliberate; a smooth purée would be a different dish entirely.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature, or warm gently. It is often eaten cool.

Serve with: Boiled yam, Ampesi, Boiled cocoyam
