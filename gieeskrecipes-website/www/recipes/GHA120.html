Kofi Brokeman — grilled plantain with groundnuts

Just-ripe plantain grilled over charcoal and eaten with shelled roasted peanuts, skins still on — a complete meal in itself, inexpensive and filling.

The most Ghanaian thing on any roadside: plantain slices grilled over charcoal and handed over with a twist of roasted groundnuts. The plantain must be just ripe, cut on the diagonal into ovals, and the grill rack brushed with oil or lined with non-stick foil to keep it from sticking. Grill until brown and cooked on each side, perhaps five to ten minutes a side. No salt, no spice, nothing else — the sweetness of the plantain against the salt and fat of the peanuts is the whole dish. The name translates, roughly, as Kofi has no money.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill, or a gas grill, Non-stick aluminium foil (alternative to oiling the rack), Basting brush, Knife

  • Just-ripe plantains — no quantity is printed in the source; allow 1 per person
  • Oil, for the grill rack

To serve

  • Shelled roasted peanuts with their skins still on — sold ready-roasted, not prepared here

Do not salt the plantain. See the note.

    Method

    1. Build a fire in a charcoal grill, or fire up a gas grill.
    2. Brush oil on the grill rack to keep the plantain from sticking. Alternatively use non-stick aluminium foil and put the plantain directly on that.
    3. Cut the ends off a just-ripe plantain.
    4. Make a shallow cut just through the peel from end to end on one side, peel it, and remove any stringy fibres.
    5. Cut the plantain on the diagonal, making ovals on the cut ends, to make several slices.
    6. Grill the plantain pieces until they are brown and cooked on each side — perhaps 5 to 10 minutes per side.
    7. Monitor them carefully to prevent burning.
    8. Serve with shelled roasted peanuts, skins still on.

    Chef Tips

    • Just-ripe is the whole thing. Green plantain will not caramelise and over-ripe collapses through the bars.
    • Brown and cooked on each side, perhaps five to ten minutes a side. The wide range is honest — it depends entirely on your coals.
    • Oil the rack or use non-stick foil. Plantain welds itself to a bare grill.
    • Do not salt it. The peanuts carry the salt, and that contrast is the dish.
    • Cut on the diagonal for ovals rather than straight across. More surface for the grill and it is how it is sold.
    • The groundnuts come with their skins on and are bought ready-roasted. The source gives no recipe for them and none is needed.
    • A complete meal in itself, inexpensive and filling, in her own words. Eat it with your fingers from the paper.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Green or over-ripe plantain.
    • An unoiled rack.
    • Salting the plantain.
    • Cutting straight across instead of on the diagonal.
    • Walking away from the grill.

    Health Benefits

    • Plantain is high in resistant starch, potassium, vitamin A and vitamin B6.
    • Peanuts complete the amino acid profile the plantain lacks, making the pair a complete protein.
    • No added fat beyond what the rack needs, unlike fried plantain.
    • Vegan, gluten-free and high in fibre.

    Cultural Note

    Kofi Brokeman is the cheapest full meal in Ghana and its name is the joke at its own expense — Kofi is broke, so Kofi is eating plantain and groundnuts, and Kofi is doing fine. Grilled plantain with roasted peanuts is also, by accident of nutrition, a complete protein: the starch and the nut between them supply every amino acid a person needs, which is a good note on which to close a Ghanaian collection.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Eat at once. Grilled plantain goes leathery within the hour.

    Reheating: Briefly on a hot grill. It will not be as good.

    Serve with: Spicy Coated Groundnuts, Sobolo, Nkate Cake
