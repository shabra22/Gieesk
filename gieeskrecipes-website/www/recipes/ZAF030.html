🍪 Beskuit

Beskuit — boerebeskuit

Buttermilk dough proved twice, packed tight into a tin and baked as one slab, then broken along the seams and dried out overnight at 100C — a preserving technology that became breakfast.

Rusks are twice-baked bread, and the second bake is the point: eight hours at a very low temperature drives out every trace of moisture, and what is left keeps for months. That was the reason they existed on a trek and it is why farm kitchens still make them by the tinful. This version uses yeast rather than baking powder, which takes longer and gives a better crumb, and amasi works in place of buttermilk. The same dough, eaten fresh instead of dried, is mosbolletjies.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl or stand mixer with a dough hook, Greased baking tray or loaf tin, Damp cloth, Baking sheets, for drying

  • 750 ml buttermilk, or amasi
  • 215 g butter, melted
  • 20 g dry yeast (2 sachets)
  • 95 g (1/2 cup) sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda
  • 1.4 kg stone-ground cake flour — or 700 g cake flour plus 700 g white bread flour

Optional, choose one

  • 2 tbsp aniseed
  • 50 g raisins or chopped dates plus 1 cup bran
  • 50 g pecans plus 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 25 g dried cranberries plus 25 g chopped almonds

Method

  1. Combine the buttermilk, melted butter and yeast in a large bowl and stir until the yeast dissolves and starts to bubble.
  2. Add the sugar, salt and bicarbonate of soda and mix well.
  3. Add two thirds of the flour and stir until combined.
  4. Keep adding flour while mixing and kneading until it forms a workable dough. Stop when the dough feels right rather than when the flour runs out — the 1.4 kg is an upper bound.
  5. Add any of the optional extras at this stage.
  6. Put the dough in a lightly oiled bowl, lay clingfilm directly on the dough, cover with a damp cloth and leave somewhere warm until doubled, about 1 hour.
  7. Shape into even golf-ball-sized pieces, roll them neatly and pack them tightly in a single layer in a greased tray or loaf tin, touching each other.
  8. Cover with a damp cloth and prove until doubled again, about 45 minutes.
  9. Heat the oven to 180C and bake for 30 to 40 minutes, until golden brown and cooked through. Cool just enough to handle.
  10. Break the slab apart along the lines of the original balls, lay the pieces flat on baking sheets, and dry them in a 100C oven overnight — at least 8 hours, until completely dry with no give at all.
  11. Store in an airtight container.

Chef Tips

  • Pack the balls tightly so they bake into one slab. They need to touch, so they tear apart along soft seams rather than being cut.
  • Completely dry means completely. Any moisture left in the middle will mould in the tin within a fortnight.
  • Add flour by feel. Stone-ground flour varies enough that a fixed weight gives an unreliable dough.
  • Yeast and bicarbonate of soda together is deliberate — the yeast for flavour and crumb, the soda for lift.
  • Eaten fresh with butter and jam instead of dried, the same dough is mosbolletjies. Some bakers add an egg for a less crumbly rusk.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cutting the baked slab with a knife instead of breaking it, which gives smooth faces that dry unevenly.
  • Drying them for four hours instead of eight and finding them mouldy a week later.
  • Adding all 1.4 kg of flour regardless, which gives a dry, dense dough.
  • Spacing the dough balls apart on the tray, so they bake as separate rolls and there is nothing to break.

Health Benefits

  • Buttermilk or amasi provides protein and calcium and makes the dough easier to digest.
  • With the bran or seed variations they are high in fibre.
  • Lower in sugar than most breakfast baking — 95 g across twenty-four rusks.

Cultural Note

Beskuit is a preserving technology that outlived its own necessity, which is true of a lot of South African food. Dunking is not optional in practice: a properly dried rusk is too hard to bite, and the coffee is part of the recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight container at room temperature, several months if properly dried.

Reheating: Not needed. If they soften in humid weather, crisp them again at 100C for an hour.

Serve with: Coffee, for dunking, Rooibos tea, Nothing else — dunking is the whole ritual
