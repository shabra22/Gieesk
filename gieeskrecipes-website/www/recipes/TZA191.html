🌽 Tanzanian Roasted Maize Flour Snack

Bisi

Tanzanian Bisi — whole dried maize kernels dry-roasted in a heavy pan until they pop slightly and become nutty and crunchy. Sold at highland markets in small bags, eaten like popcorn but with a denser, nuttier crunch. An ancient Tanzanian grain snack.

Bisi is one of Tanzania's most ancient snacks — whole dried maize roasted until hard and nutty. Unlike popcorn it does not fully pop, instead becoming dense, crunchy, and deeply nutty. It is the grain snack of highland Tanzania, sold at markets in small newspaper packets.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy dry pan or wok

  • 2 cups whole dried maize kernels
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp chilli powder — optional

Method

  1. Heat a heavy dry pan over medium heat.
  2. Add maize kernels. Stir constantly.
  3. Roast 12—15 minutes until the kernels are lightly browned, slightly puffed, and nutty-smelling.
  4. Some may pop slightly — this is normal.
  5. Remove from heat. Season with salt and chilli.
  6. Cool before eating.

Chef Tips

  • Stir constantly — maize burns quickly.
  • They will not all pop — this is correct.
  • Cool before eating — they continue cooking briefly off the heat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not stirring — burning.
  • Over-roasting — too hard to eat comfortably.

The Science

Dried maize contains around 12% moisture. During dry roasting, this moisture creates steam within the kernel — expanding the interior slightly but not enough to rupture the pericarp (the tough outer shell) as popcorn does. The result is a puffed but still dense kernel with significant Maillard-reaction flavour development.

Cultural Note

Bisi is Tanzania's oldest recorded snack — the roasted grain that has been sold at highland markets since long before colonial times. It is the food of journeys, of waiting, and of the slow rhythm of highland life.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight container 2 weeks.

Reheating: Not needed.

Serve with: As a standalone snack, With cold drinks
