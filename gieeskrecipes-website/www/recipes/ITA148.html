Crescia Sfogliata

A layered, flaky flatbread from the Marche, its dough rolled thin, brushed with fat, and coiled repeatedly before being flattened and griddled — similar in technique to piadina but visibly layered.

Crescia sfogliata's lamination technique, similar to what's used for puff pastry, gives this humble griddle bread a genuinely flaky, layered texture that sets it apart from its flatter Romagnol cousin, piadina.

Ingredients

Equipment: Rolling pin, Large flat griddle or heavy skillet

  • 400 g (3 cups) 00 flour
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) warm water
  • 8 g (1½ tsp) salt
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) lard or olive oil, softened, plus more for laminating

Method

  1. Combine the flour, water, and salt into a smooth, elastic dough. Rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
  2. Divide the dough into portions and roll each out very thin.
  3. Brush each thin sheet generously with softened lard or oil.
  4. Roll the sheet up tightly into a log, then coil the log into a spiral.
  5. Flatten the spiral gently with your palm, then roll it out again into a round disc, preserving the internal layers.
  6. Cook on a hot, dry griddle or heavy skillet for 3–4 minutes per side, until lightly charred and cooked through, with visible flaky layers at the edges.
  7. Serve warm, torn into pieces or filled with cured meats and cheese.

Chef Tips

  • Rolling the dough as thin as possible before laminating is what creates the most distinct, flaky layers.
  • Be generous with the fat between layers — this is what creates the characteristic flakiness when cooked.
  • A hot, dry pan is essential for achieving the right char without drying the bread out.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skimping on the fat between layers, resulting in a flatter, less distinctly layered bread.
  • Rolling the final disc too thin after laminating, which can tear through the layers.
  • Cooking over heat that's too low, leaving the bread pale and tough rather than lightly charred and flaky.

Health Benefits

  • Can be made vegan by substituting olive oil for lard.

Cultural Note

Crescia sfogliata is a specialty of the Marche region, its distinctive lamination technique setting it apart from similar flatbreads found elsewhere in central Italy.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; store cooked bread wrapped for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Warm briefly on a hot dry pan or griddle.

Serve with: Cured meats, Fresh cheeses, Sautéed greens
