🍞 Pane di Matera

Pane di Matera

A protected, distinctively shaped durum wheat bread from Matera, its thick, deeply cracked crust and dense crumb the result of a long natural fermentation and traditional wood-fired baking.

Pane di Matera holds an official protected geographical status, its shape historically stamped with a family seal before being sent to communal ovens, and the bread's exceptionally long shelf life once made it a practical staple for the region's farming families.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Baking stone, Bread lame or sharp knife

  • 500 g (3⅓ cups) durum wheat semolina flour
  • 10 g (2 tsp) active dry yeast (or a natural starter)
  • 300 ml (1¼ cups) warm water
  • 10 g (1¾ tsp) salt

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in the warm water and let sit for 5 minutes.
  2. Combine the semolina flour and salt, then add the yeast mixture, kneading for 12–15 minutes until smooth and elastic — durum dough requires more kneading than standard wheat.
  3. Let rise, covered, for at least 4–6 hours at room temperature, or ideally overnight in the refrigerator for a slower, more flavorful fermentation.
  4. Shape the dough into its traditional horn or double-lobed shape.
  5. Let rise again for about 1–1.5 hours until nearly doubled.
  6. Score the top deeply and bake at 230°C (450°F) with steam (a pan of water in the oven) for the first 15 minutes, then reduce to 200°C (400°F) for another 40–45 minutes until deeply golden with a thick, cracked crust.
  7. Cool completely on a wire rack before slicing.

Chef Tips

  • Durum semolina dough needs more kneading than standard wheat flour to develop proper structure.
  • A long, slow fermentation is what develops the bread's characteristic depth of flavor.
  • Steam in the oven during the initial bake helps develop the bread's thick, deeply cracked crust.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Underkneading the dense durum dough, resulting in a poorer structure.
  • Rushing the fermentation, which misses much of the bread's traditional flavor development.
  • Skipping the steam step, resulting in a thinner, less characterful crust.

Cultural Note

Pane di Matera holds a protected geographical status, its distinctive shape and exceptional keeping quality reflecting centuries of tradition in the historic city of Matera.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store at room temperature for up to a week — this bread is known for keeping unusually well.

Reheating: Refresh briefly in the oven if it has softened.

Serve with: Olive oil for dipping, As an accompaniment to any meal
