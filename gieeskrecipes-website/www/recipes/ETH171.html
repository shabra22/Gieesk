🍳 Ethiopian Chechebsa Street Version

የጎዳና ጨጨብሳ — Ye-Godana Chechebsa

Addis Ababa Street Chechebsa — kita bread torn and tossed in a street stall's large flat pan with kibbeh and berbere, plated with a fried egg on top. The street breakfast version of the home dish — faster, more dramatic, eaten standing at the stall.

Street Chechebsa is what Addis Ababa morning food culture produces — the home dish of torn kita in kibbeh taken to street scale, the large flat pan (mitad) cooking enormous quantities, a fried egg always placed on top, eaten standing at the stall before work.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large flat pan or mitad

  • 2 kita flatbreads — torn into pieces
  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • 1 tsp berbere
  • 1 tsp mitmita
  • 2 eggs — fried sunny-side up
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Heat kibbeh in a large flat pan until hot.
  2. Add berbere. Stir 30 seconds in the hot kibbeh.
  3. Add torn kita pieces. Toss vigorously to coat every piece.
  4. Cook 3—4 minutes, tossing, until every piece is spiced and slightly crisped.
  5. Season.
  6. In a separate pan, fry eggs sunny-side up.
  7. Plate chechebsa. Top with fried egg. Sprinkle mitmita.

Chef Tips

  • Street chechebsa is always hotter than home chechebsa — more berbere.
  • The fried egg is the street version addition — non-negotiable.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not enough kibbeh — the coating is the point.

Cultural Note

Street Chechebsa is the Addis Ababa morning ritual — the breakfast stall with the mitad, the cloud of berbere-kibbeh steam rising in the morning air, the crowd of workers and students eating standing before heading to their day. It is urban Ethiopian life at its most immediate.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Chai, As a complete street breakfast
