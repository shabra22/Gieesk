🌽 Fried Polenta

Polenta Fritta

Fried Polenta transforms leftover set polenta into crisp, golden bites — crunchy outside, soft and creamy within — perfect as an appetizer or a rustic side dish.

A clever, resourceful use for cooled, leftover polenta, this dish is a beloved feature of Northern Italian trattorias, sliced into wedges or sticks and fried in olive oil until the surface turns deeply golden and shatteringly crisp.

Ingredients

Equipment: Skillet, Sharp knife, Paper towels, Tongs

  • 1 batch cooked Polenta, cooled and set in a dish for at least 2 hours
  • 60 g (½ cup) fine cornmeal or flour, for dredging
  • Olive oil, for shallow frying
  • Salt, to taste
  • Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for serving
  • Fresh sage leaves (optional)

Method

  1. Turn the cooled, set polenta out onto a cutting board and slice into wedges, sticks, or 2cm-thick rounds.
  2. Dredge each piece lightly in cornmeal or flour, shaking off the excess.
  3. Heat a generous layer of olive oil in a skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering.
  4. Fry the polenta pieces in batches, without crowding, for 3–4 minutes per side until deeply golden and crisp.
  5. If using sage, fry a few leaves in the same oil for the last 30 seconds until crisp, and set aside for garnish.
  6. Transfer to paper towels to drain briefly, then season immediately with salt.
  7. Serve hot, topped with grated Parmigiano-Reggiano and the crisp sage leaves.

Chef Tips

  • The polenta must be fully cooled and firm before slicing, or it will fall apart in the pan — refrigerating it overnight gives the cleanest slices.
  • Don't crowd the pan — overcrowding drops the oil temperature and results in soggy, rather than crisp, polenta.
  • A light dredge in cornmeal gives an extra-crunchy shell compared to flour alone.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Trying to fry polenta while it's still warm and soft — it needs to be fully set to hold its shape.
  • Using oil that isn't hot enough, leading to greasy rather than crisp results.
  • Flipping too early before a proper crust has formed, causing sticking and tearing.

Health Benefits

  • Naturally gluten-free when using pure cornmeal for dredging.
  • A good way to use leftovers and reduce food waste.

Cultural Note

Fried polenta is a classic example of Italian cucina povera — 'poor kitchen' cooking — turning humble leftovers into a genuinely craveable dish through nothing more than technique and good olive oil.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately after frying while crisp.

Reheating: Re-crisp in a hot oven or air fryer for a few minutes; avoid microwaving, which makes it soggy.

Serve with: Marinara dipping sauce, Aperitivo spreads, Antipasto platters
