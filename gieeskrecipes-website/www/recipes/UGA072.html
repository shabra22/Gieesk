🐟 Nkejje in Groundnut Paste

Nkejje — madoola

Small sun-dried lake fish given a little roasting over an open fire and then served in a groundnut sauce — the standard treatment for a fish that arrives on the stick it was dried on.

Nkejje are small broad-bodied fish from Lake Victoria, about six centimetres long, and the way they reach a market is part of the dish: caught in groups, dried in the sun immediately, pinned on sticks in horizontal rows, and carried to market on the same sticks after several days. They are commonly served in groundnut paste after a little roasting over an open fire. The fish plays a real ceremonial role in Buganda, particularly at child initiation and confirmation.

Ingredients

Equipment: Open fire or charcoal grill, Saucepan, Bowl

  • Sun-dried nkejje — no quantity is given in the source
  • Groundnut paste
  • Cold water, to dissolve the paste
  • Salt

Optional

  • Onion and tomato

Method

  1. Give the dried nkejje a little roasting over an open fire.
  2. Dissolve the groundnut paste in cold water to a smooth thick sauce.
  3. Bring the sauce to the boil and cook until it is ready.
  4. Add the roasted nkejje and salt.
  5. Simmer gently to bring the two together, and serve.

Chef Tips

  • A little roasting, not a proper cook. These are already dried through; the fire is for aroma and to firm them up before they go into a wet sauce.
  • Dissolve the groundnut paste in cold water first, as with every Ugandan groundnut sauce.
  • Add the fish after the sauce is made. Cooked in from the start they disintegrate.
  • No quantities are given in the source — this is prose rather than a numbered recipe, and nothing has been estimated to fill that.
  • Madoola are the largest and shiniest type of nkejje and are the ones to look for.
  • They come to market pinned on the sticks they were dried on, which is a good sign of freshness in the drying if the sticks are clean.
  • The fish is under real pressure — overfishing by commercial exporters, pollution, and the introduced Nile perch, which eats it.
  • The same sauce works with dried mukene, which is far easier to find.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Roasting them hard until they burn.
  • Dissolving the groundnut paste in hot water.
  • Adding the fish at the start of the sauce.
  • Buying dried fish that smells ammoniac.
  • Over-simmering until nothing is left whole.

Health Benefits

  • Eaten whole with the bones, so very high in calcium.
  • High in protein, omega-3, iron and vitamin B12.
  • Groundnut paste adds plant protein and vitamin E.
  • Contains fish and peanuts.

Cultural Note

Nkejje matters in Buganda beyond eating — it has a place in child initiation and confirmation ceremonies, and the lungfish mamba is one of the oldest totems of the kingdom's original clans. Lake Victoria's small fish carry ceremonial weight that the big commercial Nile perch, which eats them, does not.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The dried fish keeps for months. The finished sauce, 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of water.

Serve with: Matooke, Posho, Boiled Sweet Potatoes
