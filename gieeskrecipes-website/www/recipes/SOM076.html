🐠 Kalluun la Dubay

Kalluun la Dubay — Tilaabiya la Dubay

Fish coated in a blended marinade of garlic, ginger, turmeric, lemon, olive oil and — unexpectedly — six olives, then baked at 200C for half an hour.

Duban covers baking and grilling both, and Somali cooks treat them as one category, which is why the same recipe travels under both words. What makes this one worth having is the marinade: it is blended rather than rubbed, so it clings evenly, and it has olives in it, which is not something you would predict in a Somali fish dish and which gives the whole thing a salty, briny backbone. It is a weeknight dish and it takes half an hour.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or small food processor, Baking dish or tray, Paper towel

  • 400 g (14 oz) tilapia fillets, or another white fish

The marinade, blended

  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 50 g (2 inches) fresh ginger
  • 6 pitted olives
  • 45 ml (3 tbsp) olive oil
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) lemon juice
  • 1.25 ml (1/4 tsp) turmeric powder
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) salt
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) ground black pepper

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 200C.
  2. Blend the garlic, ginger, olives, olive oil, lemon juice, turmeric, salt and black pepper into a smooth marinade.
  3. Rinse the fillets and pat them thoroughly dry.
  4. Coat both sides with the marinade.
  5. Bake for 30 minutes, until the fish flakes easily.

Chef Tips

  • Pat the fish properly dry before the marinade goes on. Wet fish steams in its own liquid and the marinade slides off rather than clinging.
  • Blend the marinade rather than chopping it. Blended, it coats evenly; chopped, it sits in patches and the garlic catches.
  • The six olives are the surprising ingredient and they are worth keeping — they season it from the inside and make the salt do more work.
  • Thirty minutes at 200C suits a decent fillet. A thin one will be ready sooner, and there is no visual cue in the source other than the time, so check it at twenty minutes.
  • The Mogadishu and Kismayo whole-fish-over-coals version that people describe is not written down in any source I could reach. The nearest documented method is the Swahili samaki wa kupaka — slit the flanks, marinate an hour, grill in a basket about fifteen minutes turning and basting — but that is Swahili, and I am not going to relabel it Somali.
  • If you do take it to the coals, bring the marinade with you and keep half back for basting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Putting the marinade on wet fish.
  • Baking it past the point where it flakes — fish goes from perfect to dry in a couple of minutes.
  • Skipping the olives because they sound wrong.
  • Using an oily fish, which fights the lemon and turmeric.

Health Benefits

  • White fish is high in protein and low in saturated fat.
  • Baked rather than fried, with three tablespoons of olive oil for three servings.
  • Turmeric, ginger and garlic all come in raw and barely cooked.
  • Naturally low in carbohydrate and free of dairy.

Cultural Note

That duban means both baked and grilled tells you something about how the word travelled: the recipe is published in Somali, English, French and Arabic, and the Arabic title uses mashwi, grilled, while the English says baked. Nobody involved thinks these are different dishes, and the oven is simply where the coals used to be.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a moderate oven, covered.

Serve with: Bariis Iskukaris, Insalaaddo, Basbaas
