Rof — roff, the fish stuffing

The parsley, garlic, onion and scallion paste that goes into slits cut in the fish — about a teaspoon per steak, and it makes about a cup.

10mTotal time
10mPrep
8Servings
25Calories
EasyDifficulty

Not a garnish but a structural part of ceebu jen: a coarse pounded paste of three garlic cloves, a bunch of parsley, a white onion, three scallions, a tablespoon of chilli flakes and a tablespoon of black pepper, with an optional bouillon cube. Two two-inch slits are cut in each fish steak and filled with about a teaspoon each, and the rest coats the fish including the head and tail before it rests in the fridge. The recipe makes about a cup, enough for a five-pound fish cut into seven steaks.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle, or a blender, Sharp knife

  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 bunch parsley, coarsely chopped
  • 1 white onion, coarsely chopped
  • 3 scallions, chopped
  • 1 vegetable or fish bouillon cube (optional)
  • 1 tbsp chilli flakes
  • 1 tbsp freshly ground black pepper

Method

  1. Blend or pound all the ingredients to a coarse paste — not smooth.
  2. Cut two 2-inch slits in each fish steak.
  3. Fill each slit with about 1 teaspoon of rof.
  4. Coat the rest of the fish with what remains, including the head and tail.
  5. Refrigerate until you are ready to cook.

Chef Tips

  • Coarse, not smooth. A blitzed purée leaks out of the slits; a coarse paste stays put and holds its texture through the poaching.
  • Two slits per steak, about a teaspoon each. More than that and it falls out into the broth.
  • Stuff the head and tail too. They go into the pot in a proper ceebu jen and they carry a lot of the flavour.
  • A tablespoon each of chilli flakes and black pepper sounds like a lot for one cup — it is correct, and the fish dilutes it considerably.
  • Makes about a cup, which is the right amount for a five-pound fish in seven steaks. Scale with the fish, not with the rice.
  • Refrigerating after stuffing is part of the method, not just convenience: it lets the paste work into the flesh.
  • Senegalese cooks also spell it roff and build it differently — parsley with bird's eye chilli, garlic, pepper and salt is another documented version, without the onion or scallion.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Blending it to a purée.
  • Overfilling the slits.
  • Leaving the head and tail unstuffed.
  • Using it immediately rather than letting the fish rest.
  • Cutting the chilli and pepper because the quantity looks high.

Health Benefits

  • Parsley is exceptionally high in vitamin K and vitamin C.
  • Garlic and scallion supply organosulfur compounds.
  • Negligible calories, and it replaces salt as a flavour driver.
  • Vegan without the optional bouillon cube.

Cultural Note

Stuffing the fish rather than seasoning its surface is what makes Senegalese fish cookery distinctive, and the slits-and-paste technique is the first thing a Senegalese cook teaches about ceebu jen. The paste has at least three documented formulas, which is the usual state of a thing every household makes weekly.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated in a sealed jar. It darkens but keeps.

Reheating: Not applicable — used raw.

Serve with: Thiebou Jenn, Thiébou Dieun, Ceeb bu Weex
