🌽 Polenta

Polenta

Polenta is Northern Italy's answer to cornmeal porridge — slow-simmered coarse cornmeal that turns thick, golden, and comforting, serving as the base for countless regional dishes.

Once considered peasant food across Lombardy, Veneto, and Piedmont, polenta has long since earned a place on fine dining menus, valued for its simplicity and versatility — eaten soft and creamy right off the stove, or cooled, sliced, and grilled or fried.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed pot, Wooden spoon or whisk

  • 1.5 L (6 cups) water or stock
  • 250 g (1⅓ cups) coarse polenta cornmeal
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • 2 tbsp butter or olive oil

Method

  1. Bring the water or stock to a rolling boil in a heavy-bottomed pot with the salt.
  2. Reduce heat to a steady simmer and slowly pour in the polenta in a thin stream, whisking constantly to prevent lumps.
  3. Once fully incorporated, switch to a wooden spoon and stir frequently.
  4. Cook over low heat for 35–45 minutes, stirring every few minutes, until thick, smooth, and pulling away from the sides of the pot.
  5. Stir in the butter or olive oil in the final few minutes.
  6. Serve immediately while hot and soft, or pour into a dish to cool and firm up for slicing and grilling later.

Chef Tips

  • Whisk in the polenta gradually at the start — this is the single most effective way to prevent lumps.
  • Coarse polenta gives the best traditional texture; instant polenta cooks faster but has a less rustic bite.
  • Stir regularly throughout cooking to prevent scorching on the bottom.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the cornmeal too quickly, causing lumps that are hard to smooth out.
  • Under-cooking — raw polenta tastes gritty and starchy rather than smooth.
  • Walking away for too long, letting it catch and burn on the bottom.

  • Naturally gluten-free, made purely from corn.

Cultural Note

Polenta was historically a staple that sustained entire farming communities across Northern Italy through long winters, and remains deeply tied to regional identity in Veneto and Lombardy.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate cooled, set polenta for up to 4 days.

Reheating: Reheat soft polenta with a splash of water or stock, stirring over low heat. Slice and grill/fry firm polenta.

Serve with: Braised meats, Mushroom ragù, Sausages, Gorgonzola
