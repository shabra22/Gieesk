🐐 Goat Mucomo

Mucomo — mbuzi

Eight hundred grams of goat rubbed with twenty grams each of ginger, garlic and mixed spices plus a whole lemon, rested only fifteen to twenty minutes, and roasted.

This is goat muchomo with quantities, which is rare — the roadside version is seasoned by eye with smashed onion juice. Here the seasoning is weighed: twenty grams each of ground ginger, ground garlic and mixed spices to eight hundred grams of meat, with the juice of a whole lemon. The rest is short, fifteen to twenty minutes, which is enough for the lemon and the aromatics to take hold without the acid starting to cook the meat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Charcoal grill or oven, Skewers

  • 800 g goat meat
  • 20 g ground ginger
  • 20 g ground garlic
  • 20 g mixed spices
  • 1 lemon
  • Salt

Method

  1. Cut the goat into pieces.
  2. Mix the ground ginger, ground garlic, mixed spices and salt with the juice of the lemon.
  3. Rub the mixture thoroughly into the meat.
  4. Rest it 15 to 20 minutes.
  5. Roast over charcoal or in the oven, turning, until done through.
  6. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Twenty grams each of ginger, garlic and mixed spices is about four teaspoons of each — a genuinely heavy rub, and that is the point.
  • Fifteen to twenty minutes is all the rest this gets. Longer in lemon juice and the acid starts to firm the surface of the meat.
  • Ground ginger and garlic, not fresh, is what this source specifies. It behaves differently — it clings as a crust rather than sliding off.
  • Goat is lean and it toughens if you push it. Roast it to done and no further.
  • The Mubende goat is a Ugandan heritage breed kept for special occasions, and if you can find one it is what this dish was built around.
  • This is the quantified counterpart to the roadside muchomo in Muchomo, which uses smashed onion juice and no weighed spice at all. Both are real; this one is easier to repeat.
  • Salt to taste — the source gives no weight for it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Marinating it for hours in lemon juice.
  • Using fresh ginger and garlic and expecting the same crust.
  • Overcooking lean goat.
  • Roasting over flames instead of embers.
  • Cutting the pieces too small so they dry out.

Health Benefits

  • Goat is leaner than beef or lamb, with high protein, iron and vitamin B12.
  • Roasted, so much of the remaining fat renders away.
  • Ginger, garlic and lemon all go in raw to the rub.
  • No sugar and no oil in the seasoning at all.

Cultural Note

Goat is the celebration meat across most of Uganda, and a goat roasted for guests is a different order of hospitality from a chicken. That the Mubende breed is specifically kept for special occasions, rather than for daily meat, is worth knowing about how livestock is thought about here.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Briefly over coals or in a hot oven. It dries out fast.

Serve with: Matooke, Gonja, Kachumbari
