🌶️ Soupou Kandja with Palm Oil and Rice

Soupou kandja — Kandia soup

A tomato-based okra stew in a cup of palm oil: onions and garlic, blended tomatoes, fish, shrimp or beef, finely chopped okra simmered to a silky body.

The NKENNE printing is the simplified, tomato-forward kandia. A cup of palm oil is heated, onions and garlic cooked ‘until soft and fragrant’, blended tomatoes, hot peppers and seasonings cooked 8 to 10 minutes until slightly thickened, and fish, shrimp or beef added for 10 to 15 minutes. Finely chopped okra and water or stock then simmer 20 to 30 minutes until the okra turns silky, and the pot rests 5 to 10 minutes off the heat. It is the only kandia source here that builds on blended tomatoes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender

  • 1 to 1.5 pounds fresh okra, chopped finely
  • 1 cup palm oil (or red palm oil)
  • 1 to 2 pounds protein: fish (such as tilapia or snapper), shrimp, or beef
  • 2 large onions, chopped
  • 3 to 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 to 3 tomatoes, blended or chopped
  • 1 to 2 Scotch bonnet or hot peppers, adjust for heat
  • Salt, black pepper, dried shrimp or crayfish powder, bay leaves, thyme — quantities not printed
  • Water or stock as needed
  • Cooked rice, to serve

Method

  1. Heat the palm oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onions and garlic and cook until soft and fragrant.
  2. Add the blended tomatoes, hot peppers and seasonings. Cook 8 to 10 minutes, until the sauce thickens slightly.
  3. Add the fish, shrimp or meat, stir gently, and cook 10 to 15 minutes to infuse the flavours.
  4. Add the finely chopped okra and enough water or stock to reach the consistency you want. Reduce the heat and simmer 20 to 30 minutes, stirring occasionally; the okra thickens to a silky texture.
  5. Taste and adjust the seasoning. Let the stew rest off the heat 5 to 10 minutes.
  6. Serve with rice.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Until soft and fragrant’ and ‘until the sauce thickens slightly’ are the two printed cues.
  • The page says the stew often tastes better the next day.
  • Chop the okra very fine; larger pieces give a stringier texture.
  • This is a one-cup palm-oil stew. Cut back if you want it lighter — the page does not.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Chopping the okra too coarsely.
  • Skipping the rest off the heat.
  • Adding all the liquid at once — add as needed for the consistency you want.

Health Benefits

  • Okra supplies soluble fibre.
  • Tomato gives lycopene; fish or shrimp give lean protein.
  • A full cup of palm oil is rich in saturated fat; serve in moderation.

Cultural Note

The page ties the dish to Wolof resourcefulness — affordable local ingredients, a large shared bowl, and the Wolof idea of teranga, hospitality. The author writes from a Nigerian food blog, NKENNE, and the dish’s Senegalese framing is hers.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of water.

Serve with: Steamed rice, Attieke or simple bread (page suggestion)
