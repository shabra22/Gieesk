🍖 Bombette Pugliesi

Bombette Pugliesi

Small, tightly rolled pork bundles from Puglia, stuffed with cheese and cured meat, grilled until the outside chars while the cheese inside turns molten.

Bombette, meaning 'little bombs,' are a beloved feature of Puglian butcher shops and grills, thin slices of pork rolled around a simple filling and secured tightly enough to hold together over live fire, bursting with melted cheese at first bite.

Ingredients

Equipment: Meat mallet, Toothpicks, Grill or grill pan

  • 8 thin slices pork capocollo or loin, about 80g/3oz each
  • 8 slices pancetta or thin-cut bacon
  • 150 g (1½ cups) caciocavallo or provolone cheese, cut into batons
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Toothpicks or kitchen twine

Method

  1. If needed, gently pound the pork slices to an even thinness.
  2. Lay a slice of pancetta over each pork slice.
  3. Season with garlic, parsley, salt, and pepper, then place a baton of cheese at one end.
  4. Roll each slice up tightly around the cheese, securing with a toothpick to hold its shape.
  5. Brush the rolls with olive oil.
  6. Grill over medium-high heat, turning occasionally, for about 15–20 minutes until the pork is cooked through and nicely charred on the outside.
  7. Let rest for a couple of minutes before removing the toothpicks and serving hot.

Chef Tips

  • Roll the bombette tightly so the cheese stays sealed inside rather than leaking out during grilling.
  • A charcoal grill gives the most authentic smoky char, though a grill pan works well too.
  • Let the meat rest briefly after cooking so the melted cheese doesn't immediately spill out when cut.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rolling too loosely, causing the cheese filling to leak out over the fire.
  • Overcooking, which can dry out the relatively thin pork slices.
  • Skipping the securing toothpick, risking the rolls unraveling on the grill.

Cultural Note

Bombette originated in the Valle d'Itria area of Puglia and remain a fixture of local butcher shops, which often grill them fresh to order for customers.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; refrigerate leftovers for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a covered pan or oven.

Serve with: Grilled bread, A simple green salad, A robust Puglian red wine
