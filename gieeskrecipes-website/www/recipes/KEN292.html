🥚 Kenyan Eggs Benedict

Mayai ya Benedict ya Kenya

Kenyan Eggs Benedict — perfectly poached eggs on toasted English muffins with grilled back bacon or avocado, topped with a silky hollandaise infused with Kenyan lemon. The hotel brunch showpiece — technical, elegant, and completely satisfying.

Eggs Benedict is the benchmark brunch dish — the one that reveals a kitchen's technical skill instantly. Properly poached eggs, silky hollandaise that does not break, and perfectly toasted English muffins all at the same temperature simultaneously requires organisation and precision.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep saucepan for poaching, Bain-marie for hollandaise, Whisk, Slotted spoon

  • 4 large eggs — poached
  • 2 English muffins — halved and toasted
  • 4 rashers back bacon or 1 ripe avocado — sliced

Kenyan Lemon Hollandaise

  • 3 egg yolks
  • 1 tbsp water
  • 150g clarified butter — warm
  • 2 tbsp fresh Kenyan lemon juice
  • 1 tsp white wine vinegar
  • Salt, white pepper, and cayenne

Poaching

  • 2 litres water
  • 2 tbsp white wine vinegar

Method

  1. Make hollandaise: whisk yolks and water in a bain-marie until thick and ribbon-like. Remove from heat. Whisk in clarified butter drop by drop. Add lemon juice and vinegar. Season. Keep warm.
  2. Bring poaching water to a gentle simmer with vinegar. Create a gentle whirlpool.
  3. Crack each egg into a cup. Slide gently into the water.
  4. Poach for exactly 3 minutes for runny yolks.
  5. Remove with a slotted spoon. Drain on paper towels.
  6. Toast muffins. Top with bacon or avocado.
  7. Place poached egg on each muffin half.
  8. Spoon hollandaise generously over each egg.
  9. Dust with cayenne. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Clarified butter is essential for hollandaise — whole butter causes splitting.
  • The bain-marie temperature is critical — whisk yolks until they leave a ribbon, no hotter.
  • Fresh eggs poach better — the whites hold together rather than dispersing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Broken hollandaise — butter added too fast or too hot.
  • Over-poached eggs — hard yolk.
  • Letting anything cool before plating — everything must be served simultaneously warm.

The Science

Hollandaise is a warm emulsion — the egg yolk lecithin emulsifies the butter fat into the aqueous yolk base. If the temperature exceeds 70°C the yolk proteins coagulate and the emulsion breaks. Clarified butter (butterfat only, no water) creates a more stable emulsion than whole butter, which can introduce excess water.

Cultural Note

Eggs Benedict at a Nairobi hotel is both a statement of culinary ambition and a benchmark of kitchen skill. The Kenyan version — with avocado replacing bacon and sharp Kenyan lemon in the hollandaise — is a dish that confidently stakes Kenya's claim to world-class brunch culture.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Must be made and served immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: Fresh Tropical Juice, Kenyan Masala Chai (KEN131), Side of kachumbari
