🧂 Jollof Dry Spice Mix

Jollof dry spice mix

Thirty grams each of ground ginger and dried thyme lead a mix that also carries cinnamon, nutmeg and cubeb tail pepper.

EasyDifficulty

The dry mix that the jollof sauce and the one-pot rice are both seasoned with, and it is unusual in reaching for cinnamon, nutmeg and coriander alongside the ginger and thyme. Thirty grams of ground ginger, sixteen of garlic powder, ten of ground onion, five of smoked paprika, twenty of dried chilli flakes, thirty of dried thyme, and twelve and a half grams each of cinnamon, nutmeg and coriander, with sea salt, cubeb tail pepper and optional dried prawn powder. Blend it all in a spice grinder or pestle, store airtight in a cool dark place, and use within a few months.

Ingredients

Equipment: Spice grinder or pestle and mortar, Scales, Airtight container

  • 30 g ground ginger
  • 16 g garlic powder
  • 10 g ground onion
  • 5 g smoked paprika
  • 20 g dried chilli flakes
  • 30 g dried thyme
  • 12.5 g ground cinnamon
  • 12.5 g ground nutmeg
  • 12.5 g ground coriander
  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • 1 tsp freshly ground cubeb tail pepper
  • Scant 1 tsp dried ground prawn, shrimp or crayfish powder (optional)

Method

  1. Weigh out all the ingredients.
  2. Blend them together in a spice grinder or pestle.
  3. Store in an airtight container in a cool, dark place.
  4. Use within a few months.

Chef Tips

  • Cubeb tail pepper is the unusual one — Piper cubeba, a tailed peppercorn with a resinous, allspice-like note. Nothing substitutes precisely; black pepper is the nearest.
  • Thirty grams each of ginger and thyme makes those two the backbone. Everything else sits under them.
  • Use within a few months, by her own instruction. Ground spices at this scale lose their edge.
  • The optional prawn powder makes the mix non-vegetarian, so leave it out if that matters and the mix still works.
  • Be aware there are two published printings and they differ substantially — this one has onion, paprika and cubeb, the other has none of those and adds black pepper instead.
  • The second printing's weights also sum to a hundred and sixty grams against a printed yield of about a hundred and ninety, which does not add up. Treat yields in this family loosely.
  • Make it before you make either the jollof sauce or the one-pot rice, since both call for it by the tablespoon.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Substituting black pepper for cubeb without noticing the difference.
  • Keeping it more than a few months.
  • Mixing the two printings' proportions together.
  • Storing it somewhere warm or light.
  • Including the prawn powder in a vegetarian dish.

Health Benefits

  • Ginger, cinnamon and thyme all carry documented anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial compounds.
  • Negligible calories per use.
  • Vegan without the optional prawn powder.
  • Replaces salt-heavy bouillon in several dishes.

Cultural Note

Cinnamon, nutmeg and cubeb in a jollof seasoning look surprising until you remember that West Africa was a spice-trading coast long before it was a colony — grains of paradise went north across the Sahara and cubeb came the other way. A diaspora cook formalising all of it into a weighed blend is how that history gets kept usable.

Storage & Serving

Storage: A few months in an airtight container in a cool dark place, per the author.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Jollof One-Pot Rice, Jollof Sauce, Jollof Fried Chicken
