🍵 Swahili Beef Soup

Supu ya Mchuzi

Swahili Supu ya Mchuzi — beef bone broth simmered with coconut milk, Zanzibar spices, and fresh herbs. The coastal version of bone soup — richer, more aromatic, and more deeply flavoured than the mainland version through the addition of coastal spices and coconut milk.

Swahili beef soup is the coastal enrichment of the mainland bone broth — the same restorative, nourishing preparation but elevated by Zanzibar spices and a final addition of coconut milk. Given to the sick, eaten before a long journey, and served at coastal celebrations as a starter.

Ingredients

Equipment: Very large pot

  • 800g beef bones — blanched
  • 3 litres water
  • 200ml coconut milk
  • 2 onions — halved
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger — sliced
  • 1 stalk lemon grass — bruised
  • 3 cardamom pods
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 4 cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup coriander
  • Lime wedges

Method

  1. Blanch bones 5 minutes. Drain and rinse.
  2. Place bones, water, onion, garlic, ginger, lemon grass, and whole spices in a large pot.
  3. Bring to a boil. Skim foam.
  4. Simmer on lowest heat for 75—90 minutes.
  5. Strain. Return to pot.
  6. Add coconut milk. Simmer gently 8 minutes.
  7. Garnish with coriander. Serve with lime.

Chef Tips

  • The whole spices are the key coastal signature.
  • Add coconut milk only after straining — never boil it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Hard boiling — cloudy broth.
  • Boiling coconut milk.

Cultural Note

Swahili bone soup is the coastal version of Tanzania's most restorative food — given to the sick and the recovering, perfumed with the spices of the island, enriched with coconut milk.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Heat to boiling before serving.

Serve with: As a standalone starter, With chapati
