🍬 Ethiopian Sesame Brittle

ሰሊጥ ቅቤ — Selit Brittle

Ethiopian Sesame Brittle — sesame seeds caramelised in honey and set into thin, crispy sheets. The Ethiopian sweet street candy — sold at every market in thin slabs, an ancient confection made from Ethiopia's two most precious produce: sesame and honey.

Ethiopian sesame brittle is the oldest candy in Ethiopia — sesame has been cultivated in Ethiopia for thousands of years and honey is one of the country's most important products. Together they produce a confection of remarkable simplicity and satisfaction.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pan, Baking sheet lined with parchment

  • 1 cup white sesame seeds
  • 3 tbsp raw honey
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • Pinch of salt

Optional: ¼ tsp ground cardamom

    Method

    1. Toast sesame seeds in a dry pan over medium heat — 3 minutes until golden.
    2. Add honey, sugar, and salt. Stir continuously.
    3. Cook over medium heat 5—6 minutes until the mixture turns deep golden.
    4. It should reach the hard crack stage (150°C).
    5. Quickly pour onto lined baking sheet.
    6. Spread thin with an oiled spatula.
    7. Cool 10 minutes until completely hard.
    8. Break into pieces.

    Chef Tips

    • Speed is essential at the pouring stage — the brittle sets quickly.
    • The hard crack stage (150°C) is where the sugar becomes brittle when cooled.
    • Oil your spatula before spreading — the mixture sticks aggressively.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not reaching hard crack temperature — sticky rather than brittle.
    • Slow pouring — sets before spreading.

    The Science

    At 150°C (hard crack stage), virtually all the water has evaporated from the sugar mixture and the sucrose has undergone significant inversion. When cooled, this low-water sugar matrix solidifies into an amorphous glass — the brittle snap comes from the absence of water crystalline structure.

    Cultural Note

    Ethiopian sesame brittle is the market sweet that connects present-day Ethiopia to its ancient past — sesame cultivation in Ethiopia predates recorded history, and the combination of sesame and honey has been a sweet for millennia. Buying a piece from a market vendor wrapping it in newsprint is a sensory experience that connects to centuries of Ethiopian market life.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Airtight container — 2 weeks.

    Reheating: Not applicable.

    Serve with: As a market sweet, With coffee, As a celebration candy
