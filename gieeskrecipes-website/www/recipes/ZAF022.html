🍅 Tamatiebredie

Tamatiebredie

Lamb browned hard, then stewed for two hours in nothing but tomato and onion — no stock and no water — with whole cinnamon, cardamom and clove, and white pepper at the end.

A bredie is a slow stew built on one vegetable, and the tomato one is the best known. The word probably comes from the Malagasy breedi for spinach, recorded at the Cape as early as 1815 and brought by enslaved Malagasy people — which is why a dish that now means tomato and mutton carries a name that originally meant greens. The defining rule is what you leave out: no stock, no water. The meat braises in the juices of 1.2 kg of tomatoes and two onions, and anything else you add dilutes it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large frying pan, for browning, Heavy casserole with a tight lid, Muslin bag for the whole spices (optional)

    The meat

    • 1.5 kg stewing lamb or mutton — knuckles, neck chops, rib, or a mixture
    • 2 to 3 tbsp vegetable or olive oil
    • Salt and freshly ground black pepper

    The base

    • 2 large onions, finely chopped
    • 2 cinnamon quills
    • 4 cardamom pods, lightly crushed
    • 6 whole cloves
    • 1 tsp freshly ground white pepper, plus more to serve
    • 1 tsp ground ginger
    • 1/2 tsp paprika
    • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
    • 2 tbsp tomato paste
    • 3 cloves garlic, crushed
    • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, finely grated

    The tomatoes

    • 1.2 kg ripe tomatoes, roughly chopped — or 800 g fresh plus one 400 g tin chopped tomatoes
    • 3 to 4 bay leaves
    • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
    • 3 medium potatoes, peeled and cubed (optional)

    To serve

    • Steamed basmati rice

    Method

    1. Pat the lamb dry and season it all over with salt and black pepper. Dry meat browns; wet meat steams.
    2. Brown the meat in batches in a splash of oil in a large pan over high heat, 3 to 4 minutes a side, until deep golden. Do not crowd the pan. Snip through the fat and connective tissue on neck chops first so they do not curl.
    3. In the casserole over medium heat, sauté the onions in oil for 5 to 6 minutes until softened and lightly golden, scraping up any sticky bits.
    4. Add the cinnamon, cardamom and cloves and cook for 1 minute. Then add the white pepper, ground ginger, paprika and cumin and stir for a minute more, until fragrant.
    5. Add the tomato paste, garlic and fresh ginger and cook for another minute, stirring.
    6. Add the chopped tomatoes and bay leaves, stir to combine, and add the red wine vinegar.
    7. Return the meat to the pot with the potatoes if you are using them, pressing everything down so it is covered by the tomato mixture. Add no stock and no water.
    8. Bring to a gentle simmer, cover tightly and cook on low for 1 1/2 to 2 hours, until the meat falls off the bone, stirring occasionally.
    9. Taste and adjust the seasoning. If the gravy is thin, simmer uncovered for a few minutes to reduce it.
    10. Serve with steamed basmati rice and plenty more white pepper.

    Chef Tips

    • No stock and no water. This is the rule that makes it a bredie rather than a lamb-and-tomato stew, and the tomatoes give up more liquid than you expect.
    • White pepper rather than black, and a good deal of it. It is the note people recognise without being able to name.
    • Whole cinnamon, cardamom and cloves, not ground. Tie them in a muslin bag if you would rather not find them on the plate.
    • Trim the very fatty pieces or the gravy turns greasy. Some fat is wanted; a layer of it is not.
    • If the tomatoes are sharp, a little sugar balances them. Add it at the end, once you can taste what you have.
    • For the midweek version, make it with 500 g of sliced sausage in place of the lamb and add the sugar only once the potatoes are completely soft — sugar stops potatoes softening.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding stock or water, which is the commonest way to end up with soup rather than bredie.
    • Crowding the pan while browning, so the meat greys instead of colouring.
    • Using ground spice in place of whole, which makes the sauce muddy and dusty.
    • Letting the tomato catch on the base over too high a heat — this needs a genuinely low simmer for two hours.

    Health Benefits

    • Cooked tomato is a good source of lycopene, absorbed better cooked than raw.
    • Lamb provides protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
    • Over a kilogram of tomatoes and two onions make it a stew with real vegetable content.

    Cultural Note

    Bredies come in as many kinds as there are vegetables to build them on — tamatiebredie, waterblommetjiebredie, koolbredie, boontjiebredie, pampoenbredie, wortelbredie. The form belongs to the Cape Muslim and Cape Dutch kitchens at once, which is to say it belongs to a kitchen where those two were never really separable.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Notably better the next day.

    Reheating: Reheat gently on the hob with a splash of water if it has tightened.

    Serve with: Steamed basmati rice, Blatjang, Fresh bread
