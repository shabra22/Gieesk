🍅 Tamarind Kani Mustard Sauce

Sauce kani au tamarin

Onion, garlic, plum tomatoes, a Scotch bonnet and a bay leaf simmered about 15 minutes, then tamarind paste, sugar and fish sauce stirred in and the sauce pureed smooth — the condiment for slow-roasted dibi lamb.

Thiam’s tamarind kani sauce accompanies his dibi-style leg of lamb. A tablespoon of peanut oil cooks a coarsely chopped onion and a clove of garlic over medium-low heat, stirring often, until soft and fragrant; six plum tomatoes, a chopped Scotch bonnet and a dried bay leaf are added and simmered about 15 minutes, with a little water if needed. Tamarind paste, sugar and fish sauce go in; the sauce is seasoned, the bay leaf removed, and everything pureed until smooth. It makes about 1 cup and keeps up to 3 weeks airtight in the refrigerator. The title says ‘mustard sauce’ but the ingredient list contains no mustard (the lamb recipe, not this sauce, uses Dijon); this is flagged and not corrected.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Blender or immersion blender

  • 1 tablespoon peanut oil
  • 1 large yellow onion, coarsely chopped
  • 1 large garlic clove, chopped
  • 6 plum tomatoes, coarsely chopped
  • 1 Scotch bonnet pepper, coarsely chopped
  • 1 dried bay leaf
  • 2 tablespoons tamarind paste
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 2 tablespoons fish sauce
  • Salt and pepper, to taste — quantities not printed

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a saucepan over medium-low heat. Cook the onion and garlic, stirring often, until soft and fragrant. The page prints no time for this step.
  2. Add the tomatoes, Scotch bonnet and bay leaf. Simmer about 15 minutes, stirring occasionally and adding a little water if needed, until thick like a chutney.
  3. Stir in the tamarind paste, sugar and fish sauce. Season with salt and pepper. Remove the bay leaf and puree until smooth.
  4. Store airtight in the refrigerator for up to 3 weeks.

Chef Tips

  • Three printed cues: ‘soft and fragrant’, ‘thick like a chutney’, ‘until smooth’.
  • Take out the bay leaf before blending.
  • Scotch bonnet heat varies; add it gradually.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Looking for mustard in the sauce — the printed list has none.
  • Skipping the water top-up so the tomatoes catch.
  • Leaving the bay leaf in the blender.

Health Benefits

  • Tomatoes and tamarind add acidity; fish sauce adds salt.

Cultural Note

‘Kani’ is the Senegalese hot-pepper sauce family (compare sosu kaani, Sosu Kaani, Habanero Chile Sauce, and Mendy’s kanni, Kanni, Hot Sauce for Accara). This version is Thiam’s: tamarind and fish sauce give a sweet-sour savoury depth that the other two lack.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Up to 3 weeks, airtight, refrigerated.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Slow-roasted dibi lamb, Dibi (Dibi, Grilled Lamb and Onions with Mustard, Dibi Hausa, Senegalese Grilled Beef Kebabs), Baguette
