🫘 Moin Moin in Cups

Moin moin

The shortcut version — a stew cooked separately and folded into the beans, steamed in cups under foil, with no beating at all.

This is moin moin built the opposite way round. Instead of blending raw peppers into the batter and beating air into it, you cook a proper little stew first — oil, onion, tomato and pepper, fish and seasoning, fried until the tomato's sourness has gone — and fold that into the ground beans. There is no aeration step whatsoever, which makes it denser than the beaten version but far quicker and more forgiving. Steamed in cups under kitchen foil, it is done in forty to forty-five minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Frying pan, Heatproof cups or ramekins, Kitchen foil, Large pot with a lid

  • 1 cup brown or black-eyed beans, washed and peeled
  • 1/2 cup flaked steamed mackerel
  • 2 cooking spoons tomato and pepper blend
  • 1 tsp ginger paste
  • 1-2 cooking spoons vegetable oil
  • Crayfish seasoning, to taste
  • Salt to taste
  • Cups for steaming

Used in the method but not listed in the original

  • 1 onion

Method

  1. Wash the beans to remove the skin. Blend until smooth with very little water. The easiest route is to wash the beans the night before, soak them in water and leave them in the fridge — it goes much easier on your blender.
  2. Heat the oil and fry the onion and the tomato and pepper blend. Add the fish and the seasoning. Taste for salt and correct the seasoning. Cook the stew until the tomato sourness goes off.
  3. Pour the stew into the ground beans, add the ginger and mix well.
  4. Add a little water to the beans if it is too thick. The consistency should not be too runny either.
  5. Scoop the bean mixture into cups and place them in a big pot. Cover the cups with kitchen foil.
  6. Add water at the base of the pot to help steam the beans. Add a little water at a time so the water does not cover the beans.
  7. Moin moin will cook in 40 to 45 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • Cooking the stew first is the whole difference. It drives off the tomato's sourness before it ever reaches the beans, which the beaten version relies on the long steam to do.
  • An overnight cold soak in the fridge is her peeling method, and it is gentler on a domestic blender than pulsing.
  • There is no beating step. Accept that this will be denser than Moin Moin — it is a different texture, not a failure.
  • Foil over each cup, not over the pot. It stops condensation dripping in and watering the top.
  • Water at the base only, added a little at a time. The cups must never be submerged.
  • Blend with very little water. The stew brings moisture of its own.
  • Ginger is unusual in moin moin and gives it a noticeably warmer finish. A teaspoon of paste is enough.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Folding in a raw tomato blend instead of a cooked stew.
  • Letting the water rise over the cups.
  • Blending the beans with too much water before the stew goes in.
  • Leaving the cups uncovered.
  • Expecting the light, risen texture of the beaten version.

Cultural Note

Steaming moin moin in cups rather than leaves is the ordinary urban compromise, and it changes the dish in a way everybody notices and nobody can quite fix: the leaf contributes flavour as well as a vessel. The trade is convenience and a neat shape against that faint grassy perfume, and most Nigerian kitchens make it both ways depending on the occasion.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated in the cups.

Reheating: Steam 10 minutes, or microwave covered.

Serve with: Jollof Rice, Pap or custard, Eko
