🌽 Kenyan Boiled Maize

Mahindi ya Kuchemsha

Mahindi ya Kuchemsha is the soft, tender counterpart to roasted maize — whole cobs boiled in salted water until the kernels are sweet, milky, and perfectly cooked. One of the simplest and most satisfying snacks in Kenya.

Boiled maize is a staple of Kenyan street food stalls everywhere. Large pots of salted water bubble constantly, keeping freshly cooked cobs ready for the next customer. The boiling method preserves the natural milky sweetness of fresh young maize in a way that roasting cannot — the kernels stay plump, juicy, and tender. Served with lemon and salt, it is simple food at its very best.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid, Tongs

  • 4 fresh maize cobs
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt — for boiling
  • 1 lemon — halved
  • 1 tsp salt — for serving
  • 1 tsp chilli powder — optional

Method

  1. Husk the maize cobs and remove all the silk.
  2. Bring a large pot of salted water to a rolling boil.
  3. Add the maize cobs. Cover and boil for 25—35 minutes until the kernels are tender when pierced with a knife.
  4. Remove from the water and allow to drain briefly.
  5. Mix the serving salt and chilli powder together.
  6. Dip the cut lemon into the salt mixture and rub generously over the hot cob.
  7. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Use the freshest, youngest maize available — the milkier the kernels the sweeter the result.
  • Do not overcook — overcooked maize becomes starchy and loses its sweetness.
  • Adding a pinch of sugar to the boiling water enhances the natural sweetness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking — 35 minutes maximum for most maize.
  • Using old, overripe maize — starchy and tough.

The Science

Boiling maize in salted water gelatinises the starch granules in the kernels, making them soft and tender. Salt in the water seasons the kernels throughout rather than just on the surface.

Cultural Note

Boiled maize is one of the most universal Kenyan street foods — sold from large steaming pots at roadsides, markets, and bus stops across every county.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Microwave briefly with a damp paper towel.

Serve with: Kenyan Chai, Mishkaki, Roasted Maize, Bhajia
