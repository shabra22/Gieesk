🫕 Kenyan Millet Porridge

Uji wa Wimbi

Uji wa Wimbi — Kenya's most universally consumed breakfast. Warm finger millet porridge, gently sweetened with sugar or honey and finished with milk. Nourishing, gluten-free, and beloved across every Kenyan community from infancy to old age.

Every Kenyan who has had a hard morning knows the comfort of a cup of uji wa wimbi. The slightly earthy, nutty finger millet flour transforms into a smooth, warm, dark porridge with gentle sweetening and a splash of milk. It is the first food of infants, the breakfast of farmers, and the comfort of the elderly — a food that connects generations.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium saucepan, Wooden spoon

  • 1 cup finger millet flour (wimbi)
  • 4 cups water — divided
  • 3—4 tbsp sugar or honey
  • ½ cup milk — optional
  • Pinch of salt — optional

Method

  1. Mix millet flour with 1 cup cold water until completely smooth — no lumps.
  2. Bring remaining 3 cups water to a boil.
  3. Reduce heat to medium. Pour millet slurry in a steady stream while stirring constantly.
  4. Continue stirring for 5—7 minutes until smooth and thickened.
  5. Add sugar or honey. Stir to dissolve.
  6. Add milk if using. Simmer 2 more minutes.
  7. Serve hot in cups or bowls.

Chef Tips

  • Always make a cold water slurry first — adding dry flour to boiling water creates lumps.
  • Stir constantly after adding the slurry — the moment you stop, lumps form.
  • Adjust thickness by adding more water for a thinner, drinkable porridge.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Dry flour into boiling water — always make a slurry first.
  • Stopping stirring — produces lumps.

The Science

Making a cold slurry distributes starch granules evenly in water before gelatinisation begins. This prevents clumping by ensuring each granule is surrounded by water before heat causes rapid swelling. See KEN029 for the full science explanation.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best consumed fresh.

Reheating: Add water and warm gently over low heat, stirring.

Serve with: Mandazi, Chapati, Boiled Eggs, Sweet Potatoes, Groundnuts
