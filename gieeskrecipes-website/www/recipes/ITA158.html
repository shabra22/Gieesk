Anguilla in Umido

Eel gently stewed in tomato and white wine with bay leaves, a traditional dish of the Po Delta and Venetian lagoon, where eel fishing has a centuries-old history.

Eel from the brackish waters of the Po Delta and Comacchio has been prized in Veneto and Emilia-Romagna for centuries, and this simple stew lets the rich, distinctive fish stand on its own against a light tomato and wine base.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed pan with lid

  • 800 g (1.75 lb) eel, cleaned and cut into pieces
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry white wine
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the olive oil in a heavy pan and sauté the onion until softened.
  2. Add the garlic and cook briefly, then add the eel pieces, turning to sear lightly on all sides.
  3. Pour in the wine and let it reduce slightly.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes and bay leaves, season, and bring to a gentle simmer.
  5. Cover and cook for 25–30 minutes until the eel is tender and the sauce has thickened.
  6. Uncover for the final few minutes if the sauce needs to reduce further.
  7. Finish with fresh parsley and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Ask your fishmonger to clean and cut the eel, as this can be tricky to do yourself.
  • Eel releases some of its own fat as it cooks, contributing richness to the finished sauce.
  • Serve with plenty of bread or polenta to soak up the flavorful sauce.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the eel, which can become tough rather than staying tender.
  • Using a thin, watery tomato sauce that doesn't stand up to the rich fish.
  • Skipping the initial sear, which builds flavor before the braise.

Health Benefits

  • Eel is a good source of protein and omega-3 fatty acids.

Cultural Note

Eel has been fished from the Po Delta and Comacchio lagoon for centuries, remaining a traditional specialty of Veneto and neighboring Emilia-Romagna's wetland communities.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop.

Serve with: Soft polenta, Crusty bread, A dry white wine from Veneto
