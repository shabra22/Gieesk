Atwemo — twisted cakes

Diamonds of nutmeg dough pulled through their own slits and fried at 365 degrees — if they sink and stay there, the oil is not hot enough.

Atwemo are crisp twisted biscuits, rolled thin, cut into inch-long diamonds, slit in the centre and pulled through themselves to twist. Four cups of flour with two teaspoons of baking powder, half a cup of sugar, half a teaspoon of nutmeg, half a cup of margarine, an egg and three quarters of a cup of milk. The oil goes to about 365 degrees, and the test is the best in the collection: if the oil is not hot enough they fall to the bottom and stay there; if it is too hot they bounce up immediately and brown before they are cooked through. They should be quite golden — perhaps a little browner than you think at first.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl and sifter, Rolling pin and waxed paper, Knife, Deep fryer or deep heavy pan, Long-handled slotted spoon, Paper-towel-lined colander

  • 4 cups flour
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • 1/2 tsp ground nutmeg
  • 1/2 cup margarine — 1 cube. In Ghana they use margarine; butter gives a slightly different texture
  • 1 egg
  • 3/4 cup milk — in Ghana, half evaporated milk and half water
  • 1 tsp vanilla flavoring
  • Vegetable oil for deep frying

Variations: instead of nutmeg, use grated orange rind, caraway or aniseed, or leave out the vanilla.

    Method

    1. Sift the flour, salt, nutmeg and baking powder together in a large bowl.
    2. Rub or cut the margarine into the sifted ingredients with a pastry blender or your hands.
    3. Add the sugar and mix with a spoon.
    4. Beat the egg slightly with a fork, add the milk and vanilla, and mix together.
    5. Add the liquid to the flour mixture and mix well, then knead lightly but not enough to make it tough. Add a little more flour if it is sticky, a little more milk if it seems too dry and will not hold together.
    6. Divide the dough into 4 equal parts.
    7. Roll each out to between 1/8 and 1/4 inch — thinner for harder, crispier atwemo.
    8. Cut into strips about 1/2 inch wide, then cut diagonally into diamonds about an inch long.
    9. Cut a slit in the centre of each diamond.
    10. Pick up a diamond, push one end through the slit and pull it all the way through to twist it.

    If you do not pull the dough all the way through you get what her children called 'birds' — they taste fine.

    1. Fill a deep fryer or pan no more than half full with oil and heat to about 365 degrees Fahrenheit.
    2. Carefully but quickly slip a couple of dozen atwemo in one by one. Do not drop them and splatter the oil.
    3. If they fall to the bottom and stay there the oil is not hot enough. If they bounce up immediately and brown before cooking through, it is too hot.
    4. Stir frequently and turn them so they brown on both sides. They should be quite golden — perhaps a little browner than you think at first. It takes only a few minutes a batch.
    5. Remove with the slotted spoon into a paper-towel-lined colander to cool.
    6. Store covered in an airtight container.

    Chef Tips

    • The sink-or-bounce test is the best oil cue in this collection and needs no thermometer: sinking and staying down means too cool; bouncing straight up and browning fast means too hot.
    • Quite golden, perhaps a little browner than you think at first. Under-browned atwemo are soft rather than crisp.
    • Roll thin for crisp. Her own preference is the thin end of the eighth-to-quarter-inch range.
    • Never fill the pan more than half full. It bubbles up hard when the dough goes in.
    • Knead lightly. Over-kneading these makes them tough, and the same applies when you re-roll the scraps.
    • Skip the twisting if you like and just cut triangles — but cook the untwisted ones separately, because the cooking times vary a bit.
    • They freeze beautifully, so a large batch is worth it. Margarine rather than butter is what they use in Ghana and it does change the texture.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Frying at the wrong temperature and ignoring the sink-or-bounce signal.
    • Pulling the diamond only part way through, giving 'birds'.
    • Over-kneading, especially the re-rolled scraps.
    • Filling the pan more than half with oil.
    • Mixing twisted and untwisted shapes in one batch.

    • Portion-controlled by nature, since they are small.

    Cultural Note

    Atwemo are the Ghanaian tin-of-biscuits snack, made in big batches at Christmas and Easter and kept for visitors, and the twist is the whole craft of them. The detail that half-pulled ones become 'birds' is the sort of thing only a cook who taught children to make them would write down.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 weeks in an airtight container. They freeze beautifully.

    Reheating: A low oven briefly to re-crisp.

    Serve with: Bissap, Sobolo, Ice cream or a fruit salad
