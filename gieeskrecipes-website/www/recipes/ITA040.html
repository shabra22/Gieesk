Pappardelle al Cinghiale

Pappardelle al Cinghiale pairs wide, silky ribbons of fresh pasta with a rich, wine-braised wild boar ragù — one of Tuscany's most celebrated and rustic autumn dishes.

Wild boar has roamed Tuscany's hills for centuries, and this dish channels that rugged terroir directly onto the plate — the meat marinated overnight in red wine and aromatics, then braised long and slow until deeply tender and richly flavored.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl for marinating, Large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven, Large pot for pasta

  • 1 batch Fresh Egg Pasta Dough, cut into pappardelle
  • 1 kg (2.2 lb) wild boar shoulder, cut into large chunks
  • 750 ml (1 bottle) full-bodied red wine, divided
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 4 juniper berries
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 carrot, finely chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for serving

Method

  1. The night before, combine the boar with two-thirds of the red wine, bay leaves, juniper berries, and rosemary in a bowl. Cover and marinate in the refrigerator overnight.
  2. The next day, remove the boar from the marinade, pat dry, and strain the marinade, reserving the liquid.
  3. Heat the olive oil in a large heavy pot over medium-high heat. Brown the boar in batches on all sides, then set aside.
  4. Add the onion, carrot, and celery to the same pot and cook until softened, about 8 minutes.
  5. Add the garlic and cook for 1 minute, then return the boar to the pot.
  6. Pour in the reserved marinade and remaining wine, scraping up any browned bits.
  7. Add the crushed tomatoes, season with salt and pepper, and bring to a simmer.
  8. Reduce heat to low, cover, and braise for 2½–3 hours, until the meat is fall-apart tender.
  9. Remove the meat, shred it with two forks, discarding any excess fat, and return it to the sauce, simmering uncovered for 10–15 minutes to thicken.
  10. Cook the fresh pappardelle in salted boiling water for 1–2 minutes until al dente.
  11. Toss the drained pasta with the ragù and serve with grated Parmigiano.

Chef Tips

  • The overnight marinade is essential for tenderizing the boar and mellowing its distinctive gamey flavor.
  • Shredding the meat rather than leaving it in large chunks helps it integrate throughout the sauce and cling to the wide pappardelle ribbons.
  • If wild boar is unavailable, pork shoulder makes a reasonable substitute, though the flavor will be milder.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the marinade, leaving the meat's gamey flavor too assertive and the texture tougher.
  • Rushing the braise — wild boar needs the full simmering time to become truly tender.
  • Using thin pasta instead of wide pappardelle, which can't hold up to this hearty, chunky sauce.

Health Benefits

  • Wild boar is leaner than most farmed pork, with a good protein-to-fat ratio.
  • A good source of iron from the meat.

Cultural Note

Wild boar hunting has deep roots in Tuscan rural culture, and cinghiale dishes are especially popular in autumn and winter, often served at agriturismo (farm-stay) restaurants throughout the region.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The ragù keeps refrigerated for up to 4 days, or frozen for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently over low heat, then toss with freshly cooked pappardelle.

Serve with: A robust Chianti Classico or Brunello, Crusty bread, A simple green salad
