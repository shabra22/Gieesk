🍥 Ceeb bu Weex

Ceeb bu weex — thièb bou wekh, thieboudiene blanc

The white ceebu jen — made without tomato, distinguished by a sorrel sauce called beuguedj and by the soul, a fish-free ball of pounded nététou.

Not plain rice, and the distinction matters: this is a ceebu jen cooked in broth without tomato, and it has two features the red version does not. The beuguedj is a sauce of white bissap sorrel leaves. The soul is a ball made with néré and nététou — fermented locust bean — containing no fish at all, buried in the rice to finish. About twenty centilitres of oil, roughly two litres of water, and an hour's simmer. The rice is washed three times to reduce the starch. Her own verdict is that it needs patience and care, and that it is one of her favourites.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot — marmite, Mortar and pestle, Large bowl

    Quantities printed only inside the method; see the note

    • About 20 cl oil
    • About 2 litres water
    • Fish — quantity not printed
    • Prawns — quantity not printed
    • Mixed vegetables — quantity not printed
    • White bissap sorrel leaves — quantity not printed
    • Cherry tomatoes, crushed — quantity not printed
    • Yett — quantity not printed
    • Tamarind — quantity not printed
    • Rice, washed 3 times

    For the roff

    • Parsley
    • 2 or 3 bird's eye chillies
    • 3 cloves garlic
    • Pepper and a little salt

    For the nokoss

    • 1 onion
    • 2 cloves garlic
    • A handful of nététou
    • 2 bird's eye chillies
    • 1/2 bell pepper
    • 1 bouillon cube, if you wish
    • Salt

    For the soul: néré and nététou, pounded into a ball — quantities not printed

      Method

      1. Crush the cherry tomatoes. Dice the onion and half the bell pepper, and cut a chilli in two.
      2. Put the pot on the heat with about 20 cl of oil.
      3. Add the onion, pepper and cut chilli, the yett and the crushed tomatoes, and fry until you get a brown colour.
      4. Add about 2 litres of water.
      5. When the pot comes to the boil, add the vegetables and the white bissap, cover, and cook.
      6. Pound the roff — parsley, 2 or 3 bird's eye chillies, 3 cloves of garlic, pepper and a little salt.
      7. Make a hole in the flesh of the fish and stuff it with the roff, then add the fish to the pot.
      8. Pound or chop the nokoss — onion, garlic, a handful of nététou, chillies, half a bell pepper, bouillon if using, and salt.
      9. Add part of the nokoss and the prawns, cover, and let it simmer for 1 hour.
      10. After the hour, wash the rice 3 times to reduce the starch.
      11. Remove the vegetables and fish to a large bowl, add the tamarind and a ladle of broth.
      12. Cook the rice in the remaining broth.

      The soul, a ball of pounded néré and nététou with no fish in it, is served with the dish.

        Chef Tips

        • Without tomato is the whole point. It is not unseasoned rice — one source glosses the name specifically as not to be confused with plain rice — it is rice cooked in a seasoned fish broth.
        • Fry the base until you get a brown colour. That browning is where the dish's depth comes from in the absence of tomato.
        • Wash the rice three times, by her instruction, to reduce the starch. In a dish this brothy, loose starch is what turns it claggy.
        • The soul is the unusual element — a pounded ball of néré and nététou with no fish in it at all. It is the fermented-bean counterpart to the fish stuffing.
        • Beuguedj, the white-sorrel sauce, is the other thing that marks the white version. White bissap, not the red hibiscus used for drinks.
        • Be aware the source prints no formal ingredient list — only the quantities that appear inside the method. Several ingredients are named without any amount, and this entry says so rather than inventing figures.
        • Her own framing: it requires patience and care. An hour's simmer before the rice even goes in.

        Common Mistakes to Avoid

        • Treating it as plain white rice.
        • Skipping the browning stage, which leaves it flat.
        • Washing the rice once rather than three times.
        • Leaving out the soul, which is most of what makes it the white version.
        • Using red hibiscus in place of white bissap.

        Health Benefits

        • Nététou is high in protein and its alkaline fermentation improves amino-acid availability.
        • Sorrel leaves contribute vitamin C and organic acids.
        • No tomato, so suitable for those avoiding nightshade acidity.
        • High in protein from fish and prawns together.

        Cultural Note

        That Senegal has a white ceebu jen at all surprises people who only know the red one, and it is the older-feeling dish — no tomato, which arrived late, and a fermented locust-bean ball at its centre. The soul is the detail to notice: a fish-free element in a fish dish, there for the ferment rather than the protein.

        Storage & Serving

        Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

        Reheating: Steam the rice; warm the fish and vegetables in broth.

        Serve with: Beuguedj sorrel sauce, Kaani, Attaya
