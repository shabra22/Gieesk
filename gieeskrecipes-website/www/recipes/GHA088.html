🫛 Fetri Detsi

Fetri detsi — fetri ma, light okra soup

The okra is worked with onion and water in four additions until it thickens, foams and binds itself into a mucilaginous dough.

Fetri detsi is the Ewe okra soup that banku and akple are eaten with, and the technique at its centre is unlike anything else in this collection. Eight ounces of okra are seeded, the onion is sprinkled on top and mixed in, and then water is added a little at a time — about three quarters of a cup in roughly four additions, mixing every couple of minutes — until the okra mixture thickens, foams and binds itself together to become a mucilaginous dough. That dough is then stirred into the simmering soup for five to ten minutes. It is described as a good first step for someone not used to okra.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot, Mixing bowl, Wooden spoon, Knife

    Chicken

    • 1 chicken, cut into 8-12 pieces, with the heart, gizzard and neck but not the liver
    • 2 tsp fresh grated ginger
    • 1 tsp fresh minced or ground garlic
    • 1-2 tsp seasoning salt or no-salt seasoning mixture
    • A little fresh coarsely sliced chilli pepper
    • Lemon, for cleansing the chicken
    • 1/4 cup sliced onion

    Soup

    • 8 oz okra, preferably fresh
    • 1 cup water — you will use about 3/4 cup of it
    • 1/2 cup onion, peeled and coarsely chopped
    • 1/4 cup white vegetable oil — see the note on this quantity
    • 1 1/2 tsp tomato paste (optional)
    • 1 tbsp ground shrimp
    • 2 cups water
    • A teaspoon or two of chicken seasoning
    • 1 heaping tsp ginger, grated or ground
    • 1 tsp dried ground red chilli pepper, or to taste

    Method

    1. Clean the chicken with lemon.
    2. Steam the chicken 10 minutes with the ginger, garlic, seasoning, chilli and sliced onion.
    3. Seed the okra and chop it.
    4. Sprinkle the chopped onion on top of the okra and mix it in.
    5. Add a little water, mixing, and keep adding a little more every couple of minutes.
    6. The okra mixture will thicken and foam and bind itself together to become a mucilaginous dough. You will probably need about 3/4 cup of water all together, added a little about four times.
    7. Heat the oil in the pot and add the tomato paste, ground shrimp, chicken seasoning, ginger and dried chilli.
    8. Add 2 cups of water to the pot and bring to the boil, then lower the heat to simmer.
    9. Mix the okro into the soup and let it simmer 5 to 10 minutes.
    10. Adjust the seasonings to taste and serve with banku or akple.

    Chef Tips

    • The mucilaginous dough is the whole technique: the okra thickens, foams and binds itself together before it ever goes near the soup. Water in four small additions over several minutes is what gets you there.
    • Three quarters of a cup of water across about four additions. Adding it all at once gives you wet chopped okra, not a dough.
    • Seeding the okra is optional in practice — she notes that Barbara Baeta removes the seeds to keep it tidy but says that in poorer families they would not.
    • Traditionally the chicken would be fried after steaming. She omits that step, along with Barbara, and says so — which is an honest declared departure.
    • Five to ten minutes once the okra dough goes in. Longer and the mucilage breaks down.
    • Her own framing is useful: this is a light okra soup and a good first step for someone not used to eating okra.
    • Note an inconsistency: the ingredient list says a quarter cup of white oil, the method says a couple of tablespoons. Start with two tablespoons.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding all the water to the okra at once.
    • Skipping the standing-and-mixing stage so no dough forms.
    • Simmering the okra dough longer than ten minutes.
    • Using the ingredient list's quarter cup of oil without noticing the method says less.
    • Including the chicken liver, which the source excludes.

    Health Benefits

    • Okra supplies soluble fibre, folate, vitamin K and vitamin C.
    • The mucilage has documented cholesterol-binding activity.
    • A whole chicken with giblets gives collagen and minerals.
    • Low in carbohydrate before the accompaniment.

    Cultural Note

    Banku and okra soup — akple and fetri detsi in Ewe — is the Volta Region's signature plate, and the okra worked into a self-binding dough before it enters the pot is a technique you would never guess at from the finished bowl. The note about poorer families leaving the seeds in is the kind of detail that makes a recipe a record.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 days refrigerated. The mucilage degrades.

    Reheating: Gentle heat. Do not boil.

    Serve with: Banku, Akple, Kenkey
