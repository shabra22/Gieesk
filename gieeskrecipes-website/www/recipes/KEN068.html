🌶️ Kenyan Chicken Pepper Fry

Kuku wa Pilipili

Kenyan Chicken Pepper Fry — chicken pieces boldly seasoned with crushed black pepper, fresh chilli, garlic, ginger, and spices then pan-fried until deeply caramelised and intensely flavourful. For those who like their chicken with real heat and character.

Kuku wa pilipili is chicken for pepper lovers — where most Kenyan chicken dishes lean mild to medium, pepper fry leans into heat and boldness. Crushed black pepper and fresh chilli dominate the seasoning, supported by garlic, ginger, and a concentrated tomato base that caramelises around each piece during the high-heat finishing stage.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large frying pan, Mortar and pestle, Wooden spoon

  • 1 kg chicken pieces — cut into small pieces
  • 2 tbsp crushed black peppercorns
  • 2 fresh green or red chillies — finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Season chicken with salt, paprika, cumin, and half the crushed pepper.
  2. Heat oil in a large pan over high heat. Sear chicken pieces for 4—5 minutes per side until deeply browned. Remove.
  3. In the same pan, cook onions until golden — 6 minutes.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, and chillies. Cook 1 minute.
  5. Add tomatoes. Cook for 5—6 minutes until thick.
  6. Add remaining crushed pepper. Stir 30 seconds.
  7. Return chicken. Toss to coat. Cook for 10—12 minutes until chicken is cooked through and the sauce has caramelised around each piece.
  8. Add lemon juice and coriander. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Crush the peppercorns coarsely rather than grinding finely — the larger pieces give pockets of intense heat.
  • The sauce should caramelise and cling rather than stay saucy.
  • Reduce heat if needed during the final stage to prevent burning.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using pre-ground pepper only — loses the bold, coarse character that defines pepper fry.
  • Not cooking out the tomatoes fully — the sauce stays acidic and raw-tasting.

The Science

Black pepper contains piperine, which activates the same heat receptors as capsaicin (from chilli). Crushing rather than grinding peppercorns releases the essential oils more slowly during cooking, producing a different — more rounded — heat profile than fine-ground pepper.

Health Benefits

  • High in lean protein.
  • Black pepper contains piperine which enhances absorption of curcumin from other spices.
  • Chilli provides capsaicin with anti-inflammatory properties.

Cultural Note

Chicken pepper fry reflects the spice-loving side of Kenyan cuisine — a dish for those who appreciate bold heat and concentrated flavour in their everyday cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat quickly in a hot pan.

Serve with: Ugali, Chapati, Steamed Rice, Kachumbari, Natural Yogurt to cool
