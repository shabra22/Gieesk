🍝 Bigoli in Salsa

Bigoli in Salsa

A thick, rustic wholewheat pasta from Venice tossed in a sauce of slow-cooked onions melted down with anchovies, traditionally eaten on meat-free Fridays and during Lent.

Bigoli are extruded through a special bronze die called a torchio, giving the thick strands a rough, porous surface that clings wonderfully to sauce — here, a deceptively simple onion and anchovy sauce that Venetian households have relied on for centuries.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bigolaro (bronze extruder) or pasta machine with a thick die, Large heavy pan, Large pot

  • 400 g (3 cups) wholewheat or buckwheat flour blend
  • 4 eggs (or 200ml/¾ cup water for an eggless version)
  • Pinch of salt
  • 800 g (1.75 lb) yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 8–10 anchovy fillets
  • 100 ml (scant ½ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • Fresh parsley, chopped, for serving

Method

  1. Combine the flour, eggs, and salt into a firm dough, kneading until smooth. Wrap and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Extrude the dough through a bigolaro or thick pasta die into long, rough-surfaced strands; if unavailable, cut thick strands by hand.
  3. Warm the olive oil in a large heavy pan and add the sliced onions with a pinch of salt.
  4. Cook the onions over low heat, stirring occasionally, for 30–40 minutes until completely soft and just beginning to turn golden — do not let them brown or crisp.
  5. Add the anchovy fillets to the softened onions and stir, letting them melt into the mixture to form a rustic sauce.
  6. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the bigoli until tender, about 4–6 minutes depending on thickness.
  7. Drain the pasta, reserving a little cooking water, and toss directly into the onion-anchovy sauce, loosening with pasta water as needed.
  8. Finish with black pepper and parsley, and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The onions must cook low and slow until fully melted and sweet — rushing this step leaves the sauce harsh.
  • Buckwheat flour blended into the dough gives an authentic Venetian color and slightly nutty flavor, though all-wholewheat works too.
  • Traditionally this dish is made without cheese, letting the onion and anchovy carry all the flavor.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the onions over high heat, which browns and toughens them instead of melting them down sweetly.
  • Using fresh, thin pasta instead of the thick, rough-textured bigoli the sauce is designed to cling to.
  • Oversalting, since anchovies already bring substantial salinity to the dish.

Health Benefits

  • Onions provide fiber and antioxidant compounds, especially when cooked slowly.
  • Anchovies contribute omega-3 fatty acids and calcium.

Cultural Note

Bigoli in salsa is a Venetian tradition strongly associated with Christmas Eve and other fasting days in the Catholic calendar, when meat was avoided in favor of dishes like this one.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The onion sauce keeps refrigerated for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat the sauce gently on the stovetop; cook fresh pasta just before serving.

Serve with: A light Venetian white wine
