🥜 Groundnut Soup with Chicken

Nkatenkwan — nkate nkwan, groundnut soup

Peanut butter stirred in a tablespoon at a time, then forty-five minutes to an hour on low until the peanut oil separates and rises to the top.

Nkatenkwan is the soup fufu was invented for, and the whole thing turns on one endpoint: the peanut oil has to separate and rise. Chicken simmers twenty-five minutes in stock with onion, ginger, garlic and pierced scotch bonnets until the juices run clear, skimming the froth. A blended chalé sauce goes in, then the peanut butter one tablespoon at a time so it dissolves rather than seizes. Then forty-five minutes to an hour on low, stirring regularly, until the oil breaks out on the surface and the meat is falling off the bone.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Blender, Skimming spoon, Grater

  • 2 x 850 g packs chicken drumsticks and thighs, skinned
  • 500 ml chicken stock or water
  • 1 onion, finely diced
  • 5 cm piece root ginger, grated
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 1-2 scotch bonnet chillies, pierced
  • 1/2-1 tsp hot chilli powder
  • 1 tbsp medium curry powder
  • 2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 100-150 g good-quality peanut butter

Chalé sauce

  • 1 x 400 g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 onion, roughly chopped
  • 5 cm piece root ginger, grated
  • 1 red scotch bonnet chilli, deseeded
  • 1/2 tsp crushed dried chilli flakes
  • 3 garlic cloves

To garnish

  • 3 tsp unsalted roasted peanuts, chopped
  • 3 spring onions, sliced

Method

  1. Put the chicken in the pot with the stock, onion, ginger, garlic, pierced scotch bonnets, chilli powder, curry powder, salt and pepper.
  2. Simmer 25 minutes, until the chicken juices run clear, skimming off the froth as it rises.
  3. Meanwhile blend all the chalé sauce ingredients smooth.
  4. Stir the chalé sauce into the pot.
  5. Add the peanut butter one tablespoon at a time, letting each dissolve before the next.
  6. Cook a further 45 minutes to 1 hour over low heat, stirring regularly, until the peanut oil separates and rises to the top.
  7. The result should be a soupy consistency with the meat falling from the bone.
  8. Garnish with the chopped peanuts and spring onions and serve over fufu.

Chef Tips

  • The oil separating and rising is the endpoint, and every documented groundnut soup agrees on it even where they agree on nothing else. Do not serve it before it happens.
  • One tablespoon of peanut butter at a time. Dumped in, it clumps and never fully dissolves.
  • Skim the froth during the first twenty-five minutes. It is what keeps the finished soup clean rather than muddy.
  • Pierce the scotch bonnets rather than chopping them. You get the aroma and a fraction of the heat, and you can fish them out.
  • Juices running clear is the chicken cue; falling off the bone is the finished cue. They are forty-five minutes to an hour apart.
  • Peanut quantity is the single biggest disagreement in this dish — documented versions run from 100 g to a full cup for a comparable pot. This is at the lean end; if you want it thicker, the next one up is Groundnut Soup with Kpakpo Shito.
  • Make it up to a day ahead, cool, chill, and reheat slowly with 100 ml of just-boiled water — the author's own instruction, and it genuinely improves.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the peanut butter all at once.
  • Stopping before the oil separates.
  • Not skimming the froth.
  • Chopping the scotch bonnets into the pot.
  • Rushing the final hour on high heat, which catches the peanut on the base.

Health Benefits

  • Peanuts supply monounsaturated fat, niacin, folate and vitamin E.
  • High in protein from both the chicken and the groundnut.
  • Ginger and garlic both carry documented anti-inflammatory compounds.
  • Naturally gluten-free and dairy-free.

Cultural Note

Groundnut soup is the Sunday soup — the one that takes two hours and gets eaten with fufu or rice balls while the pot is still on the stove. The insistence on cooking until the oil separates is not aesthetic: cooks across the region treat under-cooked groundnut paste as actively unpleasant to digest, and one named chef says so outright.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Up to 1 day ahead per the author; 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Slowly, with 100 ml of just-boiled water stirred in.

Serve with: Fufu, Omo Tuo, Rice balls
