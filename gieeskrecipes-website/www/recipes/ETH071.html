🐣 Ethiopian Easter Doro Wat

ፋሲካ ዶሮ ወጥ — Fasika Doro Wat

Ethiopian Easter Doro Wat — the Doro Wat prepared specifically for Fasika (Easter), made with extra berbere, extra kibbeh, three hours of slow simmering, and 12 eggs (one per apostle). The greatest expression of Doro Wat — made after the 55-day Lenten fast, the first meat meal of Easter morning.

Fasika Doro Wat is Doro Wat at its absolute pinnacle — the version made after 55 days of fasting (the Ethiopian Orthodox Great Lent), when meat has not been consumed for nearly two months. The anticipation drives the cook to exceed themselves: more berbere, more kibbeh, longer simmering, more eggs. The chicken is cut into exactly 12 pieces. The eggs number 12. The sauce must be so thick it barely pours. This is the most important meal of the Ethiopian year.

Ingredients

Equipment: Largest available heavy pot, Skewers for piercing eggs

  • 2 whole chickens (3 kg total) — cut into 12 pieces each

Marinade: 6 tbsp lemon juice, 3 tsp salt

  • 5 large onions — very finely diced
  • 7 tbsp kibbeh
  • 8 tbsp berbere
  • 8 garlic cloves — minced
  • 4 tbsp fresh ginger
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1.5 cups water
  • 12 hard-boiled eggs — pierced very deeply all over
  • 3 tsp salt

Method

  1. Marinate both chickens in lemon and salt — 1 hour. Rinse. Pat dry.
  2. Dry caramelise the onions — 35 minutes of patient stirring, no oil.
  3. Add kibbeh. Melt and combine. Cook 4 minutes.
  4. Add berbere. Toast in the kibbeh 6 minutes — the sauce should be deeply red.
  5. Add garlic, ginger, tomato paste. Cook 3 minutes.
  6. Add all chicken pieces. Coat every single piece.
  7. Add water. Bring to a boil.
  8. Reduce to lowest possible heat. Cover. Simmer 2 hours.
  9. Pierce the eggs with a skewer — 15—20 deep holes per egg.
  10. Add eggs. Simmer covered 30 more minutes.
  11. The sauce must be very thick, deeply red, and clinging to the chicken.
  12. Season. Rest 30 minutes before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Fasika Doro Wat is always made the day before — the overnight resting is essential.
  • The eggs must be pierced more deeply than for regular Doro Wat — use a skewer, not just a fork.
  • 35 minutes of dry-caramelised onions — the extra time produces more depth.
  • 8 tablespoons of berbere — this is Fasika, the greatest day.
  • This is the only day of the year when the sauce can never be too thick or too red.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Making on the day — must be made the day before.
  • Insufficient berbere — Fasika demands generosity.
  • Shallow egg piercing.

The Science

See ETH061 for Doro Wat cooking science. Fasika Doro Wat's extended cooking (3 hours vs 2.5) converts more collagen to gelatin and concentrates the sauce to a higher viscosity. The additional berbere means more carotenoids dissolved in the kibbeh — producing a more intensely red colour and more complex flavour.

Health Benefits

  • After 55 days of fasting, this meal provides the most complete protein replenishment possible.
  • The berbere's iron is particularly welcome after the legume-heavy fasting diet.

Cultural Note

Fasika Doro Wat is the food of resurrection — prepared after 55 days of abstinence, cooked through the night before Easter, served as the first meal after the overnight Easter service. Every Ethiopian Orthodox Christian knows the feeling of eating this Doro Wat after weeks of fasting — it is one of the most emotionally powerful food experiences in the world. The smell of it cooking is the smell of Easter morning.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days. Freeze 3 months. Must be made the day before.

Reheating: Gently — reheat fully.
