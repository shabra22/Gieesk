🐟 Sukuma Dried Fish Stew

Mchuzi wa Sangara wa Kukaushwa

Sukuma Dried Fish Stew — dried Nile perch or tilapia reconstituted and simmered in a thick tomato-onion sauce with minimal spicing in the traditional Sukuma style. The daily protein of Tanzania's largest ethnic group — earthy, deeply flavoured, and completely sustaining.

Dried fish stew is the Sukuma people's most important everyday dish — the fish caught in Lake Victoria's abundance is sun-dried and stored, then reconstituted throughout the year in this thick tomato stew. The drying process concentrates the fish's flavour into something much more intense than fresh fish, producing a stew of remarkable depth.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Sukuma people of Lake Victoria, Mwanza region — Tanzania's largest ethnic group

The Sukuma (WaSukuma) are Tanzania's largest ethnic group, numbering over 10 million people, living primarily around the southern shore of Lake Victoria. Their cuisine is built around the extraordinary protein resources of the lake — Nile perch (sangara), tilapia, and dagaa — and their staple ugali. Dried fish (particularly dried sangara) is preserved for the months between fishing seasons and reconstituted in thick tomato-based stews that are eaten with stiff ugali. This is the daily meal of millions of Tanzanians.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl for soaking, Large pot

  • 300g dried Nile perch (sangara) or tilapia — broken into pieces
  • 2 large onions — diced
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 cup water
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander

Method

  1. Soak dried fish in cold water for 20—30 minutes to remove excess salt and rehydrate slightly.
  2. Drain. Break into manageable pieces.
  3. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, curry powder, turmeric. Stir 30 seconds.
  5. Add tomatoes. Cook 8 minutes until completely broken down.
  6. Add fish pieces and water.
  7. Simmer covered 20—25 minutes until fish is completely tender and sauce is thick.
  8. Adjust salt (dried fish is already salty — taste before adding).
  9. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Soak first — removes excess salt and softens the fish for even cooking.
  • Taste before adding salt — dried fish is already very salty.
  • The stew should be thick enough to eat with ugali.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not soaking — over-salty result.
  • Over-salting — the dried fish provides significant salt.

The Science

Drying fish concentrates its proteins, fats, and flavour compounds through moisture removal. When reconstituted in the stew, the glutamate-rich dried fish releases these concentrated compounds into the sauce, producing a natural umami depth that fresh fish cannot achieve.

Cultural Note

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.
