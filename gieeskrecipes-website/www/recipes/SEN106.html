🌺 Bissap, Ice-Cold Hibiscus Infusion

Bissap / jus de bissap

50 g of rinsed red hibiscus flowers and 3 tablespoons of mint boiled gently 10 minutes in 1.5 litres of water, steeped until cool, filtered twice, sweetened with sugar, orange blossom water and vanilla sugar, and chilled at least 2 hours.

Dumplings And More gives a fully timed bissap: prep 10 minutes, cooking 10 minutes, resting 2 hours, total 2 hours 20 minutes, for 6 people. The flowers are rinsed quickly in cold water and drained; 1.5 litres of water is brought to the boil, the flowers and mint added and boiled gently 10 minutes; the pan is taken off the heat and left to steep until cool; the liquid is filtered, left to decant a few minutes and filtered again if needed to clear it. The sugar, vanilla sugar and orange blossom water are mixed in until completely dissolved, and the juice poured into a bottle or carafe and refrigerated at least 2 hours before serving chilled. The page says bissap is served very chilled in Senegal, at home, in restaurants, during festive meals or to accompany a spicy dish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Fine sieve or filter, Bottle or carafe

  • 50 g red bissap flowers or dried hibiscus flowers, washed and drained
  • 1.5 l water (printed ‘1,5 l’)
  • 6 tablespoons sugar, to taste
  • 3 tablespoons fresh mint leaves
  • 1 tablespoon orange blossom water
  • 2 packets vanilla sugar

Method

  1. Rinse the bissap flowers quickly with cold water and drain them.
  2. Bring 1.5 l of water to the boil in a saucepan. Add the flowers and the mint leaves.
  3. Let it boil gently for 10 minutes. Remove from the heat and let steep until cool.
  4. Filter once to remove the flowers and mint. Leave to decant a few minutes, then filter a second time if needed for a cleaner juice.
  5. Add the sugar, vanilla sugar and orange blossom water and mix until completely dissolved. Pour into a bottle or carafe.
  6. Refrigerate at least 2 hours before serving chilled.

Chef Tips

  • Printed cues: ‘steep until cool’, ‘mix until completely dissolved’, ‘at least 2 hours’.
  • This is an infusion, not a fermented or soured drink; no souring step exists.
  • The page says sugar is to be adjusted to taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling vigorously instead of gently.
  • Skipping the second filtering if the juice is cloudy.
  • Serving warm; the page insists on very chilled.
