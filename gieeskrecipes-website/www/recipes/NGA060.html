Isi ewu — goat head

A whole goat head cooked with as little water as possible, in an emulsion of 150 ml palm oil and a tablespoon of potash — with two teaspoons of ehu and about ten utazi leaves.

Isi ewu is nkwobi's twin and the difference is the protein: goat head rather than cow foot. The source states it plainly. Everything else matches — the same curdle-and-turn-yellow emulsion, the same wooden mortar, the same utazi and onion-ring garnish, the same cold drink alongside. Two things do differ in degree: the ehu is doubled to two teaspoons, and the goat is cooked with as little water as possible rather than generously covered, because the head carries its own liquid and you want the flavour concentrated. The brain, mashed into the sauce, is a second emulsifier that nkwobi has no equivalent for.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pressure cooker or large pot, Clean dry pot for the emulsion, Wooden spatula, Fine sieve, Wooden mortar, for serving

  • 1 goat head, cut into pieces
  • 150 ml palm oil
  • 1 tbsp powdered edible potash, dissolved in about 4 tbsp water and strained through a fine sieve
  • 2 tsp ground ehu (calabash nutmeg)
  • 2 beef-flavoured stock cubes
  • 2 medium onions — 1 for cooking, 1 for garnish
  • 2 habanero peppers
  • About 10 utazi leaves
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Have the goat head cut into pieces. Singe off any hair, then wash and scrub it very thoroughly — this is the longest part of the preparation.
  2. Cook the goat head with one onion, the stock cubes and salt, using as little water as possible, until done. Goat meat is quite tough, so a pressure cooker is recommended to save time.
  3. Pick the meat from the bones. Reserve the brain separately.
  4. Dissolve the potash in about 4 tablespoons of water and strain it through a fine sieve.
  5. Pour the palm oil into a clean dry pot. Pour in the strained potash mixture, stirring with a wooden spatula as you pour.
  6. The palm oil will begin to curdle and turn yellow. Keep stirring till all the oil has turned yellow.
  7. Mash the reserved brain into the emulsion — it thickens and enriches it.
  8. Add the ground ehu, the blended habanero and salt.
  9. Fold in the picked goat meat and mix until every piece is coated.
  10. Heat gently until piping hot.
  11. Serve in a wooden mortar, garnished with about 10 thinly sliced utazi leaves and rings cut from the second onion.

Chef Tips

  • As little water as possible for cooking the head. The flavour is in the liquid and you do not want to throw it away.
  • Scrubbing the head is most of the work. Singe the hair, scrub hard, rinse repeatedly. Skimp here and the dish will taste of it.
  • Save the brain. Mashed into the sauce it is a second emulsifier and it is why isi ewu is richer than nkwobi.
  • Two teaspoons of ehu, twice the nkwobi amount. The stronger flavour of goat head needs it.
  • Clean dry pot for the emulsion, same as nkwobi. Water is what breaks it.
  • Four tablespoons of water to dissolve the potash is a small amount, and that is deliberate — the less liquid in the emulsion, the thicker the sauce.
  • Pressure cooker. Goat head on the stove is a two-hour job and the source recommends the shortcut itself.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Covering the head generously with water.
  • Discarding the brain.
  • Making the emulsion in a wet pot.
  • Skimping on cleaning the head.
  • Using the nkwobi quantity of ehu, which is too little here.

Health Benefits

  • Goat head is rich in collagen, and the brain supplies phospholipids and DHA.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.
  • High in iron, zinc and B12.
  • Red palm oil provides carotenoids and vitamin E.

Cultural Note

Isi ewu is a dish of standing — a whole goat head is not something you cook casually, and it is served to be shared, in a wooden mortar, at a table where people are staying a while. Along with nkwobi and abacha it forms the southeastern bar repertoire, and all three rest on the same emulsion: palm oil, an alkali, and enough stirring to bring them together.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat, stirring, until the emulsion loosens.

Serve with: Cold palm wine, Chilled stout, Abacha
