Samaki na Chips

Tanzanian Samaki na Chips — crispy spiced-battered tilapia alongside thick-cut masala chips. Tanzania's most beloved street food combination — the batter is spiced with Tanzanian curry powder, the chips tossed in masala. Better than the British original.

Samaki na chips is the street food of Tanzania's cities — at every corner, vendors fry fish and chips side by side. The Tanzanian version is better because the batter is spiced and the chips are tossed in masala spice.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep fryer or large pot

  • 600g fish fillets — tilapia or snapper
  • 4 large potatoes — thick chips

Spiced Fish Batter

  • 1 cup flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 200ml cold water

Masala Chips Spice

  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Oil for frying

Method

  1. Make spiced batter: combine flour, baking powder, spices. Whisk in cold water. Rest 5 minutes.
  2. Soak potato chips in cold water 20 minutes. Dry thoroughly.
  3. First fry chips at 160°C for 6—8 minutes. Remove.
  4. Dip fish in spiced batter. Fry at 180°C for 4—5 minutes per side.
  5. Second fry chips at 180°C for 3—4 minutes until golden.
  6. Toss chips immediately in masala spice mix.

Chef Tips

  • Double-frying chips is the secret to crispiness.
  • Cold batter = lighter, crispier coating.
  • Masala spice on hot chips — heat blooms the spices.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Single-fry chips.
  • Warm batter.

The Science

Double frying: first fry gelatinises starch inside; second fry at higher temperature creates rapid Maillard browning on the drier exterior. Dramatically crispier than single-fry.

Cultural Note

Samaki na chips is the food that urban Tanzania runs on — the evening meal of students and workers across every city.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Oven at 200°C for 5 minutes.

Serve with: Pili Pili Sauce, Garlic Mayo, Kachumbari
