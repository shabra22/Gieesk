🌿 Ayoyo Soup

Ayoyo — jute leaf soup

Wash the leaves before cutting, otherwise the sliminess will not be activated — the one instruction that decides whether ayoyo works.

20 minPrep

5Servings

MediumDifficulty

Ayoyo is jute leaf and the soup is wanted for its slipperiness. The single most important instruction in the documented record is here: wash the ayoyo leaves before cutting, otherwise the sliminess will not be activated. A hundred and fifty grams of leaves, fifteen grams of dawadawa, a calabash nut and fifty grams of dried local herring are the northern seasoning core. Onion, dawadawa, calabash nut and herring are blended, a cup of boiling water goes in, then five minutes' wait before the leaves, and about ten minutes' simmer — blended or stirred with an ijabe broom.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot, Blender, or an ijabe (cooking broom), Knife, Colander

  • 150 g ayoyo leaves
  • 250 g beef chunks, or 200 g fresh salmon or mushrooms
  • 15 g dawadawa
  • 1 calabash nut
  • 50 g dried herring
  • 350 ml palm oil
  • 3 onions
  • 3 tomatoes
  • 1 tin tomatoes
  • 5 cloves garlic
  • 20 g ginger
  • 50 g hot green pepper
  • 1 tbsp salt
  • 1 seasoning cube dissolved in 250 ml water, or 250 ml meat stock

For the tuo, if making the full dish

  • 400 g corn flour
  • 150 g cassava flour
  • 1.5 litres water — see the note on this figure

Method

  1. Wash the ayoyo leaves BEFORE cutting them, otherwise the sliminess will not be activated.
  2. Cook the stew base — onion, tomatoes, garlic, ginger, pepper, palm oil and the meat or fish — for approximately 20 minutes.
  3. Separately blend the onion, dawadawa, calabash nut and dried herring.
  4. Add a cup of boiling water to the blended mixture.
  5. Wait 5 minutes before adding the ayoyo leaves.
  6. Blend it, or keep stirring with a dedicated cooking broom (ijabe), and let it simmer approximately 10 minutes.

Optional: use bitter salt to soften the leaves before stirring.

  1. Serve with tuo zaafi and the stew.

Chef Tips

  • Wash before cutting. The source is explicit that otherwise the sliminess will not be activated, and the slipperiness is the entire reason for the soup.
  • Wait five minutes after the boiling water goes into the blended seasoning, before the leaves. That pause lets the dawadawa and herring open up.
  • An ijabe is the Ghanaian cooking broom, the same tool used for ewedu in Nigeria. It produces a different texture from a blender — stringier and more suspended.
  • Bitter salt is offered as an optional softener for the leaves. That is an alkali, and like all alkalis it should be used sparingly.
  • A calabash nut — African nutmeg — is the aromatic here, and fifteen grams of dawadawa is the savoury base. Both are northern markers.
  • Three hundred and fifty millilitres of palm oil is a great deal for five servings, and it is printed as such. Reduce it if you want, knowing you are departing from the source.
  • This version is written for an electric pressure cooker, which is why the timings are short. On a stove, give the stew base longer.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cutting the leaves before washing them, which kills the slipperiness.
  • Adding the leaves straight after the water.
  • Over-boiling, which breaks the mucilage down.
  • Heavy-handed use of the bitter salt.
  • Treating the pressure-cooker timings as stove timings.

Health Benefits

  • Jute leaf is exceptionally high in vitamins A, C and E, calcium, iron and folate.
  • The mucilage is a soluble fibre with documented cholesterol-binding activity.
  • Dawadawa supplies B vitamins and plant protein.
  • Dried herring adds calcium, iodine and omega-3 fats.

Cultural Note

Ayoyo is to northern Ghana what ewedu is to western Nigeria — the same jute leaf, the same prized slipperiness, even the same broom used to stir it. That the instruction to wash before cutting appears in only one documented source is the kind of knowledge that usually passes from hand to hand and never gets written down.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. The mucilage degrades.

Reheating: Gentle heat. Do not boil, or the slipperiness is lost.

Serve with: Tuo Zaafi, Tuo Zaafi with Ayoyo, Bito Soup
