🍲 Potjiekos

Potjiekos

Beef shin browned over the fire and layered into a three-legged cast-iron pot with pumpkin, baby onions and ale, then topped with feta-and-herb dumplings and left alone — the not-stirring is the recipe.

Potjiekos means small-pot food and it is a method more than a dish: ingredients go into a three-legged cast-iron pot in layers, over low coals, and are not stirred, so each layer cooks in the steam of the ones below and keeps its own character. That discipline is what separates it from a stew. This version is built on beef shin with the marrow bones left in, finished with dumplings dropped on top for the last half hour.

Ingredients

Equipment: Three-legged cast-iron potjie with a lid, Braai or fire, Mixing bowl, Tongs

    The pot

    • 1.5 kg bone-in beef shin
    • Salt and pepper
    • A splash of oil, for frying
    • 1 large onion, chopped
    • 1 tbsp garlic, finely chopped
    • 2 tbsp fresh thyme, chopped
    • 2 tbsp fresh sage, chopped
    • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
    • 1 x 340 ml bottle pumpkin ale, or any amber ale
    • 3 tbsp beef stock concentrate
    • 1 tsp cornflour
    • 60 ml (1/4 cup) water
    • 1 kg pumpkin, diced
    • 12 baby onions, peeled

    The dumplings

    • 500 g cake flour
    • 2 tsp baking powder
    • 1 1/2 tsp salt
    • 100 g butter, cold
    • 100 g feta, crumbled
    • 1 tbsp fresh thyme, chopped
    • 1 tbsp fresh sage, chopped
    • 250 ml cold water

    Method

    1. Season the beef shin with salt and black pepper and brown it over a smoking hot fire to seal it.
    2. Once it is cool enough to handle, cut the meat into bite-sized chunks. Keep the marrow bones for the pot.
    3. Heat a splash of oil in the potjie and fry the onion until golden.
    4. Add the garlic, thyme, sage and cinnamon and fry a further minute.
    5. Add the meat and the bones, then pour in the ale and the stock concentrate. Put the lid on and simmer for 1 hour.
    6. Mix the cornflour with the water and add it to the pot along with the pumpkin and the baby onions. Stir thoroughly to combine — and this is the last time you stir it.
    7. Put the lid back on and cook for another 45 minutes. Make the dumpling dough while it cooks.
    8. For the dumplings, sift the flour, baking powder and salt together and rub in the cold butter with your fingertips. Add the feta and herbs, then the cold water, and mix to a soft dough.
    9. Break off golf-ball-sized pieces and set them on top of the liquid in the pot.
    10. Cook for 30 minutes with the lid on, until the dumplings are done. For colour on top, put a few coals on the lid.

    Chef Tips

    • The last stir is step six. After that the layers are meant to stay where they are, and lifting the lid to check lets out the steam that is doing the cooking.
    • Brown the shin over the fire rather than in the pot. You want serious colour, and a potjie is the wrong shape for it.
    • Keep the marrow bones in. They are most of what makes the liquid rich.
    • Coals on the lid is the traditional trick for browning anything on top, and it works on the dumplings.
    • Low and slow, always. A potjie over hot coals catches on the base and the layers collapse into each other.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Stirring it after the dumplings or vegetables go in, which turns a potjie into a stew.
    • Lifting the lid repeatedly to look, losing the steam each time.
    • Cooking over coals that are too hot, which scorches the bottom layer.
    • Dropping the dumplings into the liquid rather than sitting them on top of it — they steam, they do not boil.

    Health Benefits

    • Beef shin is high in protein, iron and collagen from the bone and connective tissue.
    • A kilogram of pumpkin makes it a genuinely vegetable-heavy main.
    • Cooked slowly with very little added fat.

    Cultural Note

    The potjie is a Dutch and trekker inheritance — a pot you could hang off a wagon and set in the coals anywhere — and the no-stirring rule is a working technique rather than folklore: unstirred, each layer holds its texture, and a pot that has been stirred loses the distinction. Potjie competitions are a real and seriously contested fixture of South African social life.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Better on the second day.

    Reheating: Reheat gently on the hob or over coals with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Roosterkoek, Pap and a tomato relish, Rice
