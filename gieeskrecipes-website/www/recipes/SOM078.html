🍳 Ukun iyo Qudaar

Ukun iyo Qudaar

Nine eggs poured over onion, tomato, pepper, spinach and parsley with cubes of cream cheese set through it, covered and cooked on the hob for ten minutes — the weekend quraac, eaten late morning with the whole family.

Quraac, or qurado, is the Somali weekend brunch — eaten somewhere between eleven and half past twelve, with everybody at the table, and it is a fixture rather than an occasion. This is its centrepiece: not an omelette folded in a pan but a covered, stovetop frittata, cooked gently under a lid until it sets. The cream cheese is the trick, cubed in and distributed so you hit pockets of it, and the way to cube it cleanly is to put it in the freezer while you chop everything else.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large frying pan with a tight lid, Mixing bowl, Whisk

  • 9 large eggs
  • 125 g cream cheese
  • 1/2 onion, diced
  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 1/2 yellow or red bell pepper, diced
  • 2 cups fresh spinach, chopped
  • 1/2 cup fresh parsley, chopped
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) olive oil
  • 10 ml (2 tsp) kosher salt, or to taste

Method

  1. Put the cream cheese in the freezer while you prepare everything else — this is what makes it possible to cube cleanly.
  2. Dice the onion, tomatoes and pepper. Chop the spinach and parsley. Crack the eggs into a bowl and whisk them thoroughly.
  3. Heat the olive oil over medium heat and cook the diced onion for about 3 minutes, until soft and translucent.
  4. Add the diced tomatoes and cook for 5 minutes.
  5. Add the diced pepper and cook for 2 minutes.
  6. Stir in the spinach and parsley and cook for 2 minutes.
  7. Cube the chilled cream cheese and distribute it across the mixture, spacing the pieces as evenly as you can.
  8. Pour the whisked eggs over everything, cover the pan tightly, and cook over medium heat for 10 minutes, until set.
  9. Serve immediately, while warm.

Chef Tips

  • Freeze the cream cheese first. Room-temperature cream cheese smears into the vegetables instead of staying in cubes, and the cubes are the point.
  • Cover it tightly. The lid is what cooks the top — without it you have a raw surface and a burnt base.
  • Medium heat and no peeking for the full ten minutes. Lifting the lid lets out the steam that is setting the top.
  • Whisk the eggs properly, not just until broken up. Under-whisked eggs set in streaks.
  • Zucchini, mushrooms, asparagus or potatoes all substitute happily, and a lighter cheese works if cream cheese is too rich for you.
  • It is a good one to cook for a crowd because nine eggs in one pan feeds four without anyone standing at the stove making individual omelettes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using soft cream cheese, so it disappears into the vegetables.
  • Cooking it uncovered.
  • Turning the heat up to hurry the setting, which browns the base.
  • Adding the eggs before the vegetables have given up their water, which makes it soggy.
  • Letting it stand — it is best straight out of the pan.

  • The cream cheese is where the saturated fat is, and a lighter cheese cuts it.

Cultural Note

Quraac is one of the more appealing Somali institutions: a late, unhurried weekend meal with the whole family around it, eaten with canjeero and shaah, and understood as time together rather than just a meal. The Italian loanwords scattered through Somali food vocabulary show up here too — much of the vegetable repertoire came through the colonial period and stayed.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm gently in a covered pan. It does not microwave well.

Serve with: Canjeero, Sabaayad, Shaah Cadays
