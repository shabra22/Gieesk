🍝 Frittata di Maccheroni

Frittata di Maccheroni

A clever Neapolitan way to transform leftover pasta into a new meal, binding it with eggs and cheese and pan-frying until both sides turn crisp and golden.

Born from resourcefulness rather than a dedicated recipe, frittata di maccheroni is a cherished way to give leftover pasta new life, and Neapolitan families often keep this dish in mind specifically when cooking slightly more pasta than needed the night before.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large non-stick skillet, Plate for flipping

  • 400 g (14 oz) cooked leftover pasta (spaghetti or similar), ideally with some sauce still on it
  • 4 eggs
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano or pecorino
  • 100 g (1 cup) diced mozzarella or provolone (optional)
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
  • 3 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Beat the eggs in a large bowl with the grated cheese, parsley, salt, and pepper.
  2. Add the leftover pasta to the egg mixture and toss thoroughly to coat every strand.
  3. Fold in the diced mozzarella or provolone if using.
  4. Warm the olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat.
  5. Pour in the pasta mixture, pressing it into an even layer.
  6. Cook for 6–8 minutes until the bottom is golden and set.
  7. Carefully flip using a large plate, sliding the frittata back into the pan to cook the other side.
  8. Cook for another 6–8 minutes until both sides are golden and the center is fully set.
  9. Slide onto a board, let rest briefly, then slice into wedges and serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef Tips

  • Pasta already coated in a bit of sauce works especially well, adding extra flavor to the finished frittata.
  • A wide, good-quality non-stick pan makes the flipping process much easier and less risky.
  • This dish is equally good warm, at room temperature, or even cold the next day, making it excellent for packed lunches.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using too little egg to properly bind the pasta together, causing it to fall apart when sliced.
  • Flipping before the bottom has properly set, resulting in a messy transfer.
  • Cooking over heat that's too high, browning the outside before the center is fully cooked.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of protein from the eggs and cheese, and a practical way to reduce food waste.

Cultural Note

Frittata di maccheroni exemplifies the resourceful, no-waste spirit of Neapolitan home cooking, turning yesterday's pasta into a satisfying dish in its own right.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; excellent eaten cold or reheated.

Reheating: Reheat in a dry skillet, or enjoy cold straight from the refrigerator.

Serve with: A simple green salad
