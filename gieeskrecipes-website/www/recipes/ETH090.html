🍵 Ethiopian Fish Soup

አሣ ሾርባ — Asa Shorba

Ethiopian Fish Soup — fish simmered with onion, garlic, turmeric, ginger, and coriander into a light, restorative broth. The fasting-period healing soup — lighter and more gently spiced than fish wat, serving as both a soup and a source of protein.

Asa Shorba is the gentle healing soup of Ethiopia's lake communities — the freshwater fish simmered with turmeric and ginger into a golden broth of genuine restorative power. Given to those fasting, those recovering from illness, and drunk on cold highland evenings.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 600g fish — whole small fish or bone-in pieces
  • 2 litres water
  • 1 onion — halved
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger — sliced
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander
  • Lime wedges

Method

  1. Place fish in pot with cold water.
  2. Bring to a gentle boil. Skim foam — 3 minutes.
  3. Add onion, garlic, ginger, turmeric, pepper, and cardamom.
  4. Simmer 20—25 minutes on lowest heat.
  5. Remove fish. Flake meat. Return to broth. Discard bones.
  6. Season. Garnish with coriander. Serve with lime.

Chef Tips

  • Skim foam carefully — produces a cleaner broth.
  • Fish broth cooks much faster than meat broth — do not over-simmer.
  • The soup should be light golden from the turmeric.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-cooking — fish falls apart and becomes mushy.
  • Not skimming foam.

The Science

Fish produces gelatine from its collagen at lower temperatures and shorter times than meat — even 20 minutes of gentle simmering extracts significant gelatin from fish bones, producing a slightly body-rich broth. The inosinate (IMP) in fish also enhances umami perception in the broth.

Health Benefits

  • Light protein from fish.
  • Turmeric anti-inflammatory.
  • Ginger aids digestion.
  • Easily digestible — ideal for recovery.

Cultural Note

Fish soup is the fasting-period healing food of Ethiopia's lake communities — given to monks on their island monasteries, to fasting families in lakeside towns, and to anyone who needs nourishment without meat.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: As a standalone healing soup, With injera for a light fasting meal
