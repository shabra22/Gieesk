🌿 Omoebe with Banga Juice

Omoebe — Nigerian black soup

The four-leaf version, with the clearest leaf preparation anywhere: bitter leaf par-boiled ten minutes uncovered, the other three soaked in salt water five minutes, then all ground together.

This is the omoebe to use if you want to understand the leaves, because it is the only source that explains how to handle them. Bitter leaf gets par-boiled for ten minutes with the lid off and then rinsed repeatedly and squeezed dry; scent leaf, uziza and ugwu are soaked in salt water for five minutes, rinsed, chopped and then ground with the washed bitter leaf and the barest amount of water. Periwinkle and fresh mackerel go in alongside the beef and pomo. There are no quantities on the page at all, and that is said plainly rather than filled in.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Colander, Bowl for salt-water soaking

  • Fresh fish (mackerel or titus)
  • Beef
  • Smoked fish
  • Pomo (cow skin)
  • Periwinkle
  • Ground crayfish
  • Yellow pepper, ground
  • Banga juice (palm fruit extract), or palm oil

The leaves

  • Scent leaves
  • Bitter leaves
  • Uziza leaves
  • Ugwu leaves

Seasoning

  • Seasoning cubes
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Par-boil the bitter leaves for 10 minutes uncovered, then rinse repeatedly with cold water and squeeze dry.
  2. Soak the scent leaves, uziza leaves and ugwu leaves in salt water for 5 minutes.
  3. Rinse them, chop into smaller pieces, then place in the blender with the washed bitter leaves, add minimal water, and grind.
  4. Cook the beef and pomo with the seasoning cubes and salt for 20 minutes, until tender and the liquid reduces.
  5. Add the smoked fish, fresh fish, banga juice, blended pepper, crayfish and periwinkles, and cook 10 minutes.
  6. Gently pour in the blended vegetables, stir, and cook 10 to 12 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • Uncovered for the bitter leaf par-boil. The lid traps the bitterness back in; ten minutes open lets it go.
  • Rinse the bitter leaf repeatedly and squeeze it dry. This is the step that decides whether the finished soup is pleasantly bitter or unpleasantly so.
  • Five minutes in salt water for the other three leaves, no longer. It is a cleaning step, not a soak.
  • Minimal water in the blender. You are making a paste, and every extra spoon of water thins the finished soup.
  • Ten to twelve minutes for the leaves here, against two minutes in the other documented version. That is a real disagreement and it will give you a darker, softer soup.
  • Banga juice is palm fruit extract — the pulp, not the oil. Palm oil is offered as the alternative and it is a noticeably different dish.
  • This version has no quantities at all. Use the meat weights from Omoebe if you want numbers to start from.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Par-boiling the bitter leaf with the lid on.
  • Skipping the repeated rinsing.
  • Over-soaking the tender leaves in salt water.
  • Flooding the blender, which waters the soup.
  • Substituting palm oil without expecting a different result.

Health Benefits

  • Four different leaves give an unusually broad micronutrient range.
  • Uziza leaf is peppery and carries piperine-family compounds.
  • Periwinkle is very lean and high in iron.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.

Cultural Note

That the only source explaining how to wash the leaves is also the source with no quantities at all is a fair summary of the Nigerian written record. The knowledge that travels is the knowledge that is hard to work out for yourself; the amounts are assumed, because anyone making omoebe has seen it made.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat. Do not boil it again.

Serve with: Iyan, Starch, Eba or garri
