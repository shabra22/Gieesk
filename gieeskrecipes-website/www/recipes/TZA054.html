🥩 Tanzanian Beef Keema

Nyama ya Kusaga

Tanzanian Nyama ya Kusaga — spiced minced beef with peas, tomatoes, and Tanzanian spices. Fast, affordable, and intensely flavourful — the everyday minced beef dish of urban Tanzania, eaten with chapati, rice, or as a filling for mandazi rolls.

Spiced minced beef is the weekday staple of Tanzania's urban households — it cooks in 25 minutes, stretches a small amount of beef to feed a large family, and absorbs the Tanzanian spice palette beautifully.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide frying pan

  • 500g minced beef
  • 1 cup frozen or fresh peas
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup coriander

Method

  1. Cook onions until golden.
  2. Add garlic, ginger, and spices. Stir 30 seconds.
  3. Add minced beef. Break apart and brown well — 8 minutes.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook 5 minutes.
  5. Add peas. Cook 5 more minutes.
  6. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Brown the mince well — underbrowned mince lacks depth.
  • Drain excess fat if using fatty mince.
  • Peas add sweetness and colour — add them last.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-browning the mince.
  • Adding too much water — should be dry, not soupy.

The Science

Minced beef provides maximum surface area for Maillard browning — each small piece caramelises independently, producing far more total browned surface than the same weight of whole beef.

Cultural Note

Spiced minced beef is the everyday protein of Tanzania's urban families — the dish that appears when the budget is tight but flavour cannot be compromised.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Briefly in hot pan.

Serve with: Chapati (TZA004), Wali (TZA017), Ugali (TZA001)
