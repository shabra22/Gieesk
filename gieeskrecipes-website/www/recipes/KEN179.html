🍌 Kisii Banana Stew

Mchuzi wa Matoke wa Gusii

Kisii Banana Stew — green cooking bananas (matoke) simmered in a rich tomato, onion, and garlic sauce with mild spices. A hearty, naturally starchy, deeply satisfying stew from the banana farms of the Kisii highlands.

The Kisii highlands are blanketed in banana farms. Green matoke cooked in a spiced tomato sauce become beautifully tender and absorb the surrounding flavours while contributing their natural starchiness.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Kisii County (Gusiiland) — Nyanza region, Western Kenya

The Gusii people of Nyanza are one of Kenya's most agriculturally productive communities. The fertile highlands of Kisii County receive abundant rainfall and produce bananas in extraordinary quantities. Green cooking bananas (matoke) are a staple food — steamed, stewed, or cooked in a spiced tomato sauce.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Wooden spoon

  • 6 large green cooking bananas (matoke) — peeled and halved
  • 2 medium onions — chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander

Method

  1. Peel bananas (wear gloves). Halve or cut into chunks.
  2. Submerge in salted water immediately after peeling.
  3. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden — 6 minutes.
  4. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  5. Add tomatoes, curry powder, and paprika. Cook for 6—8 minutes until thick.
  6. Drain bananas and add to the pot. Stir to coat.
  7. Add water. Bring to a gentle boil.
  8. Cover and simmer for 25—30 minutes until bananas are completely tender.
  9. Season and garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Wear gloves when peeling green bananas — the latex sap stains permanently.
  • Keep peeled bananas in salted water — they brown rapidly.
  • Green matoke should be completely tender when done.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Browning bananas — submerge immediately after peeling.
  • Undercooking.

The Science

Green cooking bananas are high in resistant starch. During cooking, resistant starch partially converts to digestible starch and some dissolves into the sauce, naturally thickening it.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in resistant starch — a prebiotic fibre.
  • Good source of potassium and vitamin B6.

Cultural Note

Kisii banana stew is a daily expression of the Gusii community's deep relationship with their extraordinarily fertile land.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently.

Serve with: Ugali, Sukuma Wiki, Fried Fish, Beef Stew
