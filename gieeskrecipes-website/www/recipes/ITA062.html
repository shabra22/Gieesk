Fusilli al Ferretto con Nduja

Fusilli al Ferretto are Calabria's hand-rolled corkscrew pasta, twisted around a thin wire, traditionally paired with the region's fiery, spreadable nduja sausage.

The 'ferretto' — a thin metal rod, traditionally a knitting needle — is used to roll and coil each individual piece of pasta into its distinctive spiral, a slow, meditative process still practiced by hand in many Calabrian households.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Thin metal rod or wooden skewer, Large pot, Large skillet

    Fusilli dough

    • 400 g (3⅓ cups) durum wheat semolina flour, plus more for dusting
    • 200 ml (¾ cup) warm water
    • Pinch of salt

    Sauce

    • 150 g (5 oz) nduja, crumbled
    • 2 tbsp olive oil
    • 2 garlic cloves, minced
    • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
    • 60 g (¾ cup) grated Pecorino, plus more for serving
    • Salt, to taste

    Method

    1. Combine the semolina flour, water, and salt into a firm dough. Knead for 10 minutes until smooth. Rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
    2. Pinch off small pieces of dough and roll into thin ropes. Wrap each rope around a thin metal rod or skewer, rolling it under your palm to create a tight spiral, then slide it off.
    3. Repeat with all the dough, laying the shaped fusilli on a floured tray.
    4. Heat the olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the garlic and cook briefly, then add the crumbled nduja, letting it melt into the oil, about 2 minutes.
    5. Add the crushed tomatoes and simmer for 15 minutes until thickened.
    6. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the fusilli for 5–7 minutes until tender.
    7. Drain and toss with the sauce and Pecorino.
    8. Serve immediately with extra Pecorino.

    Chef Tips

    • Nduja is quite spicy and salty — taste the sauce before adding extra salt.
    • The hand-rolling technique takes practice; work steadily rather than rushing for the most even spirals.
    • A clean, thin wooden skewer works well if a traditional ferretto isn't available.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Rolling the dough too thick, resulting in a heavy, dense pasta rather than a delicate spiral.
    • Adding nduja over too high heat, which can cause it to separate rather than melt smoothly.
    • Over-salting before tasting, given nduja's inherent saltiness.

    Cultural Note

    Fusilli al ferretto is a defining shape of Calabrian cuisine, and nduja — a spreadable, spicy pork sausage from the town of Spilinga — has become one of the region's most internationally recognized culinary exports.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: The sauce keeps refrigerated for up to 3 days; uncooked fusilli can be dried for room-temperature storage.

    Reheating: Reheat the sauce gently, then toss with freshly cooked fusilli.

    Serve with: A robust Calabrian red wine such as Cirò, Crusty bread
