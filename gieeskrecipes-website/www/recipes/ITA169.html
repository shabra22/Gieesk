Peposo alla Fornacina

A rustic Tuscan beef stew defined by a generous quantity of whole black peppercorns, said to have originated with brick-kiln workers who slow-cooked it near their ovens outside Florence.

Legend holds that peposo was invented by the workers firing the kilns used to make terracotta and bricks for Florence's great Duomo, who would place a pot near the ovens to slow-cook throughout the workday using the mouth-numbing pepper as their primary seasoning.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed pot with lid

  • 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) beef shin or chuck, cut into large chunks
  • 8 cloves garlic, left whole and lightly crushed
  • 2 tbsp whole black peppercorns, lightly crushed
  • 750 ml (3 cups) robust red wine (such as Chianti)
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the beef, garlic, crushed peppercorns, wine, tomato paste, and bay leaves in a heavy pot.
  2. Add enough water to just cover the meat if needed.
  3. Bring to a gentle simmer over low heat.
  4. Cover and cook very slowly for 2.5–3 hours, stirring occasionally, until the meat is completely tender and falling apart.
  5. If the sauce needs to thicken further, uncover for the final 20–30 minutes.
  6. Season with salt to taste — traditionally this dish uses no other seasoning beyond garlic, pepper, wine, and salt.
  7. Serve hot with plenty of the rich sauce.

Chef Tips

  • True peposo uses a generous, confident amount of whole peppercorns — this is meant to have real heat and character.
  • Low and slow is essential; this dish was traditionally cooked for hours near a kiln's gentle residual heat.
  • A robust, inexpensive Chianti works perfectly and keeps with the dish's humble origins.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Being too timid with the pepper, which is the dish's defining characteristic.
  • Rushing the cooking time, leaving the beef tough rather than properly tender.
  • Adding too many other seasonings or vegetables, straying from the traditionally minimal ingredient list.

Cultural Note

Peposo is closely tied to Impruneta, near Florence, historically associated with the terracotta and brick kiln workers said to have created the dish.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days; flavor deepens further the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, covered.

Serve with: Crusty Tuscan bread, Creamy polenta, A robust Chianti wine
