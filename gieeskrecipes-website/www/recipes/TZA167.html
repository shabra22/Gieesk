🍩 Swahili Coconut Mandazi

Mandazi wa Nazi

Swahili Coconut Mandazi — mandazi made with fresh coconut milk and a touch of cardamom, producing a richer, more fragrant, and softer result than plain mandazi. The coastal upgrade of Tanzania's everyday fried dough.

Coconut mandazi is the coastal version of the mainland's everyday fried dough — the addition of fresh coconut milk produces a mandazi that is richer, softer, and more fragrant. It is the breakfast served at coastal guesthouses and sold at coastal markets.

Ingredients

Equipment: Rolling pin, Deep frying pan

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 egg
  • 250ml warm coconut milk
  • Oil for deep frying

Method

  1. Mix flour, baking powder, sugar, cardamom, and salt.
  2. Beat egg into warm coconut milk. Add to dry ingredients.
  3. Mix to a soft dough. Knead 5 minutes.
  4. Rest covered 30 minutes.
  5. Roll to 1cm. Cut into triangles or circles.
  6. Fry at 170°C 3—4 minutes per side until golden.
  7. Drain. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Coconut milk must be warm — not cold — when adding to the dough.
  • The dough should be soft but not sticky.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cold coconut milk — dough does not come together properly.
  • Too high frying temperature.

Cultural Note

Coconut mandazi is the coast's morning gift — the same beloved fried dough the whole country eats, but enriched by the coconut that grows on every coastal palm, making it undeniably, unmistakably, coastal Tanzanian.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store 2 days. Best fresh.

Reheating: Warm in oven at 150°C.

Serve with: Chai ya Viungo (TZA144), Kahawa ya Zanzibar (TZA142)
