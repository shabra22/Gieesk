🍌 Kenyan Banana Porridge

Uji wa Ndizi

Kenyan Banana Porridge — very ripe bananas mashed into millet or sorghum porridge as it cooks, creating a naturally sweet, fragrant breakfast with no need for added sugar. A beloved Kenyan breakfast that transforms overripe bananas into something exceptional.

Banana porridge is one of the most intuitive and delicious Kenyan breakfast inventions — the very ripe banana dissolves completely into the hot porridge, sweetening it naturally while adding fragrance and creaminess. No sugar needed. It is particularly beloved in Western Kenya and Nyanza where banana cultivation is extensive.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium saucepan, Fork for mashing, Wooden spoon

  • ¾ cup finger millet or sorghum flour
  • 3 very ripe bananas — mashed to a smooth paste
  • 4 cups water
  • ½ cup milk — optional
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tbsp sugar — only if bananas are not very ripe

Method

  1. Mash the very ripe bananas until completely smooth.
  2. Mix millet flour with 1 cup cold water to a smooth slurry.
  3. Add the mashed banana to the flour slurry. Mix well.
  4. Bring remaining 3 cups of water to a boil.
  5. Pour the banana-flour slurry into the hot water in a steady stream, stirring constantly.
  6. Cook over medium heat for 8—10 minutes, stirring continuously, until thick and the banana is completely incorporated.
  7. Add salt and milk if using. Taste — add sugar only if needed.
  8. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The riper the banana, the better — black-spotted or fully overripe bananas provide the most sweetness and fragrance.
  • Mixing the mashed banana into the flour slurry before adding to hot water ensures even distribution.
  • Taste before adding any sugar — very ripe bananas make the porridge perfectly sweet on their own.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-ripe bananas — insufficient sweetness and the starchy banana taste does not dissolve into the porridge.
  • Adding banana pieces rather than mashed — they do not incorporate smoothly.

The Science

Very ripe bananas have undergone significant starch-to-sugar conversion — the starch content drops from about 70% in unripe bananas to under 3% when fully ripe, with the remainder converted to sucrose, fructose, and glucose. These natural sugars dissolve completely into the hot porridge, sweetening and flavouring it without any separate sweetening step.

Health Benefits

  • Natural fruit sugars for quick morning energy.
  • Potassium and vitamin B6 from bananas.
  • Calcium and iron from finger millet.
  • Naturally sweetened — no refined sugar needed.

Cultural Note

Banana porridge is particularly cherished in Gusii and Luo communities where banana cultivation is central to the agricultural landscape. It is a breakfast that uses the land's most abundant gift.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best consumed fresh.

Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of water.

Serve with: Kenyan Masala Chai, Boiled Eggs, Roasted Groundnuts
