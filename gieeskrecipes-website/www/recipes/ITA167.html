Pasta alla Zozzona

Rome's cheeky 'dirty pasta,' unapologetically combining the city's four great pasta traditions — carbonara, amatriciana, gricia, and cacio e pepe — into one deliberately over-the-top dish.

Zozzona, meaning roughly 'filthy' or 'messy' in Roman dialect, is a modern trattoria invention that mashes together guanciale, sausage, egg, pecorino, and tomato, essentially combining several classic Roman pastas' defining elements into one indulgent plate.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large sauté pan

  • 400 g (14 oz) rigatoni or mezze maniche
  • 150 g (5.3 oz) guanciale, diced
  • 200 g (7 oz) Italian sausage, casings removed and crumbled
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 4 egg yolks
  • 1 whole egg
  • 120 g (1¼ cups) grated Pecorino Romano, plus more for serving
  • Freshly ground black pepper, generous amount
  • Salt, for the pasta water

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the pasta until al dente.
  2. Meanwhile, cook the guanciale in a cold, dry pan over medium-low heat until the fat renders and it turns crisp, then remove and set aside, leaving the fat in the pan.
  3. In the same pan, cook the crumbled sausage until browned.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes to the sausage and simmer for 10 minutes.
  5. Whisk the egg yolks, whole egg, and pecorino together in a bowl with a generous amount of black pepper.
  6. Drain the pasta, reserving a generous cup of cooking water, and add it to the pan with the sausage-tomato mixture, tossing over low heat.
  7. Remove from the heat and quickly stir in the egg-cheese mixture along with the reserved guanciale, tossing vigorously and adding pasta water as needed to create a smooth, creamy sauce.
  8. Finish with extra pecorino and black pepper, and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Work off direct heat when adding the egg mixture to avoid scrambling it, just as with a traditional carbonara.
  • The starchy reserved pasta water is essential for pulling everything into a smooth, cohesive sauce.
  • This is a deliberately indulgent, rich dish — don't be shy with the pecorino and pepper.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the egg mixture over direct heat, causing it to scramble rather than form a silky sauce.
  • Skipping the reserved pasta water, leaving the sauce too thick and clumpy.
  • Being too restrained with the ingredients — zozzona is meant to be a maximalist, generous dish.

Cultural Note

Pasta alla zozzona is a beloved modern addition to Rome's pasta canon, born in the city's trattorie as a playful mashup of its four classic pasta traditions.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately; the egg-based sauce does not reheat well.

Reheating: Not recommended; best enjoyed fresh.

Serve with: A robust Roman red wine
