🔥 African Grill Chicken (Kati Kati)

Kati kati

Skin-on chicken pieces rubbed with onion and garlic powder, white and cayenne pepper, grilled 20–30 minutes until browned and almost cooked, then finished 4–5 minutes in a palm oil and tomato puree sauce.

Adamu writes that kati kati is one of the most ancient and distinctive dishes of the Nkom people in the Northwest province of Cameroon, and calls her recipe a modern, quick adaptation. The chicken is trimmed, patted dry and rubbed with a spice mix, then grilled on a hot barbecue, griddle or frying pan 20–30 minutes, rotating, until browned and almost cooked. Fresh tomatoes are blended and sautéed in palm oil over medium heat 4–5 minutes; the chicken goes in, the heat is lowered, 1/2–1 cup of water added, and it is covered and cooked 4–5 minutes until thoroughly cooked through. Seasoning and oil are adjusted. Served warm with fufu and njama njama.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grill, griddle or frying pan, Blender, Large saucepan

  • 3–4 pounds chicken, skin-on, cut into pieces
  • 1 1/4 teaspoons salt
  • 1 tablespoon onion powder
  • 1 1/2 tablespoons garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon white pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon cayenne pepper
  • 1 teaspoon bouillon powder (or more salt)

Sauce

  • 2 fresh tomatoes
  • Palm oil — quantity not printed
  • 1/2–1 cup water, or more as needed
  • Chicken bouillon to taste

Method

  1. Trim the fat from the chicken and pat dry. Rub with the salt, onion powder, garlic powder, white pepper, cayenne and bouillon.
  2. Preheat a hot barbecue, grill, griddle or frying pan. Grill the chicken 20–30 minutes, rotating, until browned and almost cooked.
  3. Blend the tomatoes. In a large saucepan over medium heat sauté the puree in palm oil for 4–5 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  4. Add the chicken, bring to a simmer over low heat, add 1/2–1 cup of water, cover and cook 4–5 minutes, until the chicken is well flavoured and thoroughly cooked through.
  5. Adjust the seasoning and oil. Serve warm with fufu and njama njama.

Chef Tips

  • Printed cues: ‘browned and almost cooked’ then ‘thoroughly cooked through’.
  • No internal temperature is printed; check the thickest piece is cooked through.
  • A reader comment says the old method roasts the chicken with the feathers on; the author’s reply says her recipe is the modern quick version.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the finishing simmer so the chicken stays dry.
  • Using too little oil in the sauce — the amount is not printed, so adjust to taste as the page says.
  • Pulling the chicken when it is only ‘almost cooked’.
