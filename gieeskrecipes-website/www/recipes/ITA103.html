🍰 Babà al Rum

Babà al Rum

A soft, yeasted Neapolitan cake soaked generously in rum syrup until it becomes almost spongy, said to have been born from a French pastry chef's invention adopted enthusiastically by Naples.

Baba's enriched, brioche-like dough is baked until light, then thoroughly drenched in a rum-scented sugar syrup that it absorbs almost entirely, resulting in a dessert with a genuinely surprising, almost custardy softness held together by its yeasted structure.

Ingredients

Equipment: Stand mixer with dough hook, Baba molds or a bundt pan, Saucepan for syrup

  • 300 g (2¼ cups) bread flour
  • 7 g (2¼ tsp) active dry yeast
  • 4 eggs
  • 30 g (2 tbsp) granulated sugar
  • 5 g (1 tsp) salt
  • 150 g (⅔ cup) unsalted butter, softened
  • 400 g (2 cups) granulated sugar, for the syrup
  • 600 ml (2½ cups) water
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dark rum
  • Whipped cream, for serving (optional)

Method

  1. Combine the flour and yeast in a stand mixer bowl, then add the eggs, sugar, and salt, mixing until a smooth, elastic, quite loose dough forms.
  2. With the mixer running, add the softened butter gradually, a little at a time, until fully incorporated and the dough is glossy and elastic.
  3. Cover and let rise in a warm place until doubled, about 1 hour.
  4. Divide the dough among buttered baba molds, filling each about halfway.
  5. Cover and let rise again until the dough reaches near the top of the molds, about 30–45 minutes.
  6. Bake at 190°C (375°F) for 18–20 minutes until golden and a skewer comes out clean.
  7. Meanwhile, combine the sugar and water for the syrup and simmer for 5 minutes, then remove from heat and stir in the rum.
  8. While still warm, dip or spoon the syrup generously over the baba cakes until they are thoroughly soaked and heavy with liquid.
  9. Serve at room temperature, with a dollop of whipped cream if desired.

Chef Tips

  • The dough should be quite loose and sticky compared to a standard bread dough — resist adding extra flour.
  • Soak the cakes generously and patiently, allowing the syrup time to fully penetrate rather than just sitting on the surface.
  • Day-old baba, slightly dried out, actually absorbs the rum syrup even better than very fresh cake.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding too much flour to make the sticky dough easier to handle, which results in a denser, less absorbent final cake.
  • Rushing the soaking step, leaving the interior dry rather than fully saturated.
  • Using syrup that's too cold, which doesn't penetrate the cake as readily as warm syrup.

Cultural Note

Babà is believed to have arrived in Naples via the Polish-French royal court, and the city has since made the dessert entirely its own, serving it at nearly every celebration.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, covered, for up to 3 days; the syrup helps preserve moisture.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature or gently warmed.

Serve with: Whipped cream, Fresh berries
