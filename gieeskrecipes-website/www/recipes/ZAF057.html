🌽 Tihove

Tihove

Samp boiled two and a half hours, beans cooked alongside, and crushed raw peanuts stirred through to bind them — the Vatsonga dish where the groundnut is what holds the whole thing together.

Tihove is the Tsonga cousin of Venda's tshidzimba and Tswana dikgobe: grain, legume and pounded nut, cooked long and eaten with a thin stew. The traditional tool is a wooden mortar that crushes the nuts to a fine powder, and the reason is structural rather than decorative — the groundnut is what makes the samp and the beans hold to one another instead of sitting in the bowl as two separate things. A simpler version leaves the beans out entirely and is still tihove.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, A second saucepan, for the beans, Wooden mortar and pestle, or a food processor

  • 3 cups samp (white corn)
  • 1 cup dried beans — sugar beans or cowpeas
  • 1 litre hot water, plus 2 to 3 litres more during cooking
  • 2 cups water, for the beans
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) salt, or to taste
  • 125 ml (1/2 cup) raw peanuts, crushed

Method

  1. Wash the samp and the beans separately. Soak them 15 minutes and skim off any debris that floats.
  2. Boil the samp in 1 litre of water, adding more as it is absorbed, for about 2 1/2 hours until soft. Taste it often rather than trusting the clock.
  3. Season the samp with about half a teaspoon of salt.
  4. At the same time, boil the beans in 2 cups of water until tender.
  5. Season the beans lightly with salt and set them aside.
  6. Crush the raw peanuts to a fine powder in a mortar, or grind them in a processor.
  7. Combine the cooked samp, the beans and the crushed nuts and stir thoroughly — the groundnuts are what bind the samp and the beans together.
  8. Simmer over very low heat for 30 minutes.
  9. Serve alongside beef stew with a thin gravy.

Chef Tips

  • Soaking the samp in hot water overnight cuts a good chunk off the two and a half hours.
  • Cook the beans in their own pot. Beans in with the samp go to mush long before the samp is soft.
  • Taste the samp rather than timing it. Samp varies enormously by brand and by age, and two and a half hours is a starting estimate.
  • The published version of this recipe calls for beans in four of its steps but leaves them out of the ingredient list altogether. A cup of sugar beans or cowpeas is the sensible reading, and the beanless version is also traditional.
  • Crushed, not blended. A fine powder binds; a paste just makes it claggy.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the beans and samp in one pot.
  • Under-cooking the samp, which stays chalky rather than creamy.
  • Using roasted or salted peanuts.
  • Letting the water run dry — keep topping it up through the whole two and a half hours.
  • Serving it without a gravy. It is meant to be eaten with something wet.

Health Benefits

  • Samp, beans and peanuts together form a complete protein.
  • High in fibre and slow-release carbohydrate.
  • Groundnuts supply monounsaturated fat, niacin and magnesium.
  • Contains peanuts — not suitable for anyone with a nut allergy.

Cultural Note

Tsonga food is the least written-down of South Africa's major cuisines, and tihove is one of very few Vatsonga dishes with a properly published recipe. The pattern it shares with tshidzimba and dikgobe — grain, legume, pounded groundnut — runs right across the north of the country and into Mozambique and Zimbabwe, irrespective of language.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Warm with a splash of water.

Serve with: Beef stew with a thin gravy, Mokhuse, Mashonzha
