🍚 Basic Fonio

Fonio — steamed

A cup of fonio washed until the water runs clear, then steamed in a cheesecloth-lined basket or boiled two minutes and rested — two ways, both Thiam’s.

37mTotal time
5mPrep
22mCook
4Servings
190Calories
EasyDifficulty

The base recipe for every fonio dish in this collection. The grain is submerged in warm water, swished and drained repeatedly until the water runs clear. For the traditional steamer method it goes into a cheesecloth-lined basket over simmering salted water for about 15 minutes, is fluffed, sprinkled with a few tablespoons of salted water and steamed 5 to 10 minutes more. For the stovetop method, 1 cup of water and a tablespoon of salt boil; the washed fonio is stirred in, covered, cooked on low about 2 minutes, then left off the heat 5 to 10 minutes. A cup of dry fonio makes about 4 cups.

Ingredients

Equipment: Steamer basket lined with damp cheesecloth, or medium pot with lid, Fine strainer

  • 1 cup uncooked fonio (or quinoa)
  • Salt
  • Stovetop method: 1 cup water and 1 tbsp salt — in the method, not the ingredient list
  • Steamer method: a few tablespoons of salted water for the second steam
  • Optional: a little butter or oil to keep the grains separate

Method

  1. Wash the fonio: submerge it in warm water, swish with your fingers, pour off and repeat several times until the water runs clear. Drain well.
  2. Steamer method: set the fonio in a steamer basket lined with damp cheesecloth over simmering salted water. Cover and steam about 15 minutes. Fluff with a fork, drizzle a few tablespoons of salted water over and steam another 5 to 10 minutes, until the grains are completely tender. Fluff.
  3. Stovetop method: boil 1 cup of water in a medium pot, stir in the washed fonio and the tablespoon of salt, cover tightly, reduce to low and cook about 2 minutes, until the water is just absorbed. Turn off the heat, fluff gently — especially the wetter bottom — cover and let sit 5 to 10 minutes, until tender.
  4. Fluff and serve.

Chef Tips

  • The washing is the step: the water should run clear.
  • Steaming in a basket is called ‘the traditional way’ on the JBF page.
  • Thiam also says a rice cooker works at a 1:1 ratio of fonio to water.
  • Butter or oil in the cooking water keeps the grains separate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the wash.
  • Over-stirring the stovetop fonio while it rests.

Health Benefits

  • Fonio is gluten-free and contains methionine and cysteine.
  • Rich in iron compared with most cereals.

Cultural Note

The JBF page gives no historical or cultural information about fonio or Senegal — only that it is healthy and gluten-free, and that steaming in a basket is the traditional way. That absence is recorded: the ‘fonio revival’ framing comes from other sources in this set.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam briefly or microwave covered.

Serve with: Any mafé, yassa or grilled meat
