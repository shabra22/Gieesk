🍝 Lagane e Ceci

Lagane e Ceci

One of Italy's oldest known pasta shapes, wide flat noodles paired here with chickpeas in a simple, rosemary-scented broth, a dish said to predate even ancient Roman cuisine.

Lagane is believed to be a direct ancestor of the word 'lasagna,' with references dating back to Roman times, and its pairing with chickpeas remains a defining dish of Basilicata's rustic, ancient culinary tradition.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Large pot

  • 300 g (2¼ cups) durum wheat flour
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) warm water
  • Pinch of salt
  • 300 g (1½ cups) dried chickpeas, soaked overnight
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • 1 dried chili, crumbled
  • 1.2 L (5 cups) water or vegetable stock
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus more for serving
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the flour, water, and salt into a firm dough, kneading until smooth. Rest, covered, for 20 minutes.
  2. Drain the soaked chickpeas and simmer in fresh water until tender, about 1 hour; reserve the cooking liquid.
  3. Warm the olive oil in a large pot and sauté the onion, garlic, rosemary, and chili until fragrant.
  4. Add the cooked chickpeas along with enough of their cooking liquid (or stock) to make a brothy consistency, and simmer for 15 minutes.
  5. Mash a portion of the chickpeas against the side of the pot to thicken the broth slightly.
  6. Roll the pasta dough out thin and cut into wide, irregular ribbons.
  7. Add the fresh pasta directly to the simmering chickpea broth and cook for 3–4 minutes until tender.
  8. Season to taste and serve hot with a generous drizzle of olive oil.

Chef Tips

  • Reserving and using the chickpea cooking liquid gives the dish far more depth than starting with plain water.
  • Mashing some of the chickpeas thickens the broth naturally without needing any flour or cream.
  • This dish is meant to be brothy and comforting rather than a typical saucy pasta.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Discarding the chickpea cooking liquid, missing a key source of flavor.
  • Making the pasta too thick, which takes longer to cook and can throw off the dish's balance.
  • Serving it too dry — lagane e ceci should have plenty of brothy liquid.

Cultural Note

Lagane e ceci is considered one of the oldest pasta dishes in Italy, with linguistic and historical roots tracing back to Roman times, and remains a defining dish of Basilicata.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; thickens further as it sits.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding water to loosen as needed.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A drizzle of good olive oil at the table
