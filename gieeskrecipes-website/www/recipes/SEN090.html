🌶️ Sosu Kaani, Habanero Chile Sauce

Sosu kaani / kani

Onion and garlic softened in oil, tomato paste cooked until lightly caramelised, then tomatoes, a minced habanero and a bay leaf reduced to a thick sauce — ‘used to add a kick to virtually every dish in Senegal’.

The pepper sauce that accompanies accara, pastels, fried fish and grilled meat. In a 2-quart saucepan over medium heat, oil is heated and onion and garlic cooked until soft; tomato paste is added and cooked until lightly caramelised; the chopped tomatoes, minced habanero or Scotch bonnet and a bay leaf are added and cooked until the sauce is reduced and thickened. It is cooled to room temperature and keeps for up to two weeks. The page says the sauce is used to add a kick to virtually every dish in Senegal, and names no region or ethnic group — the idea that kaani is a specifically Jola preparation is not supported by any source reached.

Ingredients

Equipment: 2-quart saucepan

  • 2 tbsp canola oil
  • 1 medium yellow onion, roughly chopped
  • 1 clove garlic, roughly chopped
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3 medium tomatoes, cored and roughly chopped
  • 1 habanero or Scotch bonnet chile, stemmed, seeded and minced
  • 1 bay leaf
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Heat the oil in a 2-quart saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion and garlic and cook, stirring, about 6 minutes, until soft.
  2. Add the tomato paste and cook about 2 minutes, until lightly caramelised.
  3. Add the tomatoes, chile and bay leaf and cook about 5 minutes, until the sauce is reduced and thickened.
  4. Season with salt and pepper. Let cool to room temperature. Remove the bay leaf before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Three printed cues: ‘until soft’, ‘until lightly caramelized’, ‘until sauce is reduced and thickened’.
  • The page keeps it for up to 2 weeks.
  • Mince the chile and wash your hands afterwards.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burning the tomato paste.
  • Under-reducing, which leaves a watery sauce.
  • Leaving seeds in if you want less heat.

Health Benefits

  • Tomatoes and onions add vitamins; habanero adds capsaicin.

Cultural Note

‘Used to add a kick to virtually every dish in Senegal’ is the page’s own description. The Saveur feature this belongs to is John O’Connor’s ‘A Feast for All’ (May 2012); the recipe has no individual byline.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Up to 2 weeks (the page does not state the storage conditions).

Reheating: Serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Accara (Accara, Black-Eyed Pea Fritters, Accara, Black-Eyed Pea Fritters from West Africa), Pastels (Pastels au Poisson, Pastels, Fried Fish Pies), Fried fish
