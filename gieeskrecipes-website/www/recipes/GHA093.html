🦀 Aprapransa with Palm Cream

Aprapransa — akpledzi, bɔbɔe, apragyaa

Skim off the palm oil rising to the top — you should end up with a cup and a half to two cups — then stir corn flour in until it pulls away from the sides.

The better-sourced aprapransa, with full quantities and two real cues. Two thirds of a cup of adzuki beans, salted cod, six shallots, ten ounces of smoked whiting, half a habanero, about three quarters of a large tin of cream of palm fruit, ten small tomatoes, powdered shrimp and two cups of toasted corn flour. The first cue is the skim: take off the palm oil rising to the top, and you should end up with about a cup and a half to two cups of oil. The second is the finish: stir the corn flour in constantly to prevent lumps until the mass pulls away from the sides.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Ladle, for skimming, Strong wooden spoon, Blender

  • 2/3 cup adzuki beans (150 ml)
  • About 1 tbsp salted cod (15 ml)
  • 6 shallots, or 1 1/4 cups chopped onion (300 ml)
  • About 10 oz smoked whiting (300 g)
  • About 1/2 habanero pepper
  • 1 large tin cream of palm fruit — you will use about 3/4 of it
  • 2 cups toasted corn flour, tom brown (500 ml)
  • About 10 small tomatoes, or several large
  • 2 tbsp powdered shrimp or crayfish (30 ml)

Method

  1. Cook the adzuki beans until tender.
  2. Build the palm fruit soup with the shallots, tomatoes, habanero, smoked whiting, salted cod and powdered shrimp.
  3. Simmer until the palm oil rises to the top.
  4. Skim off the palm oil rising to the top — you should end up with about 1 1/2 to 2 cups of oil.
  5. Set most of the soup aside, keeping some in the pot.
  6. Slowly stir the corn flour into the remaining soup, stirring constantly to prevent lumps.
  7. Stir in the rest of the soup.
  8. Keep stirring and cooking until it pulls away from the sides.
  9. Stir in the cooked beans.
  10. Serve with the skimmed palm oil spooned over.

Chef Tips

  • The skim is the step most versions omit and it is the one that makes the dish. A cup and a half to two cups of oil comes off, and it goes back on at the end rather than staying in the mass.
  • Pulls away from the sides is the finishing cue, the same test as banku and akple. That is when the corn flour has cooked.
  • Add the corn flour to only PART of the soup first, then stir the rest in. Two stages, and it is how you avoid lumps in two cups of flour.
  • Adzuki beans are her substitute for Ghanaian small red beans, declared as such. Any small red bean works.
  • Toasted corn flour, not cornmeal. This is the same tom brown that binds ayikple and dzowoe.
  • Smoked whiting and salted cod together — two preserved fish doing different jobs, the whiting for body and the cod for salt and depth.
  • King crab legs are offered as a substitution for the crab of the classic version. The dish is traditionally made with crab.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the palm oil in rather than skimming it.
  • Adding all the corn flour to all the soup at once.
  • Stopping before it pulls away from the sides.
  • Using plain cornmeal.
  • Leaving out the salted cod, which carries the savour.

Health Benefits

  • Adzuki beans supply plant protein, folate and soluble fibre.
  • Palm fruit cream is very high in provitamin-A carotenoids and tocotrienols.
  • Smoked fish and dried shrimp add protein, calcium and iodine.
  • Skimming the oil removes a substantial share of the fat from the mass itself.

Cultural Note

Aprapransa is a one-pot that collapses soup and swallow into a single mass, and the skimmed palm oil spooned back over the top is what makes it look like a celebration dish rather than a porridge. Its four names across four peoples is the clearest sign of how far a good coastal dish travels.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated. It sets very firm.

Reheating: Low heat with water or the reserved palm oil to loosen.

Serve with: Tomato stew, Fried fish, Shito
