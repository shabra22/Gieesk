🍛 Kenyan Tilapia Curry

Tilapia ya Curry

Kenyan Tilapia Curry — tilapia fillets simmered in a fragrant, warmly spiced tomato and onion sauce with curry powder, turmeric, and garam masala. All the bold flavour of a Kenyan meat curry but lighter and faster, with the delicate sweetness of tilapia.

Fish curry in Kenya draws on the Indian-Kenyan culinary heritage that has shaped coastal cooking for generations. The warmth of curry powder and garam masala pairs beautifully with the mild sweetness of tilapia, and because fish cooks so quickly, this is one of the fastest curries in the Kenyan repertoire — ready in under 30 minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pan, Wooden spoon

  • 600g tilapia fillets — cut into large pieces
  • 2 medium onions — finely chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 cup water or fish stock
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Season fish with salt, turmeric, and paprika.
  2. Cook onions in oil until golden — 8 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook for 6—8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add curry powder, cumin, and remaining turmeric. Stir 1 minute.
  6. Add stock or water. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  7. Add fish pieces. Spoon sauce over fish gently without stirring.
  8. Cover and cook for 8—10 minutes until fish is cooked through.
  9. Stir in garam masala and lemon juice gently.
  10. Scatter coriander and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Build the curry sauce fully before adding fish — the sauce should be thick and fragrant before the fish goes in.
  • Gently spoon sauce over the fish rather than stirring — tilapia is delicate.
  • Add garam masala at the very end to preserve its fragrance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the fish — it becomes dry and rubbery.
  • Thin, watery curry — reduce the sauce before adding fish.

The Science

Fish proteins cook at lower temperatures than meat, so the curry sauce must be fully developed before the fish is added. The brief cooking time means the fish benefits from the already-complex sauce rather than the sauce developing during cooking.

Cultural Note

Fish curry reflects the Indian-Kenyan culinary exchange that has enriched Kenyan coastal cooking for generations — the Kenyan spice blend bringing new depth to the naturally sweet flavour of Lake Victoria tilapia.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh. Refrigerate for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat very gently over low heat.

Serve with: Basmati Rice, Chapati, Wali wa Nazi, Kachumbari, Natural Yogurt
