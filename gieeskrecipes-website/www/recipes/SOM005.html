Bariis Iskukaris

Somalia's celebrated spiced rice, basmati simmered in a fragrant blend of cardamom, cinnamon, and cumin with raisins and caramelized onions, the centerpiece of nearly every Somali celebration.

Iskukaris means roughly 'cooked together,' referring to how the rice absorbs all its aromatics directly rather than being seasoned afterward, and the dish's warm spice blend reflects Somalia's centuries of trade across the Indian Ocean, sharing clear DNA with South Asian pilafs and Gulf-style rice.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed pot with lid

  • 500 g (2 1/2 cups) basmati rice, rinsed and soaked 30 minutes
  • 2 onions, thinly sliced
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil or ghee
  • 3 cardamom pods
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3 whole cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 50 g (1/3 cup) raisins
  • 750 ml (3 cups) water or stock
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the oil or ghee in a heavy pot and saute the onions over medium heat until deeply golden and caramelized, about 15-20 minutes.
  2. Remove half the onions and set aside for garnish.
  3. Add the cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, and cumin seeds to the remaining onions and cook briefly until fragrant.
  4. Stir in the garlic, ginger, and tomato paste, cooking for another minute.
  5. Add the drained rice and stir to coat in the spiced onion mixture.
  6. Add the raisins and water or stock, and season with salt.
  7. Bring to a boil, then reduce to the lowest heat, cover tightly, and cook for 18-20 minutes until the rice is tender and the liquid is absorbed.
  8. Let rest, covered, for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork.
  9. Serve topped with the reserved caramelized onions.

Chef Tips

  • Soaking the rice beforehand helps it cook evenly and achieve the proper fluffy texture.
  • Take the time to properly caramelize the onions - this step builds much of the dish deep flavor.
  • Resist lifting the lid while the rice steams, which lets essential steam escape.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the onions, missing the deep sweetness that slow caramelization provides.
  • Using too much liquid, resulting in mushy rather than fluffy, separate grains.
  • Skipping the resting time after cooking, which helps the rice finish steaming evenly.

Cultural Note

Bariis Iskukaris is the centerpiece of Somali celebrations, from weddings to Eid, its warm spice blend reflecting the country's long history as a crossroads of Indian Ocean trade.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently with a splash of water, covered, on the stovetop or in the microwave.

Serve with: Maraq (meat stew), Basbaas (hot sauce), A simple salad
