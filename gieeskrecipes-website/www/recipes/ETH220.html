🍶 Ethiopian Tamarind Juice

ተምር ጭማቂ — Tamar Chumaki

Ethiopian Tamarind Juice — tamarind pods soaked in warm water, kneaded to dissolve, strained, and sweetened with honey and a touch of cardamom. The refreshing sour drink of Ethiopia's lowland and eastern communities — intensely tart, naturally cooling.

Tamarind juice is the cooling drink of Ethiopia's hot lowland areas — where temperatures are high and tartness is welcome, the tamarind pod dissolved in water produces a drink that is simultaneously refreshing and nutritious.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Strainer

  • 100g tamarind pods (or 4 tbsp tamarind paste)
  • 1 litre warm water
  • 3 tbsp honey
  • ¼ tsp ground cardamom
  • Ice

Method

  1. If using pods: remove shells. Soak tamarind in warm water 10 minutes.
  2. Knead tamarind to dissolve pulp. Strain out seeds and fibres.
  3. If using paste: dissolve directly in warm water.
  4. Add honey and cardamom. Stir well.
  5. Chill. Serve over ice.

Chef Tips

  • Tamarind juice is intensely sour — adjust honey to taste.
  • Cold is essential — this is a refresher drink.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not straining thoroughly — seeds and fibres unpleasant.

The Science

Tamarind's primary acid is tartaric acid — rarer than citric or malic acid and producing a distinctive, deeper sourness. The tartaric acid content is also what makes tamarind an excellent digestive aid.

Health Benefits

  • Tartaric acid supports digestion.
  • Very high in B vitamins.
  • Iron from tamarind.
  • Naturally cooling.

Cultural Note

Tamarind juice is the drink of the Ethiopian lowlands — where the highland communities drink coffee and tella, the hot lowland areas of the Rift Valley and the east reach for tamarind to cool down.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Serve cold.

Serve with: As a cooling drink in hot weather, At Ramadan iftar as a sweet-sour refresher
