🥘 Ukun la Shiilay

Ukun la Shiilay

Eggs whisked with xawaash, parsley, green onion and jalapeño, poured over sautéed onion, mushroom and tomato, topped with cheese and finished under a lid — the everyday Somali scramble, eaten with canjeero.

This is the weekday version of Somali eggs and the one detail worth stealing is where the xawaash goes: into the raw egg, not into the hot pan. Whisked through the eggs it never touches direct heat, so it blooms as the eggs set instead of scorching in the oil — a small thing that separates a fragrant plate from a bitter one. It gets eaten with canjeero at the table, or rolled into a flatbread and carried.

Ingredients

Equipment: Frying pan with a lid, Mixing bowl, Whisk

    Per serving — scale up as needed

    • 2 large eggs
    • 30 ml (2 tbsp) parsley, chopped
    • 30 ml (2 tbsp) green onion, chopped
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) jalapeño pepper, chopped
    • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) xawaash
    • 1.25 ml (1/4 tsp) salt

    The pan

    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) olive oil
    • 35 g (1/4 cup) onion, finely diced
    • 20 g (1/4 cup) mushroom, chopped
    • 65 g (1/3 cup) tomatoes, diced
    • 45 ml (3 tbsp) cheddar cheese, grated

    Method

    1. In a bowl, combine the eggs, parsley, green onion, jalapeño, xawaash and salt. Whisk well.
    2. In a pan over medium heat, sauté the onion in the olive oil for 1 minute.
    3. Add the mushrooms and tomatoes and cook for 5 minutes.
    4. Pour in the egg mixture and scatter the grated cheese over the top.
    5. Cover and cook over low heat for 3 minutes, until just set.
    6. Serve with canjeero, or roll it into a flatbread with a spoonful of yoghurt.

    Chef Tips

    • The xawaash goes into the raw eggs. This is the whole technique — in the hot oil it scorches and turns bitter within seconds, and in the eggs it blooms gently as they set.
    • Low heat and covered for the last three minutes. Somali eggs are set soft, not browned.
    • Five minutes for the tomato and mushroom matters — you are driving off their water so the eggs do not go watery.
    • Whisk the herbs and chilli into the eggs rather than scattering them on top, so every mouthful has them.
    • It scales cleanly. Double or triple everything and use a wider pan, keeping the covered finish.
    • Seed the jalapeño if you want it milder; leave the seeds in if you want it to wake you up.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Frying the xawaash in the oil first.
    • Cooking it hard and fast, which gives you rubbery, browned eggs.
    • Not cooking the tomato long enough, so the eggs sit in liquid.
    • Over-setting it — it should still be a little soft when it comes off the heat.

    Health Benefits

    • Eggs give a complete protein with choline, selenium and B12.
    • Xawaash brings cumin, coriander, cinnamon and turmeric into breakfast.
    • One tablespoon of oil per serving and a quick cook.
    • Naturally free of gluten.

    Cultural Note

    Xawaash is the Somali spice blend and its reach is the thing to notice — it goes into the eggs at breakfast, the bean stew at lunch and the fish curry at dinner. A household's xawaash is its signature, and no two are quite the same, which is why Somali food tastes recognisable but never identical from one kitchen to the next.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately. Refrigerate up to 1 day.

    Reheating: Warm very briefly in a covered pan.

    Serve with: Canjeero, Sabaayad, Shaah Bigays
