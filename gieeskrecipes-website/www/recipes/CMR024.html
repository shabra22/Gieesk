🫘 Koki au soja (Cowpea and Soy Koki)

Koki au soja

Half a cup each of cowpeas and soy ground and beaten as for koki, mixed with slightly heated palm oil, salt and four large chillies, tied in flamed banana leaves or plastic and steamed 2 hours.

The soy variant of the book’s koki. The method follows the cowpea koki: soak about an hour, rinse, grind, beat 30 minutes in a mortar in one direction, add slightly heated palm oil in portions, then salt, then water until the dough holds together. The book allows wrapping in flamed banana leaves tied with string or in plastic. Steam 2 hours. The same ‘200 g de sel’ appears in the ingredient line and is reported as printed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle, Steamer

  • 1/2 cup cowpeas (niébé; cup size not printed)
  • 1/2 cup soy
  • 200 g salt (as printed — a suspected misprint)
  • 4 large chillies
  • 2/3 litre red palm oil
  • Water as needed — quantity not printed
  • Banana leaves, string or plastic

Method

  1. Soak the cowpeas and soy about an hour. Rinse several times.
  2. Grind to a paste, then turn it in a mortar, always in one direction, for 30 minutes.
  3. Add the slightly heated palm oil in portions, then salt. Add water until the dough holds together.
  4. Wrap in flamed banana leaves tied with string, or in plastic. Steam for 2 hours.
  5. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Same cues as Koki de niébé (CIFOR Northern Cameroon Recipe Book): light paste after the beating, dough that holds together.
  • The salt figure is as printed; check it first.
  • Soy and cowpea are measured by the cup; the cup size is not printed.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Salting at the printed 200 g without checking.
  • Skipping the 30 minutes of beating.
  • Undercooking: the page gives only the 2-hour time.

Cultural Note

As in the rest of the book, no community is named. This is the soy-enriched variant of the cowpea koki.

Storage & Serving
