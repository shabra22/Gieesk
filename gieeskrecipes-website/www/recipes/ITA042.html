Pasta e Fagioli

Pasta e Fagioli is Italy's beloved pasta-and-bean soup — thick, hearty, and endlessly comforting, with countless regional variations from Naples to Veneto.

A true dish of cucina povera, pasta e fagioli turns humble beans and small pasta shapes into a deeply satisfying meal, thickened simply by pureeing a portion of the beans rather than relying on any cream or roux.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Immersion blender or potato masher

  • 400 g (14 oz) canned cannellini or borlotti beans, drained and rinsed (or 250g dried, soaked and cooked)
  • 200 g (7 oz) small pasta (such as ditalini or small shells)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, plus more for serving
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 carrot, finely chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 1 L (4 cups) vegetable or chicken stock
  • 1 Parmigiano-Reggiano rind (optional, for depth)
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Heat the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrot, and celery, and cook until softened, about 8 minutes.
  2. Add the garlic and rosemary, cooking for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add the crushed tomatoes, beans, stock, and Parmigiano rind if using. Bring to a simmer.
  4. Remove about a third of the beans and puree with an immersion blender or mash with a fork, then stir back into the pot — this naturally thickens the soup.
  5. Simmer for 15–20 minutes to let the flavors meld.
  6. Add the pasta directly to the pot and cook until al dente, about 8–10 minutes, adding a splash more stock or water if the soup becomes too thick.
  7. Remove the rosemary sprig and Parmigiano rind. Season with salt and pepper.
  8. Serve hot with a drizzle of good olive oil.

Chef Tips

  • Mashing or pureeing a portion of the beans is the traditional way to thicken this soup without any cream or flour.
  • Add the pasta only when close to serving — it continues absorbing liquid and can turn mushy if the soup sits too long afterward.
  • A Parmigiano rind simmered in the soup adds wonderful savory depth at no extra cost.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the pasta separately and adding it at the end — cooking it directly in the soup lets it absorb flavor and naturally thickens the broth further.
  • Making the soup too far ahead with the pasta already in it, resulting in bloated, mushy pasta.
  • Skipping the bean-mashing step, leaving a thinner, less satisfying soup.

Health Benefits

  • Beans are an excellent source of plant-based protein and fiber.
  • A filling, nutrient-dense, budget-friendly meal.

Cultural Note

Nearly every region of Italy has its own version of pasta e fagioli, from Naples' tomato-forward style to Veneto's version, which sometimes includes pork rind for extra richness.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days — the pasta will continue to absorb liquid, so add a splash of stock or water when reheating.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding liquid as needed to loosen.

Serve with: Crusty bread, Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, A light red wine
