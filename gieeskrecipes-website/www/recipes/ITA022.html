🍝 Spaghetti Aglio e Olio

Spaghetti Aglio, Olio e Peperoncino

Aglio e Olio proves that the simplest Italian pasta dishes are often the best — just garlic, good olive oil, chili, and parsley, tossed with spaghetti for a dish that comes together faster than delivery.

Born as late-night, end-of-the-month kitchen-cupboard cooking in Naples, this dish relies entirely on technique and quality ingredients rather than a long ingredient list — slowly infusing olive oil with garlic without burning it is the whole game.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large skillet, Tongs

  • 400 g (14 oz) spaghetti
  • 120 ml (½ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • 6 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 1–2 dried red chilies, crumbled (or ½ tsp red pepper flakes)
  • 4 tbsp finely chopped fresh parsley
  • Fine sea salt, to taste
  • Freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for serving (optional, not traditional)

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of generously salted water to a boil and cook the spaghetti until just shy of al dente.
  2. While the pasta cooks, gently heat the olive oil in a large skillet over low heat. Add the sliced garlic and chili.
  3. Cook slowly, swirling occasionally, until the garlic turns pale gold and fragrant — 3–5 minutes. Do not let it brown or burn, or it will taste bitter.
  4. Reserve a cup of pasta water, then drain the spaghetti just before it reaches full al dente.
  5. Add the pasta directly to the skillet along with a splash of pasta water, tossing constantly over medium heat for 1–2 minutes to finish cooking and emulsify the sauce.
  6. Add more pasta water as needed until the oil clings glossy to each strand.
  7. Stir in the parsley, taste and adjust salt, and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Low and slow is the key to the garlic — burnt garlic will make the whole dish bitter.
  • Finishing the pasta in the skillet with starchy water is what creates the light, clinging sauce rather than just oily noodles.
  • Traditionalists skip the cheese entirely — it's not part of the classic Neapolitan version.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Browning or burning the garlic, which turns the whole dish bitter.
  • Skipping the pasta water, resulting in a greasy rather than emulsified sauce.
  • Overcooking the pasta before it hits the skillet, since it continues cooking there too.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in heart-healthy monounsaturated fat from olive oil.

Cultural Note

This dish is a beloved staple of la cucina povera — humble, resourceful Italian home cooking — and remains one of the most-cooked late-night meals across Italy, requiring only pantry staples.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; refrigerate leftovers for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a skillet with a splash of olive oil or water.

Serve with: A simple arugula salad, Crusty bread, A crisp white wine
