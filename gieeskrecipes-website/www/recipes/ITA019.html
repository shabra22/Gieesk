🍙 Arancini Rice

Arancini di Riso

Arancini are Sicily's beloved fried rice balls — a crisp, golden breadcrumb shell giving way to warm, cheesy risotto rice, often hiding a savory surprise of ragù or mozzarella at the center.

Named for their resemblance to little oranges (arancia means orange in Italian), these stuffed, breaded, and deep-fried rice balls are a Sicilian street food icon, traditionally made using leftover risotto — proof that some of Italy's greatest dishes began as resourceful ways to use what was on hand.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep pot or deep fryer, Three shallow bowls for breading, Slotted spoon, Thermometer

  • 1 batch cooked Risotto Base, cooled completely (or leftover risotto)
  • 2 large eggs, divided
  • 100 g (1¼ cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 150 g (5 oz) mozzarella, cut into small cubes
  • 150 g (1 cup) fine breadcrumbs
  • 80 g (⅔ cup) all-purpose flour
  • 120 ml (½ cup) water
  • Vegetable oil, for deep frying
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Mix the cooled risotto with 1 beaten egg and the Parmigiano-Reggiano until well combined.
  2. Wet your hands and take a generous handful of rice mixture. Flatten in your palm, place a cube of mozzarella in the center, and mold the rice around it into a tight ball or classic cone shape.
  3. Repeat with the remaining rice, placing formed arancini on a tray. Refrigerate for at least 1 hour to firm up.
  4. Set up a breading station: whisk the flour and water into a smooth batter in one bowl, and breadcrumbs in another.
  5. Dip each arancino into the flour batter, letting excess drip off, then roll in breadcrumbs until fully and evenly coated.
  6. Heat oil to 175°C (350°F) in a deep pot.
  7. Fry the arancini in batches, without crowding, for 4–5 minutes until deeply golden brown and heated through.
  8. Drain on paper towels and season with salt while hot. Rest for 2–3 minutes before serving — the centers are extremely hot.

Chef Tips

  • The risotto must be completely cold before shaping — warm rice won't hold together and makes the mozzarella melt out during frying.
  • Pack the rice tightly around the filling to prevent the arancini from falling apart in the oil.
  • Maintain a steady oil temperature — too hot burns the coating before the inside heats through; too cool makes them greasy.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using warm or freshly made risotto instead of properly chilled rice.
  • Skipping the chilling step after shaping, causing them to fall apart when fried.
  • Overcrowding the frying oil, which drops the temperature and results in soggy, oil-logged arancini.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of calcium from the cheese.
  • Can be adapted with vegetable or meat fillings for variety.

Cultural Note

Arancini are so central to Sicilian identity that different cities favor different shapes — round in Palermo, conical in Catania, where locals say the shape represents Mount Etna.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate uncooked, breaded arancini for up to 1 day, or freeze for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Reheat cooked arancini in a 190°C (375°F) oven for 12–15 minutes until hot through and re-crisped; avoid microwaving.

Serve with: Marinara dipping sauce, Aperitivo spreads, A simple green salad
