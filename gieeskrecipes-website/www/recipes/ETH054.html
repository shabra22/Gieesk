🍲 Ethiopian Harari Beef Stew

የሐረሪ ሥጋ ወጥ — Ye-Harari Siga Wat

Harari Beef Stew — the beef wat of Harar, Ethiopia's ancient walled Islamic city. Distinctly different from highland wats — influenced by Arab and Somali trading connections, with more cardamom, cinnamon, and ginger and less berbere intensity.

The Harari beef stew reflects Harar's position as Ethiopia's most important Islamic city and a major Arab trade hub — the spice balance is more influenced by the Arab spice tradition, with aromatic spices predominating over the raw chilli heat of highland berbere.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot

  • 700g beef — cubed
  • 3 onions — diced
  • 3 tbsp oil or kibbeh
  • 2 tbsp berbere
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ½ tsp ground ginger
  • ¼ tsp ground cloves
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp fresh ginger
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 1 cup water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Dry caramelise onions — 15 minutes.
  2. Add oil/kibbeh. Add berbere, cardamom, cinnamon, ginger, and cloves. Toast 2 minutes.
  3. Add garlic, fresh ginger, and tomatoes. Cook 5 minutes.
  4. Add beef. Add water. Simmer covered 70 minutes.
  5. Season.

Chef Tips

  • The cinnamon and cardamom distinguishes Harari stew from highland wats.
  • Slightly less berbere than Key Wat — the Arab spice influence moderates the heat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too much berbere — loses the Harari character.

The Science

See ETH021 for general wat science. The cinnamon in the Harari stew contains cinnamaldehyde — a volatile aromatic compound that creates a warm, sweet note absent from standard Key Wat.

Cultural Note

Harari beef stew is the taste of one of Africa's most ancient cities — a walled Islamic city that has been a centre of trade, scholarship, and cultural exchange for over a millennium. Its food reflects all of this history.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001) or lahoh (Harari flatbread), Harari white rice
