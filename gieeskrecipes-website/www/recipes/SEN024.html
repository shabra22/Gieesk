🍖 Lamb Mafé with Dawadawa

Mafé — lamb

Three pounds of lamb in a tomato-and-vegetable base, finished with three cups of ground roasted peanuts and left to stand until the red oil rises.

210 minTotal time
30 minPrep
150 minCook
6Servings
960Calories
MediumDifficulty

The long, layered Saveur mafé. Lamb is salted and browned in two batches; garlic, onions, bell pepper, celery and leek cook down for ten minutes; a whole cup of tomato paste is cooked with thyme and six bay leaves until it sticks and darkens. Lemon juice mixed with a tablespoon of ground dawadawa — the fermented locust-bean seasoning, here used already fermented — deglazes the pot, beef stock follows, and the lamb cooks about an hour and a half with carrots and cabbage. Roasted peanuts are puréed with a cup and a half of the cooking liquid and returned, and after a final 30 minutes the pot rests another half hour until the red oil rises to the surface.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Spider skimmer or slotted spoon, Food processor or blender, Plate

  • 3 lb boneless leg of lamb, cut into 2-inch cubes
  • Kosher salt — 1 tbsp used in the first step, more to taste (not in the printed list)
  • 3/4 cup peanut oil, divided
  • 1 tbsp finely chopped garlic
  • 2 large onions, finely chopped
  • 1 large green bell pepper, finely chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, finely chopped
  • 1/4 leek or 5 scallions, finely chopped
  • 1 cup tomato paste
  • 1 1/2 tsp dried thyme
  • 6 bay leaves
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice mixed with 1 tbsp ground dawadawa (or 1/2 cup balsamic vinegar)
  • 8 cups beef stock
  • 3 large carrots
  • 1 habanero, coarsely chopped or left whole for milder heat (optional)
  • 1/4 head green cabbage, cored
  • 3 cups roasted peanuts (or 1 1/2 lb smooth or chunky unsweetened natural peanut butter)
  • 8 oz fresh or frozen okra (optional)
  • Steamed white rice, for serving — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Season the lamb with 1 tablespoon of salt. Heat 1/2 cup of the oil in a large heavy pot over medium-high until hot and shimmering. Brown half the lamb, turning once, about 10 minutes total, until golden on all sides. Move to a plate and repeat with the rest. Leave the oil in the pot.
  2. Add the garlic, onions, bell pepper, celery and leek and cook, stirring often, until softened — about 10 minutes.
  3. Add the tomato paste, thyme, bay leaves and the remaining oil. Cook, stirring occasionally, 7 to 9 minutes, until the paste sticks to the bottom and darkens.
  4. Stir in the lemon–dawadawa mixture, scraping up the browned bits. Add the beef stock and bring to a boil. Reduce to medium-low and simmer uncovered about 15 minutes, until slightly reduced.
  5. Add the carrots, habanero if using, cabbage and the browned lamb with its juices. Season, return to a simmer, cover, and cook about 1 hour 30 minutes, stirring now and then, until the lamb falls apart easily when pierced with a fork.
  6. Lift the carrots and cabbage out with a spider skimmer into a bowl and keep warm.
  7. Process the peanuts with 1 1/2 cups of the cooking liquid, adding more as needed, until smooth and creamy. Add to the pot (peanut butter, if using, goes in directly). If using okra, pulse it to small pieces and add. Stir about 1 minute.
  8. Return to a simmer, cover, and cook about 30 minutes, until the peanut mixture melts into the sauce. Remove from the heat and let stand about 30 minutes, until the red oil rises to the surface.
  9. Serve with rice, the carrots and cabbage on the side or in the sauce.

Chef Tips

  • ‘Until the paste sticks to the bottom and darkens’ is the key tomato-paste cue — you are frying the paste, not warming it.
  • The red oil rising to the surface at the end is the finish line for the whole dish.
  • Dawadawa here is ground, pre-fermented, and added without any fermentation step on your part; balsamic vinegar is the printed alternative.
  • Use the cooking liquid, not water, to grind the peanuts — it carries flavour into the sauce.
  • Browning in two batches matters; a crowded pot steams the lamb.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Crowding the pot when browning.
  • Pulling the pot off the heat before the lamb falls apart.
  • Skipping the 30-minute rest — the oil does not rise without it.
  • Adding the vegetables back too early.

Cultural Note

Dawadawa is the Hausa name for the fermented locust-bean seasoning the Wolof call nététou. Bintou N’Daw’s printed note gives it as an alternative to balsamic vinegar — a hint that the recipe was written for kitchens where neither is a pantry staple.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of stock; the sauce tightens in the cold.

Serve with: Steamed white rice, Carrots and cabbage from the pot
