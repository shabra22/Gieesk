🍢 Tanzanian Chicken Skewers

Mishkaki ya Kuku

Mishkaki ya Kuku — chicken thigh cubes marinated in garlic, ginger, lime, and Tanzanian spices, grilled over charcoal until golden and juicy. The lighter, popular alternative to beef mishkaki — equally beloved and found at every street food jiko.

Chicken mishkaki are the street food of Tanzania's health-conscious — lighter than beef but equally fragrant and satisfying. Chicken thigh is always used — it stays juicy over charcoal where breast would dry out.

Ingredients

Equipment: Skewers, Charcoal grill

  • 600g chicken thighs — boneless, cubed
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp lime juice
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Mix marinade. Toss chicken in marinade. Refrigerate 2 hours.
  2. Thread onto skewers.
  3. Grill over medium-high coals 3—4 minutes per side.
  4. Internal temperature should reach 74°C.
  5. Serve with chapati and pili pili.

Chef Tips

  • Chicken thighs only — breast dries out.
  • Test doneness — no pink in the centre.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using chicken breast.
  • Under-cooking — food safety risk.

The Science

Chicken thigh myoglobin content gives the meat more moisture retention at high temperatures compared to breast — the fat marbling within the muscle fibres keeps the thigh juicy even when the surface is charred.

Health Benefits

  • High lean protein.
  • Lower fat than beef mishkaki.

Cultural Note

Chicken mishkaki has become the preferred street food of Tanzania's growing middle class — the lighter option at the jiko that still delivers all the satisfaction of the charcoal-grilled experience.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: Chapati (TZA004), Kachumbari, Pili Pili Sauce
