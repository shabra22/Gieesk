🫓 Masa in Masa Moulds

Masa — waina

Two and a half cups of sinasir rice, a shorter four-to-five-hour soak, the same eight-to-twelve-hour ferment — and cooked in a divoted masa mould until golden.

This version names the pan, which is the thing most masa recipes leave out: masa moulds, a traditional tray of divots that gives the cakes their shape. The timings are set out as a clock — rice soaked at five in the afternoon for four or five hours, the yeast bloomed and the batter blended at ten at night, then eight to twelve hours covered in a warm place, and frying at eight the next morning. Four tablespoons of cooked rice go in as the starter, considerably more than the other documented version, and the doneness cue is simply that it has taken on some golden colour.

Ingredients

Equipment: Masa moulds — a divoted tray, Large bowl, Blender, Spoon

  • 2 1/2 cups raw sinasir rice
  • 1 1/2 tsp instant or active dry yeast
  • 4 tbsp sugar
  • 1/4 tsp ground akaun (cooking potash), optional
  • 4 tbsp cooked rice
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 cup warm water, for the yeast
  • Water, for soaking
  • A light coating of oil, for the moulds

Method

  1. Day 1, around 5 PM: soak the rice in water 4 to 5 hours.
  2. Day 1, around 10 PM: dissolve the sugar and the akaun in 1 cup of warm water and sprinkle over the yeast. Let it bloom 8 to 10 minutes.
  3. Blend the soaked rice with the yeasted water until smooth. Add the cooked rice and blend again.
  4. Cover and rest 8 to 12 hours in a warm place.
  5. Day 2, around 8 AM: add the salt, and optional sugar if it needs it.
  6. Heat the masa moulds with a light coating of oil.
  7. Fill the divots with a spoonful or two of batter.
  8. Allow to cook about 2 to 3 minutes on each side, or till it has taken on some golden colour.

Chef Tips

  • Masa moulds are the traditional pan — a tray of divots that gives the cakes their shape. A flat frying pan is the diaspora workaround and it works, but the shape is different.
  • Four to five hours on the soak, not overnight. The other documented version soaks overnight, and they disagree.
  • Eight to twelve hours on the ferment, which is the figure both documented sources agree on exactly. This is the number to trust.
  • Four tablespoons of cooked rice as the starter, against one and a half in the other version. More backslop means a faster, more assertive ferment.
  • Golden colour is the cue rather than a strict time. Two to three minutes a side is the guide.
  • The clock laid out on the source page is genuinely useful — soak at five, blend at ten, fry at eight. It makes masa a plan rather than a project.
  • Sinasir rice is specified by name. It is the soft, sticky variety, the same type used for tuwo shinkafa.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Soaking overnight when this recipe wants four or five hours.
  • Skimping on the cooked-rice starter.
  • Filling the divots too full so they do not set in the middle.
  • Pouring oil into the moulds rather than coating them lightly.
  • Frying before it has had its full eight hours.

Cultural Note

The masa mould is one of those pieces of equipment that defines a food — a cast tray of shallow divots, heated over coals, each hole producing one small round cake with a pale middle and a browned edge. Kebbi is particularly associated with masa, and Daily Trust's reporting there found sellers who had been making it in the same spots for decades.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days at room temperature, 4 refrigerated.

Reheating: Dry pan, a minute a side.

Serve with: Miyan Taushe, Suya, Honey or sugar
