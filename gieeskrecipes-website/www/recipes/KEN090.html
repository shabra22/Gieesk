🥗 Kenyan Vegetable Medley

Mboga Mchanganyiko

Kenyan Vegetable Medley — a colourful stir-fry of cabbage, carrots, green beans, capsicum, onions, and tomatoes cooked with garlic and a simple Kenyan spice blend. The most versatile, nutritionally diverse, and universally appealing vegetable side dish in the Kenyan repertoire.

Vegetable medley is the contemporary Kenyan kitchen at its most resourceful — a colourful, quick stir-fry of whatever vegetables are available, elevated by garlic, a good spice blend, and high heat. It is the side dish that goes with everything, accommodates any dietary need, and transforms humble ingredients into something bright and satisfying.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wok or frying pan, Wooden spoon

  • ¼ medium cabbage — shredded
  • 2 medium carrots — julienned
  • 150g green beans — trimmed and halved
  • 1 red bell pepper — sliced
  • 1 green bell pepper — sliced
  • 2 medium onions — sliced
  • 2 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce — optional

Method

  1. Prepare all vegetables before starting — stir-frying moves quickly.
  2. Heat oil in a large wok or pan over high heat.
  3. Add onions and carrots. Stir-fry for 2—3 minutes.
  4. Add green beans. Stir-fry for 2 minutes.
  5. Add garlic. Cook 30 seconds.
  6. Add bell peppers and cabbage. Toss and stir-fry for 2—3 minutes.
  7. Add tomatoes, curry powder, paprika, salt, and pepper.
  8. Stir-fry for 2—3 minutes more — vegetables should be tender-crisp, not soft.
  9. Add soy sauce if using. Toss.
  10. Scatter coriander and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Prepare (mise en place) everything before turning on the heat — stir-frying happens fast.
  • Add vegetables in order of density: carrots and beans first, soft vegetables last.
  • High heat is essential — low heat steams the vegetables rather than stir-frying them.
  • Do not overcrowd the pan — cook in two batches if needed.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Low heat — produces steamed, soggy vegetables rather than bright, crisp stir-fry.
  • Adding everything at once — different vegetables need different cooking times.
  • Overcooking — vegetables should retain colour and a slight crunch.

The Science

Stir-frying in a hot pan creates the Maillard reaction on the surfaces of vegetables while the brief cooking time preserves vitamins and minerals that are lost with longer cooking methods. The high heat also drives off moisture quickly, preventing steaming and maintaining texture.

Health Benefits

  • Extraordinary vitamin and mineral diversity from multiple vegetables.
  • Rich in vitamins A, C, and K from the variety of greens.
  • High in dietary fibre from multiple sources.
  • Very low in calories while being highly filling.

Cultural Note

Vegetable medley has become a staple of Kenyan urban cooking — practical, adaptable, and nutritious. It represents the contemporary Kenyan kitchen embracing both traditional and modern ingredients in one versatile, colourful dish.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately. Refrigerate for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Reheat quickly in a hot pan to preserve texture.

Serve with: Ugali, Steamed Rice, Fried Chicken, Grilled Beef, Chapati
