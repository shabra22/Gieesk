Qamdi — Mahamri

Triangles of lightly sweet yeasted dough dropped into hot oil and splashed until they puff hollow — sold from every Somali market stall and eaten with sambuusa and a glass of spiced milk tea.

Qamdi belongs to the family of East African fried breads that includes mahamri and mandazi, and it originated in the Swahili towns along the coast before settling into Somali kitchens. It is less sweet than a doughnut and not treated as bread in Somalia the way mandazi is in Kenya and Tanzania, where it is used to scoop up beans. Here it is teatime food, paired with samosas, and sold beside bur on the street.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Wok or deep frying pan, Slotted spoon

    The yeast starter

    • 1 tbsp instant dry yeast
    • 60 ml (1/4 cup) lukewarm water
    • 1 tsp sugar

    The dough

    • 560 g (4 cups) plain flour
    • 100 g (1/2 cup) sugar
    • 2 large eggs
    • 120 ml (1/2 cup) water
    • 60 ml (1/4 cup) oil
    • 1/4 tsp ground cardamom, or 1 tsp vanilla (optional)

    For frying

    • Vegetable or canola oil, enough to deep fry

    Method

    1. Mix the yeast, lukewarm water and the teaspoon of sugar and leave it at least 10 minutes, until it is foaming.
    2. Combine the flour, sugar, eggs, water, oil, the cardamom or vanilla if using, and the activated yeast. Mix, then knead into a smooth ball.
    3. Cover and leave somewhere warm for 2 hours.
    4. Divide the risen dough into 4 equal pieces and roll each into a ball, then press into a disc.
    5. Roll each disc out to a circle about 5 mm thick and cut each circle into quarters.
    6. Cover the cut triangles and let them proof for 30 minutes.
    7. Heat the oil in a wok or deep pan over medium heat. Slide in a few triangles.
    8. As soon as each one floats, spoon hot oil over the top — this is what makes it puff hollow instead of staying flat.
    9. Turn when the underside is golden, about a minute, and fry the other side for another minute.
    10. Drain on paper towels and serve warm.

    Chef Tips

    • Splashing hot oil over the top the moment it surfaces is the technique. Without it you get a flat fried triangle rather than a hollow puff.
    • Measure the flour by scooping it in airily and levelling the cup, not by packing it. Packed flour gives a heavy dough that will not puff.
    • An overnight rise in the fridge instead of the two hours gives a better flavour if you can plan that far ahead.
    • For an eggless version, use 3 cups flour, 1/3 cup sugar, 1 tbsp yeast, 1/2 cup coconut milk, 1/2 cup water and 1/4 tsp baking powder, with the same method.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Skipping the oil-splashing, which is the difference between a puffed qamdi and a fried scrap of dough.
    • Frying in oil that is too hot, so the outside browns before the inside has risen.
    • Rolling the dough too thin, which gives a crisp biscuit with no hollow.
    • Cutting the second proof short, leaving the triangles dense.

    Health Benefits

    • Lightly sweetened compared with a doughnut — half a cup of sugar across sixteen pieces.
    • Eggs contribute protein.
    • Dairy-free as written, and the eggless variation is fully plant-based.

    Cultural Note

    Qamdi travels under half a dozen names along this coast — mahamri, mamri, mandazi, and ba khamri in Yemen — and the name changes tell you the trade route it followed. In Somalia it is street food and teatime food: bought hot, eaten with a samosa and a cup of shaah cadays.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best the day they are made. Keep a day in a paper bag; freeze for a month.

    Reheating: Warm in a hot oven for 5 minutes to bring back the crust. A microwave makes them tough.

    Serve with: Sambuusa, Shaah Cadays, Bur
