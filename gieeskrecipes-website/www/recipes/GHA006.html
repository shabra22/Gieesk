🍌 Ampesi

Ampesi — boiled plantain, yam or cocoyam

Plantains boiled in their skins fifteen to twenty minutes until fork-tender — half of the Akan pairing whose other half is kontomire stew.

Ampesi is boiled starch, and its simplicity is the point: plantain, yam or cocoyam, cooked in plenty of salted water until fork-tender and served with a stew poured beside rather than over it. Four large unpeeled plantains, fifteen to twenty minutes, fork-tender. It is one half of a fixed Akan pairing — the other half is kontomire stew, and one named Ghanaian author describes kontomire stew as one of the two components of an Akan classic, which is ampesi. Only the plantain timing is documented; yam and cocoyam times are not published anywhere consulted.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Fork, for testing, Colander, Knife

  • 4 large plantains, unpeeled
  • Water, to cover
  • Salt, to taste

Alternatives, times not documented

  • Yam, peeled and cut into rounds
  • Cocoyam (taro), scrubbed

Method

  1. Wash the plantains and trim the ends, leaving the skins on.
  2. Cut into large sections if the pot is small.
  3. Cover with water, salt it, and bring to the boil.
  4. Boil 15 to 20 minutes, until fork-tender.
  5. Drain, and peel while still warm.
  6. Serve alongside kontomire stew or garden egg stew, with the stew beside the starch rather than poured over it.

Chef Tips

  • Skins on while boiling. They hold the plantain together and slip off easily once it is cooked.
  • Fork-tender, not soft. Ampesi is meant to hold its shape on the plate — this is not fufu and it is not mashed.
  • Fifteen to twenty minutes is for plantain. Yam and cocoyam take longer, and no source consulted publishes their times, so test with a fork rather than trusting a number.
  • Salt the water generously. It is the only seasoning the starch gets.
  • Peel while warm. Cold plantain skin clings.
  • The pairing with kontomire stew is not a suggestion but the standard form — an Akan cook describes the two as the components of one classic dish.
  • Half-ripe plantain gives the best ampesi: firm enough to hold, sweet enough to taste of something.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Peeling before boiling, which lets the plantain waterlog.
  • Boiling until soft, so it breaks up.
  • Unsalted water.
  • Using a yam or cocoyam time borrowed from the plantain figure.
  • Pouring the stew over the top rather than serving it beside.

Health Benefits

  • No added fat.
  • Plantain is high in potassium, vitamin A and vitamin B6.
  • Half-ripe plantain carries substantial resistant starch, which has a lower glycaemic impact.
  • Gluten-free and vegan as written.

Cultural Note

Ampesi and kontomire stew are treated as one dish rather than two by Akan cooks, which is why a recipe for either on its own looks incomplete. It is weekday food — quick, cheap, no pounding — and the plate is assembled with the boiled starch on one side, the dark green stew on the other, and often a boiled egg and sliced avocado alongside.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated once peeled.

Reheating: Steam, or pan-fry the slices in a little oil.

Serve with: Kontomire Stew, Garden Egg Stew, Palaver Sauce
