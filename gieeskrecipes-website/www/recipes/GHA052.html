🫙 Shito, the Long Cook

Shito

Four hours, seven scotch bonnets and no chilli powder at all — simmered low, stirred every five minutes, until very dark with oil on top.

The other way to make shito: four cups of oil, four hours, and fresh scotch bonnets instead of chilli powder. The seafood is powdered first, the onions blended, and the garlic, ginger and seven scotch bonnets blended without water. Tomato paste fries about four minutes, the onion goes in for about ten until it browns and dries out, then the garlic-ginger-pepper blend simmers low for about an hour and a half, stirred every five minutes, until darkened and reduced. The powders follow for a further hour, stirred every ten. Her cue: ensure it is very dark with oil on top, and cook longer if needed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Blender, Wooden spoon, Three 500 ml jars

  • 4 cups vegetable, canola or sunflower oil, plus more as needed
  • 1 cup dry smoked crayfish or shrimp
  • 3 whole medium dry smoked herring, preferably unsalted
  • 2 very large onions
  • 1/2 head garlic
  • 1/2 cup ginger, chopped
  • 7 small scotch bonnets
  • 1 tube (130 g) tomato paste
  • 2 tsp anise seeds
  • 1 tbsp paprika
  • 3 tsp allspice powder
  • 1 heaping tbsp dried thyme
  • 1-2 shrimp bouillon cubes (optional)
  • 1 tbsp red pepper flakes (optional)

Method

  1. Powder the smoked crayfish and herring.
  2. Blend the onions.
  3. Blend the garlic, ginger and scotch bonnets WITHOUT water.
  4. Heat the oil and fry the tomato paste about 4 minutes.
  5. Add the blended onion and cook about 10 minutes, until the onions brown and dry out.
  6. Add the garlic, ginger and pepper blend. Simmer on low, stirring every 5 minutes, about 1 1/2 hours, until darkened and reduced.
  7. Add the seafood powders.
  8. Simmer a further hour, stirring every 10 minutes.
  9. Add the anise, paprika, allspice, thyme and optional bouillon and pepper flakes.
  10. Ensure the shito is very dark in colour with oil on top. Cook longer if needed.
  11. Add up to 1 cup of additional oil if needed and simmer 30 minutes before jarring.

Chef Tips

  • Blend the garlic, ginger and scotch bonnets with NO water. Water in the blend means an hour of boiling it off before the shito can start to darken.
  • Until the onions brown and dry out, about ten minutes. Dried-out onions are the foundation — wet onions will never let the sauce go dark.
  • Stir every five minutes for the long simmer. Set a timer; four hours of shito is a commitment and a scorched batch at hour three is heartbreaking.
  • Very dark with oil on top, and cook longer if needed. Her cue allows for the fact that stoves and pots differ, which is more honest than a fixed time.
  • Seven scotch bonnets and no chilli powder, against a hundred and twenty-five grams of chilli powder and no fresh chilli in the quick version. These are genuinely different sauces.
  • Anise, paprika, allspice and thyme go in at the END, after the long cook. Early and the volatile aromatics would simply boil away.
  • Unsalted smoked herring if you can get it, as the source specifies. Salted herring will make the whole batch too salty to use freely.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding water to the pepper blend.
  • Moving on before the onions have dried out.
  • Long unstirred intervals.
  • Adding the ground spices at the start.
  • Using salted herring.

Health Benefits

  • A very high dried-seafood content, so rich in protein, calcium, iodine and selenium.
  • Capsaicin from seven scotch bonnets.
  • Allspice, anise and thyme all carry documented antioxidant compounds.
  • Used by the spoonful, so the oil per serving is small.

Cultural Note

Four hours on a pot of shito is the diaspora version of a thing that in Ghana gets made in enormous batches over charcoal while other work goes on around it. The instruction to stir every five minutes for ninety minutes is what a Ghanaian cook would simply do without counting, and writing it down is what makes the recipe transmissible.

Storage & Serving

Storage: A month or more refrigerated under its oil. The long cook and the oil seal both help.

Reheating: Not needed. Served cold or at room temperature.

Serve with: Kenkey, Banku, Waakye
