🥄 Xawaash

Xawaash

The all-purpose Somali spice blend that gives the whole cuisine its signature — cumin and coriander in equal measure, warmed with cardamom, cinnamon, clove and pepper, finished with turmeric off the heat.

Xawaash is to Somali cooking what berbere is to Ethiopian: the one thing in the cupboard that makes everything else taste Somali. The name comes from the Arabic hawa'ij, meaning requirements or essentials, and the blend is generally held to have travelled from Yemen along the Red Sea trade routes. The proportions are not fixed and never have been — every household leans its own way — but cumin and coriander carry the blend, and the turmeric is stirred in after toasting because it is already a powder and would scorch in the pan.

Ingredients

Equipment: Dry frying pan, Spice grinder, or a mortar and pestle, Fine sieve, Airtight jar

    Whole spices, to toast

    • 120 g (1/2 cup) cumin seeds
    • 35 g (1/2 cup) coriander seeds
    • 2 tbsp black peppercorns
    • 1 tbsp cinnamon bark, broken into pieces
    • 1 tbsp green cardamom pods
    • 1 tsp whole cloves

    Added off the heat

    • 2 tbsp ground turmeric

    Method

    1. Put every whole spice — cumin, coriander, peppercorns, cinnamon, cardamom pods and cloves — into a dry pan over medium heat. Leave the turmeric aside.
    2. Toast for 4 to 5 minutes, stirring the whole time, until the spices smell fragrant. Under-toasting is much better than over-toasting; the moment they smell scorched the batch is finished.
    3. Tip the spices onto a cold plate and let them cool completely. Grinding them warm leaves the blend damp and it will cake in the jar.
    4. Grind in small batches, either in a spice grinder or with a mortar and pestle, until very fine.
    5. Sieve the ground spice and return anything coarse to the grinder. Repeat until nothing is left in the sieve but the cardamom husks, which you discard.
    6. Stir in the ground turmeric last, off the heat and away from the pan.
    7. Store in an airtight jar away from light. Use within about three months, while it still smells of the pan.

    Chef Tips

    • Cardamom is the dial to turn. Somali cooks add extra when the blend is going into rice, and hold back when it is going into maraq or plain meat, where too much cardamom dominates everything else.
    • Cumin and coriander are the two that matter. If you cannot source everything on the list, get those two right and the blend will still taste like xawaash.
    • Ground cumin will work if whole seeds are impossible to find, but pre-ground spice has already lost most of its oil and the difference is obvious in the finished dish.
    • The word means different things in different parts of the country: in the south xawaash is specifically the savoury blend for meat, stew and soup, while in the north it is used more broadly for any spice mix, including the one that goes into tea and coffee.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Toasting the turmeric with the whole spices, where it burns almost immediately and turns the whole batch bitter.
    • Grinding the spices while still warm, which makes a paste rather than a powder and causes the blend to clump in storage.
    • Skipping the sieve, which leaves gritty fragments of cinnamon bark and cardamom husk in what should be a fine powder.
    • Making a jar far larger than the household will use in a season — ground spice fades fast, and a year-old batch tastes of nothing.

    Health Benefits

    • Cumin and coriander seeds both carry compounds traditionally used to settle digestion.
    • Turmeric contributes curcumin, the pigment behind the blend's colour.
    • A way to season food generously with almost no salt.

    Cultural Note

    Xawaash is the single ingredient Somali cooks abroad ask relatives to post to them. Households keep their own proportions, and a cook's blend is recognisable to family at the first mouthful — which is why the recipe is usually taught by smell in someone else's kitchen rather than written down.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Airtight jar, away from light and heat. Best within 3 months, usable for 6.

    Reheating: Not applicable — but a 20-second warm in a dry pan revives an older jar before use.

    Serve with: Bariis Iskukaris, Suqaar, Maraq
