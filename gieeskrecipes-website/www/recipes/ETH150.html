🧄 Ethiopian Garlic Butter Sauce

ቅቤ ነጭ ሽንኩርት — Qibe Nech Shinkurt

Ethiopian Garlic Butter Sauce — kibbeh with abundant sliced garlic slowly cooked until the garlic is golden and fragrant, with a squeeze of lemon. The sauce used over grilled fish, tibs, and injera — kibbeh transformed by garlic into something greater.

Ethiopian garlic butter sauce takes kibbeh as its base and enriches it with slowly cooked golden garlic — the garlic caramelises into the kibbeh fat, producing a sauce of extraordinary fragrance and depth used across Ethiopian grilling and fish preparations.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small saucepan

  • 3 tbsp kibbeh
  • 6 garlic cloves — thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander or parsley
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Melt kibbeh over medium-low heat.
  2. Add sliced garlic. Cook slowly — 5—6 minutes until golden.
  3. Do not rush — garlic must be golden, not burnt.
  4. Remove from heat.
  5. Add lemon juice (it will sizzle).
  6. Add herbs and salt.
  7. Pour over fish, tibs, or use as a dipping sauce.

Chef Tips

  • Sliced garlic, not minced — it caramelises more evenly.
  • The sizzle when lemon hits the hot kibbeh emulsifies them slightly.
  • Pour immediately — this sauce is best the moment it is made.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burnt garlic — bitter and acrid.
  • Pre-making — loses its immediacy.

The Science

See ETH089 for sliced garlic caramelisation science. The lemon juice added to the hot kibbeh creates a temporary emulsion — the citric acid and water of the lemon partially emulsify into the fat, creating a less oily, more unified sauce.

Health Benefits

  • Garlic allicin — powerfully antimicrobial and antioxidant.
  • Kibbeh vitamins A, D, E.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian garlic butter sauce is the modern restaurant's finishing touch — the sauce that arrives at the table still sizzling, poured over grilled fish by the server, filling the restaurant with the smell of golden garlic in kibbeh.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Make to order — best immediately.

Reheating: Can be remelted but loses freshness.

Serve with: Over grilled fish (ETH085), On tibs (ETH022), As a dipping sauce for injera
