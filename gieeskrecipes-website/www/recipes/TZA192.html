🥯 Tanzanian Simit Bread Ring

Mkate wa Mviringo

Tanzanian Mkate wa Mviringo — a round bread ring coated in sesame seeds, lightly sweetened and baked until golden. The coastal Tanzanian bread ring sold at morning markets — the distant cousin of the Turkish simit, transformed by the Swahili coast.

The sesame bread ring on the Tanzanian coast reflects the ancient connections between the Swahili coast and the Middle Eastern and Asian trading worlds. Sold at morning markets, it is the bread ring that workers and students buy on the way to the day's work.

Ingredients

Equipment: Baking sheet, Oven at 190°C

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp yeast
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • ¾ cup warm water
  • 4 tbsp sesame seeds — toasted
  • 1 tbsp honey mixed with 1 tbsp water for glazing

Method

  1. Dissolve yeast and sugar in warm water. Rest 10 minutes.
  2. Mix flour and salt. Add yeast mixture and oil. Knead 8 minutes.
  3. Rest covered 1 hour until doubled.
  4. Divide into 8. Roll each into a rope. Form into a ring. Pinch ends.
  5. Dip in honey-water. Coat in sesame seeds.
  6. Place on baking sheet. Rest 15 minutes.
  7. Bake at 190°C for 18—20 minutes until golden.

Chef Tips

  • Honey-water dip makes the sesame seeds adhere.
  • Pinch the ring ends very firmly — they open during baking if poorly sealed.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Poorly sealed ring ends.
  • Insufficient sesame coating.

The Science

The honey-water glaze serves as an adhesive for the sesame seeds — the sugars in honey are hygroscopic and slightly sticky, binding the sesame to the dough surface. During baking, the sugar caramelises, creating an even more robust bond.

Cultural Note

The sesame bread ring is the Tanzanian coast's connection to the ancient maritime world — the bread shape carried to East Africa on the same dhows that brought cloves and cardamom from the north.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store 2 days. Freeze 2 months.

Reheating: Warm briefly in oven.
