Schiacciata all Uva

A seasonal Tuscan focaccia studded with whole wine grapes, baked until the fruit bursts and caramelizes into jammy pockets throughout the sweet, olive-oil-rich dough.

Made each autumn during the grape harvest using small, seedy wine grapes rather than table grapes, schiacciata all'uva turns a humble bread dough into a genuinely celebratory seasonal treat, the grapes' skins bursting and caramelizing as they bake.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Baking sheet or pan

  • 500 g (4 cups) bread flour
  • 7 g (2 1/4 tsp) active dry yeast
  • 280 ml (1 1/8 cups) warm water
  • 80 ml (1/3 cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus more for drizzling
  • 80 g (1/3 cup) granulated sugar
  • 8 g (1 1/2 tsp) salt
  • 800 g (1.75 lb) small wine grapes (such as Concord or similar seedy grapes)
  • 2 tbsp fennel seeds (optional)

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in the warm water and let sit for 5 minutes.
  2. Combine the flour, sugar, and salt, then add the yeast mixture and olive oil, mixing into a soft dough.
  3. Knead for 8-10 minutes until smooth, then let rise, covered, in a warm place for about 1.5-2 hours until doubled.
  4. Divide the dough into two portions.
  5. Press one portion into an oiled baking pan or sheet, stretching to fill it.
  6. Scatter about two-thirds of the grapes over the dough, pressing them in gently.
  7. Roll or stretch the second portion of dough over the top, sealing the edges.
  8. Press the remaining grapes into the top layer and scatter with fennel seeds if using.
  9. Drizzle generously with olive oil and a little extra sugar.
  10. Bake at 200C (400F) for 35-40 minutes until deeply golden.
  11. Cool slightly before slicing and serving.

Chef Tips

  • Small, seedy wine grapes are traditional and have a more intense flavor than large seedless table grapes.
  • Press the grapes gently into the dough so they stay in place rather than rolling off during baking.
  • This bread is best enjoyed the day it is made, while the grapes are still juicy and the crust is fresh.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using large, mild table grapes instead of small, intensely flavored wine grapes.
  • Not pressing the grapes in firmly enough, causing them to fall off during baking.
  • Underbaking, leaving the center doughy rather than fully cooked through.

Cultural Note

Schiacciata all'uva is a beloved seasonal treat across Tuscany, traditionally made each autumn to coincide with the wine grape harvest.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store covered at room temperature for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: A glass of Vin Santo
