🥩 Kenyan Pepper Steak

Steki ya Pilipili

Kenyan Pepper Steak — a thick sirloin or ribeye crusted with cracked black peppercorns, seared to a perfect crust, finished with a cognac-cream pepper sauce. Kenya's hotel steakhouse at its finest — using the country's excellent beef with a classic French technique.

Kenya produces excellent beef, particularly from Borana and Boran-cross cattle. The pepper steak showcases this quality — a properly seared sirloin with a deeply caramelised crust, finished with a peppercorn cream sauce that is simultaneously rich and pungent. At Nairobi's finest hotels, this is the benchmark steak dish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy cast-iron or thick-bottomed pan, Tongs

  • 2 sirloin or ribeye steaks (250g each) — room temperature
  • 2 tbsp whole black peppercorns — coarsely crushed
  • 2 tsp coarse salt
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 tbsp butter

Pepper Sauce

  • 2 shallots — finely diced
  • 2 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp brandy or cognac
  • 1 cup beef stock
  • ½ cup heavy cream
  • 1 tbsp green peppercorns in brine
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard

Method

  1. Press crushed peppercorns firmly onto both sides of each steak. Season with salt.
  2. Heat pan over highest heat until smoking.
  3. Add oil. Sear steaks for 3—4 minutes per side for medium-rare.
  4. Add butter. Baste continuously for final minute.
  5. Remove and rest 5 minutes.
  6. In the same pan: cook shallots and garlic 2 minutes.
  7. Add brandy — carefully, it may flame. Reduce.
  8. Add stock. Reduce by half.
  9. Add cream, green peppercorns, and mustard. Simmer until thickened.
  10. Pour over rested steaks.

Chef Tips

  • Room temperature steak is essential — cold steak cooks unevenly.
  • The pan must be smoking hot before the steak goes in — this is non-negotiable for a proper crust.
  • Rest the steak as long as it cooked — 4 minutes cooking means 4 minutes resting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cold steak in the pan.
  • Pan not hot enough — steams instead of sears.
  • Not resting — juices lost immediately on cutting.

The Science

The Maillard reaction requires surface temperatures above 140°C. A smoking-hot cast iron pan exceeds this threshold immediately on contact, creating the characteristic dark brown crust. The resting period allows the thermally driven redistribution of juices — cut immediately and they pool on the board; rested and they stay in the meat.

Cultural Note

Pepper steak at a Nairobi hotel represents the confident marriage of classical French technique with Kenya's excellent domestic beef — a dish that demonstrates the sophistication of Kenya's modern hospitality industry.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Warm briefly in oven — never microwave steak.
