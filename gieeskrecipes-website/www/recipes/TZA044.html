🍜 Tanzanian Chicken Coconut Soup

Supu ya Kuku wa Nazi

Tanzanian Supu ya Kuku wa Nazi — chicken simmered in a fragrant coconut milk broth with ginger, garlic, lemon grass, and coriander. The coastal healing soup — light, fragrant, and deeply nourishing.

Chicken coconut soup is the coastal Tanzanian healing food — lighter than the stew version, more brothy, and fragrant with lemon grass and coriander. Given to the sick and served as a starter at coastal celebrations.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 600g chicken pieces — bone-in
  • 400ml coconut milk
  • 4 cups water
  • 2 stalks lemon grass — bruised
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 2 medium onions — roughly chopped
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander
  • Lime wedges

Method

  1. Simmer chicken with water, onion, garlic, ginger, lemon grass, and turmeric for 35—40 minutes.
  2. Remove chicken. Shred finely. Discard lemon grass.
  3. Add coconut milk to the broth. Simmer gently 8 minutes.
  4. Return shredded chicken.
  5. Season. Garnish with coriander. Serve with lime.

Chef Tips

  • Lemon grass must be bruised — cut or pound to release its aromatic oils.
  • Never boil after adding coconut milk.
  • Lime at the table brightens every bowl.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not bruising lemon grass.
  • Boiling coconut milk.

The Science

Lemon grass contains citral — a volatile aromatic compound released when the stalk is bruised and exposed to heat. Extended boiling destroys it; the brief simmer in step 2 preserves it.

Health Benefits

  • High lean protein.
  • Coconut milk provides MCTs.
  • Ginger and turmeric are anti-inflammatory.

Cultural Note

Chicken coconut soup is the soup of the Tanzanian coast — given with love to the sick, served at celebrations, and eaten by families on cool evenings along the Indian Ocean shore.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Gently — never boil.

Serve with: With rice, As a starter, As a healing broth alone
