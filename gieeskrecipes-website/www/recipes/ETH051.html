🍲 Ethiopian Zigni (Eritrean Beef Wat)

ዝግኒ — Zigni

Tigrinya Zigni — the Northern Ethiopian and Eritrean version of beef wat. Made with a slightly different spice balance (more cardamom and cloves than southern versions), served with injera and tigrinya accompaniments. The beloved beef stew of the Tigrinya-speaking north.

Zigni is the Tigrinya-speaking community's Key Wat — similar in technique but distinguished by a different spice balance that reflects the northern highland tradition. The cardamom and cloves are more prominent; the berbere slightly different in composition from southern blends.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot

  • 700g beef — cubed
  • 3 onions — finely diced
  • 3 tbsp kibbeh
  • 4 tbsp berbere
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ¼ tsp ground cloves
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 cup water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Dry caramelise onions — 20 minutes.
  2. Add kibbeh. Add berbere, cardamom, and cloves. Toast 3 minutes.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, tomato paste. Cook 2 minutes.
  4. Add beef. Coat.
  5. Add water. Simmer covered 80—90 minutes.
  6. Season.

Chef Tips

  • The cardamom and cloves distinguish zigni from other Key Wats.
  • Same dry-caramelise technique — this never changes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Omitting the cardamom — the northern spice balance is the point.

The Science

See ETH021 for Key Wat cooking science. The addition of cardamom (1,8-cineole) and cloves (eugenol) adds two distinctive aromatic compounds to the berbere base — producing a more complex, more fragrant result than southern Key Wat alone.

Health Benefits

  • Cardamom aids digestion.

Cultural Note

Zigni is the taste of Tigray and Eritrea — the beef wat that defines the northern highland table. The cardamom note is immediately recognisable to those who know it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), With Tigrinya salad and ayib
