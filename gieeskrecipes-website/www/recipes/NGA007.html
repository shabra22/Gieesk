Iyan — pounded yam

Boiled white yam worked straight from the pot — four or five small pieces at a time, thirty seconds to a minute, hot enough to go elastic rather than sticky.

Iyan is the aristocrat of the Nigerian swallows and the worst-documented. No named source consulted publishes a weight, a yam-to-water ratio or a yield for it — the instruction lives in video. What does survive in print is the one detail that actually determines the result: the yam goes into the processor straight from the boiling pot, still fiercely hot. Cold yam will not stretch. It is a time, not a quantity, and it is the load-bearing instruction.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Food processor with a dough blade, or a mortar and pestle, Damp cling film or a wrapping leaf

  • 4-5 small pieces boiled white yam, per batch, taken straight from the boiling pot
  • Water, for boiling

Notes on the yam

  • A dense white yam (puna). Water yam will not work — that goes to ikokore

Method

  1. Peel the yam thickly and cut into even chunks. Rinse.
  2. Boil in plenty of water until a knife slides in with no resistance.
  3. Fit the dough blade to the food processor.
  4. Add 4 to 5 small pieces of yam directly from the boiling pot — do not let them cool.
  5. Press the button and let it spin 30 seconds to 1 minute, until smooth, silky and elastic with no lumps.
  6. Turn out and repeat with the next batch, working the batches together while everything is still hot.
  7. Shape into rounds and wrap, or serve at once.

Chef Tips

  • Straight from the boiling pot. This is the whole technique. Yam that has cooled goes gluey instead of elastic and no amount of further working recovers it.
  • Work in small batches — four or five pieces. Overload the bowl and the blade slides over the top without pulling the yam through.
  • Thirty seconds to a minute is enough. Past that you are heating it by friction and it starts to break down.
  • A dough blade, not a chopping blade. You are kneading, not cutting.
  • The target is stated plainly: smooth, silky and elastic, with no lumps. If you can see lumps it is not finished.
  • The traditional mortar and pestle does the same thing and gives a better texture — the processor is the declared shortcut, titled 'the Easy Way' by its own author.
  • Keep a bowl of water beside you to wet your hands when shaping. Iyan sticks to everything dry.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting the yam cool before working it.
  • Filling the processor bowl.
  • Running the machine for several minutes.
  • Using water yam instead of a dense white yam.
  • Adding water to the processor — nothing goes in but hot yam.

Health Benefits

  • White yam is a good source of potassium and vitamin C.
  • Contains resistant starch, which ferments in the colon as a prebiotic.
  • Fat-free as cooked — nothing is added but the yam.
  • Gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Iyan carries more status than any other Nigerian swallow, and the mortar is part of why: the rhythmic double-pounding is audible across a compound and signals that something is being done properly for someone. The Vanguard, writing about ikokore, measures that dish's importance to the Ijebu against 'pounded yam for Ekiti men' — iyan is the yardstick other dishes get held to.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Eat at once. It firms within the hour and does not keep well.

Reheating: Wrap in damp cling film and steam or microwave in 30-second bursts.

Serve with: Efo Riro, Egusi, Caking Method, Ofe Nsala
