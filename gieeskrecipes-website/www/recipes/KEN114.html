🍳 Kenyan Chips Mayai

Chips Mayai

Chips Mayai — Kenya's most beloved street food omelette. Freshly fried chips embedded in a spiced egg omelette, cooked until golden and set. The combination of crispy chips and soft, fragrant egg is completely irresistible and unmistakably Kenyan.

Chips mayai (literally 'chips eggs' in Swahili) is one of the great Kenyan street food inventions — simple, cheap, filling, and somehow better than the sum of its parts. Freshly fried chips are tossed into a beaten egg omelette seasoned with onions, tomatoes, and coriander, then cooked in a pan until the egg sets around the chips like a frittata. Every Kenyan who has eaten it in the street at 9pm remembers it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep frying pan (for chips), Large flat pan or non-stick frying pan (for omelette)

  • 3 medium potatoes — peeled and cut into chips
  • Oil for deep frying
  • 4 large eggs — beaten
  • 1 medium onion — finely diced
  • 1 medium tomato — finely diced
  • 1 green chilli — finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ½ tsp curry powder
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Fry the chips at 175°C until golden and crispy. Drain and season lightly with salt.
  2. Beat the eggs with salt, pepper, and curry powder.
  3. Add the diced onion, tomato, chilli, and coriander to the beaten egg. Stir to combine.
  4. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a large flat pan over medium heat.
  5. Add the fried chips to the pan in a single layer.
  6. Pour the egg mixture over the chips, ensuring all chips are coated.
  7. Cook without stirring for 3—4 minutes until the bottom is set and golden.
  8. Flip carefully (using a plate to help) and cook the other side for 2—3 minutes.
  9. Slide onto a cutting board. Cut into wedges. Serve with kachumbari.

Chef Tips

  • The chips must be freshly fried and hot when the egg goes on — cold chips produce a soggy result.
  • Use a plate larger than the pan to flip: place the plate on top, invert the pan, then slide the omelette back in.
  • The omelette should be golden and set on the outside but still slightly moist inside.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cold chips — the omelette does not set around them properly.
  • Too much egg — the chips drown in egg and the ratio is wrong.
  • Too high heat — burns the outside before the inside sets.

The Science

The chips embedded in the egg create pockets of steam during cooking as the moisture in the potato evaporates. This steam helps cook the egg from within, explaining why chips mayai sets more evenly than a flat omelette despite its thickness.

Cultural Note

Chips mayai is one of Nairobi's most celebrated street foods — the kind of meal that Kenyans living abroad specifically miss and return to immediately when they come home. It is cheap, filling, and packed with the flavours of home.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a pan — do not microwave.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Pili Pili Sauce, Avocado, Bread, Kenyan Masala Chai
