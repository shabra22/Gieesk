Sartù di Riso

A grand Neapolitan rice timbale layered with meatballs, peas, sausage, and mozzarella, baked in a dome shape and unmolded dramatically at the table — a centerpiece of celebratory Sunday meals.

Sartù reflects Naples' aristocratic French culinary influence from the Bourbon era, its elaborate, multi-component construction and dramatic dome presentation once reserved for the grandest noble tables, now a beloved feature of special family occasions.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl-shaped baking dish, Wide pan for sauce and filling

  • 500 g (2½ cups) risotto rice
  • 1 L (4¼ cups) chicken stock
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) unsalted butter
  • 100 g (1¼ cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 3 eggs
  • 200 g (7 oz) ground beef, for small meatballs
  • 150 g (5.3 oz) Italian sausage, sliced
  • 150 g (5.3 oz) frozen peas
  • 200 g (7 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 200 g (7 oz) mozzarella, diced
  • 80 g (1 cup) breadcrumbs, for the mold
  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Cook the rice risotto-style in the chicken stock until just tender, then stir in the butter, Parmigiano, and eggs once slightly cooled. Set aside.
  2. Form small meatballs from the ground beef and brown them in a pan.
  3. In the same pan, sauté the onion, then add the sliced sausage and browned meatballs, along with the crushed tomatoes, and simmer for 15 minutes.
  4. Stir in the peas in the final few minutes.
  5. Butter a bowl-shaped baking dish and coat with breadcrumbs.
  6. Press about two-thirds of the rice mixture into the dish, building up the sides to form a well in the center.
  7. Fill the center with the meatball and sausage filling, and scatter with diced mozzarella.
  8. Cover with the remaining rice, sealing the filling completely inside.
  9. Bake at 190°C (375°F) for 30–35 minutes until golden.
  10. Let rest for 10 minutes, then invert onto a serving plate to unmold, revealing the dome shape.

Chef Tips

  • Building up the rice sides evenly around the dish creates the necessary well to hold the filling.
  • Let the timbale rest before unmolding, which helps it hold together for a clean release.
  • This is genuinely a special-occasion dish — plan for the multiple components it involves.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not sealing the filling completely with rice, which can cause it to leak out during baking.
  • Unmolding too soon, before the structure has had time to set and firm up.
  • Overcooking the rice at the base stage, leaving it too soft to hold its shape once baked.

Cultural Note

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat covered in the oven until warmed through.

Serve with: A robust Campanian red wine
