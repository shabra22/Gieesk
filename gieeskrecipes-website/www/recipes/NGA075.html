🍌 Kekefiyai

Kekefiyai

Unripe plantain boiled ten minutes, then everything else added without stirring for another ten — and only then is it stirred together and simmered two minutes.

Kekefiyai is an Ijaw plantain pottage and it has the same structural rule as several other Niger Delta and Efik dishes: do not stir, and then stir. Four fingers of diced unripe plantain boil for ten minutes on their own. Then the peppers, crayfish, onions, scent leaves, dry fish, periwinkles, salt and half a cup of palm oil all go in at once — without stirring — and cook another ten minutes until the plantain softens. Only at that point is everything stirred together and simmered low for about two minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium pot with a lid, Sharp knife, Wooden spoon

  • 4 fingers of unripe plantain, washed, peeled and diced
  • 2 tbsp blended crayfish
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 4 medium fresh peppers, blended or diced
  • 1 big dry fish
  • 2 bouillon cubes
  • A handful of scent leaves
  • 1/2 cup palm oil
  • 1/2 cup periwinkle (optional)
  • Water, to cover
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Wash, peel and dice the plantains.
  2. Boil water, add the plantains, and cook 10 minutes.
  3. Add the peppers, crayfish, bouillon cubes, onions, scent leaves, dry fish, periwinkles, salt and palm oil — all without stirring.
  4. Cook 10 minutes, until the plantains soften.
  5. Now stir everything together and simmer on low heat approximately 2 minutes.
  6. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Do not stir when the second batch of ingredients goes in. Diced unripe plantain breaks down into mush if you agitate it before it has softened properly.
  • Then stir. The two-minute simmer after stirring is what brings it together into a pottage rather than a pot of separate things.
  • Unripe plantain specifically — firm and starchy. Ripe plantain will dissolve and sweeten the dish in a way it is not meant to be sweet.
  • Half a cup of palm oil for four fingers of plantain is generous and correct. It is what makes it a Delta dish.
  • Scent leaf goes in with everything else rather than at the end here, which is unusual — it has the full ten minutes to infuse.
  • Dice the plantain evenly. Uneven pieces give you some mush and some raw.
  • Periwinkle is marked optional but it is the ingredient that most says Niger Delta. Half a cup if you can get it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring before the plantain has softened.
  • Using ripe plantain.
  • Skipping the final stir-and-simmer, leaving it unintegrated.
  • Cutting back the palm oil.
  • Dicing the plantain unevenly.

Health Benefits

  • Unripe plantain is high in resistant starch and has a low glycaemic index.
  • Periwinkle is very lean and high in iron.
  • Red palm oil supplies carotenoids and vitamin E.
  • Scent leaf carries antimicrobial aromatic oils.

Cultural Note

Kekefiyai belongs to the Ijaw of the Niger Delta, where unripe plantain, palm oil and whatever comes out of the creeks make up a good part of the everyday repertoire. The dish is a pottage rather than a soup, eaten on its own, and like onunu and polofiyai it shows a cuisine built around two or three excellent local ingredients rather than a long list.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of water.

Serve with: On its own, as a one-pot, Cold palm wine, Fried fish
