🫓 White Ugali

Ugali

White Ugali is Kenya's most iconic staple — just two ingredients, maize flour and water, transformed by fire and patience into something deeply nourishing. Firm, smooth, and subtly earthy, it is the canvas on which Kenyan cuisine is painted.

More than a meal, Ugali is a cultural cornerstone eaten across nearly every Kenyan community. The technique is deceptively simple — water, flour, heat, and a wooden mwiko — yet mastering the stir, knowing when it pulls from the sides, and getting the dome just right is a skill passed from mother to child across generations.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium cooking pot, Wooden cooking stick (mwiko), Measuring cups, Serving plate

  • 4 cups (960 ml) clean drinking water
  • 2 cups (300 g) white maize flour — freshly milled for best flavour

Method

  1. Pour the water into a medium pot and bring to a rolling boil over medium-high heat.
  2. Sprinkle in a small handful of maize flour while stirring to form a thin, lump-free slurry — this is the secret to a smooth ugali.
  3. Gradually add the remaining maize flour in small batches, stirring vigorously and continuously with a mwiko to prevent lumps.
  4. Keep stirring as the mixture thickens and begins to pull away cleanly from the sides of the pot.
  5. Reduce the heat to low and cook for a further 2–3 minutes, pressing and folding the ugali firmly against the pot walls.
  6. Fold and press with the mwiko until the ugali is completely smooth, glossy, and holds its shape.
  7. Wet the inside of the pot or a serving plate with a little water, then turn the ugali out and shape into a neat dome.
  8. Serve immediately while hot alongside your favourite stew, greens, or grilled meat.

Chef Tips

  • Use freshly milled maize flour — older flour can taste stale and affects the smooth finish.
  • Never add all the flour at once. Gradual addition is the key to a lump-free ugali.
  • Keep stirring throughout — stopping even briefly can cause the base to scorch.
  • Adjust flour quantity slightly: more for firmer ugali (western Kenya style), slightly less for a softer result (coastal preference).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the flour at once — creates lumps that are nearly impossible to remove.
  • Cooking on very high heat after thickening — scorches the base without cooking the inside.
  • Using old or poorly milled maize flour — compromises flavour and texture.

  • Excellent source of complex carbohydrates for sustained energy.
  • Naturally low in fat — just 1g per serving.
  • Gluten-free when made with pure maize flour.

Cultural Note

Ugali is the heart of Kenyan cuisine, eaten in homes, restaurants, schools, and celebrations from coast to highlands. It is traditionally eaten with the right hand — a small piece pinched off, rolled into a ball, and used to scoop stew or greens. Sharing ugali from one plate is an act of togetherness.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Freeze for up to 1 month.

Reheating: Steam or microwave with a few drops of water until heated through.

Serve with: Sukuma Wiki, Beef Stew, Nyama Choma, Goat Stew, Fried Tilapia, Omena Stew, Chicken Stew, Managu, Terere, Mrenda
