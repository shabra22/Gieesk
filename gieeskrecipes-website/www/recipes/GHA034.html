🍲 Mpotompoto

Mpoto mpoto — yam porridge, asaro

Yam and sweet potato simmered in a zomi and dried-herring base, then about eighty per cent mashed — not all of it.

Mpotompoto is yam porridge, and its author grew up knowing it by that name in Ghana before learning the Nigerian name asaro. Red bell peppers, plum tomatoes, onion, garlic and habanero are blended and simmered in half a cup of zomi red palm oil for five minutes, then dried herring, ground crayfish and bouillon go in covered for ten until the stew thickens a bit. Inch cubes of West African yam and Japanese sweet potato cook five minutes uncovered, three cups of broth go in, and it simmers fifteen to twenty minutes until fork-tender. Then about eighty per cent is mashed — not all.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Heavy pot with a lid, Masher or wooden spoon, Knife

  • 3 small red bell peppers, stemmed, seeded and roughly chopped
  • 3 plum tomatoes
  • 1 small onion
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 1 habanero pepper, stemmed (optional)
  • 1/2 cup zomi red palm oil
  • 1 cup dried herring, bonga or bony fish, bones removed
  • 2 tbsp dried ground crayfish or dried ground shrimp
  • 2 tsp bouillon powder
  • 1 lb West African yam
  • 1 lb Japanese sweet potatoes
  • 3 cups chicken broth
  • Kosher salt

To garnish

  • Thinly sliced scallions

Method

  1. Blend the bell peppers, tomatoes, onion, garlic and habanero.
  2. Simmer the purée in the zomi for 5 minutes.
  3. Add the dried herring, ground crayfish, bouillon and 1/2 tsp salt, and simmer covered 10 minutes — the stew should thicken a bit.
  4. Cut the yam and sweet potato into 1-inch cubes and add.
  5. Cook 5 minutes uncovered.
  6. Add the 3 cups of broth.
  7. Simmer until the potatoes and yam are fork-tender, 15 to 20 minutes.
  8. Mash about 80 per cent of the yam and potato — not all of it.
  9. Cook 5 minutes more and serve scattered with scallions.

Chef Tips

  • Eighty per cent mashed, not all. That is the author's own figure and it is what makes mpotompoto a porridge rather than a mash — you want whole cubes still in there.
  • Zomi specifically, half a cup of it. Zomi is palm oil taken further than ordinary red palm oil and it is the flavour of the dish.
  • The stew should thicken a bit at the ten-minute covered stage. If it is still watery, the yam will not thicken it for you.
  • Fork-tender at fifteen to twenty minutes for inch cubes. Larger cubes take longer and will not mash evenly.
  • Sweet potato alongside the yam is this author's variant, and Japanese sweet potato specifically — drier and less sweet than an orange one. Plain yam is the traditional version.
  • Bones out of the dried herring before it goes in. It will break down into the porridge and you do not want to find them.
  • The dish is cross-regional: mpoto mpoto in Ghana, asaro in Nigeria, and the author knows it by both names.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mashing all of it.
  • Using ordinary red palm oil where zomi is specified, or worse, a refined oil.
  • Adding the broth before the cubes have had their five minutes.
  • Leaving bones in the dried herring.
  • Cubes much larger than an inch.

Health Benefits

  • Yam has a lower glycaemic index than potato and supplies potassium and vitamin C.
  • Zomi red palm oil is rich in provitamin-A carotenoids and tocotrienols.
  • Dried herring and crayfish both supply protein, calcium and iodine.
  • Red bell pepper is very high in vitamin C.

Cultural Note

Mpotompoto is what you make for a child, or someone recovering, or yourself on a weeknight — soft, rich and all in one pot. The eighty-per-cent rule is the sort of precision that only appears when a cook has to explain to someone else why their version came out wrong, and it is the most valuable line in the recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated. It thickens considerably.

Reheating: Low heat with broth or water to loosen.

Serve with: Fried fish, Boiled egg, Sliced avocado
