🍪 Gari Biscuits

Gari biscuits — cassava biscuits

A cup each of gari and plain flour with cold butter cut in to coarse crumbs — twelve to fifteen minutes until golden brown, and they keep two weeks.

Gari biscuits turn the cassava staple into a sweet, and the method is a straightforward rubbed-in biscuit dough: a cup of gari flour, a cup of plain flour, a teaspoon of baking soda, half a cup of sugar, a quarter teaspoon of salt, one large egg and four tablespoons of cold diced butter, with a teaspoon of water or milk if it is dry. Cut the butter into coarse crumbs, add the egg, divide into twenty pieces, flatten and bake at 350 degrees Fahrenheit twelve to fifteen minutes until golden brown. They keep up to two weeks.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Pastry cutter or fingertips, Baking tray, Oven

  • 1 cup gari flour (160 g)
  • 1 cup plain flour (150 g)
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1/2 cup sugar (100 g)
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1 large egg
  • 4 tbsp cold butter (65 g), diced
  • 1 tsp water or milk, as needed

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 350 degrees Fahrenheit.
  2. Combine the gari flour, plain flour, baking soda, sugar and salt.
  3. Cut in the cold diced butter to coarse crumbs.
  4. Add the egg, with a teaspoon of water or milk if the mixture is dry.
  5. Divide into 20 pieces and flatten each one.
  6. Bake 12 to 15 minutes, or until golden brown.
  7. Cool 1 minute on the tray, then transfer.

Chef Tips

  • Cold butter, cut in to coarse crumbs. Warm butter gives a tough biscuit; the coarse crumb is what keeps them short.
  • A teaspoon of liquid only if the dough is dry. Gari is very thirsty but the egg usually does the work.
  • Twelve to fifteen minutes until golden brown. Gari browns readily, so check at twelve.
  • Cool one minute on the tray before moving them — they firm up as they cool and will break if lifted hot.
  • They keep up to two weeks, which makes them a genuinely useful thing to bake.
  • This is a modern butter-and-plain-flour bake rather than a documented traditional Ghanaian sweet, and that is worth knowing — the author calls it a Ghanaian gari biscuit but the tradition behind it could not be verified.
  • Equal volumes of gari and plain flour by cup, but note the gram figures differ — 160 g against 150 g — because gari is denser.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using softened butter.
  • Adding liquid before testing whether the egg is enough.
  • Baking past golden brown.
  • Moving them off the tray while hot.
  • Assuming it is a documented traditional recipe.

Health Benefits

  • Gari supplies resistant starch.
  • Lower in gluten than an all-wheat biscuit.
  • Modest sugar at half a cup across twenty biscuits.
  • Egg adds protein.

Cultural Note

Gari is cheap, keeps forever and turns up in everything from a cold drink to a biscuit, which is the mark of a true staple. Whether this particular biscuit is traditional or a diaspora invention could not be established, and it is published with that question left open rather than answered either way.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Up to 2 weeks in an airtight tin, per the author.

Reheating: Not needed. A few minutes in a low oven crisps them again.

Serve with: Tea, Iced Kenkey, Sobolo
