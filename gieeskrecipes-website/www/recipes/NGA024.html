🌿 Moi Moi in Leaves

Moi moi — moyin moyin

A party-sized moi moi — 750 g of beans, two litres of water, twelve parcels at 420 g each — with the knife test that tells you when it is set.

40 minPrep
60 minCook

MediumDifficulty

This is the version to make when you are feeding a crowd, and it is the one with the best doneness test in any Nigerian bean recipe: put a knife through it, and if it comes out stained with paste it is not ready, while a slight smear means it is. Three hours before cooking you soak and wash the beans to get the coats off. Everything then goes into the blender together — beans, tomato puree, onions, crayfish, habanero and nutmeg — and the remaining water is added slowly while stirring. The water in the pot is padded to a maximum of one inch.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Large bowl, Uma leaves, banana leaves, foil bags or bowls, Large pot with a lid

  • 750 g beans, brown or black-eyed (also given as three cigar cups)
  • 5 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 4 big stock cubes
  • 1 habanero pepper
  • 2 tsp ground nutmeg
  • 800 g watery tomato puree, or 3 tatashe peppers
  • 2 big onions
  • 20 cl vegetable oil
  • 2 litres cool or warm water
  • Salt to taste

Optional fillings

  • Hard-boiled eggs, bone marrow, or corned beef

Method

  1. About three hours before cooking the moi moi, soak and wash the beans to remove the coat.
  2. Blend the soaked beans with the tomato puree, onions, crayfish, habanero and nutmeg, using some of the water.
  3. Pour into a bowl. Add the vegetable oil, and the bone marrow stock if you are using it.
  4. Slowly add the remaining water while stirring.
  5. Add salt and stir well.
  6. Pad the base of the pot with water, to a maximum depth of 1 inch, and set it on the stove.
  7. Dish the mixture into your containers, adding the meat or eggs, seal them, and place them in the pot.
  8. Cook a minimum of 1 hour before checking.
  9. Test by putting a knife through it. If the knife is stained with moi moi paste it is not done; if it just has a slight smear, it is done.

Chef Tips

  • The knife test is the clearest doneness cue in any Nigerian bean recipe. Stained means raw, slight smear means set.
  • One inch of water in the base, maximum. More and you are boiling rather than steaming.
  • Three hours of soaking is for the coats, not for softening. The beans need to swell enough that the skins slip.
  • The water goes in slowly, while stirring, after the blend. Dumped in at once it will not emulsify with the oil.
  • Uma or banana leaves cook fastest, then foil bags, then aluminium or plastic plates, which take the longest. Same batter, different timings.
  • Twelve aluminium bags at 420 g each is the stated yield — useful if you are scaling for a party.
  • For small quantities on high heat, 45 minutes is enough. The hour is for a full pot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too much water in the base of the pot.
  • Checking before the hour is up and losing the steam.
  • Adding the two litres all at once.
  • Mixing vessel types in one pot, so some set and some do not.
  • Skipping the knife test and going by time alone.

Cultural Note

Moi moi at this scale is party cooking, and the twelve-parcel yield is not an accident — it is the unit a Nigerian kitchen thinks in when there is an event. The site's own framing calls it a 'dish accessory': it almost never appears alone, but alongside jollof, fried rice, dodo, custard or pap, which is why nearly every Nigerian celebration plate has a square of it somewhere on the edge.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 2 months frozen in the wrapping.

Reheating: Steam 10 to 15 minutes, or microwave in the leaf.

Serve with: Jollof Rice, Pap or custard, Dodo (fried plantain)
