🍟 Korkasaarka Bariiska

Korkasaarka Bariiska

Saffron-coloured fried potatoes tossed with raisins, fried onion and strips of sweet pepper — the heap that goes on top of a tray of Somali rice at a family gathering.

Somali rice is rarely served bare. A korkasaar goes over the top, and this is the version that appears at ziyaras and family gatherings in Mogadishu: potatoes cut like chips and tinted orange, deep fried, then tumbled while still hot with raisins so they plump in the residual heat, and finished with onions fried in the same oil. It is sweet, savoury and bright orange, and a tray of rice looks unfinished without it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep pan or fryer, Colander, Slotted spoon

  • 4 medium potatoes
  • 2 medium onions
  • 1 red bell pepper
  • 1/4 tsp orange food colouring, or a good pinch of saffron
  • 1/2 tsp water
  • 75 g (1/2 cup) raisins
  • Vegetable oil, for deep frying

Method

  1. Wash the potatoes, then peel them and cut them into 5 mm chip shapes. Rinse in cold water and pat thoroughly dry.
  2. Slice the onions thinly and cut the bell pepper into strips.
  3. Loosen the food colouring, or steeped saffron, with the 1/2 tsp water.
  4. Toss the colouring through the potatoes until every piece is evenly tinted.
  5. Deep fry the potatoes in batches over medium heat for about 10 minutes, until cooked through and just golden at the edges.
  6. Lift them into a colander to drain and add the raisins straight away, while the potatoes are still hot enough to soften them.
  7. Fry the sliced onions in batches in the same oil until golden.
  8. Drain the onions into the same colander, add the pepper strips and toss everything together. Serve heaped over rice.

Chef Tips

  • Dry the potatoes properly after rinsing. Wet chips spit in the oil and come out limp.
  • Add the raisins to the potatoes while they are hot, not cold — the residual heat is what plumps them.
  • No salt is needed. The rice underneath is seasoned, and the raisins and pepper are carrying the interest here.
  • Saffron steeped in a spoonful of hot water replaces the food colouring and adds a genuine perfume, which is what the colouring was standing in for all along.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cutting the potatoes too thick, so the outside colours and the inside is still raw at ten minutes.
  • Frying everything in one batch, which drops the oil temperature and gives greasy, pale chips.
  • Adding the bell pepper to the hot oil — it goes in raw at the end, for crunch and colour.
  • Letting the potatoes cool before the raisins go in, leaving them hard and chewy.

Health Benefits

  • Raisins add iron, potassium and natural sweetness in place of sugar.
  • Bell pepper goes in raw, so its vitamin C survives.
  • Entirely plant-based.

Cultural Note

The korkasaar is one of those parts of a meal that never gets its own name on the menu but that everyone notices if it is missing. In Mogadishu it belongs to ziyaras and family gatherings, spooned over a communal tray of rice just before it is carried out.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best within a few hours. Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Spread on a tray and crisp in a hot oven for 8 minutes. A microwave makes it soggy.

Serve with: Bariis Maraq, Bariis Iskukaris, Hilib Ari Karis
