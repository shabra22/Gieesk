🟢 Green Shito

Kpakoshito — green pepper sauce

Green scotch bonnets, spring onion and garlic cooked five minutes to dry out, then fried ten in oil until the oil sets on top.

The green counterpart to black shito, and much quicker: a brown onion, green scotch bonnets, half an inch of ginger, four or five garlic cloves and two spring onion stalks are blended, cooked on low to medium five minutes to dry out the extra liquid, then three quarters of a cup of vegetable oil goes in and it fries on medium ten minutes, or until the oil sets on top. Half a tablespoon of all-purpose seasoning finishes it. Her note on colour is the useful one: the longer the sauce cooks, the more it changes from bright green to darker green.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Frying pan, Wooden spoon, Jar

  • 1 medium brown onion
  • 3 green scotch bonnet peppers — the method says 2, see the note
  • Ginger, about 1/2 inch
  • Garlic, a handful of cloves (4 or 5)
  • 2 spring onion stalks
  • 3/4 cup vegetable oil
  • 1/2 tbsp all-purpose seasoning
  • Salt

Method

  1. Blend the onion, green scotch bonnets, ginger, garlic and spring onion stalks.
  2. Cook on low to medium 5 minutes, to dry out the extra liquid.
  3. Add the vegetable oil.
  4. Fry on medium 10 minutes, or until the oil sets on top.
  5. Season with the all-purpose seasoning and salt.
  6. Cool and jar.

Chef Tips

  • Dry it out for five minutes BEFORE the oil goes in. Oil into a wet purée will spit and the sauce will never set properly.
  • Until the oil sets on top is the cue, at about ten minutes. Same principle as black shito, much faster.
  • Her colour note is worth knowing: the longer it cooks, the more the colour goes from bright green to darker green. Stop early if you want it vivid.
  • Green scotch bonnets, not red. The green fruit is grassier and less sweet, and the colour is the point of the sauce.
  • Note the misprint: the ingredient list says three scotch bonnets, the method says two. Start at two and add the third if you want it hotter.
  • Ginger, garlic and spring onion all carry NO quantities in the ingredient list — the figures above come from the method, where they are stated.
  • A fresher, sharper condiment than black shito, and it does not keep nearly as long. Make it in small batches.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the oil to the wet blend.
  • Over-cooking, which turns the green dull.
  • Using red scotch bonnets.
  • Taking three peppers as settled when the method says two.
  • Making a large batch — it does not keep like black shito.

Health Benefits

  • Green chillies are very high in vitamin C.
  • Garlic, ginger and spring onion all carry documented antimicrobial compounds.
  • Vegan, unlike black shito, which depends on dried seafood.
  • Used by the spoonful, so the oil per serving is small.

Cultural Note

Green shito is the fresher, sharper cousin of the black paste, and it is what you want beside grilled fish rather than beside kenkey. That only one documented recipe for it exists, against three for black shito, suggests it is more often made by eye than written down.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 weeks refrigerated under its oil. Shorter-keeping than black shito.

Reheating: Not needed. Served cold or at room temperature.

Serve with: Kenkey, Banku, Grilled fish
