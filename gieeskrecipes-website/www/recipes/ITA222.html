🍩 Zeppole di Natale

Zeppole di Natale

Simple yeasted fritters fried into golden, irregular puffs and dusted with sugar, a Neapolitan Christmas tradition distinct from the cream-filled version served for Father's Day.

Unlike the piped, cream-topped zeppole di San Giuseppe, these Christmas zeppole are simpler — just soft, fried dough puffs, freshly made and eaten warm, a humble but deeply anticipated part of the Neapolitan holiday table.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Deep pot for frying, Two spoons for shaping

  • 300 g (2¼ cups) 00 flour
  • 7 g (2¼ tsp) active dry yeast
  • 250 ml (1 cup) warm water
  • 1 tbsp granulated sugar, plus more for dusting
  • Pinch of salt
  • Vegetable oil, for frying

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in the warm water with the sugar and let sit for 5 minutes until foamy.
  2. Combine the flour and salt, then add the yeast mixture, mixing into a smooth, sticky batter — looser than a typical dough.
  3. Cover and let rise in a warm place for about 1 hour, until bubbly and doubled.
  4. Heat vegetable oil to 180°C (350°F).
  5. Using two spoons, scoop portions of the batter and carefully drop into the hot oil.
  6. Fry until deeply golden and puffed, about 3–4 minutes, turning as needed.
  7. Drain on paper towels and toss immediately in granulated sugar while still warm.
  8. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • The batter should be quite loose and sticky — resist adding extra flour to make it easier to handle.
  • Using two spoons to shape and drop the batter, rather than trying to roll it, is the traditional and easiest method.
  • These are best eaten warm, shortly after frying, while still soft inside and freshly sugared.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding too much flour to the sticky batter, which results in denser, less airy zeppole.
  • Frying at too high a temperature, browning the outside before the inside cooks through.
  • Letting them sit too long before sugaring, when the coating adheres best to warm, freshly fried dough.

Cultural Note

Zeppole di Natale are a cherished, humble part of the Neapolitan Christmas table, distinct from their more elaborate cream-filled San Giuseppe cousins served in spring.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten the day they're made, ideally within a few hours.

Reheating: Not applicable; best enjoyed fresh and warm.

Serve with: Espresso
