🍚 Thiebou Dieune Bou Wekh

Thiebou dieune bou wekh

The white version with the soul buried in the rice fifteen minutes in — and a published ingredient list carrying several misprints this entry reproduces rather than fixes.

160 minTotal time
45 minPrep
115 minCook
6Servings

A Senegalese-market institutional recipe for the white ceebu jen, included because sources for the white version are scarce. A kilo of long-grain white rice and a kilo and a half of smoked fish, with carrots, turnip leaves, eggplant, cassava at fifty grams each and two hundred grams of fresh okra. The roof stuffing is pounded parsley, onion, chilli and half a bouillon tablet. The soul — the nététou ball — is buried in the rice after fifteen minutes of its forty-minute cook and covered back over. The béguète is bissap leaves boiled and crushed with okra. Its printed ingredient list contains several evident machine-translation artifacts, flagged below and left as printed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Couscoussier — the page calls it an indé, Mortar and pestle

  • 1 kg long-grain white rice, regular, raw, enriched
  • 1.5 kg smoked fish
  • 50 g carrots
  • 50 g turnip leaves, raw
  • 50 g eggplant
  • 50 g cassava, raw
  • 200 g fresh okra
  • 2 medium onions
  • 2 cloves garlic — the page prints 'Ail: 2 Les chefs', a misprint, see the note
  • 1 small red bell pepper
  • 1 medium fresh tomato
  • 1 tbsp of a meatless bacon-bits line that appears to be a database artifact — see the note
  • 1 portion dried fish standing in for guedj — see the note
  • 1 bunch parsley
  • 100 g tomato paste
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 red chillies
  • 250 g raw tamarind
  • 10 g bouillon tablet
  • 10 g seasoning spice mix

Plus, from the method: nététou seeds for the soul, and bissap leaves for the béguète

  • 1 to 1.5 litres water

Method

  1. Make the roof: pound parsley, onion, chilli and half the bouillon tablet. Stuff the fish with it.
  2. Make a second farce with parsley, chilli, onion, bell pepper and the rest of the tablet.
  3. Fry the onions, the nokoss and the tomato pulp 5 minutes.
  4. Add 1 to 1.5 litres of water, the vegetables, the fish and the okra, and leave it to simmer a good hour.

The soul, or nététou ball

  1. Soak the nététou seeds to remove the skins, pound them with onion and dried chillies, form one large ball and set it aside.

The béguète

  1. Wash and stem the bissap leaves, boil them, and crush them with okra. Add 2 or 3 tablespoons of broth and set aside. Done when the leaves are well cooked.

The rice

  1. Steam the rice in the couscoussier about 10 minutes.
  2. Remove the fish and vegetables with a little broth and keep them warm.
  3. Pour the broth over the rice so it just covers, cover, lower the heat and cook 40 minutes.
  4. After 15 minutes, dig a hole in the rice, bury the ball of soul, cover it over and finish the cooking.
  5. Serve with the soumbara and the sorrel-and-okra mix.

Chef Tips

  • Burying the soul in the rice partway through the cook is the technique worth taking from this recipe — fifteen minutes into forty, so it steams inside the grain rather than sitting on top.
  • Broth poured so it JUST covers the rice. The same instruction appears in every well-made Senegalese rice dish and it is the difference between grains and porridge.
  • When the leaves are well cooked is the only true sensory cue on the page. Everything else is a clock.
  • Steam the rice ten minutes before it meets the broth, in a couscoussier if you have one.
  • Treat the printed ingredient list with care — several lines are evident machine-translation or food-database artifacts, including a meatless-bacon line and an Alaskan whitefish line standing in for guedj. They are reproduced above because this collection does not silently correct sources, but they are not what a Senegalese cook would use.
  • The site's own displayed timers of 170, 45, 20, 15 and 90 minutes do not map onto any step in its text. Follow the in-text times.
  • For a version of the white ceebu jen from a named Senegalese cook rather than an institutional source, Ceeb bu Weex is the one to read.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the soul at the start or at the end rather than at the fifteen-minute mark.
  • Drowning the rice in broth.
  • Following the site's displayed timers rather than its text.
  • Taking the misprinted ingredient lines literally.
  • Under-cooking the bissap leaves for the béguète.

Health Benefits

  • Very high in protein, at the source's own figure of 57 g per portion.
  • Nététou contributes fermented plant protein and B vitamins.
  • Okra and turnip leaves add soluble fibre and folate.
  • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

Cultural Note

Bouillon-cube brands publish recipes in West Africa because their product is genuinely in almost every pot, and a Senegalese-market page for the white ceebu jen is a real document of how the dish is cooked now. It is also a reminder to read any brand recipe with one eye on the ingredient list, which here has wandered through a translation engine.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam the rice; warm the fish and vegetables in broth.

Serve with: Béguète sorrel and okra sauce, Soumbara, Kaani
