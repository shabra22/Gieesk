🥚 Kenyan Egg Stew

Mayai ya Mchuzi

Kenyan Egg Stew — eggs poached directly in a fragrant tomato, onion, and garlic sauce until perfectly set. Quick, nourishing, affordable, and deeply satisfying — one of Kenya's most beloved everyday meals. Ready in 30 minutes and loved by the whole family.

Egg stew is the Kenyan kitchen at its most resourceful and most satisfying. A rich tomato sauce is built in minutes, then eggs are cracked directly into the sauce and poached until the whites are set and the yolks are still slightly runny. The yolk breaks into the sauce as you eat, enriching it further. Scooped with ugali or chapati, it is one of the most complete and comforting meals in the Kenyan repertoire.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large wide pan with lid, Wooden spoon

  • 6 large eggs
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • ½ cup water
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1½ tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Heat oil in a large wide pan over medium heat.
  2. Cook onions until soft and golden — 8 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 8—10 minutes until a thick sauce forms.
  5. Add curry powder and paprika. Stir 30 seconds.
  6. Add water and bring to a gentle simmer.
  7. Make 6 small wells in the sauce. Crack one egg into each well.
  8. Cover and cook for 5—8 minutes until the egg whites are set but yolks are still slightly runny.
  9. Season. Scatter with coriander.
  10. Serve immediately — directly from the pan.

Chef Tips

  • The sauce should be thick before you add the eggs — a thin sauce produces uneven poaching.
  • Do not stir after adding the eggs — let them poach undisturbed.
  • A wide pan allows more eggs to cook evenly without crowding.
  • Cover while the eggs cook — the trapped steam sets the tops of the whites without flipping.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Thin sauce — eggs spread too much and cook unevenly.
  • Stirring after adding eggs — breaks the yolks prematurely.
  • Overcooking — the yolks become hard and the dish loses its characteristic richness.

The Science

Poaching eggs in tomato sauce is essentially the same as poaching in water — the proteins in the egg white denature and coagulate as they heat. Tomato sauce conducts heat efficiently and adds flavour compounds that infuse the white as it sets. The acidity of the tomatoes also helps the egg white coagulate more quickly.

Cultural Note

Egg stew is one of the most universally beloved meals in Kenya — quick, affordable, and satisfying at any time of day. From breakfast in a Nairobi apartment to lunch in a Western Kenya homestead, mayai ya mchuzi is a constant across the country.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately — eggs change texture when refrigerated in sauce.

Reheating: Not recommended — the eggs overcook when reheated.

Serve with: White Ugali, Chapati, White Bread, Steamed Rice, Kenyan Masala Chai
