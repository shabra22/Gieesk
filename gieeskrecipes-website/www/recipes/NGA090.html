🌱 Miyan Zogale

Miyan zogale — moringa soup

The only northern soup in the record with its water quantified — half a cup of raw groundnut to a litre and a half — finished when there is oil at the top.

Miyan zogale is moringa leaf soup and it is the one northern soup where a named source actually states the liquid volume: a litre and a half of water to half a cup of raw shelled groundnut. Everything else in the northern repertoire leaves the denominator blank. Half a kilo of meat, a dried fish, two large bunches of moringa, a tablespoon of yaji, a teaspoon of kaun and half a flat of daddawa go in alongside, with two cooking spoons of palm oil. It takes over an hour, and the finishing cue is straightforward — there will be oil at the top when it is done.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Dry pan, for roasting the groundnuts, Blender, Wooden spoon

  • 1/2 kg meat
  • 1 medium-sized dried fish
  • 2 large bunches zogale (moringa leaves)
  • 3 large fresh tomatoes
  • 3 medium-sized fresh peppers
  • 1 medium onion
  • 1 tbsp yaji
  • 1/2 cup gyada (raw shelled groundnut)
  • 1 tsp kaun (potash)
  • 1 1/2 litres water
  • 1/2 flat daddawa
  • 2 cooking spoons palm oil
  • 3 seasoning cubes
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Pick and wash the moringa leaves thoroughly.
  2. Prepare the other ingredients — chop the onion, blend the tomatoes and peppers, grind the daddawa.
  3. Roast the groundnuts in a dry pan, then grind them to a paste.
  4. Cook the meat and dried fish with the seasoning cubes, onion and salt.
  5. Heat the palm oil and stir-fry the blended tomatoes and peppers.
  6. Add the meat and its stock, the groundnut paste, the yaji, the daddawa and the kaun, with the water, and cook through.
  7. Add the moringa leaves and simmer 3 minutes.
  8. It is done when there is oil at the top of the soup.

Chef Tips

  • Half a cup of groundnut to a litre and a half of water. That is the only stated liquid volume in any northern Nigerian soup in this collection, and it is worth having for scaling anything else.
  • Oil at the top is the doneness cue, and it is the same signal that finishes most Nigerian soups built on a fried pepper base.
  • Roast the groundnuts before grinding. Raw groundnut paste tastes green and chalky.
  • Three minutes on the leaves. Another named source gives ten — a large difference in final texture for a leaf as tender as moringa, and three is the shorter and greener of the two.
  • A teaspoon of kaun. See Kaun for what this actually is chemically and for the safety note that belongs with it.
  • Yaji in a soup rather than on meat. A tablespoon gives the whole pot the suya-spice character, which is distinctively northern.
  • Wash the moringa thoroughly. The leaves are small and sandy and there is no fixing it later.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using raw groundnut paste.
  • Cooking the moringa for ten minutes when three is enough.
  • Adding the kaun by eye rather than measuring the teaspoon.
  • Skipping the stir-fry of the tomato and pepper base.
  • Under-washing the leaves.

Health Benefits

  • Moringa leaf is exceptionally high in vitamin A, vitamin C, calcium and protein for a vegetable.
  • Groundnut adds plant protein and niacin.
  • Red palm oil supplies carotenoids and vitamin E.
  • High in protein from the meat and dried fish.

Cultural Note

Zogale is moringa, the tree that grows almost anywhere in the north and whose leaves are among the most nutrient-dense foods on the continent. That it is cooked into a soup with groundnut and daddawa rather than treated as a health supplement is the point — it has been an ordinary vegetable in northern Nigeria for far longer than it has been a superfood anywhere else.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of water.

Serve with: Tuwo Shinkafa, Tuwo Masara, Masa
