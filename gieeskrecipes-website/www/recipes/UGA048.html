🍌 Boiled Plantains in their Skins

Gonja

Medium-ripe plantains cut into four with the ends off but the skins left on, boiled hard fifteen minutes then simmered five, cooled and peeled at the table.

Boiling plantains in their skins is the northern and eastern habit, and it does two useful things: the skin holds the flesh together so it does not break up, and it stops the plantain absorbing water. You cut the ends off and quarter them, skins on, put them in hot water, and boil hard before dropping to a simmer. They come out steaming hot, which is why the five minutes of cooling is written into the recipe rather than left to sense.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan, Knife

  • 6 medium-ripe plantains
  • 500 ml (1/2 litre) hot water

Method

  1. Wash the plantains thoroughly with cold water.
  2. Cut the ends off each one, then cut each into four pieces — leaving the skins on.
  3. Put the plantains in a large saucepan.
  4. Add hot boiling water to the pan.
  5. Boil on high heat for 15 minutes, then reduce and simmer 5 minutes.
  6. Let them cool at least 5 minutes — they will be steaming hot.
  7. Remove the skins and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Skins on. They hold the flesh together and keep it from waterlogging, and they come off easily once the plantain is cooked.
  • Cut the ends off first. It lets the heat in without letting the water into the flesh.
  • Start with hot water. Cold water means a longer climb to the boil and a softer result.
  • Fifteen minutes hard, five minutes gentle. The high boil cooks it; the simmer finishes it evenly.
  • Five minutes of cooling is in the recipe for a reason. Straight out of the pan these are genuinely too hot to peel.
  • Medium-ripe. Fully green is starchy and hard; fully ripe collapses.
  • They are good with anything saucy — stew, beans, spinach, peanut butter, groundnut sauce.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Peeling before boiling.
  • Starting in cold water.
  • Boiling gently throughout, so they never cook through.
  • Trying to peel them straight off the heat.
  • Using fully ripe fruit.

Health Benefits

  • Plantain is high in potassium, vitamin B6 and vitamin C.
  • Boiled in their skins with nothing added, so no fat at all.
  • A good source of resistant starch and fibre.
  • Naturally gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Boiling in the skin is one of those small regional differences worth noticing: in central Uganda matooke is peeled and steamed in leaves, while plantain boiled in its skin is more common further north and east. Same fruit family, two quite different plates.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days, in their skins.

Reheating: Steam for a few minutes, or fry the peeled pieces.

Serve with: Binyebwa, Ugandan Brown Beans Stew, Malakwang
