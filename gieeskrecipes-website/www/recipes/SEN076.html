🍩 Beignets, Risen with Yeast

Beignets

A self-raising-flour batter with dry yeast and bicarbonate, mixed until elastic and bubbly, rested until it doubles, and dropped in spoonfuls into hot oil — beignets for a crowd (about 40).

The best-documented rise in the Senegal research. The flour, bicarbonate, yeast, sugars and salt are mixed, warm water is added gradually with a wooden spoon, and the batter is ready ‘when it becomes elastic and starts to make big bubbles’. It is covered with cling film and left in a warm place for 2 hours ‘or until the dough doubles in volume’ — the time is explicitly subordinate to the volume cue. Oil is heated; the fingers are dipped in cold water, a little batter taken with the fingertips and dropped in; the beignets are turned from time to time and removed when a good golden colour. Provenance caution: the page frames these as ‘African beignets’ and its writer is a DRC-based journalist; the technique and the cues are what this entry takes from it, not a Senegalese claim.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, Wooden spoon, Cling film, Saucepan or deep pan, Bowl of cold water for the fingers, Paper towels

  • 450 g self-raising flour (‘farine fermentante’)
  • 400 ml lukewarm water
  • 150 g sugar
  • 2 sachets vanilla sugar (optional)
  • 2 sachets dry baker’s yeast (14 g)
  • 1/2 tsp bicarbonate (optional, for lightness)
  • A pinch of salt
  • Oil for frying — quantity not printed

Method

  1. In a large bowl, mix the flour, bicarbonate, yeast, sugars and salt.
  2. Add the water gradually, mixing with a wooden spoon, until the dough becomes elastic and starts to make big bubbles — that is when it is ready.
  3. Cover the bowl with cling film and leave in a warm place for 2 hours, or until the dough doubles in volume.
  4. Heat the oil in a saucepan. When it is hot, dip your fingers in a small bowl of cold water, take a little dough with the fingertips and drop it in the oil.
  5. Turn the beignets from time to time for even cooking. Remove them when they have a good golden colour. Drain on paper towels.
  6. Eat preferably hot or lukewarm. They can be reheated 30 seconds in the microwave.

Chef Tips

  • For round beignets the dough must be properly elastic — a runny dough is hard to grip and deforms in the oil.
  • To avoid raw centres and burnt outsides, don’t let the oil get too hot: heat on high, then lower to medium before frying.
  • The page notes its dry yeast was fast-acting and needed no prior dissolving; otherwise dissolve it first in the warm water.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Judging the rise by the clock when the dough has not doubled.
  • Frying in oil that is too hot.
  • A dough too liquid to shape.

Cultural Note

Le Soleil’s 2025 reportage on Dakar’s beignet sellers records that women sell flour beignets and millet ‘dugup’ beignets at 25 to 50 FCFA, many funding their tontines from sales, and that the target texture is crisp outside, soft or melting inside. This recipe is the cue-bearing technique; the vendors’ own formulas are not printed.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best the day they are made.

Reheating: 30 seconds in the microwave, as the page says.

Serve with: Sweet tea, Sugar for dusting, Coffee
