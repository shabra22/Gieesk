🍲 Crock-Pot Jollof Rice

Jollof rice

An experiment she published with its verdict attached — right flavour, tender meat, and very mushy rice with some grains undercooked.

This is a failed experiment published honestly, which makes it more useful than most successes. A slow cooker is preheated on high about thirty minutes, everything goes in including raw rice, and it cooks an hour on high then about seven more on low. Her verdict on the beef version is printed in full: right flavour, tender meat, but very mushy rice with some grains undercooked. The vegetarian version was better, needing less tomato paste and about six hours. Her conclusion is that the oven is her first choice and the stovetop second — and that a commenter's advice was simply not to make jollof in a crock-pot at all.

Ingredients

Equipment: Slow cooker (crock-pot), Parchment paper, Wooden spoon

  • 1 1/2 cups long grain rice
  • 1 lb stewing beef, such as chuck
  • 1 cup broth
  • 1 cup tomato juice or water
  • Well-drained tomatoes, seeded if you wish, from a 28 oz tin — about a cup
  • 3 tbsp peanut or other vegetable oil
  • 2 cups onion, chopped — about 2 medium
  • 1 tbsp fresh peeled ginger, chopped
  • 1/3 cup (5 tbsp) tomato paste
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 whole chilli pepper, seeded — or about 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes
  • 1/2 tsp black or white pepper (optional)
  • 1 tsp curry powder, thyme or other herb (optional)
  • 2 tsp seasoning salt, or regular salt, or to taste
  • 1 bay leaf (optional)
  • 2 cups chopped vegetables

For the vegetarian version

  • 1-1 1/2 tbsp tomato paste instead
  • Parchment paper, tapped over the rice

Method

  1. Preheat the crock-pot on high for about 30 minutes.
  2. Add the broth, tomato juice, drained tomatoes, beef, oil, onion, ginger, tomato paste, garlic, chilli, pepper, herb, salt and bay leaf.
  3. Add the raw rice — it is not parboiled.
  4. Cook on high for an hour.
  5. Reduce the heat to low and cook for about 7 more hours.
  6. Add the chopped vegetables about an hour before serving.

For the vegetarian version: use only 1 to 1 1/2 tablespoons of tomato paste and cook about 6 hours.

  1. Tap a sheet of parchment over the rice — not taped — in place of plastic wrap.

Chef Tips

  • Read her verdict before you commit eight hours: right flavour, tender meat, but very mushy rice with some grains undercooked. She published the experiment and its result together.
  • The vegetarian version was the better of the two, with less tomato paste and about six hours rather than eight.
  • Parchment tapped — not taped — over the rice, which she substituted for plastic wrap. It is the slow-cooker equivalent of the foil-under-the-lid trick.
  • Her ranking is explicit: the oven is her first choice, the stovetop second. The crock-pot is third and she says so.
  • A commenter's verdict, quoted on the page and worth knowing: they still do not recommend making jollof in a crock-pot because it is too soft.
  • Preheat the crock-pot thirty minutes before anything goes in. Cold ceramic plus raw rice is part of why the grains cook unevenly.
  • A practice she reports from Ghana explains the parchment: cooks there often put plastic wrap over covered rice even with a lid on, apparently to help it steam.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expecting non-mushy rice from this method.
  • Skipping the thirty-minute preheat.
  • Using the beef version's tomato paste quantity in the vegetarian one.
  • Taping rather than tapping the parchment down.
  • Adding the vegetables at the start of eight hours.

Cultural Note

A cook publishing an eight-hour experiment along with the word 'yuk' is doing something more valuable than publishing a success — it tells you that jollof's texture depends on even, controllable heat, which is exactly why her oven method works and this one does not.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Steam, or microwave with a splash of water.

Serve with: Shito, Kelewele, Coleslaw
