🧈 Isijingi

Isijingi

Pumpkin puréed and thickened with maize meal, sweetened and buttered — a main course that tastes like a pudding, and the Zulu winter dish people describe with the most affection.

Isijingi is the isiZulu name for a dish that runs across most of southern Africa, and this version leans sweet: eighty grams of sugar and thirty of butter stirred in at the end, which puts it somewhere between a side dish and a dessert. Served as a main it is the sort of food that gets described as tasting like pudding, which is meant as praise and taken as such.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium saucepan with a lid, Stick blender, Whisk

  • 500 g pumpkin, peeled and cubed
  • 1 L water, divided
  • 150 g maize meal
  • 80 g sugar
  • 30 g butter

Method

  1. Put the pumpkin in a medium saucepan with 2 cups of the water, bring to the boil, then reduce to a simmer and cook until completely soft.
  2. Blend smooth and return the purée to the pan.
  3. Add the remaining water to the maize meal, then whisk that mixture into the pumpkin purée until thick and smooth.
  4. Cover and simmer over low heat for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring occasionally so it does not burn on the base.
  5. Add the sugar and butter and cook for a further 5 minutes. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Slake the maize meal in the cold water before it meets the hot purée. Dry meal into hot liquid lumps instantly.
  • Hold the sugar and butter until the last five minutes. Sugar added early catches on the base of the pan.
  • Butternut is sweeter than most pumpkin and easier to peel, and you can cut the sugar if you use it.
  • It thickens as it stands, so take it off the heat a shade looser than you want to serve it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Whisking dry maize meal into the hot purée, which is the reliable way to get lumps.
  • Leaving it unstirred on the simmer, when the sugar-rich mixture catches quickly.
  • Adding the sugar at the beginning and then wondering what the burnt note is.
  • Cooking it down too far, so it sets into a block rather than a spoonable porridge.

  • Pumpkin is high in beta-carotene, which becomes vitamin A.

Cultural Note

The dish exists in Tshivenda and Sepedi as thophi, in Xitsonga as tshopi, in siSwati as sidvudvu and in isiXhosa as umqa, and the sweetness varies with the household rather than the language. That it turns up everywhere is a reminder that pumpkin and squash were grown here long before maize was.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently with a splash of water or milk, stirring until loose again.

Serve with: Grilled or stewed meat, Amasi, Uphuthu
