🍖 Ndolé à la viande (Beef Ndolé)

Ndolé à la viande

Beef browned in oil and simmered 1 h 30, then peanut paste, dried shrimp and crushed djansan added, and boiled, cold-rinsed, pressed ndolé leaves stirred in for a final 15 minutes.

A French-language ndolé from the site Afrik. The leaves are stripped and boiled uncovered about 5 minutes in water salted with rock salt, plunged into cold water at once (the page says to avoid yellowing and to remove deposits), drained and pressed into small balls. The beef is browned in oil with the fat poured off, onions added for a few minutes, 2 dl of water added and the meat cooked about 1 h 30. Djansan, garlic, a yellow pepper, crushed dried shrimp and peanut paste go in for 15 minutes of gentle cooking, and the leaves are added ‘en pluie’ with salt and pepper for a final 15 minutes. The page serves it with maize semolina, plantain or miondo.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Colander, Frying pot

  • 1,6 kg beef (macreuse, tendron, paleron, gîte)
  • 2 dl oil
  • 150 g onions
  • 300 g peanut paste (pâte d’arachide)
  • 2 kg ndolé leaves
  • 150 g dried shrimp
  • 20 g djanssan (djansan)
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 1 yellow chilli
  • 20 g rock salt (sel gemme)
  • Salt and pepper — quantities not printed
  • About 2 litres water (printed ‘2 litre’)

Method

    Leaves

    1. Bring the rock-salted water to the boil. Boil the stripped leaves uncovered about 5 minutes.
    2. Cool them in cold water as soon as they are cooked (the page says this avoids yellowing and removes deposits). Drain and press into small balls.

    Meat

    1. Brown the meat in oil, stirring until coloured. Pour off the excess fat and keep the meat warm under a lid.
    2. Add the onions for a few minutes. Add 2 dl of water and cook the meat about 1 h 30.

    Assemble

    1. Add the djansan, garlic, chilli, crushed shrimp and peanut paste. Cook on a low heat for 15 minutes.
    2. Add the leaves ‘en pluie’, with the salt and pepper. Finish cooking on a low heat for 15 minutes. Serve with maize semolina, plantain or miondo.

    Chef Tips

    • The cold-water rinse after boiling is for colour and deposits, per the page.
    • Printed cue for the meat: ‘remuer jusqu’à coloration’ (stir until coloured).
    • Plan on about two hours of active cooking, not the 15 minutes in the page header.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Trusting the page’s 15-minute header cook time.
    • Skipping the cold-water rinse and pressing step for the leaves.
    • Leaving the excess fat in after browning.

    Cultural Note

    The page calls ndolé ‘le plat national du sud Cameroun’. The page’s byline is Amina Savoura; the recipe is credited to the site Conficulture. Both names are reported and neither is preferred.
