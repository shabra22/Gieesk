🧈 Ethiopian Spiced Clarified Butter

Kibbeh is the cooking fat, flavour base, and finishing condiment of Ethiopian cuisine — it is added to the dry-caramelised onions for every wat, used to sear tibs, dressed on top of genfo and kinche, and stirred into kitfo. The spice infusion transforms plain clarified butter into a compound of remarkable aromatic complexity.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: All of Ethiopia — the spiced clarified butter that is simultaneously condiment and cooking fat

Kibbeh (also called Niter Kibbeh — 'spiced butter') is the second great foundation of Ethiopian cooking — alongside berbere, it is used in virtually every Ethiopian dish. Clarified butter infused with a carefully chosen blend of aromatics (onion, garlic, ginger, turmeric, cardamom, cinnamon, and rue), kibbeh carries these flavours into every dish it cooks. Unlike Indian ghee (which is pure clarified butter with no additions), kibbeh is a compound spiced butter that simultaneously provides the cooking fat and the aromatic base of the dish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Fine mesh sieve, Cheesecloth, Clean glass jars

  • 500g unsalted butter

Spice Infusion

  • 1 small onion — roughly chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves — halved
  • 2 tbsp ginger — sliced
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ¼ tsp ground cloves
  • ¼ tsp dried rue (tena adam) — or thyme
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
  • ¼ tsp black pepper

Method

  1. Melt butter over medium-low heat in a heavy saucepan.
  2. Add all spices and aromatics.
  3. Gently simmer on very low heat for 20—25 minutes.
  4. The butter should foam, then clarify — the milk solids will settle and slightly brown.
  5. The butter is ready when it is golden, clear, and fragrant, and the milk solids at the bottom are light golden.
  6. Strain through cheesecloth into a clean jar — remove all spices and milk solids.
  7. The resulting golden kibbeh keeps for months.

Chef Tips

  • Very low heat — the spices should infuse gently, not fry.
  • The milk solids at the bottom should be light golden, not brown — dark milk solids mean burnt butter.
  • Strain completely through cheesecloth — any remaining milk solids will cause it to go rancid faster.
  • Freshly made kibbeh is significantly superior to commercial versions — the volatile aromatics from fresh spices are far more complex.
  • Store in a sealed jar — kibbeh keeps at room temperature for 3 months, refrigerated for 6 months.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too high heat — burns milk solids.
  • Incomplete straining.
  • Using salted butter — salt makes it go rancid faster.

The Science

Clarification removes the milk solids (casein protein) and water from butter, leaving only the pure fat. This raises the smoke point from 150°C to 250°C — making kibbeh suitable for high-heat cooking unlike plain butter. The simultaneous infusion of the aromatic spices is enabled by the fat itself — most aromatic compounds in spices are fat-soluble, meaning they dissolve into and are carried by the fat rather than the water.

Health Benefits

  • The clarification removes lactose and casein — suitable for many dairy-sensitive individuals.
  • The high smoke point means less oxidation during cooking than regular butter.
  • The spice infusion adds significant antioxidant compounds.

Cultural Note

Kibbeh is the richness of Ethiopia — made from the butter of highland cattle, infused with the aromatics of the Ethiopian spice tradition, it carries in every spoonful the complete flavour identity of the cuisine. Ethiopian cooks judge each other's kibbeh as the clearest indicator of skill — a perfectly made kibbeh produces food of incomparable quality.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Room temperature 3 months in a sealed jar. Refrigerate 6 months. Freeze 1 year.

Reheating: Melt gently — it is already clarified.

Serve with: In every Ethiopian wat, tibs, and as a condiment for genfo and kinche, The foundation of the entire cuisine
