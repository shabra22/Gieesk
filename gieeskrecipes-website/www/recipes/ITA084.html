🍮 Zabaglione al Marsala

Zabaione

A warm, airy custard of egg yolks whisked with sugar and Marsala wine over gentle heat until light and voluminous — one of Italy's oldest and simplest desserts, best served the moment it's made.

Zabaglione demands nothing but technique: continuous, vigorous whisking over gentle heat transforms just three ingredients into a foamy, warm custard, and the dish rewards patience over any shortcut, since rushing the heat curdles the eggs rather than gently cooking them.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heatproof bowl, Whisk, Saucepan for a double boiler

  • 6 egg yolks
  • 90 g (scant ½ cup) granulated sugar
  • 120 ml (½ cup) sweet Marsala wine
  • Fresh berries, for serving
  • Amaretti biscuits, crumbled, for serving (optional)

Method

  1. Set a heatproof bowl over a pot of gently simmering water, making sure the bowl does not touch the water.
  2. Add the egg yolks and sugar to the bowl and whisk continuously until pale and slightly thickened, about 2 minutes.
  3. Gradually whisk in the Marsala wine, continuing to whisk constantly over the gentle heat.
  4. Keep whisking for 8–10 minutes, until the mixture triples in volume, becomes light and airy, and holds a ribbon trail when the whisk is lifted.
  5. Remove from the heat immediately once it reaches this stage, as it can overcook quickly.
  6. Pour into glasses and serve immediately, warm, with fresh berries and crumbled amaretti if desired.

Chef Tips

  • Whisk continuously and vigorously throughout — any pause risks scrambling the eggs against the hot bowl.
  • The water beneath the bowl should simmer gently, never boil, to keep the heat gentle and even.
  • Zabaglione is best made and served immediately; it deflates and loses its airy texture if left standing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting the water boil too vigorously, which cooks the eggs too quickly and can curdle the custard.
  • Stopping whisking even briefly, which risks the eggs near the bowl's edge scrambling.
  • Making it too far ahead of serving, since the foam collapses within minutes of coming off the heat.

Cultural Note

Zabaglione is considered one of the oldest desserts in Italian cuisine, with roots tracing back to the Renaissance era in Piedmont and Emilia-Romagna.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best made and eaten immediately; does not store or hold well.

Reheating: Not applicable; zabaglione cannot be successfully reheated once made.

Serve with: Fresh seasonal berries, Sponge cake or ladyfingers on the side
