🫓 Sabaayad

Sabaayad

A flaky, layered Somali flatbread, its dough rolled, coiled, and rolled again to build delicate layers, pan-fried until golden and eaten alongside stews or dipped in sweet tea.

Sabaayad's many layers come from a lamination technique similar to paratha, the dough oiled and coiled into a tight spiral before being rolled flat again, and the result is a bread that pulls apart in soft, flaky sheets, equally at home soaking up a savory stew or dunked into a cup of spiced tea.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Flat griddle or skillet

  • 500 g (4 cups) all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 300 ml (1 1/4 cups) warm water, approximately
  • 80 ml (1/3 cup) vegetable oil, plus more for laminating and frying

Method

  1. Combine the flour, salt, and sugar in a bowl.
  2. Gradually add the warm water and 2 tablespoons of oil, kneading into a smooth, soft dough.
  3. Knead for 8-10 minutes until elastic, then rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
  4. Divide the dough into balls and roll each out into a thin round.
  5. Brush generously with oil, then roll the round up tightly into a rope and coil into a spiral.
  6. Tuck the end under and let rest for a few minutes.
  7. Roll each coiled spiral out again into a flat round, about half a centimeter thick.
  8. Heat a griddle or skillet over medium heat with a little oil.
  9. Cook each sabaayad for 2-3 minutes per side, pressing gently with a spatula, until golden and cooked through with visible flaky layers.

Chef Tips

  • Brushing the dough generously with oil before coiling is what creates the bread signature flaky layers.
  • Let the coiled dough rest briefly before re-rolling, which makes it easier to flatten without the layers tearing.
  • Pressing gently with a spatula while cooking helps the layers puff and separate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skimping on the oil during lamination, resulting in a flatter, less flaky bread.
  • Rolling too aggressively after coiling, which can tear through the layers.
  • Cooking over heat that is too low, leaving the bread pale and doughy rather than golden and crisp.

Cultural Note

Sabaayad is a beloved everyday bread throughout Somalia, its flaky layers reflecting culinary techniques shared across the wider Horn of Africa and South Asian trading world.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store wrapped at room temperature for up to 1 day, or refrigerate for up to 3.

Reheating: Reheat in a dry pan to restore crispness.

Serve with: Maraq (meat stew), Somali tea, Honey
