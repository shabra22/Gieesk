🥫 Fermented White Ant Sauce

Enswa — nswa

White ants dewinged and left covered two days to ferment, then ground, rolled into small balls and dropped into salted boiling water to harden before groundnut paste goes in.

This is the Acholi way with white ants and it is a different dish entirely from roasting them. The ants are dewinged, covered in a saucepan or bucket and left two days to ferment. Then they are ground, rolled into small balls, and dropped into water boiling with local salt, where they harden. Groundnut paste goes in and it is served with kalo. The fermentation is the step nobody expects, and the hardening of the balls is the doneness cue.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan or bucket with a cover, Grinding stone, or mortar and pestle, Saucepan, Slotted spoon

  • White ants (enswa), wings removed
  • Water
  • Kadatona — local salt
  • Odi — peanut butter or groundnut paste

No quantities are documented

    Method

    1. Remove the wings from the ants.

    The ferment

    1. Cover the ants in a saucepan or bucket and leave them about two days to ferment.

    Shaping

    1. Grind the fermented ants.
    2. Roll the ground mixture into small balls.

    Cooking

    1. Boil water and add the local salt, kadatona.
    2. Add the balls and allow them to harden.
    3. Add the peanut butter paste, odi.
    4. Serve with kalo.

    Chef Tips

    • Two days covered is the ferment, and it is what separates this from every roasted-ant dish in the collection.
    • The balls harden in the salted boiling water, and that hardening is the cue. Until they firm up they will fall apart.
    • Kadatona is the local salt and it goes into the water before the balls, not after.
    • The odi goes in last. Groundnut paste boiled hard will split.
    • Roll the balls small. Large ones do not harden through.
    • This is one of several completely different Ugandan treatments of the same insect and they should not be confused: this Acholi fermented sauce; the Ganda roasted snack and pounded-to-flour luwombo base in Nswaa; the Lango ground paste alakena in Alakena; and the Lugbara ombangulu in Ombangulu, pounded with salt and cooked in leaves.
    • A fried version exists alongside this one in the same source: roast the ants briefly to remove the wings, spread them in the sun to dry about ten minutes, sort out the remaining wings, fry them in a pan, add some salty water and let it boil off, and take them off when the water has dried up. That is a snack, not a sauce.
    • No quantities are documented. Nothing here is estimated.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Skipping the two-day ferment.
    • Adding the balls to unsalted water.
    • Lifting them out before they harden.
    • Boiling hard after the odi goes in.
    • Rolling the balls too large.

    Cultural Note

    Four Ugandan communities, one insect, four dishes that share nothing but the ingredient — fermented into balls in Acholi, pounded to flour for luwombo in Buganda, ground to a paste in Lango, pounded with salt and cooked in leaves in West Nile. If you want one example of why a Ugandan collection has to be organised by community rather than by country, this is it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Warm gently with a little water.

    Serve with: Karo, Boiled Sweet Potatoes, Malakwang

The two are not variants of one dish and are recorded separately.
