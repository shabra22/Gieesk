Salama da Sugo

Ferrara's celebrated cured sausage, its rich blend of pork cuts and red wine encased and aged before an extremely slow simmer that transforms it into something meltingly soft, traditionally served over mashed potato.

Salama da sugo requires genuine patience both before and during cooking - the sausage is aged for months before it's even cooked, then simmered for hours suspended in a pot so it never touches the bottom, resulting in an intensely rich, spoonable filling unlike any other Italian sausage.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large tall pot, Kitchen twine to suspend the sausage, Potato masher

  • 1 salama da sugo (pre-aged, available from specialty Italian butchers), about 1kg/2.2lb
  • 1 kg (2.2 lb) potatoes, for mashing
  • 80 g (1/3 cup) unsalted butter
  • 100 ml (scant 1/2 cup) whole milk
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Prick the salama all over with a needle to prevent it from bursting during the long cook.
  2. Wrap the salama in a clean cloth and tie securely with kitchen twine.
  3. Suspend the wrapped salama in a tall pot of water using the twine, tied to a wooden spoon or stick laid across the top, so it hangs without touching the bottom or sides.
  4. Bring to a bare simmer and cook very gently for 4-5 hours, topping up water as needed, keeping the sausage fully submerged but never boiling vigorously.
  5. Meanwhile, boil and mash the potatoes with butter and milk until smooth, seasoning well.
  6. Once tender, remove the salama, unwrap, and slice or spoon out the soft, rich filling.
  7. Serve generously over the mashed potato.

Chef Tips

  • Suspending the sausage so it never touches the pot bottom or sides is essential for even, gentle cooking.
  • A bare simmer, never a rolling boil, is critical throughout the long cooking time.
  • Genuine salama da sugo is typically purchased pre-aged from a specialty butcher rather than made entirely from scratch at home.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling too vigorously, which can cause the casing to split.
  • Not pricking the sausage beforehand, increasing the risk of it bursting during the long cook.
  • Rushing the cooking time, which prevents the filling from becoming properly soft and spoonable.

Cultural Note

Salama da sugo is a treasured specialty of Ferrara, its lengthy aging and cooking process making it a genuine labor of love reserved for special occasions.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate cooked, sliced salama for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a covered dish with a little liquid.

Serve with: Creamy mashed potato, A robust red wine from Emilia-Romagna
