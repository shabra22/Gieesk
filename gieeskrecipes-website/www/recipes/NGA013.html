🍲 Efo Elegusi

Efo elegusi — egusi ijebu style

The egusi that is never fried and never balled — blended smooth with onion and crayfish and stirred into a fried pepper base, deliberately on the watery side.

Of the four egusi techniques this is the one that takes a position. The ground seed is mixed smooth with blended onion and two tablespoons of crayfish and stirred straight into the stew, where it dissolves — no pre-frying in oil, no balls dropped into stock. The author says why: she is not a fan of clumpy egusi and prefers hers a little watery, like egusi ijebu. The useful conversion on the page is that one and a half cups of whole seed gives one cup ground, which no other source states.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Dry mill

  • Assorted meats — beef, ponmo, goat meat, cow leg, shaki and other offals are ideal
  • 1 1/2 cups whole egusi, or 1 cup ground egusi
  • 1/2 cup palm oil
  • 1-2 cups chicken or meat stock
  • 3 red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 2 medium onions
  • 2 scotch bonnet (atarodo)
  • 1 cup chopped ugwu leaves
  • 1/2 cup efinrin (basil) leaves
  • 3 heaped tbsp ground crayfish
  • 1/4 cup smoked prawns (optional)
  • 1 medium smoked fish or stockfish
  • 1/2 cup raw or cooked king prawns (optional)
  • 1 bouillon cube
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Blend the bell peppers, scotch bonnet and one onion roughly. Grind the egusi if you bought it whole.
  2. Heat the palm oil on medium and add half the chopped onion.
  3. Saute until fragrant, then add the blended pepper mix and the bouillon cube. Do not add salt if you are using seasoned meat stock.
  4. Fry the pepper mix 10 minutes, until reduced by almost half and the palm oil settles on top.
  5. Add 1 to 2 cups of stock and cook 5 minutes.
  6. Meanwhile blend the remaining half onion smooth, add it to the egusi with 2 tablespoons of the crayfish, and mix until smooth.
  7. Add the egusi paste to the stew, stir, and cook 5 minutes.
  8. Add the meats, smoked or stockfish, prawns and the remaining tablespoon of crayfish. Season.
  9. Cook 5 to 7 minutes, stirring.
  10. Add the ugwu leaves and cook 2 minutes.
  11. Add the efinrin or basil and cook 2 minutes.
  12. Take it off the heat and let it simmer 2 to 3 minutes on the residual heat.

Chef Tips

  • One and a half cups of whole egusi grinds down to one cup. That conversion is on the page and it is the only one in any source consulted.
  • The egusi is mixed with blended onion and crayfish before it goes anywhere near the pot. That is what lets it dissolve instead of clumping.
  • No salt if your stock is seasoned. Between the stock, the cube and three heaped tablespoons of crayfish there is plenty.
  • The pepper base wants ten minutes and the cue is the oil settling on top — the same signal as every other Nigerian fried pepper base.
  • Efinrin goes in after the ugwu, two minutes each, and the pot comes off the heat. Basil is a finishing herb here, not a cooking one.
  • Watery is intentional. If you want it thick you are making a different dish — see Egusi Soup, Caking Method or Egusi Soup, Yoruba Lumpy Style.
  • Ugwu is the author's preference alongside efinrin. Both are added chopped and raw.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Frying the egusi in oil first.
  • Adding the ground seed dry to the pot.
  • Salting on top of a seasoned stock.
  • Cooking the basil for more than two minutes.
  • Thickening it until it is no longer egusi ijebu.

Cultural Note

Ijebu cooking has its own reputation within Yorubaland — the Ijebu are credited with ikokore, with a particular way of making egusi, and with a general willingness to do things differently from Ibadan or Lagos. Egusi ijebu is thin where other egusi is thick, and a cook who makes it that way is usually making a point rather than an error.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of stock. It thickens as it stands, so expect to loosen it.

Serve with: Amala, Iyan, Eba or garri
