🍰 Parrozzo Abruzzese

Parrozzo

A dome-shaped Abruzzese almond cake, naturally gluten-free, coated entirely in dark chocolate to mimic the golden-brown crust and shape of a rustic loaf of bread once baked in wood-fired ovens.

Parrozzo's shape and dark chocolate coating are deliberately designed to resemble a humble round loaf of pane rozzo (rustic bread), a playful visual reference to Abruzzo's traditional peasant baking even as the cake itself is a genuinely elegant, almond-rich dessert.

Ingredients

Equipment: Dome-shaped or round baking pan, Electric mixer

  • 150 g (1½ cups) almond flour
  • 100 g (¾ cup) semolina flour
  • 150 g (¾ cup) granulated sugar
  • 5 eggs, separated
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) unsalted butter, melted
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 200 g (7 oz) dark chocolate, melted, for coating

Method

  1. Whisk the egg yolks with the sugar until pale and thick.
  2. Fold in the almond flour, semolina, melted butter, and lemon zest.
  3. Whip the egg whites to stiff peaks, then gently fold into the batter in stages, being careful not to deflate it.
  4. Pour into a well-buttered dome-shaped or round pan.
  5. Bake at 180°C (350°F) for 30–35 minutes until a skewer comes out clean.
  6. Cool completely, then trim the top flat if needed to help the cake sit upright.
  7. Coat the cooled cake entirely in melted dark chocolate, using a spatula to create a smooth, even shell.
  8. Let the chocolate coating set completely before slicing.

Chef Tips

  • Fold the whipped egg whites in gently and in stages to keep the batter as light and airy as possible.
  • A dome-shaped pan gives the most traditional look, though any round pan works with a bit of trimming.
  • Letting the chocolate coating set fully before slicing gives the cleanest presentation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overmixing after folding in the egg whites, which deflates the batter and results in a denser cake.
  • Coating the cake with chocolate before it has fully cooled, which can cause the coating to slide or melt unevenly.
  • Slicing before the chocolate shell has set, resulting in a messy presentation.

Cultural Note

Parrozzo is a beloved specialty of Pescara and the wider Abruzzo region, its rustic loaf shape and chocolate coating a playful nod to traditional peasant bread-baking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store covered at room temperature for up to 4 days.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: Espresso, A glass of sweet wine
