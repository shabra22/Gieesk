🍵 Attaya, Three-Round Mint Tea

Attaya / ataya

Strong Chinese gunpowder green tea boiled 15 minutes in four small cups of water, a cup of sugar added, the tea poured high from glass to glass to raise a thick foam, returned to the pot and finished with mint — then the same leaves brewed again for the second and third rounds.

Attaya is Senegal’s three-round tea. CREATE!, an NGO working in Senegal, published the clearest quantified recipe on 22 March 2018: add two-thirds of a pack (or 4 to 5 spoonfuls) of green tea leaves to four small cups (about 60 to 75 mL each) of water — the printed sentence is ungrammatical, a ‘to’ is missing, and is reproduced as printed — boil for 15 minutes, add in one cup of sugar, pour the hot tea into a cup to make foam, pour the tea back into the pot and heat again, then add the mint and pour back into serving cups. The page names the three rounds: ‘Friends share this first glass of tea, called lewel. After adding more sugar and mint, or nana, to the teapot, the process begins again for a second (naarel) round of tea. The third (nettel) round is the sweetest of all.’ The one operative cue is foam: ‘The higher up you pour from and the thicker the foam, the better.’ THE RATIO IS VERY STRONG — four to five spoonfuls of leaf against roughly 240 to 300 mL of water and a cup of sugar — consistent with attaya but a warning to the reader; and nothing in the sources prints per-round tea or sugar quantities.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small metal teapot or kettle, Small glasses (kas), Charcoal or gas stove

  • 2/3 of a pack (or 4 to 5 spoonfuls) of green tea leaves — pack size not printed (Chinese gunpowder green tea in the CREATE! description)
  • 4 small cups of water (about 60 to 75 mL each)
  • 1 cup sugar
  • Fresh mint (nana), added at the end — quantity not printed
  • More sugar and mint for the second and third rounds — quantities not printed

Method

  1. Add the green tea leaves to the four small cups of water in a pot (the printed sentence is ungrammatical; this is the reading it implies). Boil for 15 minutes.
  2. Add in one cup of sugar.
  3. Pour the hot tea into a cup to make foam. The higher you pour from, and the thicker the foam, the better.
  4. Pour the tea back into the pot and heat again.
  5. Add the mint and pour back into the serving cups. This is the first round (lewel).
  6. Add more sugar and mint to the same pot and begin again for the second round (naarel), then again for the third, sweetest round (nettel).

Chef Tips

  • The one cue is foam height and thickness; the first brew is strong and bitter.
  • Other printings: foam ‘1 cm minimum’ (Au Sénégal) against ‘about half the glass’ (a Princeton student reflection) — recorded unreconciled.
  • Au Sénégal also records plugging the teapot spout with paper to stop it boiling over and washing the glasses between rounds.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Reading the leaf-to-water ratio as typical tea — it is not.
  • Skipping the high pour; the foam is the point.
  • Rushing the three rounds.

Cultural Note

The three rounds are given four incompatible meanings, all recorded: bitter as death, mild as life, sweet as love (Au Sénégal, and afrik.com); youth, mid-life and old age (CREATE!, 2019); a mother’s, a friend’s and a lover’s love (a Brooklyn tea blog); life, love and death (CorpsAfrica). The name of the first round is printed ‘lewel’ (CREATE!) and ‘leweul’ (Au Sénégal). ‘Teranga is about the way we treat our guests’ (Pierre Thiam, quoted by CREATE!). One always makes extra tea for people passing by (Au Sénégal).
