🥥 Kashaato

Qumbe Cad — Kashaato

White coconut sweets from the Banaadir coast — milk and sugar cooked to a set, shredded coconut stirred in, poured out and cut into squares while still warm.

Kashaato is the sweet Somali cooks warn each other about. It has four ingredients and no room at all for improvisation: the measures and the cooking time are the recipe, and a cook who rounds the milk up or turns the heat down gets toffee or a sticky tray instead of a clean white sweet. Households in Mogadishu have long had one person who could reliably make it, and everyone else borrowed her cup.

Ingredients

Equipment: 3-litre heavy saucepan, Kitchen scales, or a 237 ml cup measure, Oiled tray or marble slab, Wooden spoon

  • 200 ml milk (2% or whole)
  • 400 g (2 cups) granulated sugar
  • 60 g (2/3 cup) fine shredded desiccated coconut
  • 1/4 tsp mint extract, or cardamom, vanilla or rose water (optional)
  • 2 tbsp sprinkles or extra shredded coconut, to finish

Method

  1. Oil a tray or a marble slab and set it where you can reach it — once the mixture is ready there is no time to look for one.
  2. Pour the milk into a 3-litre saucepan over medium heat and add the sugar.
  3. Stir steadily and let it cook for 8 minutes.
  4. Add the shredded coconut and keep stirring as it cooks on.
  5. Stir in the mint extract, or whichever flavouring you are using.
  6. At 15 minutes total cooking time, pour the mixture out onto the oiled surface and spread it level.
  7. Scatter the sprinkles or extra coconut over the top immediately, while the surface is still tacky.
  8. After 2 minutes, cut into squares or diamonds. Let them cool completely before lifting them off.

Chef Tips

  • Weigh the sugar and coconut, and measure the milk at exactly 200 ml. This is the one Somali sweet where the measures cannot be approximate — a cup that is not 237 ml will throw the whole batch.
  • If it comes out sticky and will not set, the heat was too low. Raise the heat next time, or cook a little longer.
  • If it turns out soft and chewy like toffee, there was too much sugar, or it was held too long over too low a flame.
  • Cooking a couple of minutes past the 15 gives a more crumbly, fudge-like sweet, which some households prefer.
  • The mint is not traditional everywhere — cardamom is the commoner choice — but it does cut the sweetness, which is why some cooks keep it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding more coconut than the recipe says, hoping for more coconut flavour. It stops the sweet setting at all.
  • Reducing the sugar to make it less sweet. Kashaato is a sugar confection and the sugar is structural; cutting it produces a failure, not a lighter sweet.
  • Cooking over low heat to be safe, which is the single most common reason a batch never sets.
  • Leaving it more than a couple of minutes before cutting, after which it shatters instead of slicing.

Health Benefits

  • Coconut contributes a little dietary fibre and manganese.
  • Portioned small by design — one or two squares with tea is the traditional serving.

Cultural Note

Kashaato belongs to the Banaadir coast, where coconut has been part of the kitchen for as long as the dhow trade has run. It is made for weddings, Eid and the arrival of guests, and cut into small diamonds so a single tray goes round a large gathering.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight tin at room temperature, up to 2 weeks. Do not refrigerate — it goes damp.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: Shaah Cadays, Xalwo, Somali coffee
