🐠 Baked Fish with Chermoula and Couscous (Essaouira-Style)

Seksou b'l-hout — couscous with fish

Skin-on fish fillets spread with a lime-coriander-cumin chermoula and baked 15 minutes, served on instant couscous with carrot and courgette.

45mTotal time
20mPrep

2Servings

A chermoula of coriander, garlic, cumin, turmeric, lime rind and juice, olive oil, water and chopped chilli is spread over two fillets and chilled. Vegetables are sautéed and covered until done, instant couscous is soaked in boiling water for 10 minutes, and the fish bakes 15 minutes at 180°C, with the couscous and vegetables added around it for the last 2 minutes. The author tasted seksou b'l-hout in the fishing port of Essaouira.

Ingredients

Equipment: Oven dish, Small pan with lid, Bowl for couscous

    Chermoula

    • 4 tbsp chopped coriander leaves
    • 1 chopped clove garlic
    • 1 tsp cumin seed
    • 1/4 tsp turmeric
    • 1/4 tsp grated lime rind
    • 4 tbsp olive oil
    • 3 tbsp lime juice
    • 2 tbsp water
    • salt
    • 1 red hot pepper, chopped without seeds

    Fish and garnish

    • oil for greasing
    • 2 fish fillets with skin, circa 400 g (bergylt for instance)
    • 1 tbsp olive oil
    • 1 tbsp chopped onion
    • 1 small zucchini
    • 3 carrots in thin slices
    • 100 g instant couscous
    • 100 ml boiling water
    • salt
    • coriander leaves

    Method

    1. Mix the chermoula ingredients. Spread 2 tbsp over each fillet and put in the fridge.
    2. Soften the onion in the olive oil, add the carrot and zucchini, and cook covered until done, stirring now and then.
    3. Preheat the oven to 180°C (350°F, gas 4). Pour the boiling water over the couscous, leave to stand 10 minutes and stir with a fork.
    4. Bake the fish 15 minutes on the middle shelf, adding the couscous and vegetables around it for the last 2 minutes.
    5. Serve with coriander leaves.

    Chef Tips

    • The fish bakes by time only (15 minutes); the page relays no doneness cue, so check that it flakes.
    • Vegetables: cook covered until done.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Overbaking thin fillets.
    • Leaving the couscous unfluffed.

    The author writes that she sampled seksou b'l-hout in Essaouira, a fishing port on the Atlantic coast of Morocco.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten fresh.

    Reheating: Not recommended for fish.
