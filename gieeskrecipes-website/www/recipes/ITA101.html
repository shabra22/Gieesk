🍨 Gelato alla Crema

Gelato alla Crema

Italy's classic custard-based ice cream, denser and less sweet than typical American ice cream, made from a gently cooked egg-and-milk base and churned to a smooth, elastic texture.

The lower fat content and slower churn speed of true Italian gelato give it a denser, silkier texture than ice cream, and the base recipe here — really just a rich custard, frozen — is the foundation from which countless other gelato flavors are built.

Ingredients

Equipment: Ice cream maker, Saucepan, Whisk, Fine sieve

  • 500 ml (2 cups) whole milk
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) heavy cream
  • 5 egg yolks
  • 130 g (⅔ cup) granulated sugar
  • 1 vanilla bean, split and scraped (or 1 tbsp vanilla extract)
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Warm the milk, cream, and vanilla bean (seeds and pod) in a saucepan over medium heat until just below a simmer.
  2. Whisk the egg yolks, sugar, and salt together in a bowl until pale and slightly thickened.
  3. Gradually pour the warm milk mixture into the eggs, whisking constantly to temper them.
  4. Return everything to the saucepan and cook over low heat, stirring constantly, until the custard thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon, about 5–8 minutes.
  5. Strain the custard through a fine sieve to remove the vanilla pod and any bits of cooked egg.
  6. Chill the custard thoroughly, at least 4 hours or overnight, before churning.
  7. Churn in an ice cream maker according to the manufacturer's instructions until thick and creamy.
  8. Transfer to a container and freeze for at least 2 hours before serving to firm up further.

Chef Tips

  • Cook the custard low and slow — rushing over high heat risks scrambling the eggs.
  • Chilling the base thoroughly before churning is essential for a smooth, properly frozen texture.
  • Gelato is traditionally served slightly softer than American-style ice cream — let it sit a few minutes at room temperature before scooping if very firm.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the custard over heat that's too high, which can curdle the eggs.
  • Skipping the chilling step before churning, resulting in a coarser, icier texture.
  • Over-churning, which can incorporate too much air and lose gelato's characteristic density.

Health Benefits

Cultural Note

Gelato's lower fat content and denser churn distinguish it from American ice cream, and it remains a daily ritual across Italy, sold fresh in gelaterie rather than pre-packaged.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Freeze in an airtight container for up to 2 weeks.

Reheating: Not applicable; let soften slightly at room temperature before serving if very firm.

Serve with: Fresh berries, A shot of espresso poured over for an affogato
