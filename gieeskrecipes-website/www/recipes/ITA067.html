Spaghetti alla Nerano

Born on the Amalfi Coast in the tiny bay of Nerano, this pasta layers fried zucchini into a creamy sauce built from their own cooking oil, butter, and a generous amount of melting provolone.

Legend places the dish's invention at a single restaurant in Nerano in the 1950s, where thinly fried zucchini rounds are worked into a sauce so silky it seems to contain cream, though the richness comes entirely from cheese, butter, and the zucchini's own oil.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Deep frying pan, Large sauté pan, Slotted spoon

  • 400 g (14 oz) spaghetti
  • 1 kg (2.2 lb) firm zucchini, thinly sliced
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • 60 g (4 tbsp) unsalted butter
  • 200 g (2 cups) provolone del Monaco or a mild provolone, grated
  • 40 g (½ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • A few fresh basil leaves
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Fry the zucchini slices in batches in hot vegetable oil until golden, then drain on paper towels, reserving a little of the frying oil.
  2. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the spaghetti until very al dente.
  3. While the pasta cooks, warm a little of the reserved zucchini oil with the butter in a wide pan and add most of the fried zucchini, mashing some of it lightly to help it melt into a sauce base.
  4. Drain the pasta, reserving a cup of cooking water, and add it to the pan with a splash of the water.
  5. Remove from the heat and toss vigorously while adding the provolone and Parmigiano gradually, allowing the residual heat to melt the cheese into a creamy sauce without becoming stringy or clumping.
  6. Fold in the reserved whole zucchini slices and torn basil, plate, and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Working the cheese in off the direct heat, gradually, is essential to avoid a greasy, separated sauce.
  • Keep some zucchini slices whole for texture, mashing only a portion into the sauce.
  • A good melting provolone is non-negotiable here — the cheese carries the whole dish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the cheese at once over high heat, which can cause it to seize and clump instead of melting smoothly.
  • Under-seasoning the zucchini, which needs a good amount of salt while frying to develop real flavor.
  • Using watery, out-of-season zucchini, which won't fry as well or contribute enough flavor to the sauce.

Cultural Note

The dish takes its name from Nerano, a small bay near Massa Lubrense on the Sorrentine Peninsula, and remains most closely tied to the restaurant Maria Grazia, where it is said to have first been served.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; the sauce does not hold or reheat particularly well.

Reheating: Gently in a pan with a splash of water, stirring constantly to keep the sauce from breaking.

Serve with: A crisp white wine from the Amalfi Coast, such as Falanghina
