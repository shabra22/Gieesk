🍖 Digaag la Foorneeyay

Digaag la Foorneeyay

A whole chicken with xawaash, lemon zest, garlic and butter worked under the skin rather than over it, roasted hot then moderate, rested a full fifteen minutes, and served with a drained yogurt dip.

The trick that makes this worth doing is getting the marinade between the skin and the meat. Rubbed on the outside it seasons the skin and runs off; spooned underneath it sits directly against the flesh for the whole seventy minutes. Everything else is about keeping the bird out of its own juices, on a rack or a bed of vegetables, so the underside roasts rather than poaches.

Ingredients

Equipment: Roasting tin with a rack, Sieve and muslin, for draining the yogurt, Small bowl

  • 1.6 kg (3 1/2 lb) whole chicken

The marinade

  • 2 tbsp butter, softened
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 1/4 tsp salt, to taste
  • 1 tbsp lemon zest
  • 1 tsp ground black pepper
  • 2 1/4 tsp xawaash spice blend
  • 2 cloves garlic, crushed to a paste
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice

To finish and roast

  • 1/4 tsp xawaash spice blend plus 1/4 tsp salt, to sprinkle
  • 2 onions and 2 carrots, thickly sliced, or a roasting rack
  • 600 g potatoes, cut into large chunks

For the yogurt dip

  • 120 ml (1/2 cup) yogurt, drained
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • 1/2 tsp salt, to taste
  • 2 green onions, chopped

Method

  1. Drain the yogurt in a sieve lined with muslin — an hour gives a thick dip, overnight in the fridge gives a very thick one.
  2. Take the chicken out of the fridge and let it come to room temperature.
  3. Mix the butter, olive oil, salt, lemon zest, black pepper, xawaash, garlic paste and lemon juice into a marinade.
  4. Slide your fingers between the skin and the breast to loosen the skin, working carefully so it does not tear.
  5. Spoon the marinade in under the skin and spread it over the meat.
  6. Turn the bird over and do the same on the underside, then rub whatever is left all over the outside.
  7. Mix the extra 1/4 tsp xawaash with the 1/4 tsp salt and sprinkle it over the skin.
  8. Set the chicken on a rack, or on a bed of thickly sliced onion and carrot — it must not sit in its own juices.
  9. Roast at 232C / 450F for 30 minutes.
  10. Add the potatoes around the bird, drop the oven to 204C / 400F and roast for another 40 minutes.
  11. Rest the chicken for 15 minutes before carving. Meanwhile mix the drained yogurt with the olive oil, lemon juice and salt, and scatter the green onions over it.

Chef Tips

  • Under the skin, not on it. This is the whole point of the recipe and it is worth the five minutes of careful finger work.
  • The bird has to be raised up. Sitting in its juices, the underside steams and the skin there never crisps.
  • Rest it for the full fifteen minutes. Carve it straight from the oven and a surprising amount of the juice ends up on the board.
  • Do not stuff the cavity. Stuffing blocks the hot air, and the extra time needed to cook it dries out the rest of the bird — spoon rice in afterwards if you want the look.
  • Xawaash is what makes this Somali; berbere, curry powder or plain cumin will all work but taste like different dishes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Tearing the skin while loosening it, after which the marinade leaks out during roasting.
  • Roasting the bird flat in the tin, so it poaches in its own juices underneath.
  • Carving immediately, losing the juices the rest would have kept.
  • Starting with a fridge-cold bird, which roasts unevenly — the outside is done before the centre is safe.

Health Benefits

  • Chicken provides lean protein, niacin, selenium and B6.
  • Drained yogurt makes a high-protein dip in place of a cream or mayonnaise sauce.
  • Roasted on a rack, so the fat drains away rather than being absorbed.

Cultural Note

Chicken is not universally admired across Somalia — in the camel and goat country of the central regions it has traditionally been looked down on and barely eaten. Roast chicken is city and diaspora food, and the xawaash under the skin is what brings it into the Somali repertoire.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; keep the bones for stock.

Reheating: Cover and warm in a moderate oven. Uncovered reheating dries the breast.

Serve with: Bariis Maraq, Basbaas, Green salad
