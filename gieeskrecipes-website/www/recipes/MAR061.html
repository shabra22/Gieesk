🫓 Batbout (Moroccan Pocket Bread)

Batbout / mkhamer / matlou' (بطبوط)

A stovetop pocket bread of white, durum and wholewheat flour, rolled thin, risen until puffy and cooked in a hot dry pan until it balloons.

2h 15mTotal time
30mPrep
30mCook

130Calories
MediumDifficulty

Batbout is cooked on a griddle rather than baked. The yeast is proofed with sugar until foamy, the dough rests 10 to 15 minutes, balls are rolled to a thin round about 1/8 inch thick, and left to rise an hour or a little longer until light and puffy. In a very hot pan, each batbout is turned as soon as it is set (about 10 to 15 seconds), then cooked until puffed with air and browned on both sides.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large skillet or griddle, Rolling pin

  • 1 tbsp dry yeast
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 3 cups white flour, preferably bread flour or high-gluten
  • 2 cups durum flour or fine semolina
  • 1 cup whole wheat flour
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 3 tbsp olive oil or vegetable oil
  • 2 cups warm water, approx.

Method

  1. Mix the yeast with a teaspoon of the sugar in a little warm water and set aside until foamy.
  2. Combine the flours, salt, remaining sugar and oil; add the yeast mixture and water and knead into a dough. Cover with a towel and leave to rest 10 to 15 minutes.
  3. Divide into balls and roll each into a thin round about 1/8 inch (0.3 cm) thick.
  4. Leave to rise for an hour or a little longer, until light and puffy.
  5. Heat a large pan or griddle over medium heat for several minutes until very hot. Cook each batbout, turning as soon as it is set (about 10 to 15 seconds), before air bubbles begin to appear.
  6. Continue until the breads have puffed with air and are browned on both sides.

Chef Tips

  • Cues quoted: yeast foamy; dough light and puffy; cooked until puffed and browned on both sides.
  • The author says durum or semolina is central and white flour is kept to about half; small Ramadan versions about 5 cm are eaten as bite-sized sandwiches.
  • No oven is used.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rolling too thick, which stops the pocket forming.
  • A pan that is not very hot.
  • Under-rising.

Cultural Note

The author notes the names mkhamer, toghrift and matlou' vary by region and that mini batbout are eaten at Ramadan.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best fresh; keeps a day or two sealed.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a dry pan.

Serve with: Fillings of kefta, eggs or cheese
