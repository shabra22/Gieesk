🫘 Bessara (Fava Bean Soup or Dip)

Bessara / bissara — dried fava bean puree (بصارة)

Dried split fava beans simmered with garlic, cumin and paprika and pureed into a thick soup, served with olive oil, cumin and harissa.

9h 15mTotal time
15mPrep
1hCook
6Servings
290Calories
EasyDifficulty

The beans are soaked at least 8 hours or overnight, drained and peeled of loose skins, then simmered with water, olive oil, garlic and spices, partly covered, for about an hour until tender. They are drained over a bowl, pureed and thinned with the reserved liquid, and finished with lemon juice. The author says it is a popular street food as well as humble family fare, especially in the north, and traditionally a bit bland out of the pot, so condiments matter.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, Immersion blender or food mill

  • 12 oz dried split fava beans (about 2.25 cups)
  • ⅓ cup olive oil
  • 6 to 7 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 6.5 cups water
  • 2 tsp paprika
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1.5 tsp salt, or to taste
  • 1 to 2 tbsp lemon juice, to taste (optional)
  • Condiments (no quantities printed): salt, cumin, ground cayenne, harissa, extra virgin olive oil

Method

  1. Soak the beans at least 8 hours or overnight.
  2. Drain and remove any loose skins.
  3. Simmer with the water, oil, garlic and spices, partly covered, about an hour, until the beans are tender.
  4. Drain over a bowl, saving the liquid. Puree, thinning with the reserved liquid.
  5. Stir in the lemon juice and reheat gently over medium-low. Serve with the condiments.

Chef Tips

  • Doneness cue: beans tender; the traditional version cooks them until they break down on their own.
  • The soak is time only (a soak, not a ferment).
  • Serve hot with bread for dipping.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-soaking, which leaves the beans hard.
  • Skipping the condiments; it is meant to be bland out of the pot.
  • Pureeing without reserving cooking liquid.

Cultural Note

The author calls bessara a popular street food as well as humble family fare, especially popular in the north of Morocco.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 3 days in the fridge; thickens when cold.

Reheating: Thin with water and reheat gently.

Serve with: Khobz
