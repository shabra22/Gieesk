🍑 Prosciutto di San Daniele con Fichi

Prosciutto di San Daniele con Fichi

Delicate, sweet San Daniele prosciutto, prized for its notably mellow, less salty character, draped simply over ripe fresh figs for a striking autumn antipasto.

Prosciutto di San Daniele, produced in a small Friulian town under strict protected-origin rules, is generally considered sweeter and less intensely salty than Parma ham, making it a particularly graceful match for ripe, honeyed fresh figs.

Ingredients

Equipment: Sharp knife, Serving platter

  • 150 g (5.3 oz) Prosciutto di San Daniele, thinly sliced
  • 8 ripe fresh figs, halved
  • A drizzle of honey
  • Freshly cracked black pepper
  • A few fresh basil or mint leaves (optional)

Method

  1. Arrange the halved figs on a serving platter, cut side up.
  2. Drape or wrap each fig half with a slice of San Daniele prosciutto.
  3. Drizzle lightly with honey.
  4. Finish with a crack of black pepper and a few torn basil or mint leaves if using.
  5. Serve immediately, at room temperature.

Chef Tips

  • Genuinely ripe, fragrant figs make all the difference in this simple pairing.
  • San Daniele milder character pairs especially well with fruit compared to saltier prosciutto varieties.
  • Serve at room temperature rather than chilled, which allows both the ham and figs to show their full flavor.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using underripe figs, which lack the sweetness this simple dish depends on entirely.
  • Over-drizzling with honey, which can overwhelm the delicate balance between the ham and fruit.
  • Serving straight from the refrigerator, which mutes the flavors of both ingredients.

Cultural Note

Prosciutto di San Daniele holds a protected designation of origin, produced exclusively in the small Friulian town it's named for, and is considered one of Italy's two most prestigious cured hams alongside Parma.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best assembled and eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: A chilled glass of Prosecco or a light Friulian white wine
