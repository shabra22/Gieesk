The Ethiopian celebration meat platter is the most ambitious expression of Ethiopian cooking — multiple meat preparations, each requiring different techniques and times, assembled on a single large injera in the traditional mesob serving style. This recipe is a guide for assembling the complete celebration feast.

Ingredients

Equipment: Everything from each individual recipe

    Assemble from individual recipes:

    • Key Wat — beef + boiled eggs
    • Ye-Beg Key Wat — lamb
    • Kitfo Leb-Leb — warmed minced beef
    • Tibs — beef sautéed
    • Dulet — organ meat mix
    • Quanta Fried — dried beef
    • Ayib — in a mound
    • Gomen — collard greens
    • Tikel Gomen — cabbage
    • Large injera as the base

    Method

    1. Day before: make Key Wat and Ye-Beg Key Wat. Make Quanta.
    2. Morning of: prepare Dulet.
    3. Two hours before: make Gomen and Tikel Gomen.
    4. One hour before: hand-mince beef for Kitfo. Prepare Ayib.
    5. 30 minutes before: make Tibs.
    6. Lay large injera on the mesob (basket table).
    7. Arrange components artfully: wats around the edges, kitfo in the centre, ayib alongside, vegetables between meat components.
    8. Serve with additional rolled injera in a basket.

    Chef Tips

    • The wats must be the hottest items when assembled.
    • Kitfo and tibs are made last — they are the fastest items.
    • The ayib mound is always at the centre alongside the kitfo.
    • Communal eating from the shared injera is the tradition — everyone eats from the same platter.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not staging over 2 days — impossible in one day alone.

    Cultural Note

    The Ethiopian celebration meat platter is the highest expression of Ethiopian hospitality — the mesob table laid with every meat preparation the kitchen can produce, shared communally, eaten with hands and injera. To be invited to eat from this table is to be considered family.
