🍅 Tanzanian Tomato and Onion Salad

Kachumbari ya Tanzania

Tanzanian Kachumbari — fresh tomatoes, red onion, and green chilli tossed with lemon juice, coriander, and salt. Tanzania's equivalent of the East African kachumbari — the fresh salad that accompanies nyama choma, pilau, and ugali.

Kachumbari is as Tanzanian as it is Kenyan — the shared Swahili coastal salad tradition produces identical preparations in both countries. The tomatoes must be ripe and firm, the onion wafer-thin, and the lemon generous.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl

  • 4 large ripe firm tomatoes — diced
  • 1 medium red onion — very thinly sliced
  • 1—2 green chillies — thinly sliced
  • 3 tbsp fresh lemon or lime juice
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper

Method

  1. Dice tomatoes into even 1cm pieces.
  2. Slice onion as thinly as possible.
  3. Combine tomatoes, onion, and chilli in a bowl.
  4. Add lemon juice, coriander, salt, and pepper.
  5. Toss gently. Taste and adjust.
  6. Serve immediately — do not rest long or tomatoes become watery.

Chef Tips

  • Wafer-thin onion is the signature — use a sharp knife or mandoline.
  • Ripe but firm tomatoes only — soft tomatoes become watery immediately.
  • Serve within 15 minutes of assembling.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Thick onion slices.
  • Soft tomatoes.
  • Making too far ahead.

The Science

Salt draws water from the tomato and onion cells through osmosis — after 15 minutes the kachumbari becomes watery as the cells release their fluid. Serving immediately preserves the fresh, bright texture.

Cultural Note

Kachumbari is the condiment that makes every Tanzanian meal more alive — the fresh acidity and brightness that cuts through the richness of pilau, the fat of nyama choma, and the starch of ugali.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best served immediately.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Nyama Choma, Pilau, Ugali, Fish, Grilled meat
