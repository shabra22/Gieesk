🥛 Iraqw Milk and Millet Drink

Kinywaji cha Maziwa na Mtama — Waaraqw

Iraqw Milk and Millet Drink — finger millet cooked briefly and mixed with fresh soured milk, chilled and served as a refreshing drink. The Cushitic dairy-grain tradition of the Mbulu Highlands — nourishing, cooling, and deeply characteristic of the Iraqw people.

The Iraqw milk and millet drink is a cold preparation — unlike hot porridge, this is a refreshing, chilled drink made from cooked millet gruel thinned with soured milk and served cool. It is drunk throughout the day in the Mbulu Highlands, particularly in the hot dry season.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Whisk

  • ½ cup finger millet flour
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 cup natural soured milk or yogurt
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Cook millet flour with 2 cups water into a thin, pourable porridge.
  2. Cool completely.
  3. Stir in soured milk, honey, and salt.
  4. Chill for at least 1 hour.
  5. Serve cold — stir before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Should be pourable — thinner than eating porridge.
  • Serve very cold for maximum refreshment.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not chilling sufficiently.
  • Too thick — should pour like a drink.

Cultural Note

Iraqw milk and millet drink is the taste of the Mbulu Highlands afternoon — the cool, slightly sour drink that refreshes after a morning of farming the terraced fields. It carries in one glass the two most important elements of Iraqw food culture: the ancient grain and the cattle milk.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Serve cold.

Serve with: As a refreshing standalone drink, With a light meal
