🌰 Curry Spiced Jackfruit Seeds

Ffene seeds

Jackfruit seeds boiled half an hour, dried six hours so the second skin loosens, peeled and then fried twenty minutes with curry powder until golden and crisp outside.

Jackfruit is eaten unripe as a meat substitute, ripe as fruit and overripe as jam or juice, and the seeds — which most people throw away — make a snack that stands in for roast potatoes. The preparation has one non-obvious step: after boiling, the seeds are spread out and dried for six hours, which loosens the second of three protective coverings so it will actually come off. There are three layers, two come off, and the third is edible and stays on.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pan for boiling, Tray or flat plate, for drying, Frying pan

  • 480 to 720 ml (2 to 3 cups) jackfruit seeds
  • 10 ml (2 tsp) curry powder
  • Salt
  • 2 to 3 sprigs rosemary or thyme
  • A splash of vinegar, or lemon juice
  • Oil, for frying

Method

  1. Open the jackfruit, remove all the seeds and set them aside.
  2. Remove the gummy outer covering from each seed and wash them.
  3. Put the seeds in a clean pan, cover with water and bring to the boil.
  4. Boil about 30 minutes, until soft and tender when pierced with a fork.

The drying

  1. Drain the seeds and spread them on a tray or flat plate to cool and dry. They should dry for about 6 hours.
  2. Once dried and the second covering has loosened, peel it off each seed. The third layer underneath is edible and stays on.

The fry

  1. Put a frying pan on high heat. Add oil, salt, curry powder and the seeds, and mix well.
  2. Let them cook in the oil about 20 minutes on medium-high heat, stirring occasionally. They can also be baked if you have an oven.
  3. Add a splash of vinegar or lemon juice and the rosemary or thyme.
  4. Take them off the heat once they are a vibrant golden brown and crispy outside.
  5. Serve as a snack, or in place of roast potatoes.

Chef Tips

  • The six-hour dry is the step that makes this possible. It loosens the second covering — try to peel a wet boiled seed and you will give up.
  • Three coverings, two removed, one edible. You do not need to peel the innermost layer and you should not try.
  • Use ripe jackfruit seeds, and discard any that have started to sprout or look green. That is a firm instruction, not a preference.
  • Seed size varies by jackfruit variety, so the boiling time is a guide and the fork test is the real measure.
  • Twenty minutes in the pan is longer than it sounds necessary and it is what gets the crisp exterior.
  • The vinegar and the herbs go in at the end. Earlier and the acid stops it browning.
  • They genuinely work as a roast-potato substitute, which is the best way to think about using them.
  • A whole jackfruit gives a great many seeds, and this is the dish that stops them being waste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Trying to peel the seeds before they have dried.
  • Using sprouting or green seeds.
  • Peeling off the third, edible layer.
  • Cutting the frying short.
  • Adding the vinegar at the start.

Health Benefits

  • Jackfruit seeds are high in fibre, protein, thiamine and riboflavin.
  • A good source of resistant starch, magnesium and potassium.
  • Turns something usually discarded into food, which is the best kind of nutrition.
  • Pan-fried rather than deep-fried, and entirely plant-based.

Cultural Note

Jackfruit trees are common in central Uganda and the fruit is eaten at every stage of ripeness, which is a good example of using a plant completely. The seeds were traditionally roasted in embers; the curry powder here is the modern Ugandan reflex, and it suits them.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days in an airtight container.

Reheating: A hot pan or oven for a few minutes.

Serve with: Ugandan tea, Toasted Pumpkin Seeds, As a side in place of potatoes
