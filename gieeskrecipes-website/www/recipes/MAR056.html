🍋 Preserved Lemons

Lemoun markad / lhamd markad (الحامض المرقد)

Lemons quartered, packed with coarse salt in a jar and left to cure for a month or longer until the rinds are soft and mellow.

30dTotal time
20mPrep
10Servings
20Calories
EasyDifficulty

Whole lemons are cut partway into four attached wedges, the crevices packed with salt, and pushed tightly into a clean jar. After a few days the lemons soften and release juice; more salted lemons fill the jar, salt goes on top, and fresh lemon juice tops up anything not submerged. The author says to leave them to cure for at least a month and up to a year, and that the longer they sit, the darker and softer they become.

Ingredients

Equipment: Clean glass jar with lid, Sharp knife

  • 10 fresh lemons, for preserving
  • 2 additional lemons, for juicing (if needed)
  • 1 cup coarse salt, preferably kosher
  • 1 clean jar, barely large enough to accommodate 9 or 10 compressed lemons

Method

  1. Wash and dry 7 or 8 of the lemons (the page prints 7 or 8 for the first round; the yield is 10).
  2. Partially cut through each lemon from top to bottom to make four attached wedges. Generously fill the crevices with salt; no need to measure, a rough tablespoon or so per lemon.
  3. Squeeze the salted lemons shut and pack them into the jar, wedged in as tightly as possible so they cannot move around.
  4. Close the jar and set aside for a few days. The lemons will slightly soften and more juice will be released.
  5. Add as many more salted lemons as will fit. When the jar is as full as possible, add some salt to the top. If the lemons are not submerged, top them off with fresh lemon juice.
  6. Close the jar and put it in a cupboard to cure for at least one month or as long as a year. The page gives time only for readiness; see tips for what to look for.
  7. Once opened, store in the fridge. Rinse off excess brine before using.

Chef Tips

  • WHAT TO LOOK FOR: the source gives time only. A blogger describing Kitty Morse's method (a secondhand report) says that in about three weeks the lemons get very soft and the brining liquid thick and cloudy. Benlafquih says the lemons grow darker and softer with time.
  • An oily-looking layer on the brine is harmless, says the author; discard the jar if you see mold. Other sources say a lacy white film is harmless.
  • Thicker-skinned lemons take longer to cure than thin-skinned ones.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not submerging the lemons; top up with lemon juice.
  • Using a jar far too large, which leaves air.
  • Using the lemons before they are soft, when they taste harsh.

Cultural Note

The author names doqq and boussera as traditional lemon varieties and says cinnamon, bay and other spices in the jar are not traditional.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Cured lemons keep in the fridge for months once opened.

Reheating: Not heated.

Serve with: Chicken tagines
