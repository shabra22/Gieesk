🥐 Feteer Meshaltet (Egyptian Layered Pastry)

Feteer meshaltet — فطير مشلتت

A stretched, ghee-layered dough folded into squares and baked until puffed and golden, plain or filled with cheese, olives, coconut and raisins.

2h 35mTotal time
40mPrep
25mCook
4Servings
560Calories
HardDifficulty

A smooth, sticky dough is kneaded 10 minutes by stand mixer or 15 by hand, divided into four, drizzled with ghee and rested an hour. Each piece is stretched very thin on a ghee-drizzled surface, folded in thirds twice into a square, and rested again in the pan for 30 minutes. A plain feteer uses one square and a filled one uses two sealed together; it bakes at 380°F (190°C) until the bottom is golden, then under the broiler for a couple of minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Stand mixer or large bowl, Large smooth work surface, Baking pan, Oven with broiler

    Dough

    • 3 cups flour
    • 1/2 teaspoon salt
    • About 1 and 1/2 cups warm water (you may need a little more or less)
    • 1 to 2 cups melted ghee

    Fillings (choose one)

    • 2 cups mozzarella cheese
    • 1/2 cup pitted chopped black olives
    • 1/2 green or red pepper cut into slices
    • 1-2 cups shredded coconut
    • 1/2 cup raisins
    • 1/3 cup powdered sugar
    • 2 tablespoons cream for brushing the top

    Method

    1. Knead the flour, salt and water to a smooth, sticky dough for 10 minutes in a stand mixer or 15 by hand.
    2. Divide into 4 pieces, drizzle generously with ghee and rest an hour.
    3. On a ghee-drizzled surface, stretch each piece very thin. Fold in thirds with ghee between the folds, twice, to make a square.
    4. Plain: put one square in a pan and rest 30 minutes, then stretch it larger and thinner. Filled: use two squares, add the filling and seal the edges; brush the coconut version with cream and the cheese version with ghee.
    5. Bake in a preheated 380°F (190°C) oven on the central rack until the bottom is golden brown; it puffs. Turn on the broiler for a couple of minutes until the top is golden too.

    Chef Tips

    • Cues: stretched very thin; bottom golden brown; top golden. The one-hour and 30-minute rests are NO CUE, TIME ONLY.
    • The page gives no yield; four squares make 2 filled or 4 plain.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Tearing the dough; keep your hands and the surface well greased.
    • Too little ghee between folds, which gives bread instead of flaky layers.

    Cultural Note

    Sawsan Abu Farha writes that the pastry dates to the pharaohs as a temple offering and later became a delicacy at religious holidays and weddings (her statement). She first saw feteer being made on her honeymoon in Cairo.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best the day it is baked (our note).

    Reheating: Warm in a moderate oven to restore flakiness.

    Serve with: Honey, jam, feta or green olives (page suggestion)
