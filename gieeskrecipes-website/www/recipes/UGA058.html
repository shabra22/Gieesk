🥚 Ugandan Eggroll

Eggroll

A hard-boiled egg wrapped in curried mashed potato into a palm-sized ball, double-coated in beaten egg and breadcrumbs, and deep-fried golden.

The Ugandan eggroll is a whole hard-boiled egg inside a shell of curried mashed potato, breaded and deep-fried — sold on the street and at kiosks all over Kampala. It has nothing to do with a Chinese egg roll. Five potatoes and five eggs makes three: three eggs get boiled for the centres and two get beaten for the coating. The double dip — egg, crumbs, egg, crumbs again — is what gives it a shell that holds.

Ingredients

Equipment: Two pans for boiling, Two bowls, Deep pan for frying, Slotted spoon

  • 5 medium potatoes, peeled and washed
  • 5 large eggs — 3 to boil, 2 to beat
  • 240 ml (1 cup) breadcrumbs
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) curry powder
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) butter or margarine
  • Salt
  • Oil, for frying

Instead of breadcrumbs

  • Finely crushed dehydrated soya meat, the packaged kind

Method

  1. Cut the potatoes in half and put them in a pan with enough water. Boil until soft and falling apart.
  2. Drain and set them aside to cool.
  3. Boil 3 of the eggs until the yolk is hard. Take them off the heat and cool them in cold water.
  4. In a bowl, beat the remaining 2 eggs with a pinch of salt until frothy. Set aside.
  5. In another bowl, mash the slightly cooled potatoes.
  6. Add the butter, salt and curry powder and keep mixing until no lumps are visible.
  7. Peel the boiled eggs.

Building the rolls

  1. Using your hands, gently add the mashed potato onto an egg, slowly building the roll until it is a palm-sized ball. Make sure the ball is firm.
  2. Gently roll the ball in the whisked egg, then in the breadcrumbs, back into the egg, and thoroughly in the breadcrumbs again.
  3. Repeat until all the rolls are ready.
  4. Deep-fry the rolls until golden brown.
  5. Serve with mayonnaise or ketchup and a side of kachumbari or coleslaw.

Chef Tips

  • Double-coat: egg, crumbs, egg, crumbs. A single coat splits in the fryer and the potato comes away from the egg.
  • Make the ball firm. A loose one falls apart the moment it hits the oil.
  • Mash the potatoes while slightly warm, not hot and not cold. Hot they are too wet, cold they will not come smooth.
  • No lumps in the mash. A lump is a weak point in the shell.
  • Boil the eggs until the yolk is hard. A soft yolk turns to liquid in the fryer.
  • Cool the boiled eggs in cold water. It stops the cooking and makes them far easier to peel.
  • If you have no breadcrumbs, finely crushed dehydrated soya meat works — it absorbs the excess liquid and makes the rolls crispy.
  • Palm-sized is the standard and it is substantial. One is a snack, two is a meal.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Single-coating.
  • A loose, under-packed ball.
  • Mashing hot potatoes.
  • Soft-boiled centres.
  • Frying in oil that is not hot enough, so the crumb absorbs it.

Health Benefits

  • Eggs give complete protein with choline, selenium and vitamin B12.
  • Potatoes add potassium, vitamin C and fibre.

  • Deep-fried, which is the limit on it.

Cultural Note

The Ugandan eggroll shares a lineage with the Indian egg chop and the British Scotch egg, and like the samosa it has become entirely local. It is sold at kiosks next to rolex stalls, and asking for an eggroll in Kampala gets you this rather than anything folded from pastry.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days. Uncooked balls keep a day refrigerated.

Reheating: A hot oven for 10 minutes. Never a microwave.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Coleslaw, Ugandan tea
