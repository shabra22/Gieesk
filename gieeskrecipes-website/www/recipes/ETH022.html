🥩 Ethiopian Sautéed Beef

ጥብስ — Tibs

Ethiopian Beef Tibs — small cubes of beef sautéed fast and hot in kibbeh with onions, tomatoes, jalapeños, rosemary, and a touch of berbere until perfectly browned outside and juicy within. Ethiopia's most popular restaurant dish — fast, intensely flavoured, and utterly satisfying.

Tibs is Ethiopia's restaurant staple — the dish ordered more than any other in Ethiopian restaurants worldwide. The technique is pure and fast: very hot kibbeh, beef cubes that sear quickly, onions, tomatoes, and the brightness of fresh jalapeño and rosemary. Done in 15 minutes, it is one of the fastest great dishes in African cooking.

Ingredients

Equipment: Cast iron pan or heavy skillet

  • 600g beef sirloin or tenderloin — cut into 2cm cubes
  • 3 tbsp kibbeh
  • 1 large onion — diced
  • 2 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 2 jalapeños — sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tsp berbere
  • 1 sprig fresh rosemary
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Heat kibbeh in a heavy pan over high heat until smoking.
  2. Add beef cubes in a single layer — do not crowd.
  3. Sear without stirring for 2 minutes. Turn. Sear other sides.
  4. Add onion. Stir and cook 2 minutes.
  5. Add garlic, jalapeño, and rosemary. Stir 1 minute.
  6. Add tomatoes. Stir. Cook 2 minutes.
  7. Add berbere, black pepper, and salt.
  8. Toss everything together for 1 more minute.
  9. The beef should be medium-done — seared outside, still juicy within.
  10. Serve immediately on injera or in the pan.

Chef Tips

  • Very hot pan is the most important step — if it doesn't sizzle aggressively when the beef goes in, the pan isn't hot enough.
  • Do not crowd — cook in batches if needed.
  • Tibs should take 12—15 minutes total — it's a fast dish.
  • Serve immediately — tibs waits for nobody.
  • Rosemary is the Ethiopian aromatic signature for tibs.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Insufficiently hot pan — beef steams instead of sears.
  • Crowding the pan.
  • Over-cooking — tibs should be juicy.
  • Delaying service.

The Science

The Maillard reaction on the beef cubes requires the surface temperature to reach 140°C — this is why the pan must be very hot before adding the beef. Any moisture on the beef surface must evaporate before browning can begin. Patting beef dry before cooking removes this moisture barrier.

Cultural Note

Tibs is Ethiopia's everyday celebration — ordered when someone feels like something special without the full commitment of a wat. It is the most ordered dish at Ethiopian restaurants worldwide, loved by Ethiopians and international guests alike for its bold, immediate flavour.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Briefly in hot kibbeh.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Ayib (ETH096), As part of an Ethiopian combination platter
