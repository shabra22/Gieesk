🔥 Poulet au Yassa, Charcoal-Grilled

Poulet au yassa

A kilo of onions to a kilo and a half of chicken — the highest onion ratio in the collection — grilled over charcoal, then braised half an hour.

130 minTotal time
25 minPrep
45 minCook
6Servings
560Calories
MediumDifficulty

Published by a Senegalese country portal under an organisation byline, and included for one reason: its onion ratio. A kilo of onions to a chicken of a kilo and a half is the most onion-forward yassa documented anywhere in this collection, and it is printed plainly. Five lemons, ten grams of chilli, one clove of garlic, five spoons of oil. The chicken marinates twenty minutes to an hour in chopped onion, garlic and lemon juice, grills over charcoal, then browns in a casserole with half the onions before water goes in for half an hour and the remaining marinade follows. The rice is steamed five to ten minutes, then cooked half an hour with the water kept level with the rice.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill, Casserole, Couscoussier or steamer, for the rice, Dish, for marinating

  • 1 chicken of 1.5 kg
  • 1 kg onions
  • 5 lemons
  • 10 g chilli
  • 1 clove garlic
  • 5 spoons oil — the spoon size is not printed
  • Salt and pepper — no quantity printed

For the rice, per 1 kg of rice

  • 1 kg rice
  • 1.5 litres water
  • Salt to taste

Water for the braise: quantity not printed

    Method

    1. Singe the cleaned and gutted chicken, cut it into pieces and place it in a dish.
    2. Finely chop the onions and garlic over the chicken, and add the lemon juice, salt and pepper.
    3. Marinate 20 minutes to 1 hour.
    4. Remove the chicken and grill it over charcoal.
    5. In a casserole heat the oil, add half the onions and the chicken, and brown.
    6. Add water and cook half an hour.
    7. Add the remaining marinade.
    8. Serve hot, with white rice served separately.

    The rice

    1. Bring 1.5 litres of water to the boil in a casserole and add salt to taste.
    2. Wash the rice and steam it 5 to 10 minutes.
    3. Pour the rice into the boiling water, keeping the water level equal to the rice level.
    4. Cook half an hour.

    Chef Tips

    • A kilo of onions to a kilo and a half of chicken. That ratio is the reason this entry exists — it is the most onion-heavy documented yassa and it tells you what the dish is really about.
    • Keeping the water level equal to the rice level is the same instruction Senegalese cooks give everywhere, phrased slightly differently. It is the single most transferable rice tip in this collection.
    • Half the onions into the casserole and the rest in with the marinade at the end, so some collapse into sauce and some stay fresher.
    • Charcoal, by the source's own method. The grilling step is where the flavour that distinguishes a Senegalese yassa from a French onion braise comes from.
    • Steam the rice five to ten minutes before it goes into the boiling water. Pre-steaming is standard in Senegalese rice cookery and this source states it plainly.
    • Be aware this source gives NO doneness cue for any step — only elapsed times and the words brown and grill. Judge by eye and use the cues from the named-author versions at Chicken Yassa to Yassa with Fish Balls and Prawns.
    • The spoon size for the five spoons of oil is not printed. Tablespoons is the reasonable reading and is offered as a reading only.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cutting the onion quantity because it looks like too much.
    • Skipping the charcoal grilling.
    • Flooding the rice rather than keeping the water level with it.
    • All the onions in at once.
    • Treating the absent cues as an invitation to guess at times.

    Cultural Note

    A country portal publishing the national dishes under its own name is a particular kind of document — not a cook's recipe but a statement of what the dish officially is. Its kilo of onions is probably the most honest thing about it, because that is the quantity a Dakar household actually uses and most printed recipes quietly reduce.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

    Reheating: Gentle covered heat.

    Serve with: White rice, served separately, Kaani, Bissap
