🍩 Tanzanian Andazi Buns

Maandazi ya Mtaani

Tanzanian Street Maandazi — the classic Tanzanian fried dough sold outside every school, mosque, market, and bus stop. Slightly sweet, lightly cardamom-scented, crispy outside and soft within. The morning snack that Tanzania's children grow up with.

Street maandazi is the morning food of Tanzania — sold in stacks by women who begin frying at 4am, bought by schoolchildren, workers, and commuters. The street version is made in large batches, cut and fried quickly, and sold warm. They are the everyday mandazi, unpretentious and beloved.

Ingredients

Equipment: Rolling pin, Deep frying pan

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 egg
  • 200ml warm milk
  • 2 tbsp butter — melted
  • Oil for deep frying

Method

  1. Mix flour, baking powder, sugar, cardamom, salt.
  2. Beat egg into warm milk. Add melted butter.
  3. Combine wet and dry ingredients. Mix to a soft dough.
  4. Knead 5 minutes. Rest covered 30 minutes.
  5. Roll to 1cm. Cut into triangles or squares.
  6. Fry at 170°C for 3—4 minutes per side until golden.
  7. Drain. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • The dough should be soft and slightly sticky.
  • Do not over-knead.
  • Fresh is everything — buy at the pan, not after sitting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-kneading — tough result.
  • Too high temperature — raw inside.

Cultural Note

Street maandazi are the food that Tanzania's schoolchildren have eaten outside the school gate for generations — bought with the few coins given for morning break, eaten hot, the sugar and cardamom the most comforting smell of childhood.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh. Store 2 days.

Reheating: Warm in oven at 150°C.

Serve with: Chai (TZA020), As a standalone, With chai ya tangawizi
