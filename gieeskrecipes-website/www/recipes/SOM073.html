🧈 Subag

Subag

Butter melted slowly, the foam skimmed off, the clear fat poured away from the milk solids and then scented with cardamom — the fat that xalwo, muqmad and canjeero are all built on.

Subag is Somali clarified butter and it sits underneath a surprising amount of the cuisine: authentic xalwo is made with it, muqmad is preserved in it, canjeero is brushed with it. It is also the ingredient most often quietly replaced with oil, because ghee is expensive — halwa shops in particular use oil to keep costs down, and the difference is obvious to anyone who has had both. Removing the milk solids is what lets it keep without refrigeration, which is the whole reason pastoral households made it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small heavy-bottomed pot, Spoon, for skimming, A second clean saucepan, Fine strainer lined with cheesecloth, Clean jar with a lid

  • 450 g (1 lb) unsalted butter

The only aromatic Somali sources attest

  • 4 green cardamom pods, lightly crushed, or 1/2 tsp ground cardamom

Method

  1. Melt the butter in a small heavy-bottomed pot over low heat, about 5 minutes. Do not rush it and do not let it brown at this stage.
  2. Skim the white foam off the surface and discard it.
  3. Let it stand a moment so the milk solids settle to the bottom, then pour the clear golden fat carefully into a second clean saucepan, leaving the solids behind.

To scent it

  1. Add the crushed cardamom and hold it over very low heat for 5 to 10 minutes, until fragrant. Do not let it brown or the cardamom catch.
  2. Strain through a cheesecloth-lined strainer into a clean jar.
  3. Cool completely at room temperature before you put the lid on, then store.

Chef Tips

  • No published Somali subag recipe exists that I could find, so the mechanics here are the universal clarified-butter ones — melt, skim, decant off the solids, infuse gently, strain. They are not in dispute; what is undocumented is the Somali spicing.
  • Cardamom is the only aromatic any Somali source attests, and it comes from the muqmad recipe rather than from a subag recipe. Somali subag is not the fenugreek-and-herb blend its Ethiopian cousin niter kibbeh is, so do not reach for that spice list.
  • Getting every last milk solid out is what makes it keep. Solids left in are what turn ghee rancid.
  • Cool it completely before sealing. Trapped steam condenses into water and water is what spoils it.
  • For preserves like muqmad, Somali practice is to cut the subag half-and-half with oil, specifically because that stops it solidifying solid in the jar.
  • Browning the butter deliberately — taking it past melting until it colours and the foam subsides — is genuine Somali technique and it gives a nuttier fat. It is how the muqmad butter is treated.
  • Keeping quality is the one thing I cannot give you a sourced figure for. A spiced, strained clarified butter is commonly given two months refrigerated; Somali accounts of meat submerged in it run from several months to over a year. Treat two months cold as the conservative answer.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Melting it over high heat, which browns the solids before you can separate them.
  • Pouring the solids in with the fat.
  • Burning the cardamom, which makes the whole batch acrid.
  • Sealing it warm.
  • Using salted butter, which makes it unusable for xalwo.

Health Benefits

  • Clarifying removes almost all the lactose and casein, so many people who cannot manage butter can manage ghee.
  • A source of fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K.
  • A high smoke point, so it fries cleanly without burning.
  • It is pure fat. Nothing about it is low-calorie.

Cultural Note

Subag is what pastoral households did with more milk than they could drink, and its whole value is that it keeps without a fridge. The FAO records the Afar churning method used in Somalia — sour milk in a skin bag, slung over the worker's shoulders and swung as they go about the day — which is as good an illustration as any of a technology built around not staying in one place.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight jar. Two months refrigerated is the conservative figure; traditional practice keeps it far longer at ambient temperature, but the documentation on that is thin.

Reheating: Warm gently to liquefy. It sets solid when cold, which is normal.

Serve with: Xalwo, Muqmad, Canjeero
