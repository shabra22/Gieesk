🐐 Kenyan Goat Stew

Mchuzi wa Mbuzi

Mchuzi wa Mbuzi is Kenya's most celebrated everyday goat dish — bone-in pieces of goat meat slowly simmered in a rich tomato, onion, and garlic sauce until completely tender and deeply flavourful. Goat stew is to Kenya what Sunday roast is to Britain — a weekly ritual of family, warmth, and nourishment.

Goat is the most widely eaten red meat in Kenya, and for good reason — it has a richer, more complex flavour than beef, develops extraordinary depth during slow cooking, and is found everywhere from the pastoral north to the coastal south. A well-made goat stew — the bones falling apart, the sauce dark and fragrant — is one of the great pleasures of Kenyan home cooking.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: National — goat has been herded and eaten across Kenya for thousands of years

Goat is the most widely consumed red meat in Kenya, valued across every community from the coastal Swahili to the pastoral Maasai, Turkana, and Somali communities. Goat stew — slow-cooked with onions, tomatoes, and spices — is one of the most fundamental preparations, present at family meals and celebrations alike.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Wooden spoon, Sharp knife

  • 1.2 kg goat meat — bone-in, cut into serving pieces
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 2 medium carrots — cut into chunks
  • 2 medium potatoes — quartered
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 cups water or stock
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Season the goat pieces with salt, pepper, and paprika.
  2. Heat oil in a large pot over high heat. Brown the goat pieces in batches — 4—5 minutes per batch. Remove and set aside.
  3. In the same pot, cook onions over medium heat until deeply golden — 10 minutes.
  4. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  5. Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 8—10 minutes until thick.
  6. Add curry powder and ground coriander. Stir 1 minute.
  7. Return the goat. Add water or stock, carrots, and potatoes.
  8. Bring to a boil, reduce to very low heat, cover, and simmer for 75—90 minutes until the goat is completely tender.
  9. Season, garnish with coriander, and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Goat requires longer cooking than beef — 90 minutes minimum for bone-in pieces.
  • Bone-in cuts give far more flavour than boneless — the marrow enriches the sauce.
  • A pressure cooker reduces cooking time to 35—40 minutes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Undercooking — goat must be completely tender, not chewy.
  • Not browning the meat — the stew will lack depth.
  • Too much water — dilutes the rich goat flavour.

The Science

Goat meat has less marbling than beef but more connective tissue in certain cuts. Long, slow simmering converts this collagen to gelatin, producing a tender result and a naturally rich sauce without added thickeners.

Cultural Note

Goat meat is deeply woven into Kenyan food culture — served at weddings, celebrations, ceremonies, and everyday family meals across every community from the coast to the highlands to the pastoral north.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on stovetop with a splash of water.

Serve with: White Ugali, Steamed Rice, Chapati, Mukimo, Sukuma Wiki, Kachumbari
