🫒 Garden Egg Abomu

Abomu

Garden eggs quartered without cutting through, steamed, mashed in a mortar — and the cue is the bubbles reducing as the oil forms a surface layer.

The garden egg version of abomu, from the same author, and it carries two things the other does not. The cutting instruction is precise: quarter the garden eggs with two lengthwise cuts that do not go all the way through, so they hold together while steaming and give up their water. And the finishing cue is the best in either recipe — after the palm oil goes in, watch for the bubbles reducing as the oil forms a surface layer. She is also explicit that mashing must be done in a mortar, because blending will not yield the same texture.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle, or asanka, Steamer or pot with a lid, Knife

  • 15 small garden eggs, or 8 large ones
  • 1 medium onion
  • 5 kpakposhito (pettie belle chilli), or preferred chilli
  • 2 medium fresh tomatoes
  • A piece of koobi (salted dry tilapia)
  • 2 stock cubes
  • 1 ladle palm oil — see the note on the unit
  • Salt

Protein, choose one

  • Sardines, grilled mackerel, fried fish or eggs

Method

  1. Cut the garden eggs in quarters — two lengthwise cuts, not all the way through.
  2. Steam them with the tomatoes, onion, pepper and koobi until tender, keeping the water.
  3. Mash in a mortar. Blending will not yield the same texture.
  4. Add the crushed stock cubes.
  5. Heat the mixture and add the palm oil.
  6. Watch for the bubbles reducing as the oil forms a surface layer.
  7. Fold in your chosen protein and serve.

Chef Tips

  • The quartering instruction matters: two lengthwise cuts that stop short of going through. The garden egg opens up to steam but stays in one piece, so it does not waterlog.
  • Bubbles reducing as the oil forms a surface layer is the finishing cue, and it is the clearest endpoint in either abomu recipe. Before that the water has not cooked off.
  • Mortar, not blender. Her own words are that blending will not yield the same texture, and she is right — the fibre needs crushing rather than cutting.
  • Keep the steaming water. It goes back in and carries the flavour of the koobi and the garden eggs.
  • A ladle of palm oil is not a standard measure and no conversion is offered. Start with two tablespoons and judge by the surface layer.
  • Fifteen small garden eggs or eight large — the source gives both, and the small ones are more bitter and more flavourful.
  • The protein is a free choice: sardines, grilled mackerel, fried fish or eggs. That flexibility is how the dish is actually eaten.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cutting the garden eggs all the way through.
  • Discarding the steaming water.
  • Using a blender.
  • Adding the oil and serving immediately, before the bubbles subside.
  • Salting before tasting, when the koobi and stock cubes are both salty.

Health Benefits

  • Garden eggs are high in fibre, folate and polyphenols, and the small bitter ones most of all.
  • Koobi and the chosen fish both supply protein, iodine and calcium.
  • Red palm oil provides provitamin-A carotenoids.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.

Cultural Note

Two abomu recipes from one writer — one of cocoyam leaves, one of garden eggs — make clear that abomu is a way of cooking rather than a particular dish: steam, mash in earthenware, finish with hot palm oil, eat from the bowl. The bubble cue is the sort of thing a cook watches for without ever naming, and it is worth having written down.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Warm gently, or serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Boiled yam, Ampesi, Fried plantain
