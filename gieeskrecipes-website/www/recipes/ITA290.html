🥙 Puccia Salentina

Puccia

A dense, chewy round bread from Salento, split open while warm and filled generously with cured meats, cheese, and vegetables, Puglia's own answer to the sandwich.

Puccia's firm, close crumb makes it especially well suited to being sliced open and stuffed, and it has become a fixture of Salento's street food scene, filled with anything from tuna and capers to grilled vegetables and local cheeses.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Baking sheet

  • 500 g (4 cups) bread flour
  • 10 g (3 tsp) active dry yeast
  • 280 ml (1⅛ cups) warm water
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • 10 g (1¾ tsp) salt
  • 80 g (½ cup) black olives, pitted and chopped (optional)
  • Prosciutto, cheese, tomatoes, and greens, for filling

Method

  1. Dissolve the yeast in the warm water and let sit for 5 minutes.
  2. Combine the flour and salt, then add the yeast mixture and olive oil, mixing into a firm dough.
  3. Knead for 8–10 minutes, then fold in the chopped olives if using.
  4. Let rise, covered, for about 1.5–2 hours until doubled.
  5. Divide into portions and shape into small, dense rounds.
  6. Let rise again for 20–30 minutes.
  7. Bake at 220°C (425°F) for 18–20 minutes until golden with a firm crust.
  8. Cool slightly, then split open and fill generously with your choice of prosciutto, cheese, tomatoes, and greens.

Chef Tips

  • This bread is meant to have a dense, chewy crumb rather than a light, airy one, which is what makes it perfect for holding a substantial filling.
  • Puccia is best filled and eaten while still slightly warm.
  • Fillings vary widely by personal preference — this bread is a versatile base for almost any combination.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overproofing the dough, which can result in too airy a crumb, losing the traditional dense texture.
  • Filling the bread while too hot, which can wilt delicate greens too quickly.
  • Underbaking, leaving the crust too soft to hold up to a hearty filling.

Cultural Note

Puccia is a beloved street food staple of Salento in southern Puglia, its dense, sturdy crumb making it the region's favored base for hearty, portable sandwiches.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store unfilled bread wrapped at room temperature for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm briefly in the oven before filling if desired.

Serve with: As a complete meal on its own
