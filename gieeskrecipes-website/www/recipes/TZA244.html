🫕 Sukuma Millet Ugali

Ugali wa Mtama — Sukuma

Sukuma Millet Ugali — stiff porridge made from finger millet (mtama) instead of maize, in the traditional Sukuma style. Darker, earthier, and more nutritious than maize ugali — the ancient grain ugali that predates the introduction of maize to East Africa.

Millet ugali is the pre-colonial ugali of the Sukuma people — finger millet (eleusine coracana) was the staple grain of East Africa long before maize arrived from the Americas. Darker, slightly sour, denser, and considerably more nutritious than maize ugali, it is making a comeback as traditional food knowledge is reclaimed.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Sturdy wooden spoon

  • 2 cups finger millet flour (or sorghum flour)
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Mix ½ cup millet flour with 1 cup cold water until smooth.
  2. Bring remaining water to a boil. Add salt.
  3. Pour flour paste into boiling water, stirring constantly.
  4. Reduce heat to medium. Keep stirring.
  5. Gradually add remaining flour, stirring vigorously to prevent lumps.
  6. Cook 20—25 minutes, stirring constantly, until very stiff and pulling away from the sides.
  7. Millet ugali takes longer than maize and requires more effort.
  8. Mould with a wet spoon into portions.

Chef Tips

  • Millet ugali is significantly harder to stir than maize — it is denser.
  • Do not stop stirring — millet sticks and burns easily.
  • It should be dark brownish-grey and very firm when done.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-cooking — must be very stiff.
  • Not stirring constantly.

The Science

Finger millet contains approximately 11% protein (vs 8% for maize) and significantly more calcium, iron, and amino acids including methionine — a limiting amino acid absent from most cereals. It also has a lower glycaemic index than maize, releasing glucose more slowly into the bloodstream.

Health Benefits

  • Higher protein than maize ugali.
  • Rich in calcium — unusual for a cereal.
  • Very high in iron.
  • Lower glycaemic index.

Cultural Note

Millet ugali is the pre-colonial staple that fed East Africa for thousands of years before maize arrived. Among the Sukuma, it remains a traditional food eaten on ceremonial occasions and by elders who prefer its richer flavour and greater nutrition.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh. Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Briefly with a splash of water.

Serve with: Dried fish stew (TZA243), Any meat or vegetable stew, Sukuma wiki (TZA106)
