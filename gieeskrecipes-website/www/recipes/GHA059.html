🫘 Every Day Waakye

Waakye

From dried beans — boiled briefly, soaked an hour, then cooked covered with the rice until both are done, watching that it does not burn.

The version made from dried beans rather than a tin, and the one most like how waakye is actually cooked in Ghana. A cup of dried black-eyed peas is boiled briefly, soaked an hour and drained, then cooked with a teaspoon of baking soda, a cup of rice, half a cup of chopped onion, a tablespoon of oil, a teaspoon of salt and two and a half cups of water, covered, until the rice and beans are cooked. Her warning is the whole difficulty of the dish: watch out that it does not burn. A full teaspoon of soda here, against half to one in the tinned version.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot with a tight lid, Colander, Wooden spoon

  • 1 cup dried black-eyed peas
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1 cup rice
  • 1/2 cup chopped onion
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 1/2 cups water

Method

  1. Boil the dried black-eyed peas briefly.
  2. Soak them 1 hour, then drain.
  3. Put the drained peas, baking soda, rice, onion, oil, salt and water into the pot.
  4. Cover and cook until the rice and beans are cooked.
  5. Watch out that it does not burn.
  6. Serve with the waakye tray.

Chef Tips

  • Watch out that it does not burn — her own warning and the reason the rice-cooker version exists. The beans release starch and the base catches easily.
  • The quick-boil-and-hour-soak is a shortcut for an overnight soak. It works and it saves the planning.
  • A full teaspoon of baking soda here, against half to one in the tinned version. Dried beans need more alkali to soften and colour.
  • Two and a half cups of water for a cup of rice plus a cup of dried beans. The beans are only partly hydrated after an hour, so they drink a good deal.
  • Drain after soaking. The soaking water carries the compounds that cause wind, and discarding it is standard practice.
  • Dried beans give a far better texture than tinned — they hold their shape and taste of something. This is the version worth the extra time.
  • No doneness cue beyond until the rice and beans are cooked, so taste a bean rather than trusting a clock.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving it unwatched, so the base burns.
  • Keeping the soaking water.
  • Using the tinned version's lower soda amount.
  • Lifting the lid repeatedly.
  • Judging doneness by the rice alone rather than the beans.

Health Benefits

  • Dried beans retain more fibre and resistant starch than tinned.
  • Rice and black-eyed peas together give a complete protein.
  • High in folate, iron and soluble fibre.
  • Discarding the soaking water reduces the oligosaccharides that cause wind.

Cultural Note

Every day waakye is the honest name for it — this is the pot that gets made at home rather than the shortcut, and the burning warning is what every Ghanaian cook knows without being told. The alkaline soda doing the work that millet leaves do in a village pot is the one genuine substitution in the recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Steam, or microwave with a splash of water.

Serve with: Shito, Waakye Stew, Gari and boiled egg
