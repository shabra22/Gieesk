🥛 Maize Meal Porridge

Obuugi

Maize flour slaked with two cups of cold water before it ever meets heat, stirred continuously until it bubbles, then finished with milk and thinned with hot water as needed.

Obuugi is the everyday Ugandan porridge and the first solid food a great many Ugandan children eat. It is also the clearest demonstration of the one rule that governs every flour-and-water dish in the country: the flour goes into cold water first, off the heat, mixed smooth. Put maize flour into hot water and you get lumps you will never get out. Milk goes in once it bubbles, and hot water — never cold — is what thins it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Wooden spoon, Kettle

  • 90 ml (6 heaped tbsp) maize meal flour
  • 480 ml (2 cups) cold water
  • 240 ml (1 cup) hot water
  • 100 ml milk
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) sugar

Method

  1. Pour the maize flour into the saucepan.
  2. Add 1 cup of cold water and mix smooth.
  3. Add the second cup of cold water and mix again.
  4. Turn the heat on and put the pan on the stove.
  5. Mix gently and continuously with a wooden spoon until it starts bubbling.
  6. Add the milk and mix well.
  7. Add the hot water bit by bit as you stir, if it is too thick.
  8. Add the sugar and mix well.
  9. Serve immediately — let it cool before serving to children.
  10. Fresh fruit on top is good, if you want it.

Chef Tips

  • Cold water first, off the heat, mixed smooth. This is the whole recipe and it applies to every flour porridge in Uganda.
  • Continuous gentle stirring until it bubbles. Not vigorous — you are preventing a skin and a stuck bottom, not whipping it.
  • Hot water to thin it, never cold. Cold water dropped into a hot porridge makes it seize into lumps.
  • The milk goes in after it bubbles. Added at the start it can catch and curdle.
  • Bubbling is the doneness cue. There is no time to watch.
  • Cool it before giving it to a child. It is a weaning food and it comes off the stove far hotter than it looks.
  • It works as an alternative to oats, and holds fruit well.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the flour to hot water.
  • Skipping the smooth mix and hoping stirring will fix it later.
  • Thinning with cold water.
  • Adding the milk at the start.
  • Serving it to children straight off the heat.

Health Benefits

  • An easily digested source of carbohydrate and energy.
  • Milk adds calcium, protein and vitamin B12.
  • Gluten-free and very low in fat.
  • Widely used as a weaning food, which is what the smooth texture is for.

Cultural Note

Obuugi is so ordinary in Uganda that nobody thinks of it as a recipe, which is exactly why the technique is worth writing down. The cold-slaking rule is the same one that governs posho, millet porridge and every other flour dish, and learning it once solves all of them.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best fresh. Refrigerate up to 24 hours.

Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of hot water, stirring.

Serve with: Fresh fruit, Mandazi, Roasted groundnuts
