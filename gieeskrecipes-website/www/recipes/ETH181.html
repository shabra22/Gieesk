🥞 Ethiopian Pancake (Serdo)

ሰርዶ — Serdo

Ethiopian Serdo — a thick, slightly sweet wheat pancake with cardamom and egg, cooked in a small amount of oil until golden. The Ethiopian street pancake — softer than Mulmul, cooked on both sides unlike injera, served with honey.

Serdo fills the gap between injera (fermented, complex) and plain pancake (simple, neutral) — a slightly sweet, cardamom-fragrant thick pancake that is the street breakfast of Ethiopia's urban centres.

Ingredients

Equipment: Non-stick pan

  • 1.5 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 egg
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 cup milk or water
  • 1 tbsp oil

Oil for cooking

    Method

    1. Whisk flour, egg, sugar, cardamom, baking powder, and salt.
    2. Add milk gradually — smooth, thick batter.
    3. Heat pan. Add a little oil.
    4. Pour ¼ cup batter. Cook 3—4 minutes until bubbles appear on surface.
    5. Flip. Cook 2 more minutes.
    6. Serve with honey.

    Chef Tips

    • Thicker than a Western pancake — more like a drop scone.
    • Wait for bubbles on the surface before flipping.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Flipping too early — sticks.

    The Science

    The baking powder in Serdo (unlike Mulmul, which uses yeast) provides an immediate CO2 release when mixed with the liquid and acid in the batter — producing a less complex but faster rise than yeast fermentation.

    Cultural Note

    Serdo is the everyday pancake of Ethiopian street breakfast — not as elaborate as injera, not as austere as Kita, it occupies the comfortable middle ground of Ethiopian morning food culture.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately.

    Reheating: Briefly in a dry pan.

    Serve with: With honey, With chai, As a street breakfast
