🍬 Groundnut Toffee

Groundnut "cakes" — toffee, Ghana-style

Half a cup of sugar with two tablespoons of water, cooked on medium WITHOUT STIRRING until it turns brown — then the nuts go in off the heat.

The Ghanaian toffee, and its method differs from brittle in one important way: the sugar is cooked with water and is not stirred at all once it is dissolved. Three quarters of a cup of dry roasted unsalted peanuts are coarsely crushed between two sheets of waxed paper. Half a cup of sugar and two tablespoons of water go into a saucepan and are stirred briefly on medium-high just until the sugar is mostly dissolved, then left on MEDIUM WITHOUT STIRRING until the mixture turns brown — probably around ten minutes. Off the heat, the nuts are stirred in quickly and the toffee is turned onto a wetted board and pressed flat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan — one source reading gives 2 quart, Rolling pin or meat tenderiser, and waxed paper or a bag, Glass cutting board or a lasagne or cake pan, Spatula, spoon or knife, Cookie sheet, for the coconut version

  • 3/4 cup dry roasted unsalted peanuts
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • 2 tbsp water

For the coconut version: 3/4 cup dried, unsweetened flaked or grated coconut in place of the peanuts, toasted first.

    Water or a little margarine or butter, for the board and the spatula

      Method

      1. Coarsely crush the peanuts — easiest between 2 pieces of waxed paper or in a plastic or paper bag — with a rolling pin or other heavy object like a meat tenderiser.
      2. Wet a glass cutting board or pan, like a lasagne or cake pan, with a little water and set it aside.
      3. Also wet the spatula, spoon or knife, or rub a little margarine or butter on it, and set it aside too.
      4. Put the water and sugar into the saucepan and briefly stir on medium-high heat just until the sugar is mostly dissolved.
      5. Turn it to medium and let it continue cooking on MEDIUM WITHOUT STIRRING it at all until the mixture turns brown — probably around 10 minutes.
      6. When it is a nice golden brown, remove the pan from the heat, turn off the stove, and quickly stir in the nuts.
      7. Immediately turn the toffee onto the wet cutting board and use the prepared spatula or knife to press it flat.
      8. As the candy cools it will harden. Break it into pieces, or score it while still warm into squares, diamonds or triangles and break them off when hard, or take small spoonfuls of the warm — but not hot — candy and roll it into balls.

      For the coconut version: preheat an oven to 350 degrees Fahrenheit, spread the coconut on a cookie sheet and toast it, shaking the pan every couple of minutes. It will probably take only 4 to 6 minutes to lightly toast. Then follow the same caramel method, adding the toasted coconut in place of the peanuts.

        Chef Tips

        • WITHOUT STIRRING is the instruction she capitalised, and it is the difference between toffee and a grainy mess. Once the sugar is dissolved, leave the spoon out of it.
        • Wet the board and the spatula BEFORE you start the sugar. There is no time to find them once the nuts are in.
        • A nice golden brown, at around ten minutes. The time is a guide; the colour is the cue.
        • The warm-but-not-hot window is the only time you can roll it into balls. Miss it and you are breaking shards instead.
        • Score it while warm if you want neat squares or diamonds. Scoring does nothing once it has set.
        • The water is what makes this toffee rather than brittle — two tablespoons, and it lets the sugar dissolve evenly before it colours.
        • The coconut version uses the identical caramel: four to six minutes of toasting at 350, shaking every couple of minutes, then into the same syrup.

        Common Mistakes to Avoid

        • Stirring the syrup while it colours.
        • Preparing the board after starting the sugar.
        • Waiting for dark brown, by which point it is bitter.
        • Trying to roll balls from hot candy, or from set candy.
        • Scoring it after it has hardened.

        Cultural Note

        Every West African country makes a version of sugar and nuts, and the Ghanaian one is called toffee whether or not it contains any dairy. The author's line about it — that Ghanaians use available ingredients to make simple candies — is the whole story of a sweet with three ingredients and no equipment.

        Storage & Serving

        Storage: 2 weeks in an airtight tin once completely cool. Humidity makes it sticky.

        Reheating: Not applicable.

        Serve with: Bissap, Sobolo, Atwemo
