🥛 Saykouk (Barley Couscous with Whey)

Sekouk belboula — milky barley couscous (سكوك)

A cooling summer bowl of steamed barley couscous topped with cold whey (lben) and a little sugar.

1h 15mTotal time
15mPrep
1hCook
3Servings
360Calories
EasyDifficulty

Barley semolina is steamed three times in an oiled, salted dish, separating the grains between rounds. Once cooled it is put in a dish, covered with cold whey and sweetened with sugar if you like. Morocco World News recommends it in hot weather.

Ingredients

Equipment: Couscoussier or steamer, Large dish

  • 250g barley couscous semolina
  • 1/2L of whey (Lben)
  • 1 tablespoon crystal sugar

Method

  1. Steam the barley semolina in a large, oiled, salted dish. Once the steam crosses the semolina, return it to a bowl, add a little warm water and separate the grains.
  2. Steam a second time for 30 minutes, then a third time (no duration is printed for the first or third).
  3. Cool the couscous completely.
  4. Put the cooled couscous in a dish, pour cold whey over it, and add sugar if you like.

Chef Tips

  • The page gives only one cue: steam has crossed the semolina.
  • Serve cold, in the heat of the day.
  • Cooking with Alia swells the grain in boiling water for 10 minutes instead of steaming, until it has absorbed all the water and become plump; the two methods differ.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not separating the grains between steamings.
  • Serving the whey warm.
  • Adding sugar before tasting the whey.

Health Benefits

  • Barley provides fibre; whey adds protein and probiotics when unpasteurised.
  • Light, low-fat meal for hot days.

Cultural Note

Morocco World News writes that in hot weather 'nothing beats' a bowl of saykouk; Cooking with Alia adds that it is traditionally made with barley couscous.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best fresh; the whey softens the grain within an hour.

Reheating: Not reheated; serve cold.
