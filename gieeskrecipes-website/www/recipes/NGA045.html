🥘 Banga Soup, Delta Version

Banga

Fifteen hundred grams of palm nut extract diluted with double its quantity of hot water, with an oburunbebe stick in the pot that comes out at the end.

This is the tinned-extract route, and it gives the one printed dilution ratio in the whole banga record: palm nut extract diluted with double its quantity of hot water. The oburunbebe stick goes in with the spice and the crayfish, infuses for the rest of the cook, and is pulled out before serving. The warning on the page is worth heeding — too much banga spice will make the soup really bitter, so start with one tablespoon of the two. What marks the Delta version off from the Igbo one, the author says, is the absence of ugu and scent leaf.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Wooden spoon

  • 1500 g palm nut extract
  • Hot water, double the quantity of the extract
  • 1 medium catfish, or any preferred fresh fish
  • Assorted meats of your choice
  • 2-3 medium pieces stockfish (panla, okporoko)
  • 1-2 medium dried fish
  • 1/2 cup fresh or smoked shrimps (optional)
  • 1 tbsp ground dried crayfish or prawns
  • 1-2 tbsp banga spice
  • 1-2 tbsp crushed obeletientien leaves, or dried bitter leaves
  • 1 oburunbebe stick
  • 2 scotch bonnet (atarodo)
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 2 tbsp dried pepper (atagungun)
  • 1/2 cup periwinkles
  • Bouillon cubes
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Gut, clean and wash the fish thoroughly. Use salt to remove catfish slime, or the freeze-and-thaw method.
  2. Boil the meats with the chopped onions, a bouillon cube, salt and seasoning until tender but not soft.
  3. Add the dried stockfish and the blended scotch bonnet, and boil until the stockfish is tender.
  4. Add the smoked fish, switch off the heat and let it simmer 2 to 3 minutes.
  5. Transfer the palm nut extract to a pot and dilute it with hot water, nearly double its quantity. Include the meat stock.
  6. Place on medium heat and mix thoroughly to break up any clumps. Do not cover the pot.
  7. Boil 10 to 12 minutes.
  8. Add the banga spice — start with 1 tablespoon — the oburunbebe stick, the ground crayfish, dried pepper, bouillon and salt. Cook 5 minutes.
  9. Add the fresh catfish and shrimps, reduce to low heat, and cook 7 to 10 minutes.
  10. Blend the bitter leaves, add to the soup and stir. Cook 2 minutes, then switch off the heat.
  11. Let it rest 2 to 3 minutes on the residual heat.
  12. Remove the oburunbebe stick before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Double the quantity of hot water to the extract. That is the only printed banga dilution ratio in any source consulted — 1500 g of extract to roughly three litres.
  • Start with one tablespoon of banga spice, not two. The author is explicit: be really careful, too much will make your banga soup really bitter.
  • Do not cover the pot. Same instruction as every other Delta banga and owo source.
  • Take the oburunbebe stick out at the end. It is an infusion, not an ingredient, and it keeps giving bitterness if left in.
  • Hot water, not cold, for diluting the extract. Cold water makes the concentrate seize into lumps that will not break up.
  • Tender but not soft on the meats. They carry on cooking in the palm sauce for another twenty minutes.
  • What makes it the Delta version rather than the Igbo one: no ugu, no scent leaf. Ofe akwu adds them.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Diluting the concentrate with cold water.
  • Using the full two tablespoons of spice at the start.
  • Leaving the oburunbebe stick in the served soup.
  • Covering the pot.
  • Cooking the meats until they are falling apart before the palm sauce stage.

Health Benefits

  • Palm nut pulp is rich in carotenoids and tocotrienols.
  • Periwinkle and catfish are both very lean proteins.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.
  • High in iodine and selenium from the mixed seafood.

Cultural Note

The oburunbebe stick is the detail that most clearly marks banga out. It is a piece of woody stem that goes into the pot to infuse and comes out before serving — not an ingredient you eat, and not something any other Nigerian soup uses. One source says plainly that without the stick and the other banga spices you have a palm nut stew rather than banga, which is a strong claim for a piece of bark.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 6 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat, lid off.

Serve with: Starch, Iyan, Eba or garri

  • THE DILUTION RATIO, as printed and the only one found anywhere: dilute the palm extract 'with hot water (nearly double quantity)'. For 1500 g of extract that is roughly 3 litres.
  • Times as printed: smoked fish then switch off and simmer 2 to 3 minutes; boil the diluted extract 10 to 12 minutes; spice, stick, crayfish and seasoning 5 minutes; fresh fish and shrimps 7 to 10 minutes on low; blended bitter leaves 2 minutes; rest 2 to 3 minutes on residual heat; remove the stick.
  • THE SPICE WARNING, verbatim: 'Be really careful when adding Banga spice...too much will make your Banga soup really bitter.' And 'Do not cover pot' is also as printed.
  • THE REGIONAL DISTINCTION, as printed: 'The Igbo version is called Ofe Akwu which includes the addition of Ugu or Scent leaves which is without in the Delta version.' See Ofe Akwu.
  • NOTE: 'Assorted Meats of your choice' and 'Bouillon cubes' carry no quantities on the page. INGREDIENT/METHOD MISMATCH: the list offers 'Crushed Obeletientien leaves OR Dried bitter leaves' but step 10 instructs only to blend bitter leaves, so the beletientien branch has no corresponding method step — and 'crushed' in the list conflicts with 'blend' in the method.
  • A TENSION IN HER OWN RULE, recorded: she says the Delta version is without ugu or scent leaf, yet she herself lists obeletientien or dried bitter leaves, the Kitchen Butterfly banga includes a teaspoon of bitterleaf, and Nigerian Food TV permits scent leaf as a substitute for obeletientien. Her rule excludes ugu and scent leaf specifically, not all leaves — but Nigerian Food TV's substitution would violate it.
  • ON THE STICK'S NAME: 'oburunbebe stick' here and in Nigerian Food TV; 'obunrubebe stick' in Dunni Obata's version; part of the ground blend in the Itsekiri account; and 'Banga stick' or 'banga spice stick' on the Kitchen Butterfly page. No source quantifies it beyond 'one' or 'optional'.
  • ON THE SPICE BLEND'S COMPONENTS, four overlapping but non-identical sets with inconsistent transliteration and none fully quantified: atariko plus rigije plus belentientien (Kitchen Butterfly); Aidan fruit plus Rohojie (Nigerian Food TV, which glosses banga spice as 'Aidan fruit+Rohojie'); beletete plus oburunbebe plus ataiko plus irugeje (Itsekiri National Congress); and Uyayak or Aidan fruit plus Rogojie plus Tyko plus Obunrubebe stick (Dunni Obata, BellaNaija), who also offers pre-packed banga spices at 2 tablespoons as the shortcut.
