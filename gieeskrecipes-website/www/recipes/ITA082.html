🍮 Panna Cotta con Coulis di Frutti di Bosco

Panna Cotta

This silky Piedmontese 'cooked cream' sets into a delicate, barely-wobbling custard without any eggs, finished here with a bright, tart berry coulis to cut its richness.

Panna cotta's simplicity is deceptive — just cream, sugar, vanilla, and gelatin — but achieving its characteristic delicate wobble, firm enough to unmold yet soft enough to yield to a spoon, is a genuine test of technique and restraint.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small saucepan, Ramekins or small molds, Blender or fine sieve

  • 600 ml (2½ cups) heavy cream
  • 100 g (½ cup) granulated sugar
  • 1 vanilla bean, split and scraped (or 2 tsp vanilla extract)
  • 3 sheets gelatin (or 1½ tsp powdered gelatin)
  • 250 g (2 cups) mixed berries (strawberries, raspberries, blueberries)
  • 3 tbsp granulated sugar, for the coulis
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

Method

  1. Soak the gelatin sheets in cold water for 5 minutes until softened.
  2. Warm the cream, sugar, and vanilla bean (seeds and pod) in a saucepan over medium heat, stirring until the sugar dissolves — do not let it boil.
  3. Remove from the heat, squeeze out the softened gelatin, and whisk it into the warm cream until fully dissolved.
  4. Strain the mixture to remove the vanilla pod, then divide among ramekins or molds.
  5. Refrigerate for at least 4 hours, or until fully set.
  6. For the coulis, blend the berries with the sugar and lemon juice, then pass through a fine sieve to remove seeds.
  7. To serve, briefly dip each mold in warm water and invert onto a plate, or serve directly in the ramekin, topped with the berry coulis.

Chef Tips

  • Never let the cream boil — this can affect the texture and cause the gelatin to set unevenly.
  • The classic panna cotta wobble comes from using just enough gelatin to hold its shape — err on the side of slightly less rather than more.
  • If unmolding, dip briefly in warm (not hot) water and run a thin knife around the edge to release it cleanly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling the cream, which can cause a skin to form or affect how the gelatin sets.
  • Using too much gelatin, resulting in a rubbery texture rather than the delicate, barely-set wobble that defines good panna cotta.
  • Unmolding before it is fully set, causing it to collapse rather than hold its shape.

Health Benefits

  • The berry coulis adds vitamin C and antioxidants to balance the rich cream base.

Cultural Note

Panna cotta hails from Piedmont in Italy's northwest, a region also known for its rich dairy tradition, and has become one of the most widely adapted Italian desserts internationally.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, covered, for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Serve chilled; this dessert is not reheated.

Serve with: Fresh berries, A drizzle of honey as an alternative to the coulis
