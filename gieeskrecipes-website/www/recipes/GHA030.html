🐟 Fante Fante

Fante fante — fresh fish stew

The tilapia is steamed dry in its own juices for ten minutes and only then joins the sauce — so it holds together instead of breaking up.

Fante fante is the fresh fish stew of the Fante coast, from Cape Coast and Elmina, and the technique that makes it work is cooking the fish separately. Whole tilapia in large chunks is marinated in a blend of onion, ginger, garlic and chilli, then steamed in a dry pan over low heat, covered, for ten minutes in its own juices and flipped gently. Meanwhile koobi fries in half a cup of hot palm oil, the blended tomato and remaining chilli paste go in, and it simmers covered ten minutes until reduced. Okra and the fish join for five minutes at the end.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Heavy pan with a lid, Dry pan, for steaming the fish, Knife

  • 1 whole tilapia, cut into large chunks
  • 5 fresh tomatoes, roughly chopped
  • 1 whole white onion
  • 7 large green chilli peppers
  • 2 large ginger pieces
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 koobi (salted fish), soaked and cleaned
  • 4 okra fingers
  • 1/2 cup palm oil
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Blend half the onion, the ginger, garlic and 4 of the chilli pieces, and marinate the fish in it with salt.
  2. Heat the palm oil and fry the koobi.
  3. Blend the tomatoes with the remaining onion and chilli paste and add to the pan.
  4. Cover and simmer 10 minutes, until reduced.
  5. Meanwhile steam the tilapia in a dry pan over low heat, covered, 10 minutes in its own juices. Flip gently.
  6. Slice the okra and the remaining chilli into the sauce.
  7. Add the tilapia and cook 5 minutes.
  8. Serve.

Chef Tips

  • Steam the fish dry and separately. A dry pan, low heat, lid on, ten minutes in its own juices — it firms up enough to survive going into the sauce.
  • Flip gently, once. Tilapia chunks fall apart if turned more than that.
  • Seven green chillies for two servings is a lot, and they are split — four into the marinade, the rest into the sauce. Green chilli rather than scotch bonnet gives a grassier heat.
  • Fry the koobi in the palm oil before the tomatoes. Salted fish in hot oil is the flavour base of the whole stew.
  • Until reduced is the sauce cue at ten minutes covered. It should be thick enough to coat the fish rather than swim it.
  • Four okra fingers go in near the end with the last chilli. They thicken slightly and add their own texture without taking over.
  • Soak and clean the koobi first. It is heavily salted and will otherwise make the whole stew inedible.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking the tilapia in the sauce from raw, so it disintegrates.
  • Turning the fish repeatedly while it steams.
  • Skipping the koobi soak.
  • All seven chillies into the marinade.
  • Adding the okra at the start, so it collapses.

Health Benefits

  • Tilapia is lean, high in protein and low in mercury.
  • Okra contributes soluble fibre, folate and vitamin K.
  • Red palm oil supplies provitamin-A carotenoids.
  • Green chillies are high in vitamin C.

Cultural Note

Cape Coast and Elmina are fishing towns and fante fante is what you eat there when the catch comes in — the whole dish is built around not ruining a fresh fish. That it is named simply for the people who make it suggests how central it is to them.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. Fresh fish stew does not keep well.

Reheating: Gentle heat, briefly. The fish will break up if stirred.

Serve with: Banku, Kenkey, Boiled yam
