🫔 Tanzanian Chapati Roll

Mkeka wa Chapati

Tanzanian Chapati Roll — a warm chapati filled with spiced beef or chicken, kachumbari, pili pili sauce, and coriander, rolled up and served immediately. The portable Tanzanian lunch — everything in one hand.

The chapati roll is the Tanzanian answer to the burrito — a warm chapati loaded with spiced meat, fresh salad, and sauce, rolled up and eaten on the move. It is sold at every bus station, office district, and market.

Ingredients

Equipment: None — just a board

  • 2 freshly made chapatis
  • 150g spiced minced beef or chicken — warm

Fillings

  • ¼ cup kachumbari
  • 2 tbsp pili pili
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander
  • 1 green chilli — sliced
  • Salt

Method

  1. Warm chapatis briefly in a dry pan.
  2. Lay flat. Spread mayonnaise across the centre.
  3. Add warm spiced meat along the centre.
  4. Top with kachumbari, chilli, and coriander.
  5. Add pili pili to taste.
  6. Fold in the sides. Roll up tightly.
  7. Wrap in paper. Eat immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The chapati must be warm — cold chapati tears and does not roll.
  • Do not overfill — rolling becomes messy.
  • The pili pili goes in based on the customer's preference.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cold chapati.
  • Overfilling.

The Science

A warm chapati is more plastic (flexible) than a cold one — gluten strands remain relaxed and extensible at higher temperatures, allowing the chapati to roll without cracking. Below approximately 40°C, the starch granules re-crystallise and the chapati becomes brittle.

Cultural Note

The chapati roll is Dar es Salaam's office lunch — fast, portable, and satisfying. It has become the unofficial street food of Tanzania's urban working class, eaten standing at the vendor's counter or walking back to the office.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: As a standalone, With extra pili pili, With cold drink
