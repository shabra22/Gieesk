🍚 Ofe Akwu

Ofe akwu — Igbo palm nut soup

The Igbo banga — same palm nut base, but with scent leaf and ugu added, and served over boiled rice rather than with a swallow.

Ofe akwu is the Igbo version of banga and the two differences are exactly what the author names: the leaves and the accompaniment. A quarter cup of scent leaf and half a cup of ugu go in near the end, which the Delta version deliberately leaves out, and the whole thing is eaten with boiled white rice rather than with starch or a swallow. The technique detail worth having is that the fresh fish is cooked five or six minutes, lifted out so it does not break up, and returned for the last two minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Blender, Slotted spoon

  • 600 g assorted meats
  • 1 medium fresh fish
  • 2-3 stockfish pieces
  • 1 tin (400 g) palm nut extract
  • 1/4 cup scent leaves (basil, efinrin)
  • 1/2 cup ugu leaves
  • 2 tbsp ground crayfish
  • 2 bouillon cubes
  • Chicken or beef seasoning
  • Small chunk ogiri okpei or dawadawa
  • 3-4 scotch bonnet
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Season and boil the meats until tender, adding the stockfish as they go.
  2. Blend the scotch bonnets and the ogiri okpei or dawadawa with a little water, add to the pot, and simmer 2 minutes.
  3. Combine the palm nut extract with the meat stock on low heat and adjust the seasoning.
  4. Add the fresh fish and cook 5 to 6 minutes, then remove it to prevent it breaking.
  5. Return the cooked meats with the crayfish and cook 5 minutes.
  6. Add the scent leaves.
  7. Return the fish and cook 2 minutes.
  8. Add the ugu.
  9. Take it off the heat and let it stand 2 to 3 minutes, so the oil settles on the surface.
  10. Serve with boiled rice.

Chef Tips

  • Scent leaf and ugu are what make it Igbo rather than Delta. The author states the distinction outright and it is the cleanest regional marker in the banga family.
  • Lift the fish out after its first five or six minutes and put it back at the end. It is the only way to serve it whole.
  • Boiled white rice, not a swallow. Ofe akwu over rice is the standard pairing in the southeast.
  • Let it stand two or three minutes off the heat so the oil settles on top. That glossy orange surface is what a finished ofe akwu looks like.
  • A 400 g tin is the practical route. For the from-scratch palm nut cream see Palm Nut Cream from Scratch.
  • No oburunbebe stick here, and no banga spice blend. That is the other difference from the Delta versions — the Igbo soup leans on ogiri and scent leaf instead.
  • Ogiri okpei or dawadawa, a small chunk, blended with the peppers. It dissolves completely and you will not see it in the finished soup.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the fresh fish in for the whole cook.
  • Omitting the scent leaf, which makes it a Delta banga.
  • Serving it with a swallow rather than rice.
  • Stirring after the fish goes back in.
  • Adding banga spice, which belongs to the Delta version.

Health Benefits

  • Palm nut pulp is rich in carotenoids and tocotrienols.
  • Scent leaf carries aromatic oils with antimicrobial activity.
  • Ugu supplies iron, calcium and fibre.
  • Very low in carbohydrate before the rice.

Cultural Note

That one soup is called banga in the Delta, ofe akwu in Igboland and obe eyin in Yorubaland — and that each community argues for its own spices, leaves and accompaniment — makes the palm nut soup family a good map of southern Nigeria. The ingredients barely change. What changes is the aromatics and what you eat it with, and those are the things people are most attached to.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat. Add the greens fresh if you can.

Serve with: Boiled white rice, Eba or garri, Boiled yam
