🌰 Corzetti al Sugo di Noci

Corzetti al Sugo di Noci

Corzetti are Liguria's coin-shaped pasta, traditionally stamped with an ornately carved wooden press bearing a family or town crest, served here with a creamy Ligurian walnut sauce.

The carved wooden stamps used to shape corzetti have historically been passed down as family heirlooms, each disc pressed with its own unique decorative pattern — a beautiful marriage of craft and cuisine unique to this small corner of Liguria.

Ingredients

Equipment: Corzetti stamp (or a round cutter and fork for a simpler version), Rolling pin, Food processor, Large pot

    Corzetti dough

    • 400 g (3⅓ cups) all-purpose flour
    • 2 eggs
    • 100 ml (⅓ cup + 1 tbsp) dry white wine
    • Pinch of salt

    Walnut sauce

    • 150 g (1½ cups) walnuts
    • 1 garlic clove
    • 60 g (¼ cup) ricotta or mascarpone
    • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
    • 40 g (½ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
    • A few fresh marjoram or basil leaves
    • Salt, to taste

    Method

    1. Combine the flour, eggs, white wine, and salt into a smooth, firm dough. Knead for 8–10 minutes. Rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
    2. Roll the dough thin, about 2–3mm.
    3. Use a corzetti stamp to cut and imprint each disc in one motion (or use a round cutter followed by lightly pressing a decorative pattern with a fork).
    4. Lay the shaped corzetti on a floured tray to dry slightly while you make the sauce.
    5. Pulse the walnuts, garlic, ricotta, olive oil, and Parmigiano in a food processor until a thick, coarse sauce forms. Season with salt.
    6. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the corzetti for 3–4 minutes until tender and floating.
    7. Reserve some pasta water, then drain and toss the pasta with the walnut sauce, loosening with pasta water as needed.
    8. Garnish with marjoram or basil and serve immediately.

    Chef Tips

    • If a traditional stamp isn't available, a simple round cutter with a decorative fork-pressed pattern gives a similar rustic charm.
    • Toasting the walnuts lightly before processing deepens their flavor considerably.
    • The ricotta or mascarpone gives the sauce body without needing a heavy cream base.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Over-processing the walnut sauce into a completely smooth paste, losing its pleasant rustic texture.
    • Rolling the dough too thick, which obscures the decorative stamped pattern.
    • Skipping the wine in the dough, which contributes to corzetti's distinct flavor and texture.

    Cultural Note

    Corzetti stamps were historically carved by local artisans and often bore a family's coat of arms, making each set of pasta discs a small, edible piece of Ligurian heraldry and heritage.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Uncooked corzetti can be dried and stored at room temperature; the sauce keeps refrigerated for up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Best prepared fresh; the walnut sauce is served at or near room temperature rather than heated.

    Serve with: A crisp Ligurian white wine such as Vermentino, A simple green salad
