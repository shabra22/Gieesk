🍲 Carbonada Valdostana

Carbonade

Beef slow-braised in red wine with onions until deeply tender, historically made from salted beef preserved for the long Alpine winters of Valle d'Aosta.

Carbonada was traditionally made using beef that had been salted and preserved to last through winter months when fresh meat was scarce in the high valleys, and the dish's rich, wine-dark sauce remains one of the region's most comforting classics.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl for marinating, Heavy-bottomed pot with lid

  • 1 kg (2.2 lb) beef chuck, cut into chunks
  • 3 large onions, sliced
  • 500 ml (2 cups) robust red wine
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 sprig rosemary
  • Flour, for dredging
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the beef with the wine, bay leaves, and rosemary in a bowl, cover, and marinate in the refrigerator for at least 12 hours.
  2. Remove the beef, reserving the marinade, and pat dry.
  3. Season the beef and dredge lightly in flour.
  4. Warm the olive oil in a heavy pot and brown the beef well on all sides, then set aside.
  5. In the same pot, sauté the onions until deeply softened and beginning to caramelize, about 15–20 minutes.
  6. Return the beef to the pot and pour in the reserved marinade.
  7. Cover and simmer gently for 1.5–2 hours, until the meat is very tender and the sauce has thickened.
  8. Season to taste and serve hot with plenty of sauce.

Chef Tips

  • The long marinade genuinely improves the final flavor and tenderness — don't skip or rush it.
  • Let the onions cook long and slow until truly caramelized, which builds a naturally sweet base for the sauce.
  • This dish is even better reheated the next day, once the flavors have had time to settle.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the onions, missing the deep sweetness that slow caramelization provides.
  • Skipping the marinating step, which misses much of the dish's flavor development.
  • Cooking at too high a heat, which can toughen the beef rather than tenderizing it.

Cultural Note

Carbonada Valdostana reflects Valle d'Aosta's tradition of preserving meat for the long Alpine winter, and remains one of the region's most cherished cold-weather dishes.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days; flavor improves the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop or in the oven, covered.

Serve with: Creamy polenta, Mashed potatoes
