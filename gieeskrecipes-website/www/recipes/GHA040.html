🥛 Iced Kenkey

Ice kenkey

Leftover kenkey blended with ice-cold water, sugar and evaporated milk to about the consistency of rice water.

Iced kenkey is what happens to yesterday's kenkey, and it is one of the best cheap street drinks in West Africa — a cold, sour, milky, faintly grainy thing sold from coolers all over Ghana. A cup of leftover kenkey goes into a blender with half a cup of ice-cold water, a tablespoon of sugar and a third of a cup of evaporated milk, and the target is about the consistency of rice water. No cooking, no fermenting: the sourness is already there from the kenkey. Her own note is that it is an acquired taste for Westerners.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Glass or cup, Sieve (optional)

  • 1 cup kenkey, leftover
  • 1/2 cup ice-cold water
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1/3 cup evaporated milk

Method

  1. Break up the leftover kenkey.
  2. Blend it with the ice-cold water.
  3. Add the sugar and the evaporated milk.
  4. Blend until it reaches about the consistency of rice water.
  5. Pour and serve cold.

Chef Tips

  • About the consistency of rice water is the cue — pourable and slightly thick, not a smoothie and not a drink you could mistake for milk.
  • Ice-cold water, not room temperature. It is served cold and there is no cooking stage to lose heat from.
  • Use kenkey that is a day old. Fresh warm kenkey is for eating; the leftover ball is what this drink exists for.
  • Evaporated milk rather than fresh. It is sweeter and thicker and it is what is actually used in Ghana.
  • Sieve it if you want it smooth. Street versions are left slightly grainy and that is part of the character.
  • Her honest note, worth passing on: it is an acquired taste for Westerners. Sour fermented corn, milk and sugar is an unfamiliar combination if you did not grow up with it.
  • No fermentation step here at all — the sourness comes ready-made in the kenkey, which is why this entry needs no fermentation cue of its own.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using warm fresh kenkey.
  • Room-temperature water.
  • Blending it to a thin liquid or a thick paste rather than rice-water consistency.
  • Fresh milk instead of evaporated.
  • Over-sweetening — a tablespoon for two servings is the printed amount.

Health Benefits

  • Carries the lactic acid bacteria of the fermented kenkey it is made from.
  • Evaporated milk adds calcium, protein and vitamin D.
  • Rehydrating and cooling in hot weather.
  • Uses up leftovers that would otherwise be wasted.

Cultural Note

Iced kenkey is sold from insulated flasks by hawkers and in sachets from shops, and it is the clearest example in Ghanaian food of nothing going to waste — yesterday's staple becomes today's cold drink. The sourness that makes it strange to outsiders is exactly what makes it refreshing in Accra heat.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Same day. It separates and sours further.

Reheating: Not applicable. Serve cold.

Serve with: Drunk on its own, cold, Bofrot, Nkate cake
