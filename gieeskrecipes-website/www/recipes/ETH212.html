ቡና — Buna

Ethiopian Buna — strong coffee brewed in a clay jebena, served black in small cups with sugar on the side. The everyday version of the coffee ceremony — the morning ritual of millions of Ethiopians. With salt in Harrar, with sugar everywhere else, with butter in some highland communities.

Ethiopian everyday coffee is the simplified ceremony — the jebena on the charcoal, the ground coffee, the patient brewing, and the small cup. It is the first thing done every morning in millions of Ethiopian homes, the punctuation mark of every social visit.

Ingredients

Equipment: Jebena (clay coffee pot) or small saucepan, Small cups (sini)

  • 3 tbsp medium-coarse ground Ethiopian arabica coffee
  • 600ml cold water
  • Sugar to taste

Optional: pinch of cardamom, pinch of salt (Harrar tradition), 1 tsp butter (highland tradition)

    Method

    1. Fill jebena with cold water. Place on flame.
    2. When water boils, add ground coffee.
    3. Reduce heat. Simmer gently 5—8 minutes.
    4. Let the grounds settle 1—2 minutes.
    5. Pour slowly and from a height into sini cups — the height creates a light foam.
    6. Serve with sugar on the side.

    Chef Tips

    • Ethiopian coffee is always drunk black — no milk.
    • Pouring from a height is essential — aerates the coffee and creates the characteristic small foam.
    • The Harrar tradition of adding a pinch of salt to the cup (not the jebena) is worth trying — it reduces bitterness.
    • If using a saucepan: bring to a gentle boil, remove from heat, steep 5 minutes, pour through a fine sieve.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding milk — not Ethiopian tradition.
    • Under-brewing — must be strong.

    The Science

    Jebena brewing is similar to Turkish/Ibrik coffee — the fine grounds are not filtered, allowing complete extraction of oils and all soluble compounds. This produces a coffee with significantly more body and antioxidants than paper-filtered coffee.

    Health Benefits

    • Among the world's highest antioxidant coffee.
    • Chlorogenic acids support cardiovascular health.
    • The unfiltered method retains maximum beneficial compounds.

    Cultural Note

    Ethiopian coffee is the thread that holds Ethiopian social life together — no visit is complete without it, no morning is begun without it, no conversation is deepened without it. The small cup of strong black coffee is the most fundamental Ethiopian hospitality offering.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Drink immediately.

    Reheating: Reheat gently but quality drops.

    Serve with: With sugar, With Dabo Kolo (ETH162), As the morning ritual
