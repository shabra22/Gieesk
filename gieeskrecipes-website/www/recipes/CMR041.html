🌽 Sanga (Palm-Nut Soup with Kale and Sweetcorn)

Sanga

A UK vegan adaptation: onion and garlic fried in oil, palm-nut cream and water simmered, then kale and sweetcorn added, served with basmati rice.

52 minTotal time
10 minPrep
42 minCook
6Servings
430Calories
EasyDifficulty

The Vegan Society’s page describes sanga as a popular Cameroonian dish, more specifically within the southern and central regions, traditionally made with cassava leaves, palm oil and maize; this version substitutes kale for the cassava leaves and uses palm-nut cream as the base. Blended onion and garlic are fried in hot oil over medium-high heat, the palm-nut cream added and fried a few minutes, then the water, covered until boiling and then uncovered and simmered about 20 minutes. Kale and salt simmer about 20 minutes and sweetcorn another 5. The rice is cooked 13 minutes without lifting the lid and rested 10.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot with lid, Blender

    Sanga

    • 1 white onion
    • 4 cloves garlic
    • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
    • 800 g palm-nut cream / fruit
    • 1 L water
    • 250 g kale, chopped, stalks removed
    • 1 tablespoon salt
    • 500 g sweetcorn

    Rice

    • 300 g basmati white rice
    • 475 ml water

    Method

    1. Blend the onion and garlic and fry in the hot oil over medium-high heat.
    2. Add the palm-nut cream and fry a few minutes.
    3. Add the water, cover until boiling, then uncover and simmer about 20 minutes.
    4. Add the kale and salt and simmer about 20 minutes.
    5. Add the sweetcorn and simmer about 5 minutes more.
    6. Cook the rice 13 minutes without lifting the lid, then rest 10 minutes. Serve together.

    Chef Tips

    • The page gives times only, no doneness cues.
    • Do not lift the lid during the rice cooking.
    • This is a UK adaptation: kale replaces the traditional cassava leaves.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Lifting the lid on the rice.
    • Frying the palm-nut cream too long.
    • Expecting the flavour of the cassava-leaf original.

    Cultural Note

    The page says sanga is popular in the southern and central regions and traditionally made with cassava leaves, palm oil and maize. A second Afrik printing (Amina Savoura, 27 June 2017) describes the Central African original with black nightshade (zom) and fresh maize but prints no quantities.
