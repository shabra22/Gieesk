🍗 Jollof Rice and Chicken

Jollof rice

The household version — basmati pre-soaked twenty minutes, no parboiling, no foil, 800 ml of liquid to 500 g of rice, and the smoke if it happens is an accident.

This is the same author's everyday jollof and it contradicts her party version on almost every structural point. Basmati rather than long grain, pre-soaked at least twenty minutes rather than parboiled, no foil under the lid, and no deliberate burn — she notes that the rice may stick and even burn, that some people in Nigeria consider that the best part, and that it does lend a smoky barbecue flavour, but it is described as something that happens rather than something you do. Six tomatoes and half a chilli, with no sweet pepper at all.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a lid, Blender, Sieve, Tea cloth

  • 500 g long grain or basmati rice, about 6 teacups, rinsed and drained
  • 800 ml water or stock
  • 6 medium fresh tomatoes, or 400 g tinned tomatoes
  • 3 medium onions — 1 sliced, 2 blended
  • 1/2 or less fresh chili, to taste
  • 1 tbsp tomato puree
  • 2 tbsp curry powder
  • 2 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 stock cube
  • 2 tbsp coconut or vegetable oil, or butter
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • Salt to taste

Optional

  • Mixed herbs, shrimp, meat chunks

Method

  1. If you are using basmati, pre-soak it at least 20 minutes. Rinse and drain.
  2. Blend the tomatoes, 2 onions and the chili until smooth.
  3. Heat the oil, add the sliced onion and stir-fry 1 to 2 minutes.
  4. Add the blended mixture, the tomato puree, curry powder and thyme, and season with salt. Cook 10 to 12 minutes on medium.
  5. Add the water or stock, stir and season.
  6. Add the rice, stir, cover and bring to the boil.
  7. Add the butter, stir, reduce the heat and steam 15 to 20 minutes until cooked.
  8. If it is too dry, add stock or water and stir gently.
  9. When cooked, remove from the heat and cover the pot opening with a tea cloth before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Pre-soak basmati at least twenty minutes. The author's own note is that it does taste quite different from parboiled long grain, and she prefers it.
  • Eight hundred millilitres to five hundred grams of rice. That is the clearest liquid-to-rice ratio of the three documented jollofs.
  • Season well up front and give it a good stir at the beginning — her own tip, and it matters because you will barely stir it again.
  • Cook on low heat, do not over-stir, and do not add too much liquid at a time. That is the whole of her technique advice.
  • No foil under the lid here, and no parboiling. If you want the party technique, it is Party Jollof Rice — and note that recipe rules out the basmati this one prefers.
  • Six tomatoes to half a chilli and no sweet pepper at all. That makes this a markedly more tomato-led jollof than the party version.
  • The tea cloth over the pot opening at the end absorbs condensation. Same finish as the party version.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not soaking the basmati.
  • Over-stirring during the steam.
  • Adding liquid in large amounts partway through.
  • Under-seasoning at the start.
  • Expecting party-jollof smoke from a method that does not produce it deliberately.

Health Benefits

  • Tomato-led, so high in lycopene, which becomes more available once cooked.
  • Basmati has a lower glycaemic index than most parboiled long grain.
  • Gluten-free.
  • Moderate in fat for a jollof.

Cultural Note

Household jollof and party jollof are not the same dish and this pair of recipes proves it from inside one kitchen. The everyday version is quicker, more tomato-forward and cooked in a normal pot with a normal lid; the party one is a production with foil, staged stock and a deliberate burn. Both are honestly jollof, and the burnt grains at the bottom are prized either way.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Steam, or microwave with a splash of water under a damp cover.

Serve with: Moi Moi, Dodo (fried plantain), Grilled chicken
