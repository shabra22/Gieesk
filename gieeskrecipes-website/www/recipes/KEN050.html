🍖 Kenyan Beef Bone Soup

Supu ya Mfupa

Supu ya Mfupa is Kenya's most restorative soup — beef bones simmered for hours with onions, tomatoes, garlic, ginger, and spices until the broth is rich, deeply flavourful, and nourishing. Sold at every Kenyan butchery and known for being the ultimate remedy for a cold or a long day.

Supu ya mfupa is the Kenyan version of bone broth — long before it became a global wellness trend, Kenyans were simmering beef bones for hours to extract every drop of nutrition and flavour from them. The result is a rich, golden, deeply savoury broth with soft, succulent meat falling from the bones. At Kenyan butcheries, a cup of supu is often served as a starter before the main meat, and it has a legendary reputation as a cure-all.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large stockpot, Ladle, Strainer

  • 1.5 kg beef bones — knuckle, marrow, or neck bones
  • 2 large onions — roughly chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — halved
  • 4 garlic cloves — crushed
  • 2 tbsp fresh ginger — sliced
  • 2 medium carrots — cut into chunks
  • 1 celery stalk — chopped
  • 2 litres water
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 2 bay leaves
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice — for serving

Method

  1. Roast the bones (optional but recommended): place on a baking tray at 200°C for 20 minutes until lightly browned. This deepens the flavour dramatically.
  2. Place the bones in a large pot. Cover with cold water and bring to a boil. Boil for 5 minutes and discard this first water — it removes impurities.
  3. Add 2 litres of fresh cold water. Add the onions, tomatoes, garlic, ginger, carrots, celery, peppercorns, bay leaves, curry powder, and paprika.
  4. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce heat to the lowest setting. Simmer partially covered for 2—3 hours. The broth should bubble gently, never boil aggressively.
  5. Skim any foam or fat from the surface every 30 minutes for the clearest broth.
  6. Season with salt in the final 30 minutes.
  7. Taste and adjust. Serve hot with coriander and a squeeze of lemon, with pieces of the marrow bones for scooping.

Chef Tips

  • Roasting the bones first is not strictly necessary but creates a dramatically richer, darker broth.
  • The first boil-and-discard removes blood and impurities — do not skip it for a clean-tasting soup.
  • Never boil aggressively — the fat emulsifies into the broth and makes it cloudy and greasy.
  • Marrow bones are the most prized — scoop the marrow out with a small spoon and eat it spread on chapati.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the first boil-and-discard — produces a murky, slightly gamey broth.
  • Boiling too vigorously — makes the broth cloudy and greasy.
  • Not simmering long enough — the richness comes from time.

The Science

Long simmering of beef bones extracts collagen from the connective tissue and cartilage, converting it into gelatin. This gelatin gives supu its characteristic silky mouthfeel and body. Roasting the bones first creates Maillard compounds on the surface that dissolve into the broth during simmering, creating the rich, complex flavour of a good bone soup.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in collagen and gelatin — supports joint, gut, and skin health.
  • Provides minerals including calcium, phosphorus, and magnesium from the bones.
  • Easily digestible — one of the gentlest and most nourishing foods for recovery from illness.
  • Hydrating and deeply warming.

Cultural Note

Supu ya mfupa is one of Kenya's most beloved comfort foods and has a legendary status as a restorative meal. At Kenyan butcheries, a cup of hot bone soup is as much a part of the experience as the meat itself — warming, nourishing, and deeply satisfying.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days — fat solidifies on top and can be removed. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop until hot throughout.

Serve with: Chapati, White Ugali, Fresh Bread, Lemon Wedges, Pili Pili Sauce, Fresh Coriander
