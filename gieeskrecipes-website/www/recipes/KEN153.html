🥥 Kenyan Coconut Cassava Bites

Mihogo ya Nazi

Kenyan Coconut Cassava Bites — cubes of boiled cassava coated in a sweet, fragrant coconut and sugar mixture with cardamom. A simple coastal Swahili treat that transforms humble cassava into something indulgent and aromatic.

Coconut cassava bites are a coastal Kenyan treat that perfectly illustrates the Swahili instinct to improve anything with coconut. Boiled cassava cubes are rolled in a mixture of grated coconut, sugar, and cardamom while still warm — the coconut adheres and the whole thing becomes fragrant, slightly chewy, and deeply satisfying.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan, Mixing bowl

  • 600g cassava — peeled, boiled until tender, and cut into bite-sized cubes
  • 1 cup desiccated or freshly grated coconut
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • ¼ tsp ground cinnamon
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Peel and boil cassava as per KEN035. Cut into bite-sized cubes while warm.
  2. Mix coconut, sugar, cardamom, cinnamon, and salt in a bowl.
  3. While the cassava is still warm, roll each piece in the coconut mixture.
  4. Press the coating gently to adhere.
  5. Arrange on a plate. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Chef Tips

  • Coat the cassava while it is still warm — the warmth helps the coconut mixture adhere.
  • Freshly grated coconut gives a more moist, fragrant result than desiccated.
  • A tiny drizzle of condensed milk over the coated pieces adds extra sweetness and helps the coating stick.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Coating cold cassava — the mixture does not adhere well.
  • Overcooking cassava — it falls apart when coated.

The Science

Warm cassava releases surface starch that acts as a natural adhesive for the coconut coating. The warmth also slightly softens the coconut, creating a better texture integration than coating cold cassava.

Cultural Note

Coconut cassava bites are a coastal Swahili treat that showcases the coast's genius for combining two abundant local ingredients into something more than the sum of their parts.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Consume within 4 hours — the coating softens over time.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: Kenyan Masala Chai, As a standalone snack
