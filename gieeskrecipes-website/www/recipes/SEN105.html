🌺 Mint Hibiscus Cooler (Bissap)

Bissap / hibiscus water

Dried red hibiscus and mint brought to a boil in 2 cups of water, honey stirred in off the heat, the remaining quart added, infused 10 to 15 minutes, strained and chilled over ice.

Thiam’s bissap, from ‘Senegal: Modern Senegalese Recipes From the Source to the Bowl’ (Lake Isle Press, 2015), is the clearest quantified hibiscus water in the research. In a saucepan 2 cups of the water, the hibiscus flowers and the mint are brought to a boil; the heat is turned off, the honey stirred in and the mixture left to cool; the remaining quart of water is added and the pitcher set aside to infuse 10 to 15 minutes; strained into a pitcher and refrigerated; served cold over ice, garnished with extra mint. The page’s own cultural line: hibiscus is native to West Africa and hibiscus water is one of Senegal’s most popular beverages; it is called ‘Agua de Jamaica’ in Mexico.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Strainer, Pitcher

  • 1 quart plus 2 cups water
  • 1/4 pound dried red hibiscus flowers
  • 1/4 cup fresh mint leaves, plus extra sprigs for garnish
  • 1/2 cup honey or sugar

Method

  1. In a saucepan, bring 2 cups of the water, the hibiscus flowers and the mint to a boil.
  2. Turn off the heat, stir in the honey, and let cool.
  3. Add the remaining 1 quart of water.
  4. Set aside to infuse for 10 to 15 minutes.
  5. Strain into a pitcher and refrigerate.
  6. Serve cold over ice, garnished with extra mint.

Chef Tips

  • The only timing is the 10 to 15 minute infusion; no colour or taste cue is printed.
  • The method says ‘stir in the honey’ though the ingredient list offers ‘honey or sugar’ — the page does not say how to dissolve sugar instead.
  • ‘1 quart plus 2 cups’ is the page’s own phrasing and has not been summed.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling all the water with the flowers; the method boils only 2 cups.
  • Leaving the flowers in the pitcher past the infusion.
  • Using the second recipe’s ratio with this one’s quantities — they differ about ten-fold.

Health Benefits

  • Hibiscus is tart and rich in anthocyanins; honey or sugar is the sweetener.

Cultural Note

‘Also known as roselle, hibiscus is native to West Africa, and hibiscus water is one of Senegal’s most popular beverages.’ Compare the white-calyx bissap used in sauces and broths (see Lamb Chops with Bissap Sauce and Riz Wolof note).

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerated; no storage time is printed.

Reheating: Served cold.

Serve with: Grilled fish, Fried fish, Spicy dishes
