🍅 Thieboudienne, Senegalese Jollof Rice and Fish

Ceebu jen — Yummy Medley version

Blue snapper steaks stuffed with a parsley-garlic-scotch-bonnet paste, simmered briefly in a tomato-paste sauce, and jasmine rice cooked in the sauce for 15–20 minutes.

120 minTotal time
60 minPrep
60 minCook
8Servings
655Calories
MediumDifficulty

A blog version built for home kitchens. Fish steaks get two pockets each and are stuffed with a rough paste of parsley, garlic, scotch bonnet, bouillon, pepper and salt, then broiled or fried. A cup of tomato paste fries about five minutes in a cup of oil with sliced onion; a second paste of scotch bonnet and garlic goes in two minutes; bouillon, pepper and 8 cups of water follow. Vegetables go in firmest first and come out as each turns tender, the fish simmers 2–5 minutes, and soaked broken jasmine rice cooks in the sauce on low-medium heat 15–20 minutes, with an optional foil seal.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or mortar, Large pot with lid, Broiler or frying pan, Foil (optional)

    Fish and stuffing

    • A handful of parsley (about a heaping cup)
    • 3 garlic cloves
    • 1 scotch bonnet/habanero pepper
    • 1 tsp shrimp bouillon
    • 1 tsp black pepper
    • 1 tsp salt
    • 2.5 lb blue snapper, cut into steaks, or another white fish

    Rice and vegetables

    • 2 cups broken jasmine rice (or regular jasmine rice), soaked
    • 1 sweet potato
    • 1 purple eggplant
    • 4 carrots
    • 1/2 head cabbage
    • 2 bell peppers
    • 3 scotch bonnet/habanero peppers

    Sauce

    • 1 cup vegetable oil
    • 1 cup tomato paste
    • 1 large onion, sliced
    • 3 garlic cloves
    • 2 scotch bonnet/habanero peppers
    • 3 tbsp shrimp bouillon
    • 1 tsp black pepper
    • Salt, to taste
    • Lime wedges, to serve

    Method

    1. Grind the parsley, garlic, scotch bonnet, bouillon, pepper and salt to a rough paste. Cut two pockets in each piece of fish and stuff them. Broil or fry the fish until golden brown on each side.
    2. Grind the scotch bonnets and garlic to a second rough paste.
    3. Sauté the sliced onion in the oil over medium until translucent. Stir in the tomato paste and fry about 5 minutes. Add the pepper-garlic paste and fry about 2 minutes more.
    4. Add the bouillon, black pepper and 8 cups of water. Taste and adjust the salt.
    5. Add the firmest vegetables first — sweet potato, then carrots — removing each when tender, then the rest.
    6. Simmer the fish in the sauce 2 to 5 minutes and remove gently.
    7. Add the drained rice so the liquid just covers it (add water if needed). Cover and simmer on low-medium 15 to 20 minutes; foil may be used to seal the lid.
    8. At the 15-minute mark check; if the rice is not done cook 5 more minutes. If the liquid has gone and the rice is still firm add 4 tablespoons of water, cover and continue.
    9. Serve hot with the vegetables, fish and lime wedges.

    Chef Tips

    • Check the rice at 15 minutes: ‘the rice has absorbed the moisture and is soft’ is the cue; if the water is gone and the rice is still firm, add 4 tbsp of water.
    • Lift each vegetable out as it turns tender so none overcooks.
    • Fish simmers only 2 to 5 minutes in the sauce — it was already cooked.
    • Foil under the lid seals the pot for the rice stage.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Leaving the fish in for the rice cook.
    • Using too little water for the rice and burning the bottom.

    Cultural Note

    The page calls thieboudienne ‘the National dish of Senegal’ and relays, as an oral tradition, the attribution of the dish to Penda Mbaye of St. Louis; the page does not assert it as fact, and no other recipe source in this set repeats it. The ‘Senegalese jollof’ in the title is the page’s framing.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

    Reheating: Steam gently with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Lime wedges
