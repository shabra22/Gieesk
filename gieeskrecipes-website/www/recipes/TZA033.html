🫕 Tanzanian Tripe Stew

Matumbo

Tanzanian Matumbo — beef or goat tripe slow-cooked for 2 hours in a rich spiced tomato and onion sauce until completely tender. A beloved Tanzanian dish — a food of patience, of community, and of extraordinary flavour when done properly.

Matumbo requires patience — the tripe must be thoroughly cleaned, blanched, and then slow-cooked for a minimum of 2 hours until it reaches the gelatinous, tender texture that makes it so satisfying. The slow cooking also produces a naturally thickened, deeply flavoured sauce from the collagen in the tripe.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot

  • 800g beef tripe — thoroughly cleaned and blanched
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 4 tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp coriander
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 3 cups water
  • ¼ cup coriander

Method

  1. Thoroughly clean tripe under cold running water. Rub with salt and lemon.
  2. Blanch in boiling water for 10 minutes. Drain and cut into 3cm pieces.
  3. Cook onions until golden. Add garlic, ginger, spices. Stir 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook 8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add tripe pieces and water.
  6. Simmer covered on very low heat for 90—120 minutes until very tender.
  7. Adjust seasoning. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Thorough cleaning is the most important step.
  • A pressure cooker reduces time to 35—40 minutes.
  • It must be very tender — chewy matumbo is undercooked.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Insufficient cleaning.
  • Under-cooking.

The Science

Tripe is composed almost entirely of smooth muscle and collagen. Extended slow cooking (90—120 minutes) converts the collagen to gelatin, producing the characteristic tender, slightly gelatinous texture and naturally thickening the sauce.

Cultural Note

Matumbo is Tanzania's communal working-class dish — the meal that requires patience and rewards it with extraordinary flavour. It is eaten at local restaurants and roadside stalls where it is always simmering in large pots from early morning.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days. Freeze 2 months.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: Ugali (TZA001), Chapati (TZA004)
