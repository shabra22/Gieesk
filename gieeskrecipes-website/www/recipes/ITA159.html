Risi e Bisi

A springtime Venetian dish sitting somewhere between a soup and a risotto, sweet fresh peas and rice simmered together in broth until thick and comforting, traditionally served with a spoon.

Historically served to the Doge of Venice on the feast of Saint Mark, risi e bisi occupies its own particular texture category — looser than risotto but thicker than soup — and depends heavily on genuinely sweet, fresh spring peas.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide, heavy-bottomed pan, Ladle

  • 300 g (1½ cups) Vialone Nano or Arborio rice
  • 600 g (1.3 lb) fresh peas, shelled (or 400g/14oz frozen)
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 80 g (3 oz) pancetta, diced
  • 1.2 L (5 cups) vegetable or chicken stock, hot
  • 60 g (¼ cup) unsalted butter, divided
  • 60 g (¾ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Melt half the butter in a wide pan and sauté the onion and pancetta until softened.
  2. Add the peas and cook for a few minutes.
  3. Add the rice and stir to coat in the butter and vegetables.
  4. Begin adding the hot stock a ladle at a time, stirring frequently, similarly to making risotto but with more liquid overall.
  5. Continue for about 18–20 minutes, until the rice is tender and the mixture has a loose, brothy consistency, looser than a typical risotto.
  6. Remove from the heat and stir in the remaining butter and Parmigiano.
  7. Finish with fresh parsley, season to taste, and serve immediately in shallow bowls, eaten with a spoon.

Chef Tips

  • The best version depends on genuinely sweet, fresh peas — this dish is traditionally made in spring when peas are at their peak.
  • Keep it looser than a standard risotto; risi e bisi should be eaten with a spoon, not a fork.
  • Add the stock a little more generously than you would for risotto, since the final texture should be noticeably soupier.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Making it too thick, missing the dish's characteristic loose, brothy texture.
  • Using old, starchy peas rather than young, sweet ones, which changes the character significantly.
  • Treating it exactly like risotto rather than allowing for its slightly different, soupier consistency.

Cultural Note

Risi e bisi holds a special place in Venetian history, traditionally served to the Doge on the feast of Saint Mark, the city's patron saint, each April 25th.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh, as it thickens considerably upon standing.

Reheating: Reheat gently with extra stock or water to loosen back to the right consistency.

Serve with: A light Venetian white wine
