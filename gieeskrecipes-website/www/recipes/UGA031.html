Kabalagala — Ugandan banana pancakes

Ripe sweet bananas mashed with cassava flour into a soft non-sticky dough, cut into rounds with a glass and shallow-fried — chewy, mildly sweet, and traditionally with no sugar in it at all.

Kabalagala are the small banana pancakes sold everywhere in Uganda, and the traditional version is nothing but ripe sweet bananas and plain cassava flour — which gives a well-textured, mildly sweet, chewy pancake. This version adds two tablespoons of sugar and half a teaspoon of baking soda to soften it, and the cook who wrote it says twice, unprompted, that neither belongs traditionally. Leave both out and you have the older pancake; the recipe works either way.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Cling film or clean plastic sheet, Round cookie cutter or a drinking glass, Frying pan

  • 480 ml (2 cups) ripe sweet bananas
  • 720 to 960 ml (3 to 4 cups) cassava flour, sifted
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) sugar — not traditional
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) baking soda — not traditional
  • 720 ml (3 cups) oil, for frying, or red palm oil

Method

  1. Peel the ripe bananas, put them in a bowl and mash them until almost smooth.
  2. Add the sugar and baking soda and mix well, if you are using them.
  3. Add the cassava flour gradually until a soft non-sticky dough forms.
  4. Let the dough rest 10 minutes.
  5. Lay a clean sheet of cling film on a flat surface and spread the dough out by hand to about a centimetre thick.
  6. Cut out rounds with a cookie cutter or the rim of a glass, and repeat until the dough is used up.
  7. Heat the oil over medium heat.
  8. Fry the pancakes until golden brown and fragrant.
  9. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Traditional kabalagala have no sugar and no baking soda. They are added here to soften the texture, and the cook who published this recipe says so herself, twice. Leave both out for the older pancake — plain cassava flour and ripe sweet banana gives a chewier, mildly sweet result.
  • Not all four cups of flour will be used. The cue is the dough not sticking to your hands or the bowl, and that is the only measure that matters.
  • Add the flour gradually. Dumped in at once you overshoot and get a stiff, dry dough you cannot rescue.
  • A centimetre thick, and spread by hand on cling film rather than rolled. The dough is too soft for a rolling pin.
  • Red palm oil is a legitimate and very good alternative for frying, and it changes the colour and flavour noticeably.
  • Medium heat. Too hot and they brown before the inside cooks.
  • Regular ripe bananas work in place of the small sweet ones, if that is what you have.
  • The ebook version of this recipe fries on high heat rather than medium and uses 3 to 4 cups of flour rather than 4 — a small unreconciled difference in the same cook's two publications.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the flour at once.
  • Using underripe bananas.
  • Trying to roll the dough with a pin.
  • Frying too hot.
  • Skipping the ten-minute rest.

Health Benefits

  • Ripe bananas supply potassium and vitamin B6, and most of the sweetness comes from the fruit.
  • Cassava flour is naturally gluten-free.
  • No dairy, no egg and, in the traditional version, no added sugar at all.
  • They are shallow-fried, so the oil is the limiting factor.

Cultural Note

Kabalagala are sold from baskets and roadside tables all over Uganda and are among the first things a Ugandan child learns to want. The sugar-and-soda question is a real generational marker: the older pancake is chewier and much less sweet, and plenty of people prefer it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days in an airtight container at room temperature.

Reheating: A minute in a dry pan. They go chewier, which is not a bad thing.

Serve with: Ugandan tea, Mandazi, Roasted groundnuts
