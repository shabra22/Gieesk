🫙 Snoekpate

Snoekpate

Smoked snoek flaked and mashed with mayonnaise and creme fraiche, sharpened with lemon and black pepper, packed into a dish and chilled — four ingredients, no cooking, and no salt needed at all.

There is almost nothing to this and that is the point: good smoked snoek does not need help. The mayonnaise and creme fraiche are there to carry it rather than flavour it, the lemon cuts the smoke, and no salt goes in because the fish brought plenty. The only real work is the bones, and snoek has a reputation for them that it fully deserves.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Fork, Small serving dish

  • 250 g smoked snoek
  • 60 ml (1/4 cup) thick mayonnaise
  • 60 ml (1/4 cup) creme fraiche
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • Freshly ground black pepper, to taste

To serve

  • Toast, crackers, crudites or small sweet pepper halves

Method

  1. Flake the fish, taking great care to remove every bone. Go through it twice.
  2. Mash the flaked fish with the mayonnaise and the creme fraiche until it comes together but still has some texture.
  3. Add the lemon juice and black pepper to taste and mix well. Do not add salt.
  4. Pack into a small serving dish and chill well — an hour is enough, longer is better.
  5. Serve with toast, crackers, crudites or halved baby peppers.

Chef Tips

  • No salt. The smoked fish has more than enough and there is no rescuing an over-salted pate.
  • Go through the fish twice for bones. A single bone in a pate ruins the whole dish for whoever finds it.
  • Leave some texture. Blitzed smooth in a processor it becomes a paste and loses what makes it snoek.
  • Chill it properly before serving. Warm, it tastes oily; cold, it tastes of smoke.
  • Served in halved baby sweet peppers rather than on toast, it makes a very good gluten-free canape.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding salt.
  • Rushing the bones.
  • Processing it to a smooth paste.
  • Serving it at room temperature.

Health Benefits

  • High in protein and omega-3 fatty acids.
  • No added salt at all.
  • Naturally gluten-free, depending on what you serve it with.

Cultural Note

Snoek pate is what a West Coast household makes from the end of a smoked fish, and it turns up as a starter in restaurants that would not otherwise serve snoek at all. The fish's journey from cheap catch to fashionable canape has not changed the recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, covered, up to 3 days.

Reheating: Never — it is a cold dish.

Serve with: Melba toast, Crudites, A dry Cape white wine
