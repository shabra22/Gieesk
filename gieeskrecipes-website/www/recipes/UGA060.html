🌸 Banana Blossom Patties

Banana blossom

Banana blossom stripped to its tender core and cooked to drive off the bitter sap, then sliced thin and bound with egg, flour and vegetables into two-minute-a-side patties.

The banana blossom is the flower at the end of the bunch, and households get excited about it — it becomes either a groundnut stew or these patties. Getting to the edible part is the work: you break it off the bunch and pull away the outer coverings until you reach a soft tender inner part, and that is what has to be cooked to remove the bitter sap. Boiled, it turns dark and is ready. Roasted, it does the same job and picks up smoke, which is the variation here.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, Frying pan, Spatula

  • 1 whole banana blossom, prepared and chopped into thin slices
  • 1 large green pepper, finely diced
  • 1 large carrot, grated
  • 2 medium onions, finely diced
  • 2 medium tomatoes, finely diced
  • 2 eggs
  • 45 ml (3 tbsp) baking flour
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) dark soy sauce
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • A handful of coriander, finely chopped
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) rosemary
  • Chilli flakes
  • Salt
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) oil, per batch

Method

    Preparing the blossom

    1. Break the blossom off the bunch and remove the outer coverings until you reach the soft tender inner part.
    2. Cook that inner part to remove the bitter sap — boil it until it turns a dark colour, or roast it, which does the same job and adds a smoky flavour.
    3. Let it cool, then chop it into thin slices.

    The patties

    1. Put all the ingredients in a large bowl and mix well.
    2. Put a frying pan on medium heat and add a teaspoon of oil.
    3. Once the oil is hot, add three spoons of the mixture to the pan and spread them out evenly.
    4. Let the patty fry about two minutes, then flip and cook the other side the same length of time.
    5. Take it off the heat and repeat until the mixture is used up.
    6. Serve as they are, or with mayonnaise, ketchup, rice, or in a bun with cheese.

    Chef Tips

    • The bitter sap must be cooked out. Boiling is the traditional method and the blossom turns dark when it is done — that colour change is the cue.
    • Roasting instead of boiling is a deliberate variation. It removes the sap and adds smoke at the same time, and it is worth trying once you know the boiled version.
    • Strip down to the soft tender inner part. The outer coverings are tough and bitter and no amount of cooking fixes them.
    • Two minutes a side, medium heat. These are thin patties and they set fast.
    • Three spoons per patty and spread evenly. Thicker ones do not cook through in four minutes.
    • The egg and the three tablespoons of flour are the only binder. Do not add more flour or they go stodgy.
    • The other traditional use for a blossom is a groundnut stew, and households are pleased to get one either way.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not cooking out the sap, leaving them bitter.
    • Using the tough outer layers.
    • Making the patties too thick.
    • Adding extra flour.
    • Frying on high heat.

    Health Benefits

    • Banana blossom is high in fibre, potassium, magnesium and antioxidants.
    • Carrot, pepper, tomato and coriander go in raw to the mixture.
    • Only a teaspoon of oil per batch.
    • Contains egg and gluten.

    Cultural Note

    Banana blossoms come free with every bunch of matooke and are eaten across Asia as well as East Africa, though the treatments differ completely. That Ugandan households are excited when one appears says how much of this cuisine runs on using the whole plant — fruit, leaves, spines for steaming, and the flower.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

    Reheating: Warm in a dry pan.

    Serve with: Rice, Kachumbari, Posho
