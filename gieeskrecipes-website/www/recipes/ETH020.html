🫕 Ethiopian Millet Porridge

የዳጉሳ ገንፎ — Ye-Dagusa Genfo

Ethiopian Millet Porridge — finger millet (dagusa) flour cooked into a thick, slightly sour porridge in the Ethiopian genfo tradition, served with kibbeh and berbere. The western Ethiopian grain porridge — slightly earthier and more nutritious than teff, with a distinctive nutty flavour.

Millet genfo is the western and southwestern Ethiopian answer to the highland barley genfo — finger millet (locally called dagusa or teff-like in some regions) grows where the rainfall is higher and the soil different from the teff highlands. The result is a slightly darker, earthier, extremely nutritious porridge.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Strong wooden spoon

  • 1.5 cups finger millet flour (dagusa)
  • 3 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Serving: 2 tbsp kibbeh (ETH092)

  • 1 tsp berbere

Method

  1. See ETH009 (Genfo) for the full technique.
  2. Bring water to a boil. Add salt.
  3. Add millet flour in a thin stream, whisking constantly.
  4. Stir vigorously over medium heat 15—18 minutes until very stiff.
  5. Mound in bowls. Well in the centre. Fill with kibbeh and berbere.

Chef Tips

  • Millet genfo is slightly more sticky than barley — stir with extra energy.
  • The result will be darker in colour than barley or corn genfo.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not stirring enough — millet sticks aggressively.

The Science

Finger millet has significantly more tannins than other grains — these polyphenol compounds give millet its characteristic slightly astringent taste and its dark colour. The tannins also have significant antioxidant properties.

Health Benefits

  • Finger millet has exceptional calcium content.
  • High in iron.
  • Tannins have antioxidant properties.
  • Very high in amino acids.

Cultural Note

Millet genfo is the porridge of the Ethiopian highlands' western fringe — where the landscape transitions from the highlands to the Great Rift Valley, where the cultures blend, and where finger millet has been cultivated for longer than any other grain in the region.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: With added water.

Serve with: With kibbeh (ETH092) and berbere (ETH091)
