🍲 Kenyan Goat Bone Soup

Supu ya Mbuzi

Supu ya Mbuzi — Kenyan Goat Bone Soup — goat bones simmered for hours until the broth is golden, rich, and intensely flavourful. Considered by many Kenyans to be even more restorative than beef supu. The legendary butchery cure-all.

At any Kenyan butchery that handles goat, there is almost always a pot of supu ya mbuzi on the fire — the intense, deeply savoury broth made from simmering goat bones for hours. Goat bones yield a particularly rich, fragrant broth that beef bones cannot quite match. Served with the marrow bones and a chapati for dipping, it is one of the most warming, restorative meals in Kenyan culinary tradition.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large stockpot, Ladle, Strainer

  • 1.5 kg goat bones — knuckle, neck, and marrow bones
  • 2 large onions — roughly chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — halved
  • 4 garlic cloves — crushed
  • 2 tbsp fresh ginger — sliced
  • 2 medium carrots — cut into chunks
  • 2.5 litres water
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 2 bay leaves
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice — for serving

Method

  1. Wash bones thoroughly. Place in a large pot, cover with cold water, bring to a boil for 5 minutes, then discard this water.
  2. Add 2.5 litres fresh cold water. Add all remaining ingredients except coriander and lemon.
  3. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to the lowest simmer. Cook partially covered for 2.5—3 hours.
  4. Skim foam from the surface regularly for the first hour.
  5. Season with salt in the final 30 minutes.
  6. Serve hot with coriander, lemon juice, and chapati for dipping.

Chef Tips

  • The first boil-and-discard is essential for a clean, clear broth.
  • Goat supu should simmer gently — never boil hard.
  • Roasting the bones for 20 minutes at 200°C before simmering deepens the colour and flavour significantly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the first boil-and-discard.
  • Boiling too vigorously — makes the broth cloudy.

The Science

Goat bones are smaller and denser than beef bones, with a higher ratio of cartilage to total mass. This means goat supu extracts relatively more gelatin per kilogram of bones than beef supu, producing a broth with excellent body.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in gelatin and collagen supporting gut and joint health.
  • High in minerals from the bones.
  • Deeply warming and easily digestible.

Cultural Note

Goat bone soup holds a special place in Kenyan culinary culture — it is the restorative served to those recovering from illness, the warming welcome for a guest on a cold evening, and the start of every great butchery meal.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on stovetop.

Serve with: Chapati, White Ugali, Lemon Wedges, Pili Pili Sauce, Fresh Coriander
