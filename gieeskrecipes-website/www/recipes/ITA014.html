Gnocchi di Patate

Gnocchi di Patate are pillowy, tender potato dumplings — a comforting staple across every region of Italy, equally at home with a simple sage butter or a rich tomato ragù.

The secret to great gnocchi lies almost entirely in restraint — starchy potatoes baked (not boiled) to keep excess moisture out, and just enough flour worked in to bind the dough, so the dumplings stay light rather than dense and gummy.

Ingredients

Equipment: Baking sheet, Potato ricer or masher, Bench scraper, Fork for ridges

  • 1 kg (2.2 lb) starchy potatoes (such as Russet), whole and unpeeled
  • 200–250 g (1½–2 cups) all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • 1 large egg, lightly beaten
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a pinch (optional)

Method

  1. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F). Pierce the potatoes a few times and bake directly on the rack for 45–60 minutes until completely tender.
  2. While still hot (using a towel to hold them), peel the potatoes and pass through a potato ricer onto a clean work surface. Spread out to cool slightly and release steam.
  3. Sprinkle the flour, salt, and nutmeg over the riced potato, then add the egg.
  4. Gently bring the mixture together with a bench scraper, folding just until a soft dough forms — avoid overworking it.
  5. Divide the dough into pieces and roll each into a long rope about 2cm thick on a lightly floured surface.
  6. Cut into 2cm pillows. Roll each piece gently over the back of a fork to create ridges, or leave plain.
  7. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook the gnocchi in batches — they're done when they float to the surface, about 1–2 minutes.
  8. Lift out with a slotted spoon and toss immediately with your sauce of choice.

Chef Tips

  • Bake the potatoes rather than boiling them — boiling adds excess water that makes for dense, gluey gnocchi.
  • Work with the potato while it's still hot for the best texture, and work quickly to prevent it cooling and becoming gummy.
  • Use as little flour as the dough will tolerate — a wetter dough handled gently makes lighter gnocchi.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling the potatoes, resulting in waterlogged, heavy gnocchi.
  • Overworking the dough, which develops gluten and makes the gnocchi tough and rubbery.
  • Adding too much flour out of caution — this is the most common cause of dense gnocchi.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of potassium and vitamin C from the potatoes.
  • Naturally dairy-free base (before sauce is added).

Cultural Note

Gnocchi is traditionally eaten on Thursdays in many Italian households and restaurants — an old saying, 'giovedì gnocchi,' ties back to the dish once being an affordable, filling meal before the traditionally meatless Friday.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Uncooked gnocchi freeze beautifully — freeze in a single layer, then bag once solid, for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Cook frozen gnocchi directly from frozen in boiling water, no need to thaw.

Serve with: Sage brown butter, Tomato ragù, Pesto alla Genovese, Gorgonzola cream sauce
