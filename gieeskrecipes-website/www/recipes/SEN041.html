🥖 Ndambe, Lamb and Black-Eyed Pea Stew on a Baguette

Ndambe — ndambé

Cubed lamb braised with palm oil, tomato paste, paprika and black-eyed peas, cooked until the vegetables turn deep reddish-brown — spooned into a split baguette.

Ndambe is the Dakar street-corner breakfast: black-eyed peas stewed with tomato and potato, ladled into a warm baguette so the juice glues the bread together. This is the Saveur printing by Binta Fall and Cherif Mbodji, made with lamb. Lamb is browned in red palm oil, onion, pepper, scallion and parsley cook to translucency, then tomato paste to a deep reddish-brown. Garlic, smoked paprika, vinegar, canned peas, bay and stock go in; lamb simmers uncovered until tender, a potato is added, covered, and cooked until it begins to break down. Quarters of baguette are split and filled.

Ingredients

Equipment: Dutch oven, Bread knife

  • 1 tbsp African red palm oil
  • 10 oz boneless leg of lamb, cut into 1/2-inch cubes
  • 2 medium yellow onions, finely chopped
  • 1 medium red bell pepper, stemmed, seeded and finely chopped
  • 2 medium scallions, finely chopped
  • 1/2 cup parsley leaves, finely chopped
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tbsp distilled white vinegar
  • 3 3/4 cup drained, canned black-eyed peas, rinsed well (printed ‘cup’)
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 1/2 cups beef, chicken or vegetable stock
  • 1 medium red potato, peeled and coarsely chopped
  • 1 baguette, cut crosswise into quarters

Method

  1. Heat the palm oil in a Dutch oven. Brown the lamb until lightly browned all over, 2–4 minutes.
  2. Add the onions, bell pepper, scallions, parsley, black pepper and salt. Cook until the onions are translucent, 2–4 minutes.
  3. Add the tomato paste and cook until the vegetables soften and turn a deep reddish-brown, 10–12 minutes.
  4. Add the garlic, paprika and vinegar, then the black-eyed peas, bay leaves and stock. Simmer uncovered until the lamb is tender, about 30–35 minutes.
  5. Add the potato, cover, and cook on medium-low until the potatoes are soft and beginning to break down, 40–45 minutes. Season.
  6. Split the baguette quarters, spoon the stew onto the bottom halves, close with the tops and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • ‘A deep reddish-brown’ is the explicit colour target for the vegetables and tomato paste — wait for it.
  • The potato goes in late and breaks down; it is the thickener.
  • The warm baguette should be filled with a stew juicy enough to act as glue.
  • Lamb is the Saveur recipe’s choice; the companion feature calls lamb a variation on a usually meatless base.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stopping the tomato stage before the colour deepens.
  • Adding the potato too early.
  • Using a stale or cold baguette.

Cultural Note

‘Ndambe, traditionally cooked for dinner, evolved to be served on baguettes for breakfast,’ writes Megan Zhang in the Saveur companion piece — the sandwich ‘started from the streets’, in Mbodji’s words, and Thiam recalls it as ‘something that was mostly for peasants’. The same article says there are as many recipes for ndambe as there are people who make it.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Gentle heat, covered, with a splash of stock.

Serve with: Baguette, Café Touba or attaya
