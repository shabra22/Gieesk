🥦 Kenyan Grilled Vegetable Platter

Sahani ya Mboga Zilizochomwa

Kenyan Grilled Vegetable Platter — colourful seasonal vegetables charred over high heat until smoky and tender, drizzled with herb oil, lemon, and pili pili. A vegetarian showpiece that celebrates Kenya's diverse and high-quality vegetable production.

Kenya's vegetable production is exceptional — the fertile highlands produce capsicums, courgettes, aubergines, mushrooms, and asparagus of extraordinary quality. Grilled over high heat with herb oil, they become smoky, tender, and deeply satisfying.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grill pan or charcoal grill, Bowl for herb oil

  • 2 courgettes — sliced lengthwise
  • 2 red capsicums — quartered
  • 1 yellow capsicum — quartered
  • 1 large aubergine — sliced
  • 200g portobello mushrooms — halved
  • 1 bunch asparagus
  • 1 red onion — cut into wedges

Herb Oil

  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander
  • 1 tbsp fresh parsley
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • ½ tsp salt
  • Pili pili sauce for drizzle

Method

  1. Make herb oil: combine olive oil, garlic, herbs, lemon, and salt.
  2. Brush all vegetables with herb oil.
  3. Heat grill pan or charcoal grill to high heat.
  4. Grill each vegetable until char marks form and they are tender — capsicums 4—5 min per side; courgette and aubergine 3—4 min; asparagus 2—3 min; mushrooms 4—5 min.
  5. Arrange on a large platter.
  6. Drizzle with remaining herb oil and pili pili sauce.

Chef Tips

  • High heat is the key — low heat produces steamed, not grilled, vegetables.
  • Do not move vegetables immediately after placing — let them develop char marks.
  • Arrange by colour for restaurant-level visual impact.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Low heat — no char, no smoke, no flavour.
  • Moving too early — prevents char mark development.

The Science

The characteristic smoky flavour of grilled vegetables comes from partial combustion of surface sugars and the deposition of volatile compounds from the grill smoke. Aubergine contains air pockets in its flesh that collapse during grilling — this is why properly grilled aubergine becomes dramatically tender.

Cultural Note

The grilled vegetable platter demonstrates the breadth of Kenya's agricultural production — a showcase of the country's vegetable-growing excellence that stands proud alongside the meat dishes.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten freshly grilled.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a hot pan.

Serve with: As a side to Mixed Grill (KEN251), With Herb Rice (KEN276), As a vegetarian main
