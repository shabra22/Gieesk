🥕 Fonio Pilaf with Dates, Carrots and Peanuts

Pilaf de fonio aux dattes

Medjool dates, roasted peanuts, cayenne and carrots softened in red palm oil, fonio stirred in with stock, then baked covered in a square dish — Thiam’s Thanksgiving stuffing.

55 minTotal time
15 minPrep
40 minCook
7Servings
360Calories
MediumDifficulty

Thiam serves this at his Thanksgiving table because he finds it lighter than a bread stuffing. Palm oil warms in a saucepan; dates, half the peanuts, cayenne and diced carrots cook 8 to 10 minutes until the carrots soften slightly. Fonio is stirred into the oil, stock goes in, and the pot is covered and cooked without stirring about three minutes until the grains absorb the stock. It is then transferred to a greased 8-inch square dish, covered with foil, and baked about 20 minutes at 325° until tender and heated through, fluffed, seasoned and topped with the remaining peanuts and parsley.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium saucepan with lid, 8-inch square baking dish, Foil

  • 1/4 cup red palm oil or vegetable oil, plus more for greasing
  • 5 oz pitted Medjool dates, roughly chopped
  • 1/2 cup roughly chopped unsalted roasted peanuts, divided
  • 1/2 tsp cayenne
  • 2 medium carrots, cut into 1/4-inch dice
  • 1 cup fonio or quinoa, rinsed and drained
  • 1 1/2 cups chicken stock
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper — quantities not printed
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped flat-leaf parsley

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 325° (the page prints no unit). In a medium saucepan over medium heat, warm the palm oil. Add the dates, 1/4 cup of the peanuts, the cayenne and the carrots and cook, stirring, 8 to 10 minutes, until the carrots soften slightly.
  2. Add the fonio and stir to coat in the oil. Add the stock and bring to a boil. Cover and cook without stirring about 3 minutes, until the fonio absorbs all the stock.
  3. Transfer to the baking dish greased with palm oil and smooth the top. Cover with foil and bake about 20 minutes, until tender and heated through.
  4. Uncover, fluff with a fork and season with salt and pepper. Move to a serving bowl and top with the remaining peanuts and the parsley.

Chef Tips

  • Do not stir while the fonio absorbs the stock — about three minutes covered.
  • Grease the dish with palm oil, as the page says.
  • Couscous or quinoa can replace the fonio in equal amounts.
  • The only oven temperature in the fonio set is 325°, with no unit printed.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stirring while it absorbs.
  • Baking uncovered and drying it out.

Cultural Note

The parent article, ‘A Brooklyn Thanksgiving with the Flavors of Senegal’, is where this appeared. The headnote calls fonio ‘a delightfully nutty-tasting type of millet’ and ‘a ubiquitous staple in Senegal’.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Covered in a 325° oven or a splash of stock on the stove.

Serve with: Roast turkey, Roast lamb
