🎃 Toasted Pumpkin Seeds

Ensujju seeds

Dried pumpkin seeds coated with a tablespoon of water, salt, paprika and pepper and stirred in a heavy dry pan for twenty minutes until they brown and begin to pop.

This is the simplest thing in a Ugandan kitchen and the only technique in it is patience. A tablespoon of water is what carries the salt and spice onto the seeds — there is no oil at all. Then it is twenty minutes of stirring in a heavy pan until they are completely dry, browned and starting to pop, and then more stirring off the heat until they cool, because that is when they turn crunchy rather than chewy.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy cast pan, Wooden spoon

  • Dried pumpkin seeds
  • About 15 ml (1 tbsp) water
  • Salt
  • Paprika
  • Black pepper

No oil

    Method

    1. Put all the ingredients in a heavy cast pan and mix until all the seeds are well coated.
    2. Over medium heat, keep stirring the seeds until they are completely dry, have browned, and are starting to pop. This takes around 20 minutes.
    3. Take the pan off the heat and keep stirring until the seeds are fully cool and crunchy.
    4. Serve.

    Chef Tips

    • The tablespoon of water is the whole trick. It carries the salt and paprika onto the seeds and then evaporates — no oil is needed or wanted.
    • Keep stirring off the heat until they are cool. That is when they go crunchy; stop early and they stay slightly chewy.
    • A heavy pan holds even heat, which matters over twenty minutes of dry toasting.
    • The popping is the doneness cue. Browned and starting to pop, and no further.
    • Medium heat throughout. Hot and fast scorches the outside while the middle is still soft.
    • No quantities are given in the source beyond the tablespoon of water — this is cooked by eye, seasoned to taste, with as many seeds as you have.
    • Save the seeds whenever you cook pumpkin. Dried and stored, they are always available.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding oil.
    • Taking the pan off and leaving them to sit, so they never crisp.
    • Using too high a heat.
    • Using a thin pan.
    • Stirring intermittently rather than constantly.

    Health Benefits

    • Pumpkin seeds are high in magnesium, zinc, iron and plant protein.
    • A good source of healthy unsaturated fats and vitamin E.
    • Dry-toasted with no oil at all.
    • Uses a part of the vegetable that is normally thrown away.

    Cultural Note

    Pumpkins in Uganda are used whole — leaves as a green, young fruit cooked with beans, seeds toasted like this — and none of it is framed as thrift. It is simply what you do with a pumpkin, and the snack is as ordinary and as good as it sounds.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 2 weeks in an airtight container once fully cool.

    Reheating: Not needed. A minute in a dry pan refreshes them if they soften.

    Serve with: Ugandan tea, Curry Spiced Jackfruit Seeds, Roasted groundnuts
