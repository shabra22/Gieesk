🐑 Moroccan Steamed Lamb

Lham mbekhar — butter-rubbed lamb steamed over herbs, served with salt and cumin

Large pieces of lamb on the bone, rubbed with butter and steamed over a bouquet of herbs until buttery soft, then dipped in salt and cumin. Simple, and one of the most Moroccan things you can do with a lamb.

There is no sauce and almost no seasoning in this dish, which is what makes it good. The lamb is rubbed with soft butter (or smen), salt, pepper and a little saffron, then steamed in a covered pot above boiling salted water with a bouquet of parsley or cilantro and a handful of mint. Small onions, potatoes and carrots join the pot partway through. At the table everyone dips the meat in salt and cumin. The recipe offers pot, pressure-cooker and oven methods; the oven version wraps meat and vegetables in parchment, then foil, at 325°F (160°C) for 2 to 3 hours.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with a steamer rack or couscoussier, Tight-fitting lid, Cheesecloth (optional)

  • 4 1/2 lbs lamb, large pieces on the bone, trimmed of fat
  • 3 tbsp soft unsalted butter (can use part or all smen instead)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp pepper
  • 1/2 tsp saffron threads, crumbled (optional)
  • 1 small bunch parsley and/or cilantro, tied into a bouquet
  • small handful of mint sprigs (optional)
  • 1/2 lb small onions, peeled and halved (optional)
  • 1/2 lb small new potatoes, peeled (optional)
  • 1/2 lb carrots, peeled and cut into 1/2" slices (optional)

To serve

  • salt and cumin

Method

  1. Pierce the meat and rub the butter, salt, pepper and saffron into it.
  2. Bring a large quantity of salted water to a boil in the base of the pot. Set the herb bouquet and mint in the steamer section and lay the lamb on top.
  3. Close the lid tightly and steam for 1 1/2 hours without peeking.
  4. Add the potatoes and carrots (and onions if using), close the lid tightly again and continue steaming another 1 to 1 1/2 hours.
  5. Pressure cooker version: cook with pressure over medium heat for 1 hour, add the vegetables, then continue steaming 30 to 45 minutes.
  6. Oven version: wrap meat and vegetables in parchment and then foil; place in a preheated 325°F (160°C) oven for 2 to 3 hours.
  7. Serve with small dishes of salt and cumin for dipping.

Chef Tips

  • Do not lift the lid during the first 1 1/2 hours. The steam is the cooking medium.
  • Doneness cue from the source: the lamb is tender enough to pull easily from the bone.
  • The source says serving salt and cumin on the side is traditional; let guests season each bite.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting the pot boil dry. Check the water without breaking the seal for too long.
  • Using a loose lid. A tight seal, or a damp cheesecloth under it, keeps the steam in.
  • Salting the meat heavily. The dip is where the salt goes.

Health Benefits

  • Steaming adds no cooking fat beyond the butter rubbed on the meat.
  • Carrots, onions and potatoes bring fibre and vitamins to the plate.

Cultural Note

The page says it is often made after Eid al-Adha and describes it as not a fancy dish, which is part of its charm; the salt and cumin served alongside are the whole seasoning table.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps 3 days in the fridge in a covered container with its juices.

Reheating: Steam again for 10 to 15 minutes, or warm covered in a low oven.

Serve with: Khobz, Mint tea
