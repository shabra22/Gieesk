🐟 Sukuma Lake Fish Soup

Supu ya Samaki wa Ziwa — Wasukuma

Sukuma Lake Fish Soup — whole tilapia or Nile perch simmered with tomatoes, onions, and local herbs in the minimal-spice Sukuma tradition. The lakeside soup of Tanzania's largest ethnic group — the freshest possible fish from Lake Victoria, simply prepared.

Sukuma fish soup is the dish that has fed the people around Lake Victoria for millennia — the fresh fish of the lake prepared with minimal intervention to let the quality speak. The Sukuma traditionally added very few spices, relying instead on the extraordinary freshness of the lake fish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 800g whole tilapia or Nile perch — cleaned and gutted
  • 2 onions — roughly chopped
  • 4 tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 3 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander
  • Fresh lemon wedges

Method

  1. Combine fish, onion, garlic, and water in a pot.
  2. Bring to a boil. Skim foam.
  3. Reduce heat. Add tomatoes.
  4. Simmer gently 25 minutes.
  5. Remove fish. Flake gently. Return to broth.
  6. Season. Garnish with coriander. Serve with lemon.

Chef Tips

  • The freshness of Lake Victoria tilapia needs no elaborate spicing.
  • Lemon at the table is essential — the Sukuma squeeze generously.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-spicing — this is intentionally simple.

Cultural Note

Sukuma lake fish soup is the taste of Lake Victoria — the great freshwater sea that defines the geography, economy, and culture of Tanzania's most populous people. To eat this soup is to understand why the Sukuma have always lived by the lake.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh.

Reheating: Gently.
