🥓 Bisteeki Hilib

Bisteeki Hilib

Beef sirloin sliced paper-thin, marinated in a mortar-ground paste of cilantro, garlic and vinegar, and seared thirty seconds a side — with sweet-and-sour onions and peppers spooned over.

Bisteeki is a steak dish built for thin cuts, which is what Somali butchers sell: sirloin sliced so fine that thirty seconds a side is genuinely all it needs. The marinade is ground in a mortar rather than mixed in a bowl, which bruises the cilantro and the garlic into something far more aromatic, and the vinegar in it does useful work on the connective tissue if the meat sits a day or two.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle, Heavy frying pan or griddle

    For the steaks

    • 450 g (1 lb) beef sirloin, sliced very thin
    • 3 cloves garlic, minced
    • 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
    • 1 tbsp vinegar
    • 60 ml (1/4 cup) olive oil
    • 1 tbsp vegetable seasoning, or 2 tsp salt
    • 1 tsp ground black pepper, to taste

    For the topping

    • 1 onion, sliced
    • 2 tbsp olive oil
    • 1 tbsp vinegar
    • 1 tsp sugar
    • 1/4 green bell pepper, sliced
    • 1/4 red bell pepper, sliced
    • 1 small carrot, sliced thin
    • 1 tsp vegetable seasoning, or a good pinch of salt

    Method

    1. Grind the cilantro, minced garlic and vegetable seasoning together in a mortar until they become a rough paste.
    2. Work in the vinegar, the black pepper and the olive oil, then transfer to a bowl.
    3. Coat the steaks on both sides with the marinade and leave them at least 30 minutes. They can sit covered in the fridge for a day or two, and the acid will tenderise them further.
    4. For the topping, soften the sliced onion in the 2 tbsp olive oil with the seasoning.
    5. Add the vinegar and the sugar, then the sliced bell peppers and carrot.
    6. Take the pan off the heat while the vegetables still have some crunch, and keep them warm.
    7. Get a heavy pan very hot and cook the steaks for 30 seconds a side, working in batches and moving them to the hottest part of the pan.
    8. Pile the steaks on a platter, spoon the vegetables over and serve immediately.

    Chef Tips

    • Thirty seconds a side. These are not steaks you rest and slice; they are thin enough that a minute in total is overcooking.
    • Grind the marinade in a mortar rather than stirring it in a bowl. Bruising the cilantro and garlic releases far more than chopping does.
    • If your butcher will not slice it thin enough, freeze the sirloin partly and slice it with a very sharp knife.
    • Do not crowd the pan. Thin steaks release moisture instantly and a full pan steams them grey.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cooking them longer to be safe, which turns thin sirloin tough and dry within seconds.
    • Using a pan that is not properly hot, so there is no sear at all.
    • Cooking the topping vegetables until soft — they are meant to stay crisp against the meat.
    • Serving it later. This is a dish that has to leave the pan for the table.

    Health Benefits

    • Sirloin is a lean cut, high in protein, iron, zinc and B12.
    • The vegetables go in barely cooked, so their vitamin C survives.
    • Very fast cooking means little added fat beyond the marinade.

    Cultural Note

    Busteeki is widely eaten in Mogadishu, part of the vocabulary of the city's restaurants alongside steak and fish. The thin cut is what makes it a household dish too — a pound of sirloin sliced fine feeds four people, which a thick steak would not.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days. The marinated raw meat keeps 1 to 2 days.

    Reheating: Not recommended — reheating cooks it further. Eat it cold in a sandwich instead.

    Serve with: Bariis Iskukaris, Sabaayad, Basbaas
