🫘 Ewa Agoyin, Unbleached

Ewa agoyin

The thirty-minute version, with the palm oil heated but not to smoking point — fried till very dry but not burnt, and no bleaching at all.

This is ewa agoyin for a weeknight, and it is the direct counter-argument to the bleached version. The beans go in with the onion, seasoning and salt from the start and boil for thirty minutes flat, then get mashed smooth. The sauce uses two cooking spoons of palm oil rather than three hundred millilitres, and the instruction is explicit: heat the oil but not to smoking point. Fried till very dry but not burnt, it is a brighter, redder, far less oily sauce than the Lagos street version — and whether that makes it a different dish or a sensible one is a real argument.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium pot, Frying pan, Wooden spoon

    For the beans

    • 1 cup brown beans
    • 1 small onion
    • 1 sachet ginger and garlic
    • 70 cl water
    • Seasoning and a little salt

    For the pepper sauce

    • 1 cooking spoon wet ground dry pepper
    • 1 shawa fish
    • 1 cooking spoon ground onion
    • 1 tsp ginger powder
    • 2 cooking spoons palm oil
    • 1 beef seasoning cube
    • Salt to taste

    Method

    1. Wash the beans and chop the onion. Pour everything into the pot, add the seasoning and a little salt, and boil 30 minutes.
    2. When the beans are cooked, use a spoon to mash them into a smooth paste.
    3. For the sauce, heat the palm oil — but not to smoking point.
    4. Fry the onion, pepper, fish, seasoning, ginger powder and salt.
    5. Fry till very dry but not burnt.
    6. Spoon the sauce onto the beans and serve.

    Chef Tips

    • Not to smoking point. That is the whole position: the oil keeps its colour and its carotene, and the sauce comes out red rather than black.
    • Everything into the pot at once, including the salt. The other named version withholds salt to speed the beans up; this one does not and still gets there in thirty minutes.
    • Seventy centilitres of water to one cup of beans is a tight ratio — this is a thick, dry mash rather than a loose one.
    • Fry the sauce till very dry. That is the cue, and 'dry' means the oil has separated and nothing is bubbling wet any more.
    • Two cooking spoons of oil instead of three hundred millilitres is the honest difference in richness. Expect a lighter dish and do not expect the street-stall taste.
    • Wet ground dry pepper means the dried pepper already blended with a little water — no soaking stage at all here.
    • Ginger appears twice, fresh in the beans and powdered in the sauce. Both are small amounts and both are noticeable.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Taking the oil to smoking point and half-bleaching it, which gives you neither sauce.
    • Stopping the sauce while it is still wet.
    • Adding more water to the beans than the 70 cl.
    • Expecting the colour and oiliness of the bleached version.
    • Mashing the beans cold.

    Health Benefits

    • Unbleached red palm oil retains its carotenoids and vitamin E, which bleaching degrades.
    • Far lower in total fat than the bleached-oil version.
    • Cowpeas supply plant protein, folate and soluble fibre.
    • Ginger has documented anti-inflammatory activity.

    Cultural Note

    That two careful Nigerian cooks publish ewa agoyin with and without bleached oil, and that one of them names bleaching as essential while the other rules it out, is the clearest single example in this collection of a dish whose identity is genuinely unsettled. The unbleached version is healthier and faster; the bleached one tastes like the street. Both are honestly ewa agoyin.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 4 days refrigerated. Keep the sauce separate.

    Reheating: Low heat, loosening the beans with water.

    Serve with: Agege Bread, Dodo (fried plantain), Boiled yam
