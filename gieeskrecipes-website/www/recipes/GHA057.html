🟤 Kaaklo

Kaaklo — kaklo, plantain fritters

Two chefs' version bound with half a cup of toasted corn flour, with selim, calabash nutmeg and star anise a tablespoon each.

The two-chef version of plantain fritters, and the one that uses toasted corn flour as the binder rather than wheat flour or nothing at all. Four over-ripe plantains, half a small onion, two inches of ginger, two habaneros and then a tablespoon each of selim peppers, calabash nutmeg and star anise — a very aromatic trio. Everything but the flour and oil is blended, the half cup of toasted corn flour is stirred in, and quarter-cup scoops fry five to ten minutes, flipping halfway, until the fritters are nicely golden brown.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Deep pan, Quarter-cup measure or scoop, Slotted spoon

  • 4 over-ripe plantains
  • 1/2 small onion
  • 2 inch piece ginger, peeled
  • 2 habanero peppers
  • 1 tbsp selim peppers
  • 1 tbsp calabash nutmeg
  • 1 tbsp star anise
  • 1/2 cup toasted corn flour
  • 2 cups vegetable oil
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Blend everything except the toasted corn flour and the oil.
  2. Stir the toasted corn flour into the blended mixture.
  3. Heat the oil for frying.
  4. Scoop about a quarter cup of batter per fritter into the oil.
  5. Fry 5 to 10 minutes, flipping halfway, until the fritters are nicely golden brown.
  6. Drain and serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Toasted corn flour as the binder, half a cup to four plantains. It is the same ablemanu that binds ayikple and aprapransa, and it gives a nuttier fritter than wheat flour.
  • A tablespoon each of selim, calabash nutmeg and star anise is a lot of aromatic for four plantains, and it is what makes this version distinctive.
  • Quarter-cup scoops, five to ten minutes, flipping halfway. This is the only documented plantain fritter with both a size and a time.
  • Nicely golden brown is the cue. Darker is fine; raw in the middle is the failure, which is why the time window is generous.
  • Star anise is unusual in Ghanaian cooking and is this version's outlier — it is not in any other documented fritter.
  • Two habaneros for four plantains makes these genuinely hot. Reduce if that is not what you want.
  • Two chefs are credited on the source, which is rare — most Ghanaian recipes in print carry one byline or none.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using plain cornmeal instead of toasted corn flour.
  • Scoops much larger than a quarter cup, which stay raw inside.
  • Frying at high heat for a short time.
  • Merely ripe plantain.
  • Reducing the aromatics to a pinch each.

Health Benefits

  • Over-ripe plantain is high in potassium and vitamin A.
  • Toasted corn flour adds fibre and B vitamins.
  • Selim and calabash nutmeg both carry documented aromatic compounds.
  • Vegan and gluten-free.

Cultural Note

That the clearest frying instructions for a Ghanaian street fritter come from a local American television segment with two named chefs is a reminder of where diaspora cooking gets documented. The toasted corn flour binder is the detail worth keeping — it links this snack to aprapransa and ayikple through one shared ingredient.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Same day. They soften quickly.

Reheating: A hot oven briefly.

Serve with: Bambara nuts, Red Red, Shito

TWO CHEFS are credited; the display author field carries the first. Cook time as printed: 30 minutes. Quantities confirmed across two fetches (TWO-PASS).
  • Ingredients exactly as printed, in the source's own terse format: 'Over ripe plantains 4; Onion ½ of a small; Ginger, peeled 2 inch; Habanero peppers 2; Selim peppers 1 Tbs; Calabash nutmeg 1 Tbs; Star anise 1 Tbs; Salt tt; Toasted corn flour ½ Cup; Vegetable oil 2 Cups.'
  • PRINT FLAGS, recorded as printed: 'tt' for salt, which is shorthand for 'to taste'; and 'better' appearing where 'batter' is meant.
  • Method as printed: blend everything except flour and oil; stir in the flour; fry scoops of about ¼ cup '5 to 10 minutes flipping halfway until your fritters are nice and golden brown'.
  • THE DONENESS CUE, as printed: 'nice and golden brown', with a 5-to-10-minute window and a stated scoop size. This is the ONLY documented Ghanaian plantain fritter that gives both a portion size and a frying time, which is why it is published alongside the other two.
  • NO YIELD is printed. The four servings above is an editorial reading of four plantains at quarter-cup scoops.
  • THE THREE DOCUMENTED FRITTERS AND THEIR BINDERS, unreconciled: TOASTED CORN FLOUR here, half a cup; NO FLOUR AT ALL in Fafa Gilbert's Ewe version; and ½ cup all-purpose flour plus ½ teaspoon baking powder in Sandhya Ramakrishnan's adaptation. Three named sources, three different binders, and the resulting fritters are substantially different in texture. Nothing is averaged.
  • STAR ANISE is an outlier in the Ghanaian spice record and appears in no other documented recipe in this collection. It is reproduced as printed.
  • ON THE NAMES: kaaklo, kaklo, kakro and krakro all appear across sources for what is recognisably the same fritter, with krakro given as the Akan name and kaklo as the Ga one by some sources. No source consulted sets out the orthography, so each entry keeps the spelling its own source printed.
  • A HISTORICAL VERSION REFUSED: Whitby 1968, the CSIR Food Research Institute bulletin, has a 'Kaklo (Ewe)' entry which is a FERMENTED DOUGH. It was refused under this collection's fermentation rule because no observable endpoint is given. So the oldest recorded form of this fritter is a ferment that cannot be published here, and all three published versions are unfermented.
  • NOT GA-SPECIFIC: neither this version nor the Ewe one is attributed to the Ga by its source, despite kaklo being given elsewhere as the Ga name. The national attribution reflects that.
