Pesce all Acqua Pazza

Whole fish gently poached in a light, brothy tomato and white wine 'crazy water,' a wonderfully simple southern Italian technique that keeps delicate fish moist and flavorful with minimal effort.

The playful name 'crazy water' is said to reference the improvised, bubbling poaching liquid fishermen would prepare right on their boats using seawater, garlic, and whatever tomatoes were on hand, a technique now beloved in home kitchens for its simplicity and reliability.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide, deep pan with lid

  • 1 whole fish (such as sea bass or bream), about 1kg/2.2lb, cleaned
  • 3 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 1 dried chili, crumbled
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 150 ml (⅔ cup) dry white wine
  • 400 ml (1⅔ cups) water or light fish stock
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Fresh parsley, chopped
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the olive oil in a wide, deep pan and sauté the garlic and chili until fragrant.
  2. Add the cherry tomatoes and cook for a few minutes until they begin to soften.
  3. Pour in the wine and let it reduce slightly, then add the water or stock.
  4. Bring to a gentle simmer, season with salt, and carefully lower in the whole fish.
  5. Cover and poach gently for 15–20 minutes, spooning the broth over the fish occasionally, until just cooked through.
  6. Finish with fresh parsley and serve the fish whole, spooning the flavorful broth generously over.

Chef Tips

  • A gentle simmer, not a rolling boil, is essential to keep the fish tender and intact.
  • Spooning the broth over the fish periodically while it cooks helps it cook evenly.
  • This technique works beautifully with almost any whole fish, adjusting cooking time to size.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling too vigorously, which can break apart the delicate fish.
  • Overcooking, which dries out the fish despite the moist poaching liquid.
  • Using a thin, underseasoned broth that doesn't provide enough flavor.

Cultural Note

Pesce all'acqua pazza reflects the resourceful, improvised cooking of southern Italian fishermen, its playful 'crazy water' name capturing the dish's simple, bubbling origins right on the boat.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; not suited to storage or reheating.

Reheating: Not recommended; best enjoyed immediately after cooking.

Serve with: Crusty bread for the broth, A crisp white wine
