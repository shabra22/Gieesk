Shito — black pepper sauce

The cue is exact — the contents turn from deep red to very dark brown and the oil rises to the surface when the sauce is ready.

Shito is the black chilli-and-dried-fish paste that sits beside everything in Ghana, and every household has its own recipe. This one is built on half a litre of oil, three red onions, three garlic cloves, eight ground guinea peppers, three inches of ginger and seventy-five grams of green kpakpo shito chillies with their seeds, then a hundred and twenty-five grams of chilli powder, fifty grams of dried prawn powder and fifty of crayfish or smoked fish powder. It cooks on low thirty to forty minutes, stirring almost continuously, until it turns from deep red to very dark brown and the oil rises.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-based pan, Wooden spoon, Blender (optional), Sterilised jars

  • 500 ml rapeseed oil, or sunflower or vegetable oil, plus extra if required
  • 3 red onions, finely diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, very finely chopped
  • 8 guinea peppers, ground
  • 7.5 cm piece fresh root ginger, finely grated
  • 1 tbsp chopped thyme or rosemary leaves (optional)
  • 75 g green kpakpo shito chillies, finely diced with the seeds — or green scotch bonnets, or 2 tbsp dried chilli flakes
  • 5-6 tbsp tomato purée
  • 100 ml good-quality chicken stock
  • 125 g chilli powder
  • 50 g dried ground prawn or shrimp powder
  • 50 g crayfish or smoked fish powder
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp sea salt

Method

  1. Fry the onions 2 to 3 minutes, until translucent.
  2. Add the garlic, ground guinea peppers, ginger, the optional herb and the chillies, and fry a few minutes.
  3. Stir in the tomato purée and the stock to make a thick paste.
  4. Add the chilli powder and cook, stirring, 10 minutes.
  5. Add the prawn and crayfish powders.
  6. Cook on low 30 to 40 minutes, stirring almost continuously.
  7. The contents should transform from deep red to very dark brown and the oil will rise to the surface when the sauce is ready.
  8. Blend if you prefer it smooth.
  9. Jar with about half an inch of oil on top. Keeps in the fridge up to a month.

Chef Tips

  • The colour change is the cue and it is unmistakable: deep red to very dark brown, with oil rising to the surface. Every documented shito gives the same endpoint in different words.
  • Stirring almost continuously for the last thirty to forty minutes. There is no way round it — the solids will catch and burn otherwise, and burnt shito is unsalvageable.
  • Half an inch of oil on top in the jar. That oil seal is what makes shito keep a month in the fridge, so do not skim it off.
  • Kpakpo shito chillies WITH THE SEEDS — the exclamation mark is hers. The seeds are a deliberate part of the heat.
  • A hundred and twenty-five grams of chilli powder is a great deal and it is this version's main heat source, rather than fresh scotch bonnets. A long-cook version uses seven scotch bonnets and no chilli powder at all.
  • Guinea peppers are grains of paradise. Eight, ground, is the printed amount.
  • Documented cooking times run from about fifty minutes to about three hours. This is the quick one; the long one is at Shito, the Long Cook and tastes deeper.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving it to cook unstirred.
  • Stopping while it is still red.
  • Skimming the oil layer off before storing.
  • Deseeding the kpakpo shito.
  • Rushing the final low simmer on higher heat.

Health Benefits

  • Dried prawn and crayfish powders are very high in protein, calcium and iodine.
  • Capsaicin from the chilli has documented metabolic and analgesic activity.
  • Ginger and garlic both contribute anti-inflammatory compounds.
  • A small spoonful goes a long way, so the per-serving oil is modest.

Cultural Note

A jar of shito travels with Ghanaians everywhere — it is the thing sent in suitcases to relatives abroad, and the thing a student takes to boarding school. That the headnote concedes every household has its own recipe is the honest position: the three documented versions share a colour change and a dried-shellfish base and almost nothing else.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Up to a month refrigerated under its oil layer, per the author.

Reheating: Not needed. Shito is served at room temperature or cold.

Serve with: Kenkey, Banku, Fried fish
