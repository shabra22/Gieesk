🍝 Ethiopian Minced Meat Sauce

ምንጭ አብሽ — Minchet Abish

Ethiopian Minchet Abish — seasoned minced beef cooked with berbere, kibbeh, onions, tomatoes, and a touch of niter kibbeh into a thick, richly spiced meat sauce eaten with injera or ayib. Ethiopia's everyday minced meat dish — less ceremonial than kitfo, more accessible, deeply satisfying.

Minchet Abish is the everyday minced beef dish of Ethiopia — cooked through (unlike kitfo), sauced (unlike tibs), and deeply spiced with berbere. It is the weekday version of the celebration dishes — completely satisfying and far simpler to prepare.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pan

  • 500g minced beef
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 2 tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • 2 tbsp berbere
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • ½ cup water
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Dry caramelise onions — 15 minutes.
  2. Add kibbeh. Add berbere. Toast 2 minutes.
  3. Add garlic, ginger, tomato paste, and tomatoes. Cook 5 minutes.
  4. Add minced beef. Break up. Brown thoroughly — 8 minutes.
  5. Add water. Simmer 20 minutes until thick.
  6. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Break the mince up thoroughly — no lumps.
  • The sauce should be thick enough to scoop with injera.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Lumpy mince.
  • Too thin — cook until thick.

The Science

See ETH026 for Key Wat cooking science. The difference with Minchet Abish is the surface area — minced beef has dramatically more surface area than cubed beef, so the Maillard reaction occurs much faster and the berbere permeates the meat more rapidly.

Cultural Note

Minchet Abish is the weekday workhorse of Ethiopian cooking — prepared quickly, deeply satisfying, and eaten by everyone from students to elders. It is the dish that makes injera irresistible even on the plainest day.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Ayib (ETH096), As part of a combination platter
