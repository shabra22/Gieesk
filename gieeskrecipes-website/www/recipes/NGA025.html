🤍 Ekuru

Ekuru — white moin moin

Plain peeled beans blended semi-smooth and beaten ten to fifteen minutes with a mixer — or thirty by hand — until very fluffy, then steamed to a crumble.

Ekuru is moin moin with everything taken out. No peppers, no oil, no fish, no seasoning in the batter at all — just peeled beans, blended deliberately not-too-smooth and then worked until they are frothy. The target texture is crumbly, and that is entirely a function of how much air you beat in: under-aerate and you get moin moin texture or hard ekuru, which is the author's own warning. The pepper and the oil live in a separate sauce eaten with it, either ata dindin or ebatu.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Hand mixer, or a wooden spoon or ladle, Steamer or deep pot, Banana leaves, ramekins or foil

  • 3 cups black-eyed or brown beans
  • Water, for soaking and blending

For wrapping

  • Banana leaves (moin moin leaves), ramekins, or foil

Method

  1. Soak and peel the beans.
  2. Blend with a little water till semi-smooth — that is, not too smooth.
  3. Mix with a hand mixer 10 to 15 minutes until very fluffy and frothy. With a wooden spoon or ladle instead, mix for 30 minutes.
  4. Scoop the batter into banana leaves, ramekins or foil and seal.
  5. Steam till fully cooked, roughly 30 minutes on medium heat.
  6. Serve crumbled, with ata dindin or ebatu.

Chef Tips

  • Semi-smooth, not smooth. This is the opposite target from moin moin, where the author blends very fine and frothy. The slightly coarser grind is part of what gives ekuru its crumble.
  • Ten to fifteen minutes with a mixer, thirty by hand. Ekuru gets more beating than any other bean dish in the repertoire and that is the point.
  • No salt in the batter. The author's own ingredient list mentions salt but the method never adds it — the seasoning comes entirely from the sauce.
  • The failure mode is stated plainly: if the batter is not fluffy enough you end up with moin moin texture, or hard ekuru.
  • Eaten with the hands. Crumble it, spoon the sauce over, mix it on the plate, and the author's line is worth quoting — 'so starts forming "Butter"'.
  • Ramekins work but leaves are better. The leaf is the only flavour this dish gets from its wrapper.
  • If you are making ebatu, the vegetables steam in the same pot alongside the ekuru for the first fifteen minutes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Blending the beans too smooth.
  • Under-beating, which gives dense ekuru.
  • Seasoning the batter, which makes it moin moin.
  • Steaming submerged rather than above the water.
  • Serving it whole in a slab instead of crumbled.

Cultural Note

Ekuru is the plainest thing in the Yoruba repertoire and it exists to carry a sauce. That separation — bland steamed protein on one side, a fiercely fried pepper sauce on the other, combined by the eater — is a recurring shape in Yoruba cooking, and it is the same logic as ewa agoyin, where plain mashed beans meet a dark pepper sauce made separately.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated in the wrapping.

Reheating: Steam 10 minutes. It firms up cold and softens again with steam.

Serve with: Ata Dindin, Ebatu, Eko
