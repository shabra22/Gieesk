Sagnitegli con Fagioli

Molise's own hand-cut ribbon pasta, simmered directly into a hearty bean and vegetable broth in the same rustic tradition found throughout Italy's central-southern mountain regions.

This dish shares its spirit closely with Basilicata's lagane e ceci, using Molise's own hand-cut pasta simmered directly in a bean-based broth, reflecting the shared cucina povera traditions of Italy's mountainous interior regions.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Large pot

  • 300 g (2 1/4 cups) durum wheat flour
  • 150 ml (2/3 cup) warm water
  • Pinch of salt
  • 300 g (1 1/2 cups) dried borlotti beans, soaked overnight
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 carrot, chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 dried chili, crumbled
  • 400 g (14 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 80 ml (1/3 cup) extra virgin olive oil, plus more for serving
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the flour, water, and salt into a firm dough, kneading until smooth. Rest, covered, for 20 minutes.
  2. Drain the soaked beans and simmer in fresh water until tender, about 1 hour, reserving the cooking liquid.
  3. Warm the olive oil in a large pot and saute the onion, carrot, and celery until softened, then add the garlic and chili.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes and cooked beans along with their reserved liquid, and simmer for 15 minutes.
  5. Roll the pasta dough out thin and cut into wide, irregular ribbons.
  6. Add the fresh pasta directly to the simmering bean broth and cook for 3-4 minutes until tender.
  7. Season to taste and serve hot with a drizzle of olive oil.

Chef Tips

  • Reserving and using the bean cooking liquid gives the dish real depth that plain water alone will not provide.
  • The rustic, irregular shape of the hand-cut pasta is entirely traditional - do not aim for perfect uniformity.
  • This dish should be brothy and substantial, closer to a hearty soup than a saucy pasta dish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Discarding the bean cooking liquid, missing a key source of flavor.
  • Making the pasta too thick, which extends the cooking time and can throw off the dish balance.
  • Serving it too dry - this dish should have plenty of brothy liquid.

Cultural Note

Sagnitegli con fagioli reflects Molise's rustic, mountain cucina povera tradition, closely related to similar hand-cut pasta and bean dishes found throughout central-southern Italy.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; thickens further as it sits.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding water to loosen as needed.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A drizzle of good olive oil at the table
