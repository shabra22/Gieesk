🐟 Kenyan Garlic Butter Fish

Samaki wa Siagi na Vitunguu Saumu

Kenyan Garlic Butter Fish — tilapia or white fish fillets pan-seared until golden and crispy, then finished with a fragrant garlic butter sauce with lemon, fresh coriander, and a hint of chilli. Simple, luxurious, and ready in under 20 minutes.

Garlic butter fish is the Kenyan kitchen at its most elegant and effortless — a simple pan sauce of butter, garlic, lemon, and coriander transforms a piece of fish into something restaurant-worthy in minutes. It has become a favourite in Nairobi restaurants and urban homes for exactly this reason.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large frying pan, Spatula

  • 2 tilapia fillets or white fish fillets (about 500g total)
  • 4 tbsp butter
  • 5 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 tbsp fresh coriander — chopped
  • ½ tsp chilli flakes — optional
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Pat fish dry and season with salt, pepper, and paprika.
  2. Heat oil in a pan over high heat. Add fish fillets skin-side down.
  3. Cook without moving for 3—4 minutes until golden and crispy on the bottom.
  4. Flip carefully. Cook for 2—3 minutes on the other side.
  5. Remove fish and set aside.
  6. Reduce heat to medium. Add butter to the same pan.
  7. When butter is melted and foaming, add garlic and chilli flakes. Cook for 30 seconds until fragrant — do not burn.
  8. Add lemon juice. Swirl to combine.
  9. Return fish to the pan. Spoon the garlic butter sauce over the fish.
  10. Scatter with coriander and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • A hot pan and dry fish are the secrets to a good sear — no moisture, maximum heat.
  • Do not move the fish during the first sear — let the crust form naturally.
  • The garlic butter sauce comes together in under 1 minute — have everything ready before you start.
  • Brown butter (cook until it turns golden and smells nutty) makes this extraordinary.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Wet fish — it steams instead of searing.
  • Burning the garlic — 30 seconds maximum on medium heat.
  • Moving the fish too early — it tears.

The Science

When butter reaches around 150°C it begins to brown (beurre noisette) — the milk solids caramelise through the Maillard reaction, developing a complex, nutty aroma and flavour that plain butter does not have. This is why garlic butter sauce made in a hot pan tastes so much more complex than butter simply melted.

Cultural Note

Garlic butter fish represents the contemporary urban Kenyan kitchen — drawing on classic technique with local ingredients to create something that feels both luxurious and accessible.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended — garlic butter fish is best fresh.

Serve with: Roasted Sweet Potatoes, Steamed Rice, Kachumbari, Garlic Bread, Steamed Greens
