🐟 Zaramo Coconut Fish Stew

Mchuzi wa Samaki wa Mizizi — Wazaramo

Zaramo Coconut Fish Stew — whole fish pieces simmered with cassava, local greens, and coconut milk in the traditional Zaramo style of the Dar es Salaam coast. The original coastal fish stew of Tanzania's capital region — before the city, there was this.

Zaramo fish stew predates Dar es Salaam as a city — the Wazaramo cooked this dish on the coast centuries before the city was founded. The combination of coastal fish with cassava from the hinterland and coconut from the coastal palms is distinctly Zaramo — neither purely inland nor purely coastal, but the cuisine of the people who bridge both worlds.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Zaramo people of the Dar es Salaam hinterland — the original inhabitants of Tanzania's capital region

The Zaramo (Wazaramo) are the Bantu people who originally inhabited the coastal area where Dar es Salaam now stands — they are the indigenous people of Tanzania's capital and its surroundings. Their traditional cuisine blends coastal fish and coconut with inland cassava and green vegetables from the coastal forest. The Zaramo coconut fish stew, made with roots and greens foraged from the coastal forest alongside fish from the Indian Ocean, represents the original food culture of what is now the country's largest city.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid

  • 600g whole fish pieces — tilapia or snapper, bone-in
  • 200g cassava — peeled and cubed
  • 2 cups mixed greens — sweet potato leaves or spinach
  • 300ml coconut milk
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ½ cup water

Method

  1. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden. Add garlic, turmeric, tomatoes. Cook 6 minutes.
  2. Add cassava and water. Cover and cook 15 minutes until nearly tender.
  3. Add coconut milk. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  4. Add fish pieces. Spoon sauce over.
  5. Cover and simmer gently 12 minutes.
  6. Add greens on top. Cover 3 more minutes until wilted.
  7. Season with salt.

Chef Tips

  • Add greens at the very end — they need only 3 minutes.
  • Never boil after coconut milk is added.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling coconut milk.
  • Over-cooking fish.

Cultural Note

Zaramo coconut fish stew is the original food culture of Dar es Salaam — what was eaten on this coast before the city was built, before the port was developed, before anyone called this place by its Arab name. It is Tanzania's urban origin story told through food.

Storage: Best eaten fresh.
