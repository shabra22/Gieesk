Brovada e Musetto

A distinctive Friulian dish pairing a coarse pork sausage with fermented turnips, whose tangy, sour character comes from a lengthy marination in grape pomace left over from wine production.

Brovada's unusual sour flavor comes from turnips fermented for weeks in the leftover skins and seeds from winemaking, a practical way to preserve the vegetable through winter that also produces a genuinely distinctive tang unlike anything else in Italian cooking.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot for the sausage, Separate pan for the turnips

  • 1 musetto sausage (or a coarse pork sausage), about 500g/1.1lb
  • 600 g (1.3 lb) brovada (fermented turnips) or substitute sauerkraut
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tbsp lard or olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Prick the musetto sausage all over and simmer gently in water for about 1.5–2 hours until tender, following any specific package instructions.
  2. Meanwhile, drain the brovada and rinse briefly if very sour.
  3. Warm the lard or oil in a pan and sauté the onion until softened.
  4. Add the brovada and cook over medium heat, stirring occasionally, for 25–30 minutes until tender, adding a splash of water if it looks dry.
  5. Season the brovada with salt and pepper.
  6. Once the sausage is tender, remove and slice thickly.
  7. Serve the sliced sausage alongside a generous portion of the brovada.

Chef Tips

  • True brovada, fermented in grape pomace, has a genuinely distinctive tang — sauerkraut is a reasonable but milder substitute if unavailable.
  • Follow specific cooking instructions for musetto sausage, as timing can vary by brand and size.
  • Rinse the brovada briefly before cooking if it tastes overwhelmingly sour for your preference.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the sausage until it splits and loses its texture.
  • Not cooking the brovada long enough, leaving it too raw and overly sharp in flavor.
  • Substituting a very different pickled vegetable without adjusting for the milder or sharper tang.

Health Benefits

  • Fermented vegetables like brovada provide probiotics; a good source of protein from the sausage.

Cultural Note

Brovada e musetto is a defining winter dish of Friuli, its fermented turnips a practical historical method for preserving vegetables through the cold months using leftover winemaking byproducts.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop.

Serve with: Polenta, A robust red wine from Friuli
