🍚 Yakayake

Yakayake — amo yakayake

Coarse cassava dough with corn flour, steamed about fifteen minutes over a perforated lid — and readiness is indicated by the vapour escaping.

Yakayake is a Volta Region staple and the dough is the thing to get right: coarser than banku dough but the same as gari dough. Cassava is peeled, milled, drained in a basket and then packed into a sack to drain further under a rock. Corn flour and salt are mixed in thoroughly. A quantity of the mixture is poured onto a pot of water covered with a perforated lid and a fine lace, and steamed over boiling water about fifteen minutes. Readiness is indicated by the vapour escaping. The process repeats until the desired amount is cooked.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mill or grater, Draining basket, Sack and a heavy rock, Pot with a perforated lid, Fine lace or muslin

  • Cassava, peeled and milled
  • Corn flour
  • Salt

No quantities are published. See the note.

    Method

    1. Peel and mill the cassava.
    2. Drain it in a basket.
    3. Pack it into a sack and drain further under a rock.
    4. Add corn flour and salt to the dough and mix thoroughly — the dough should be coarser than banku dough, the same as gari dough.
    5. Bring a pot of water to the boil and cover it with a perforated lid and a fine lace.
    6. Pour a quantity of the mixture onto the lace.
    7. Steam about 15 minutes.

    ENDPOINT: readiness is indicated by the vapour escaping.

    1. Repeat until the desired amount is cooked.
    2. Serve with fish and chilli pepper, or a green leafy soup, or beans.

    Chef Tips

    • The vapour escaping is the readiness cue and it is a genuinely clever one — while the dough is raw it blocks the steam, and when it is cooked the vapour comes through.
    • Coarser than banku dough, the same as gari dough. That texture comparison is the most useful thing in the source, because you can check it against things you know.
    • Drain under a rock after the basket. Two stages of draining, and the dough will not hold together if you skip the second.
    • A perforated lid with fine lace over it is the traditional steamer. Any steamer insert lined with muslin will do the same job.
    • Fifteen minutes per batch, repeated. This is cooked in portions rather than all at once.
    • Corn flour is mixed into the cassava dough, so yakayake is not pure cassava. The proportion is not published.
    • Eaten with fish and chilli, or with amadetsi or amatadi — green leafy soup or stew — or with beans.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Milling the cassava too fine, giving banku dough rather than gari dough.
    • Only one draining stage.
    • Steaming without a cloth, so the dough falls through.
    • Cooking it all in one layer rather than in batches.
    • Ignoring the vapour cue and going by the clock alone.

    Health Benefits

    • Gluten-free and fat-free.
    • Cassava supplies resistant starch.
    • The double draining removes some water-soluble cyanogenic compounds.
    • Steamed rather than fried.

    Cultural Note

    A staple whose doneness is read from the steam coming through it is a nice piece of practical engineering, and the name — dished by a senseless person, after a vendor's own joke — is among the better food etymologies on record. That the Ewe name for it also attaches to the Ga Homowo dish kpokpoi suggests a shared origin nobody has traced.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Same day. It dries out.

    Reheating: Re-steam.

    Serve with: Fish and chilli pepper, Ademe Detsi, Beans
