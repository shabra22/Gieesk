🔥 Shisa Nyama Braaied Wors

Shisa nyama — braaivleis

A whole boerewors coil skewered crosswise so it can be turned in one piece, braaied on coals you can hold your hand over for five to eight seconds, never pierced, never pressed, and rested three minutes before it is cut.

Shisa nyama is isiZulu for burn the meat, and it names the fire, the gathering around it and the place itself — township butcheries that started braaiing on Saturdays to move stock and became social institutions. You pick raw meat at the counter and it goes straight onto the coals. Wors is the centre of it, and the whole method is about heat and restraint: medium coals rather than steak heat, tongs rather than a fork, and no pressing down with a spatula, because everything you squeeze out is what you came for.

Ingredients

Equipment: Braai with wood or charcoal coals, Grid, or coals for direct cooking, 2 long metal skewers, Tongs — not a fork

  • 1 whole boerewors coil, about 1 kg (Boerewors)

Equipment rather than ingredients

  • 2 long metal skewers

To serve

  • Pap or Uphuthu
  • Chakalaka
  • Fresh white bread
  • Potato salad

Method

  1. Build a fire and let it burn down to coals with no flames. Bigger is better — you want a settled bed, not a blaze.
  2. Test the heat: hold your hand about 10 cm above the grid and count. Five to eight seconds is medium, and medium is what wors wants. Two to three seconds is steak heat and it is too hot for this.
  3. Keep the coil whole. Do not cut it into lengths — the loop holds the juices in.
  4. Push the two metal skewers all the way through the coil at 90 degrees to each other, so you can turn the whole thing in one piece with tongs.
  5. Lay the coil on the grid and braai for 10 to 15 minutes, turning it regularly with tongs so no side crisps or burns.
  6. Never pierce it with a fork and never press it with a spatula.
  7. It is ready when the outside is golden brown and the inside is cooked through but still juicy.
  8. Take it off, rest it for 2 to 3 minutes, then cut and serve with pap, chakalaka and bread.

Chef Tips

  • The hand test is the single most useful thing here: five to eight seconds at 10 cm above the grid. Wors wants noticeably cooler coals than steak.
  • The two crossed skewers are the trick that keeps a coil from breaking up when you turn it, and they let you cook it directly on open coals if you want to.
  • No fork, ever. Every hole you make is juice on the coals instead of in the sausage.
  • No pressing. A spatula squeezes out exactly what you are cooking for.
  • No South African source gives an internal temperature for boerewors — every one of them goes by look and feel. Golden outside, cooked but juicy inside, and a spring rather than a squish when you press the tongs against it.
  • Two genuinely different approaches exist: about 6 minutes hot and fast directly on open coals with a single turn, or 10 to 15 minutes on a grid over medium coals turning regularly. The gentler one is more forgiving.
  • The three-minute rest matters as much as it does for a steak.
  • Scrape off any coal that has stuck to it before serving.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Braaiing over flames or over steak-hot coals.
  • Cutting the coil into lengths first.
  • Piercing it to check it.
  • Pressing it down with a spatula.
  • Turning it once and hoping — regular turning is what cooks it evenly.
  • Cutting into it the second it leaves the fire.

Health Benefits

  • High in protein, iron, zinc and B12.
  • Cooked over coals, so a good deal of the fat renders out.
  • Boerewors is high in fat and salt by definition. This is celebration food, and it is honest about it.

Cultural Note

Shisa nyama started as a Saturday sales tactic at township butcheries and turned into one of the few genuinely shared social institutions in the country: Gugulethu, Soweto, Durban, Pretoria, everywhere. You choose your meat raw at the counter, it goes onto the coals, and you eat it standing up with pap and chakalaka. The word means burn the meat, and it refers to the whole occasion rather than the cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Slice and warm briefly in a pan. It will not be the same and everyone knows it.

Serve with: Pap and Chakalaka, Braaibroodjie, Fresh white bread and tomato relish
