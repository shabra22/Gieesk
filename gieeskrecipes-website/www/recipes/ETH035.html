🍵 Ethiopian Lamb Soup

የበግ ሾርባ — Ye-Beg Shorba

Ethiopian Lamb Soup — lamb bones and meat simmered slowly with Ethiopian aromatic spices into a light, deeply flavourful broth. The celebration soup — served at weddings and religious feasts as the opener, before the main wats appear.

Lamb shorba is the Ethiopian celebration soup — lighter and more fragrant than beef shorba, it is served as the opening of a lamb celebration feast. The lamb bones produce a cleaner, more delicately flavoured broth than beef.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 1 kg lamb bones and meat — ribs and shoulder
  • 2 litres water
  • 1 onion — halved
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • 2 tsp salt
  • Fresh parsley and lime

Method

  1. See ETH034 — substitute lamb bones for beef.
  2. Lamb fat is lighter than beef fat — the broth will be less rich.
  3. Simmer 75—80 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • Cardamom is the distinction for lamb shorba — it pairs beautifully with lamb.
  • Lamb broth is lighter in colour than beef — a paler golden.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not skimming foam.

The Science

Lamb fat has a higher proportion of shorter-chain fatty acids than beef — these are more flavourful and more aromatic when dissolved into the broth, producing the characteristic fragrance of lamb shorba.

Health Benefits

  • Collagen from lamb bones.
  • Cardamom aids digestion.
  • Light and easily digestible.

Cultural Note

Lamb shorba at an Ethiopian celebration is the announcement that something wonderful is coming — the light, fragrant soup that opens the appetite before the great wats arrive.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Bring to boil.

Serve with: As the celebration opener, With injera on the side
