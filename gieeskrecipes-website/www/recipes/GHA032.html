🫑 Chalé Sauce with Roasted Peppers

Chalé sauce

The later printing — six hundred grams of fresh tomato and two roasted red peppers, simmered until the tomato tartness diminishes.

The same sauce as printed three years later, and it has changed substantially. The fresh tomato option more than doubles, from two hundred and fifty grams to six hundred; two roasted red bell peppers appear, which were not there before; and the chilli flakes drop from a tablespoon to half a teaspoon. The roasted peppers make it rounder and sweeter — closer to the pepper-forward bases used across West Africa and further from a straight tomato sauce. The cue is unchanged and still the best part: simmer thirty-five to forty minutes until the tomato tartness diminishes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Saucepan, Grill or oven, for the peppers

  • 400 g tin tomatoes, or 600 g fresh tomatoes
  • 2 roasted red bell peppers
  • 30 g tomato purée
  • 1 small white onion, roughly diced
  • 5 cm piece fresh root ginger, grated
  • 1 small red scotch bonnet chilli — use half and deseed it if you have a low heat tolerance
  • 1/2 tsp dried chilli flakes — see the note on the missing unit
  • 3 garlic cloves (optional)
  • Sea salt, to taste

To cook it

  • 1 tbsp cooking oil
  • 1 onion, finely diced
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp extra-hot chilli powder

Method

  1. Roast the red bell peppers and remove the skins.
  2. Blend the tomatoes, roasted peppers, tomato purée, onion, ginger, scotch bonnet, chilli flakes, garlic and salt to a fairly smooth paste.

This is the uncooked chalé sauce.

  1. To cook it: heat the oil and sauté the finely diced onion until softened.
  2. Add the curry powder and extra-hot chilli powder.
  3. Add the blended mixture.
  4. Simmer 35 to 40 minutes, until the tomato tartness diminishes.

Chef Tips

  • Roast the peppers properly and skin them. Raw red pepper blended in gives a thin, vegetal note; roasted gives the sweetness this version is reaching for.
  • Until the tomato tartness diminishes, same as the earlier printing. Thirty-five to forty minutes and taste rather than watch.
  • The heat guidance is the author's own and worth following: use half the scotch bonnet and deseed it if your tolerance is low.
  • Six hundred grams of fresh tomato against two hundred and fifty in the earlier printing. If you have made the old version, this will taste noticeably milder and fuller.
  • Half a teaspoon of chilli flakes, down from a tablespoon. With the extra-hot chilli powder still at a teaspoon in the cooking stage, the total heat is similar but less raw.
  • One printing of this version omits salt from the list entirely while another includes it to taste. Salt it — the earlier printing used a full teaspoon.
  • Use it anywhere the earlier version is called for. The volumes are the same even though the composition is not.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Blending the peppers raw.
  • Leaving the salt out because one printing omits it.
  • A whole undeseeded scotch bonnet if you are heat-sensitive.
  • Treating this and the 2017 version as interchangeable in flavour.
  • Rushing the simmer.

Health Benefits

  • Roasted red peppers are exceptionally high in vitamin C and carotenoids.
  • Cooked tomato supplies bioavailable lycopene.
  • Vegan and low in calories.
  • Ginger and garlic contribute anti-inflammatory compounds.

Cultural Note

Watching a cookbook author's foundation sauce change between printings is a small window into how recipes actually develop — the roasted peppers were presumably an improvement she made and kept, and the chilli flakes came down because the rest of the heat was doing enough. Neither printing is a correction of the other.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated, or frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat.

Serve with: Jollof Rice, Groundnut Soup, Grilled fish
