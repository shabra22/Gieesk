🍰 Tiramisù

Tiramisù

Italy's most famous dessert layers espresso-soaked ladyfingers with a silky mascarpone cream, dusted with cocoa — 'pick-me-up' in Italian, and it lives up to the name.

Tiramisù's exact origin is disputed between Treviso and Friuli in the Veneto region, but its formula is beloved worldwide: bitter espresso-soaked biscuits layered with an airy, barely-sweet mascarpone cream, chilled until the layers meld into something greater than the sum of its parts.

Ingredients

Equipment: Double boiler or heatproof bowl, Electric mixer, Shallow dish for dipping, Serving dish, roughly 23x33cm

  • 6 egg yolks
  • 150 g (¾ cup) granulated sugar
  • 500 g (2 cups) mascarpone cheese, at room temperature
  • 300 ml (1¼ cups) strong brewed espresso, cooled
  • 3 tbsp coffee liqueur (optional)
  • 2 packets (about 300g/10.5oz) savoiardi (ladyfinger biscuits)
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder, for dusting

Method

  1. Whisk the egg yolks and sugar together in a heatproof bowl set over a pot of gently simmering water, whisking continuously until pale, thick, and doubled in volume, about 8–10 minutes.
  2. Remove from the heat and let cool slightly, then fold in the mascarpone in two additions until smooth and no lumps remain.
  3. Combine the cooled espresso with the coffee liqueur, if using, in a shallow dish.
  4. Quickly dip each ladyfinger into the espresso mixture, just long enough to soak without turning soggy, and arrange in a single layer in the serving dish.
  5. Spread half the mascarpone cream evenly over the soaked biscuits.
  6. Repeat with a second layer of dipped ladyfingers and the remaining cream.
  7. Cover and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, ideally overnight, to let the layers set and meld.
  8. Just before serving, dust generously with cocoa powder through a fine sieve.

Chef Tips

  • Dip the ladyfingers quickly — a second or two per side is plenty, as they continue absorbing liquid after being layered.
  • Whisking the egg-sugar mixture over gentle heat (a zabaglione-style base) results in a more stable, food-safe cream than using raw eggs.
  • Tiramisù genuinely improves after resting overnight, so make it a day ahead if possible.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Oversoaking the ladyfingers, which turns the finished dessert soggy and collapses its structure.
  • Dusting with cocoa too early, which can absorb into the cream and lose its visual contrast.
  • Serving immediately without chilling time, when the layers haven't yet had a chance to set and meld together.

Health Benefits

  • A source of protein and calcium from the eggs and mascarpone, though best enjoyed as an occasional treat.

Cultural Note

Tiramisù is a relatively modern classic, generally dated to the 1960s-70s in the Veneto region, yet it has become perhaps Italy's single most internationally recognized dessert.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, covered, for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Serve chilled directly from the refrigerator; this dessert is not reheated.

Serve with: A small glass of espresso or dessert wine alongside
