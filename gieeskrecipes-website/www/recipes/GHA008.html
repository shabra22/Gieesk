🍠 Yam Oto

Oto — mashed yam with palm oil

Yam cubed into half-inch pieces, boiled, mashed smooth and worked with fifty millilitres of palm oil and onion.

The yam form of the dish, and the one Osseo-Asare describes as sacred — mashed yam, palm oil and hard-boiled eggs, an Akan as well as a Ga tradition, given without salt or pepper when it is used ceremonially. This version is the plain household method: half a medium yam cut into half-inch cubes, boiled in salted water until cooked, strained, seasoned and mashed until smooth, then worked with fifty millilitres of palm oil and chopped onion and mashed again. Roasted ground nuts and boiled eggs finish it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, Colander, Masher, Knife

  • 1/2 piece medium yam
  • 50 ml palm oil
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • Roasted ground nuts
  • Boiled eggs
  • Seasoning

Method

  1. Peel the yam, cut into half-inch cubes and wash.
  2. Boil in salted water until cooked.
  3. Strain, season, and mash until smooth.
  4. Add the palm oil.
  5. Add the chopped onion.
  6. Mash again until the colour is even.
  7. Top with roasted ground nuts and boiled eggs and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Half-inch cubes, washed after cutting. Washing cut yam removes the surface starch that otherwise makes the mash gluey.
  • Fifty millilitres is about three and a half tablespoons — markedly less oil than the plantain version's seven, so the two are not interchangeable by quantity.
  • Mash smooth BEFORE the oil goes in, then mash again after. Two stages, and the source is specific about the order.
  • Raw chopped onion goes in at the end here, rather than being softened in the oil as the plantain version does. A sharper, more pungent result.
  • Yam varies enormously in moisture. If the mash is stiff, loosen with a spoonful of the cooking water rather than more oil.
  • For the ceremonial form, leave out the salt and pepper entirely. Osseo-Asare records that oto is given plain when it is an offering.
  • No cooking time is printed for the yam here — only 'till cooked'. A second documented yam version gives 7 to 8 minutes, which is short; test with a fork.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not washing the cut yam.
  • Adding the oil before the first mash.
  • Borrowing the plantain version's seven tablespoons of oil.
  • Overworking it into a paste.
  • Salting it when it is intended as an offering.

Health Benefits

  • Red palm oil provides provitamin-A carotenoids and vitamin E tocotrienols.
  • Yam is high in potassium and vitamin C and has a lower glycaemic index than potato.
  • Eggs and groundnuts together supply complete protein.
  • Less oil per serving than the plantain version.

Cultural Note

That the same dish is both a wedding-morning breakfast and a libation offering is the thing to understand about oto. The plain version — no salt, no pepper — is poured or placed rather than served, which is why the seasoning instruction in a household recipe is also, quietly, the line between food and ritual.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Gentle heat with a splash of water. The palm oil re-melts.

Serve with: Boiled eggs, Roasted groundnuts, Sliced avocado
