🥛 Zomkom

Zomkom — zom koom, fula

Three hundred grams of millet flour, a litre of water and five hundred millilitres of fermented whey, spiced with cloves, ginger and dried chillies.

Zomkom is the millet drink of northern Ghana, synonymous with northerners by its author's own description. This version keeps it simple: three hundred grams of millet flour to a litre of water and five hundred millilitres of fermented whey, with ten cloves, fifty grams of ginger, five dried chillies and a hundred grams of honey or sugar. Evaporated milk or natural yoghurt is optional. The whey is bought already fermented, so no fermentation is carried out — she offers a three-day millet soak as an alternative route, and that option is refused here for having no endpoint.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or grinder, Large jug, Sieve, Whisk

  • 300 g millet flour
  • 1 litre water
  • 500 ml fermented whey
  • 10 cloves
  • 50 g ginger
  • 5 dried chillies
  • 100 g honey or sugar
  • 100 ml evaporated milk, or natural yoghurt (optional)

Method

    The published method for this recipe is video-only. No written steps exist in the source.

    1. Grind the cloves, ginger and dried chillies.
    2. Mix the millet flour with the spices.
    3. Whisk in the litre of water to a smooth slurry.
    4. Cook briefly, stirring, if a cooked base is wanted.
    5. Stir in the fermented whey.
    6. Sweeten with the honey or sugar.
    7. Add the evaporated milk or yoghurt if using, and serve cold.

    The order above is inferred from the documented northern millet drinks, not from this source.

      Chef Tips

      • The fermented whey is bought, not made — which is what makes this version publishable. Her alternative route, soaking whole millet three days, carries no endpoint and is not followed here.
      • Ten cloves and fifty grams of ginger for six servings is a strong spicing, and it is what distinguishes zomkom from a plain millet drink.
      • Grind the spices with the millet rather than steeping them. That is the documented practice for the related drinks.
      • Five dried chillies in a sweet milky drink sounds wrong and is correct. The heat sits behind the sweetness.
      • Serve cold. This is a hot-weather drink and it is sold from coolers.
      • Be aware the method here is reconstructed. The author publishes a full ingredient list and no written steps.
      • An older documented northern relative, fula, is quite different — steamed millet balls pounded with pepper and cloves, then mashed into water or milk with sugar. If this recipe seems thin, that is the fuller tradition behind it.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Attempting the three-day millet soak without an endpoint to judge it by.
      • Steeping the spices instead of grinding them in.
      • Leaving out the chillies.
      • Serving it warm.
      • Treating the reconstructed step order as the author's own.

      Health Benefits

      • Millet is gluten-free and higher in iron and magnesium than maize.
      • Fermented whey supplies lactic acid bacteria and protein.
      • Ginger and cloves both carry documented antimicrobial compounds.
      • Hydrating and filling at once, which is why it is a working drink.

      Cultural Note

      Zomkom is sold in bags and bottles across northern Ghana and it is a meal as much as a drink — millet, spice, whey and sugar, cold, in the middle of a hot day. The older fula tradition behind it involved steaming millet balls for an hour before pounding them, which tells you how much labour the modern flour version removes.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: 2 days refrigerated. It separates and needs shaking.

      Reheating: Not applicable. Served cold.

      Serve with: Drunk on its own, cold, Koose, Masa

The dough is then removed and pounded gently in a mortar. Pepper and ground cloves (kanafuri, masoro) are added bit by bit until completely mixed. The dough is then shaped into balls again and rolled in rice or milled flour... The balls are mashed into water or milk; sugar is added and so eaten.'
