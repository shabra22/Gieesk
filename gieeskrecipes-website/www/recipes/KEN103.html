🫓 Kenyan Triangle Chapati

Chapati ya Pembetatu

Kenyan Triangle Chapati — the same beloved Kenyan layered chapati but folded into a triangle before rolling, producing a perfectly triangular flatbread with distinct flaky layers in every section. Visually striking and just as delicious as the classic round version.

Triangle chapati uses the same technique as the classic Kenyan layered chapati — the folding and rolling that creates the characteristic flaky layers — but shapes the bread into a triangle rather than a round. The triangular shape makes it particularly satisfying to tear and eat with stews, and its distinctive appearance has made it popular at restaurants and at home celebrations.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Large flat pan or griddle

  • 3 cups (375g) all-purpose flour — plus extra for dusting
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 cup warm water
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • Oil or ghee for cooking

Method

  1. Combine flour, salt, and sugar. Add 2 tbsp oil. Mix.
  2. Gradually add warm water. Knead for 8—10 minutes until smooth and elastic.
  3. Rest covered for 1 hour.
  4. Divide into 6 equal balls.
  5. Roll each ball thin. Brush with oil. Fold into a triangle: fold one third over, then the other third on top to make a triangle.
  6. Roll the triangle out thin again.
  7. Heat a dry pan over medium heat. Cook the triangle chapati for 2—3 minutes each side, adding a little oil or ghee at the edges.
  8. Press gently with a cloth or spatula as it cooks — this helps the layers puff and separate.
  9. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • The triangle fold is simple: fold like folding a letter into thirds, forming a triangle shape.
  • Brushing with oil between layers is the secret to the flaky, separating layers.
  • Pressing while cooking encourages the layers to separate and creates the characteristic puffed appearance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not enough oil between layers — the chapati stays flat rather than flaky.
  • Too thick — roll thin for the best layered result.

The Science

The oil brushed between dough layers prevents them from fusing during cooking. When the chapati is heated, steam generated between the layers causes them to puff and separate — creating the characteristic flaky texture.

Cultural Note

Triangle chapati has become particularly popular in Kenyan restaurants and urban households for its visual appeal and the satisfying way it tears into distinct layers when pulled apart.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Wrap in foil for up to 3 days. Freeze for 2 months.

Reheating: Warm in dry pan for 1—2 minutes per side, or microwave briefly under a damp cloth.

Serve with: Beef Stew, Egg Curry, Sukuma Wiki, Kenyan Masala Chai, Kachumbari
