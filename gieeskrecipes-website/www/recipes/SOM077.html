🍢 Hilib la Solay

Hilib la Solay — Kebab Hilib

Beef cubed at an inch and a half, marinated in nothing but garlic, cilantro, olive oil, salt and pepper, threaded alternately with peppers, onion and quartered underripe tomato, and grilled twelve minutes turning every three.

The restraint is the surprise here. If you expect a Somali marinade to be heavy with xawaash, this one says otherwise: garlic, cilantro, olive oil, salt, pepper, and nothing else — no spice blend, no yoghurt, no acid. What it does instead is pay attention to the mechanics. The tomatoes are chosen firm and underripe and cored so they hold on the skewer instead of collapsing into the fire, and how long the meat marinates depends entirely on the cut, with tenderloin going straight to the coals and anything tougher sitting overnight.

Ingredients

Equipment: Skewers, Grill or braai with coals, or a griddle pan, Sharp knife

    The meat

    • 1 kg (2.2 lb) beef tenderloin or top sirloin, cut into 4 cm (1 1/2 inch) cubes

    The marinade

    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) garlic, minced
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) cilantro, chopped
    • 59 ml (1/4 cup) olive oil
    • 5 ml (1 tsp) salt
    • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) ground black pepper

    The skewers

    • 2 sweet peppers, one red and one yellow, cut into 4 cm squares
    • 1/2 onion, cut into 4 cm squares
    • 3 firm, underripe tomatoes, quartered and cored

    Method

    1. Cut the sweet peppers and the onion into large pieces, about 4 cm square.
    2. Quarter the tomatoes and remove the core from each quarter.
    3. Combine the minced garlic, chopped cilantro, salt, black pepper and olive oil to make the marinade.
    4. Cut the meat into 4 cm cubes and turn them through the marinade until thoroughly coated.
    5. If you are using anything other than tenderloin, cover and marinate for 3 hours or overnight in the fridge. Tenderloin needs no long marination and can go straight on.
    6. Thread the meat and vegetables onto skewers, alternating as you go.
    7. Grill over high heat for 12 minutes, turning the skewers every 3 minutes so each of the four faces gets its turn.
    8. Rest for 5 minutes before serving, if you can wait.

    Chef Tips

    • Firm, underripe tomatoes, quartered and cored. This is the single smartest instruction in the recipe — ripe tomatoes slide off the skewer into the coals and take your dinner with them.
    • Let the cut decide the marinating time. Tenderloin goes straight on; sirloin and anything tougher wants three hours to overnight.
    • No xawaash. If you were expecting a spice-heavy marinade, the most authoritative Somali source says otherwise, and the meat tastes of meat as a result.
    • Every three minutes, four turns, twelve minutes. Uneven turning is how you get one charred face and one raw one.
    • Cut everything to roughly the same size so the vegetables and the meat finish together.
    • The recipe says rest it five minutes to let the juices settle — and then admits its own authors prefer to eat them straight off the fire, hot. Both positions are defensible.
    • Soak wooden skewers for half an hour first, or use metal ones.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using ripe tomatoes.
    • Not coring the tomato quarters.
    • Marinating tenderloin overnight, which turns it mushy for no gain.
    • Turning it once or twice instead of every three minutes.
    • Packing the skewer so tight that the sides of the meat never get any heat.

    Health Benefits

    • Beef supplies protein, iron, zinc and B12.
    • Grilled, so much of the fat renders away.
    • The peppers, onion and tomato go on the same skewer, which makes this close to a complete plate.
    • Naturally low in carbohydrate.

    Cultural Note

    Grilled meat on skewers travels the whole Indian Ocean and the whole Muslim world, and the Somali version sits comfortably in that company while declining to over-spice itself. Solay is the word for skewering, and the same root shows up in beer iyo kilyo la solay, the skewered liver and kidney of pastoral cooking.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Raw marinated meat keeps 2 days.

    Reheating: Best briefly in a hot pan. Microwaving makes it grey and tough.

    Serve with: Bariis Iskukaris, Insalaaddo, Sabaayad
