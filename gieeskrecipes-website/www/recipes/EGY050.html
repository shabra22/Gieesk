🧀 Gebna bel Tamatem (Egyptian White Cheese and Tomato Dip, with Homemade Cheese)

Gebna bil-tamatem — جبنة بالطماطم

Crumbly fresh cheese mixed with tomato, onion, pepper, mint and cumin, served with baladi bread; the page also gives a simple vinegar-curdled milk cheese.

12h 50mTotal time
30mPrep
20mCook
4Servings
170Calories
EasyDifficulty

Cottage cheese or a homemade gebna kareesh is mixed with diced tomato, onion, pepper, chili, olive oil, dried mint and cumin and chilled 30 minutes. To make the cheese, milk is heated slowly to 190°F, salt and vinegar are added, and the milk is held at a gentle simmer on very low heat for 5 minutes until it curdles, then drained in cheesecloth and left to dry in the fridge overnight.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Cheesecloth, Thermometer

    Dip

    • 1 cup cottage cheese, store bought or see recipe below
    • 1 finely chopped small red onions, or 1 teaspoon onion powder
    • 1 ripe tomato, seeded and diced
    • 1 bell pepper, diced
    • 1 finely chopped green chili pepper, optional
    • 1 tablespoon extra virgin olive oil
    • 1 teaspoon dry mint
    • ½ teaspoon ground cumin
    • salt & pepper, to taste

    Gebna kareesh (homemade cheese)

    • 5 cups full fat milk
    • 1 cup heavy cream, optional
    • 1 teaspoon Himalayan salt
    • 6 tablespoon s white distilled vinegar

    Method

    1. Cheese: heat the milk (and cream) slowly to 190°F (88°C) and stir in the salt.
    2. Add the vinegar and let the milk gently simmer on very low heat for 5 minutes, until it curdles and separates from the whey.
    3. Drain in doubled cheesecloth, cool, squeeze, and drain the liquid overnight in the fridge. The cheese is done when it is all dry.
    4. Mix the cheese with the dip ingredients and chill 30 minutes.
    5. Serve with aish baladi or pita.

    Chef Tips

    • Cues: milk curdles and separates from whey; cheese dry. The overnight drain and the 30-minute chill are NO CUE, TIME ONLY.
    • The card calls for 6 tablespoons of vinegar for 5 cups of milk, while the body says 1 tablespoon of acid per cup; the card also lists a bell pepper the body does not mention.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Squeezing too little, which leaves the cheese watery.

    Cultural Note

    Nermine Mansour calls it a staple in many Egyptian homes and a nostalgic taste of childhood, and says making gebna from leftover milk started her in cheesemaking.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate covered up to 2 days (our estimate).

    Reheating: Serve cold.

    Serve with: Aish baladi
