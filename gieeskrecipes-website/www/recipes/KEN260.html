🍝 Kenyan Seafood Pasta

Pasta ya Samaki ya Kenya

Kenyan Seafood Pasta — linguine with Indian Ocean prawns, calamari, and mussels in a white wine, garlic, tomato, and fresh herb sauce. The luxury pasta of Kenya's coastal restaurants — the Indian Ocean's finest in a bowl.

Seafood pasta at a Mombasa coastal restaurant is the meeting of Italian pasta technique and Indian Ocean seafood — when the seafood is this fresh and this varied, the dish is extraordinary.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot for pasta, Large wide pan

  • 400g linguine or spaghetti
  • 200g prawns — peeled
  • 200g calamari rings
  • 200g fresh mussels — cleaned
  • 5 garlic cloves — sliced
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • ½ cup white wine or fish stock
  • 1 tsp chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ cup pasta water
  • 2 tsp salt
  • Fresh coriander and parsley

Method

  1. Cook pasta al dente. Reserve 1 cup pasta water.
  2. In a wide pan, heat olive oil over high heat.
  3. Cook garlic and chilli 30 seconds.
  4. Add calamari rings — cook 2 minutes. Remove.
  5. Add prawns — cook 2 minutes until pink. Remove.
  6. Add mussels and wine. Cover 4—5 minutes until opened.
  7. Add tomatoes and paprika. Cook 4 minutes.
  8. Return all seafood. Add pasta and pasta water.
  9. Add butter. Toss vigorously. Scatter herbs.

Chef Tips

  • Seafood timing is critical — calamari and prawns are done in 2 minutes; cook them separately to avoid overcooking.
  • Discard any mussels that do not open during cooking.
  • This dish should be assembled and eaten immediately.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking calamari.
  • Not discarding unopened mussels.
  • Waiting too long to serve — seafood pasta deteriorates quickly.

The Science

See KEN252 for calamari cooking science. In pasta, the calamari should be pulled before fully cooked — it continues cooking in the hot pasta for the final 30 seconds of tossing.

Cultural Note

Seafood pasta on the Kenyan coast is the happy accident of Italian pasta culture meeting East African fishing traditions — the result is something genuinely world-class when the seafood is as fresh as Kenya's Indian Ocean waters provide.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Must be eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not recommended.
