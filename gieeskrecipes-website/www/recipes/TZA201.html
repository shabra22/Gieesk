🍬 Tanzanian Coconut Candy

Kashata ya Nazi

Tanzanian Kashata ya Nazi — the definitive Swahili coconut candy. Fresh grated coconut and sugar cooked together until caramelised and set into firm, slightly chewy squares fragrant with cardamom. The sweet that every Tanzanian grows up eating — at Eid, at the market, at home.

Kashata is the Swahili coast's most beloved sweet — a coconut candy of extraordinary simplicity and depth. The caramelisation of the sugar around the fresh coconut creates a flavour that is simultaneously sweet, nutty, and slightly chewy. Made in three classic versions: plain white (sugar only), pink (with rose water or hibiscus), and the peanut-cashata hybrid. All are essential to the Tanzanian sweet tradition.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Greased plate or baking tin, Wooden spoon

  • 2 cups freshly grated coconut
  • 1 cup sugar
  • ¼ cup water
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • Pinch of salt

Pink version: add 1 tsp rose water + 2 drops red food colouring

    Peanut version: add ½ cup roasted groundnuts with the coconut

      Method

      1. Combine sugar and water in a heavy saucepan over medium heat.
      2. Cook without stirring until syrup reaches soft-ball stage (115°C) — 6—8 minutes.
      3. Add grated coconut, cardamom, and salt.
      4. Stir continuously over medium heat 6—8 minutes until the mixture thickens, pulls from the sides, and appears slightly dry and grainy.
      5. For pink version: remove from heat, add rose water and colouring, stir quickly.
      6. Immediately pour onto a greased surface.
      7. Spread to 1cm thickness.
      8. Cut into squares while warm.
      9. Allow to cool and set completely — 30 minutes.

      Chef Tips

      • Work quickly once off the heat — kashata sets fast.
      • The mixture is ready when it pulls completely from the sides and looks dry and grainy.
      • Cut while still warm — fully set kashata is too hard to cut cleanly.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Not working fast enough — sets before spreading.
      • Under-cooking — stays soft and sticky.

      The Science

      Kashata sets through sugar recrystallisation. When the sugar-coconut mixture is cooked past soft-ball stage and the water largely evaporates, the sugar recrystallises as it cools — forming the characteristic firm, slightly grainy texture. The coconut fat partially inhibits full recrystallisation, producing the characteristic slightly chewy result.

      Cultural Note

      Kashata is the sweet that unites all of Tanzania's coastal communities — made for Eid, sold at every market, given as a gift, and eaten by every Tanzanian from childhood to old age. Its three versions (white, pink, and peanut) are as different as siblings — each with its own character.

      Storage: Airtight container 2 weeks at room temperature.

      Reheating: Not applicable.
