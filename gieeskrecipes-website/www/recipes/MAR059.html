🫒 Moroccan Marinated Olives with Preserved Lemon and Harissa

Zitoun mchermel (زيتون مشرمل)

Green, purple or black olives tossed with garlic, preserved lemon rind, parsley, paprika, cumin and harissa, covered in olive oil and rested a few days in the fridge.

2dTotal time
10mPrep
6Servings

EasyDifficulty

Gavin writes that these marinated olives, called zitoun mchermel in Arabic, are much loved in Morocco by Jews and Arabs alike. Everything is tossed in a bowl, spooned into a jar, covered with olive oil and chilled for two or three days before serving, or a few hours if in a hurry. They keep up to a week in the fridge.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, Jar or container with lid

  • 2 cups (about 3/4 lb) green, purple, or black olives, or a mix, well drained
  • 2 or 3 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • the rind of 1 preserved lemon, cut into small dice, or more to taste
  • 1 or 2 bay leaves
  • 3 tablespoons finely chopped flat leaf parsley
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp harissa, or to taste
  • 6 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, plus more to cover
  • 1 or 2 tbsp lemon juice, to taste

Method

  1. Place the olives in a bowl with the garlic, preserved lemon, bay leaves, parsley, spices, harissa, 3 tablespoons olive oil and lemon juice and toss lightly.
  2. Spoon into a jar or container, cover with a layer of olive oil and close tightly.
  3. Store in the refrigerator for 2 or 3 days before serving. Will keep up to 1 week.

Chef Tips

  • The page gives time only for readiness: 2 or 3 days, or a few hours if you are in a hurry.
  • Keep the olives under a layer of oil in the fridge.
  • Use well-drained olives so the marinade stays flavourful.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not draining the olives.
  • Leaving the jar at room temperature.
  • Skipping the oil layer.

Cultural Note

Storage & Serving

Storage: Up to 1 week in the fridge.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature.

Serve with: Khobz
