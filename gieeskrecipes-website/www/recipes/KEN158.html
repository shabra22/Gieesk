🥜 Kenyan Sugar-Coated Groundnuts

Karanga za Sukari

Kenyan Sugar-Coated Groundnuts — roasted groundnuts coated in a crispy white sugar shell with cardamom and vanilla. The sweet counterpart to the classic salted roasted groundnut — equally beloved and completely addictive.

Sugar-coated groundnuts are the celebration version of Kenya's favourite snack — sold at weddings, school prize-givings, market stalls, and festive gatherings. The groundnuts are coated in a white sugar shell that cracks satisfyingly when bitten, revealing the roasted nut within. The addition of cardamom gives them a distinctly Kenyan aromatic character.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Wooden spoon, Greased tray

  • 2 cups roasted groundnuts — skins on or removed
  • 1 cup sugar
  • ¼ cup water
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Combine sugar and water in a heavy saucepan over medium heat.
  2. Stir until sugar dissolves. Stop stirring and bring to a boil.
  3. Cook without stirring until the syrup reaches 115°C (soft ball stage) — about 8—10 minutes.
  4. Add groundnuts, cardamom, vanilla, and salt. Stir vigorously.
  5. Keep stirring over medium-low heat as the sugar begins to crystallise around the nuts — it will turn white and grainy.
  6. Continue stirring until all nuts are coated in dry, white sugar crystals — about 3—5 minutes.
  7. Remove from heat. Pour onto a greased tray.
  8. Quickly separate any clumps with a fork.
  9. Cool completely — about 15 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • The crystallisation stage (when the syrup goes white and grainy) is expected and correct — keep stirring through it.
  • Once the sugar crystallises, the nuts dry quickly — work fast to separate them.
  • For a brown-sugar caramelised version: cook the syrup to 130°C instead of 115°C.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stopping stirring when the sugar goes grainy — the nuts clump permanently.
  • Syrup too hot — the sugar caramelises instead of forming the white crystal coating.

The Science

At 115°C, the sugar solution is supersaturated. When agitated (stirring), it crystallises rapidly — the stirring provides nucleation points for crystal formation, coating each nut in a thick layer of fine sugar crystals rather than a glassy caramel.

Cultural Note

Sugar-coated groundnuts are one of the most cherished celebration sweets in Kenya — bags of karanga za sukari appear at weddings, school events, and festive gatherings as tokens of sweetness shared with guests.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight container for up to 3 weeks.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: As a celebration sweet, Kenyan Masala Chai, At weddings and events
