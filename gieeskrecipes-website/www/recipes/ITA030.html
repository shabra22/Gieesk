Lasagne alla Bolognese

Lasagna alla Bolognese layers delicate green spinach pasta sheets with rich ragù and silky béchamel — the true, Bologna-born ancestor of the baked pasta dish known worldwide.

Unlike the tomato-and-ricotta-heavy versions found abroad, authentic Bolognese lasagna uses thin sheets of fresh pasta (traditionally colored green with spinach), layered with the same slow-cooked ragù used for tagliatelle, bound together with a delicate béchamel rather than ricotta.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large baking dish, Saucepan for béchamel, Whisk, Large pot for blanching pasta

  • 1 batch Ragù alla Bolognese (as in ITA029, doubled for layering)
  • 12–15 sheets fresh lasagna pasta (spinach-green traditional, or plain)

Béchamel

  • 80 g (⅓ cup) butter
  • 80 g (⅔ cup) all-purpose flour
  • 1 L (4 cups) whole milk, warmed
  • ¼ tsp freshly grated nutmeg
  • Salt and white pepper, to taste

Assembly

  • 150 g (2 cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano

Method

  1. Prepare the béchamel: melt the butter in a saucepan, whisk in the flour, and cook for 1–2 minutes to form a roux.
  2. Gradually whisk in the warm milk, a little at a time, until fully smooth. Simmer, stirring constantly, for 8–10 minutes until thickened. Season with nutmeg, salt, and white pepper.
  3. If using fresh (unbaked) pasta sheets, briefly blanch each in salted boiling water for 30 seconds, then lay flat on a towel — this step can be skipped with fresh no-boil sheets.
  4. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F).
  5. Spread a thin layer of ragù on the base of a baking dish, followed by a layer of pasta sheets.
  6. Spread a layer of béchamel over the pasta, then a layer of ragù, then a scatter of Parmigiano. Repeat the layering — pasta, béchamel, ragù, cheese — until you have 5–6 layers, finishing with a generous layer of béchamel and Parmigiano on top.
  7. Bake for 35–40 minutes until bubbling and golden on top.
  8. Rest for 10–15 minutes before slicing — this allows the layers to set for clean portions.

Chef Tips

  • Resting the baked lasagna before cutting is essential — slicing immediately causes the layers to slide apart.
  • Thin, delicate pasta sheets are traditional in Bologna — thick, chewy sheets are a departure from the authentic style.
  • Make the ragù and béchamel a day ahead to streamline assembly day.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using ricotta instead of béchamel — this is a distinctly non-Bolognese substitution common in other regional and international styles.
  • Skipping the rest time after baking, resulting in a lasagna that falls apart when sliced.
  • Making the béchamel too thick or too thin — it should be pourable but coat the back of a spoon.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of protein, calcium, and iron.
  • Best enjoyed as a hearty, occasional main course given its richness.

Cultural Note

The oldest known lasagna recipe in Italian dates to a 14th-century manuscript, though the Bolognese ragù-and-béchamel version as known today solidified in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days, or freeze baked or unbaked for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat covered in a 180°C (350°F) oven until warmed through, about 20–25 minutes from chilled.

Serve with: A simple green salad, A robust Sangiovese or Lambrusco, Crusty bread
