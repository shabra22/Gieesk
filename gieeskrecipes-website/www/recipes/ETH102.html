🫘 Ethiopian Chickpea Flour Stew

ሽሮ ወጥ — Shiro Wat

Ethiopian Shiro Wat — spiced roasted chickpea flour stirred into a sauce of sautéed onion, garlic, and water until smooth and thick. The quickest Ethiopian main course — done in 25 minutes, deeply satisfying, and the most frequently eaten fasting dish in the country.

Shiro Wat is the weekday solution to Ethiopian fasting — while Misir Wat takes an hour and Kik Alicha takes longer, Shiro takes 25 minutes. The shiro powder (pre-spiced roasted chickpea flour) is simply stirred into a sautéed onion base with water until thick and smooth. Eaten with injera, it is one of the most satisfying mouthfuls in Ethiopian cooking.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: All of Ethiopia — the fasting dish that can be made in 25 minutes

Shiro is the roasted and spiced chickpea (or broad bean) flour that is the foundation of Shiro Wat — Ethiopia's most practical fasting dish. The shiro powder, already containing berbere and other spices, is simply stirred into a base of sautéed onion and water until it forms a smooth, thick stew. It can be made in 25 minutes, requires minimal ingredients, and is extraordinarily satisfying. There are two main varieties: thick shiro (firfir) and thin shiro. Shiro is the food that urban Ethiopians eat most frequently during the 200+ annual fasting days.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan

  • 1 cup shiro powder (spiced roasted chickpea flour)
  • 2 onions — very finely diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 4 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 tsp berbere — optional, for extra heat
  • 1 tsp salt

Homemade shiro powder: roast and grind chickpeas + berbere + spices

    Method

    1. Heat oil in a saucepan. Cook onion until very golden — 10 minutes.
    2. Add garlic. Cook 1 minute.
    3. Add extra berbere if using. Stir 30 seconds.
    4. Add water. Bring to a simmer.
    5. Whisk shiro powder gradually into the simmering water — add slowly to prevent lumps.
    6. Stir continuously over medium heat for 12—15 minutes until very smooth and thick.
    7. The shiro should be pourable but thick.
    8. Season.

    Chef Tips

    • Whisk the shiro powder in gradually — adding all at once creates lumps.
    • Stir continuously — shiro sticks to the bottom easily.
    • Thick shiro (less water) is called firfir-style; thin shiro (more water) is also traditional.
    • Add a piece of kibbeh on non-fasting days — transforms it from good to great.
    • Shiro powder can be bought at Ethiopian grocery stores or made at home.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding powder all at once — lumps.
    • Not stirring — sticks and burns.
    • Too thin — should be thick enough to scoop with injera.

    The Science

    Shiro powder is pre-cooked through roasting — the chickpea starch has already been partially gelatinised. When added to simmering water, this pre-gelatinised starch hydrates rapidly into a smooth paste without the lumping problems of raw starch. The continuous stirring distributes heat evenly and prevents the high-starch mixture from scorching.

    Cultural Note

    Shiro Wat is the everyday miracle of Ethiopian fasting cuisine — the dish that makes 200+ days of plant-based eating not just bearable but genuinely delicious. Every Ethiopian home has shiro powder in the pantry. It is the last-resort meal, the quick weekday lunch, and the beloved constant of fasting life.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 2 months.

    Reheating: Gently with added water.
