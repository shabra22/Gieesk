🥜 Mbakhal with Peanut Flour

Mbakahl aux arachides

The same buried-nététou-ball rice, with 250 g of raw peanut flour sprinkled over before the lid goes on.

Afrik’s peanut mbakhal is the fisherman’s version with one addition: long-grain rice, and 250 g of raw peanut flour sprinkled over the rice before covering. The skeleton is unchanged — tiof head and dried fish boiled in salted water, nététou pounded with chile and onion into the broth, rice cooked in the sauce with two fingers of liquid above it, a second nététou ball buried in the centre and removed after 20 minutes, the finished plate topped with cooked nététou and drizzled with butter or palm oil.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid, Mortar and pestle

  • 250 g raw peanut flour
  • 1 kg long-grain rice
  • 125 g nététou
  • 1 large onion
  • 2 chiles
  • 1 tiof head, or 600 g fresh fish
  • 1 piece of dried fish
  • 100 g indigenous butter (clarified)
  • Lemons — quantity not printed

Method

  1. Boil the tiof head and dried fish in salted water. Pound 25 g of nététou with a chile and the onion and stir the paste in.
  2. Remove the fish and keep warm. Cook the rice in the sauce, leaving two fingers of liquid above the rice at the start; add hot water if needed.
  3. Pound the remaining nététou with the second chile and salt and form a ball. Bury it in a well in the rice.
  4. Sprinkle the raw peanut flour over everything, cover and continue cooking.
  5. After 20 minutes, remove the ball.
  6. Serve the fish with the rice, top with the cooked nététou mixture and drizzle with indigenous butter or palm oil.

Chef Tips

  • The method is SINGLE-PASS — treat the quantities as a guide until checked against the printed page.
  • The peanut flour goes in raw and is cooked only by the covered steam; the page prints no cue for when it is done.
  • Two fingers of liquid above the rice at the start is the printed cue for the water.
  • Use long-grain rice as printed; the other mbakhal says only ‘riz’.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the flour into the liquid so it lumps instead of sprinkling over.
  • Forgetting to remove the nététou ball.

Cultural Note

The page’s total time is 2 hours, prep and cooking together. Mbakhal is a Wolof dish; a Saloum version, with dried shrimp, yet and guedj, is at Mbakhalou Saloum, Peanut Rice of the Saloum Islands.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 1 day refrigerated.

Reheating: Steam gently with a splash of water.

Serve with: Lemon wedges
