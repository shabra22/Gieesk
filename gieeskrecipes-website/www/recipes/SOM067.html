🍋 Casiir Liin Dhanaan

Casiir Liin Dhanaan

Whole lemons — skin, pith and all — pulsed five times with sugar, ice and water and strained, which draws the oil out of the peel and gives a lemonade no squeezed juice can match.

The Somali way with lemonade is to put the whole fruit in. Scrubbed lemons are cut into pieces and blended, briefly, with the sugar and water, so the aromatic oil in the skin comes out along with the juice. Five pulses and no more: go further and the pith turns the whole jug bitter. The mint is a later addition, picked up in the Gulf and kept because it works; the original Somali version has none.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Sieve, Jug

  • 2 lemons, scrubbed well
  • 100 g (1/2 cup) white granulated sugar
  • 1 L (4 cups) water
  • 6 ice cubes
  • 20 g (1/2 cup) fresh mint leaves, packed (optional — the Somali original has none)
  • More ice, to serve

Method

  1. Wash and scrub the lemons very well — the skin is going into the drink.
  2. Cut each lemon into 12 pieces.
  3. If you are using mint, wash it and pluck the leaves off the stalks.
  4. Put the lemon pieces, the mint, the sugar, the ice cubes and the water into the blender.
  5. Pulse 5 times. No more — this is the whole technique.
  6. Strain the juice into a jug, pressing gently on the solids.
  7. Serve over fresh ice, straight away.

Chef Tips

  • Five pulses. The point is to bruise the peel and release its oil, not to liquidise the pith, which is where bitterness lives.
  • Scrub the lemons properly, since the skin is an ingredient rather than a wrapper.
  • Scale the sugar with the fruit: for four lemons, use a full cup.
  • Drink it fresh. It deteriorates in the fridge within hours — the peel keeps leaching bitterness into it after the blending stops.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Blending instead of pulsing, which gives a bitter, cloudy drink.
  • Skipping the strain, leaving fragments of peel through every glass.
  • Making it hours ahead, which is the reliable way to turn a good jug bitter.
  • Using unwashed lemons, when the skin is going in whole.

Health Benefits

  • Whole lemons contribute more vitamin C and more of the peel's flavonoids than juice alone.
  • Mint is traditionally used to settle the stomach.
  • The sugar can be cut right back if the lemons are sweet.

Cultural Note

Lemonade sits in the everyday Somali rotation of lunchtime drinks alongside grapefruit, tamarind and mango juices, all pressed to order. This is the household method rather than the restaurant one, and it comes down through mothers rather than menus.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Make and drink within the hour. It does not keep.

Reheating: Not applicable — served cold.

Serve with: Sambuusa, Bajiye, Nafaqo
