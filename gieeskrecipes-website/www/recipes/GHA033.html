🥜 Peanut Sauce

Peanut sauce — nkate nkwan sauce

Her stated childhood favourite — chalé sauce and stock simmered, peanut butter dissolved in gradually, then blended and left to infuse up to two hours.

This is groundnut soup turned into a sauce, and the author calls it her ultimate childhood comfort food. Onion is sautéed, the spices and aromatics go in, then half a litre each of uncooked chalé sauce and vegetable stock, simmered fifteen to twenty minutes. Peanut butter is added gradually until dissolved, the whole thing is blended smooth, and then eight green kpakpo shito chillies go in for a final simmer of thirty minutes or more. Her note is that she often leaves it up to two hours so the flavours really infuse, but thirty to forty minutes is good enough.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Blender or stick blender, Grater

  • 1 tbsp groundnut oil
  • 1 onion, finely diced
  • 1 tbsp extra-hot chilli powder
  • 1 tbsp curry powder
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 5 cm piece fresh root ginger, grated — unpeeled if organic
  • 1 red scotch bonnet chilli, pierced
  • 3 tbsp crushed roasted peanuts
  • 2 tsp sea salt
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 500 ml uncooked chalé sauce
  • 500 ml good-quality vegetable stock
  • 100-200 g organic peanut butter
  • 8 green kpakpo shito (cherry) chillies

Method

  1. Heat the groundnut oil and sauté the onion about 2 minutes.
  2. Add the chilli powder, curry powder, garlic, ginger, pierced scotch bonnet, crushed peanuts, salt and black pepper.
  3. Add the uncooked chalé sauce and the vegetable stock.
  4. Simmer 15 to 20 minutes.
  5. Add the peanut butter gradually, until dissolved.
  6. Blend smooth.
  7. Add the green kpakpo shito chillies.
  8. Simmer 30 minutes or more — up to 2 hours for a fuller flavour.

Chef Tips

  • Her own timing note is the useful one: she often leaves it simmering up to two hours so the flavours really infuse, but thirty to forty minutes is good enough. Both are her words.
  • Peanut butter gradually until dissolved, as in every groundnut preparation. It will not come back together if you let it clump.
  • Blend AFTER the peanut butter has dissolved and BEFORE the kpakpo shito goes in. The whole chillies are meant to stay whole and infuse.
  • Half a litre each of chalé sauce and stock — equal parts, which makes this the most straightforward ratio in her repertoire.
  • Crushed roasted peanuts go in early with the spices, separately from the peanut butter. Two textures of the same nut, and it matters.
  • A tablespoon each of extra-hot chilli powder and curry powder is a lot. A second printing of this sauce halves both, so taste as you go.
  • Vegan as written with vegetable stock, which is worth knowing — few West African sauces this rich are.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the peanut butter all at once.
  • Blending after the kpakpo shito has gone in.
  • Leaving out the crushed peanuts because the peanut butter is already there.
  • Treating thirty minutes as the maximum rather than the minimum.
  • Using cooked chalé sauce where uncooked is specified.

Health Benefits

  • Peanuts supply monounsaturated fat, niacin, folate and vitamin E.
  • Vegan as written with vegetable stock.
  • Ginger, garlic and chilli all contribute documented bioactive compounds.
  • Good plant protein for a sauce.

Cultural Note

Turning groundnut soup into a sauce you keep in the fridge is a diaspora move and a sensible one — the flavour of nkatenkwan without the two-hour pot of chicken. That the author names it her childhood comfort food rather than a traditional dish is the honest framing, and the up-to-two-hours simmer is the bit that connects it back to the soup it came from.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated, or 2 months frozen, per the author's second printing.

Reheating: Gentle heat, loosening with stock or water.

Serve with: Rice, Fufu, Grilled chicken
