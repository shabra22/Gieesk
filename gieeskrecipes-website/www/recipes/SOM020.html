🥗 Cabbaar

Cabbaar

A refreshing Somali salad of finely chopped tomato, onion, and cucumber, brightened with lime and a touch of chili, served alongside rich rice and meat dishes to add balance.

Cabbaar's crisp, bright simplicity is a deliberate counterpoint on the Somali table, meant to cut through the richness of dishes like Bariis Iskukaris or Suqaar with something cool, acidic, and fresh.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl

  • 3 tomatoes, finely diced
  • 1 cucumber, finely diced
  • 1 small red onion, finely diced
  • 1 green chili, finely chopped (optional)
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the diced tomatoes, cucumber, onion, and chili if using in a bowl.
  2. Add the lime juice, olive oil, and cilantro.
  3. Toss well and season with salt to taste.
  4. Let sit for a few minutes before serving to let the flavors meld.

Chef Tips

  • Dicing everything finely and uniformly is traditional and makes the salad easier to eat alongside rice.
  • Letting the salad rest briefly before serving allows the lime and salt to properly season the vegetables.
  • Adjust the chili and lime to taste, balancing brightness against the richer dishes it typically accompanies.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Dicing the vegetables too large, losing the salad intended light, easy-to-eat texture.
  • Skipping the lime, which is essential to the salad refreshing, palate-cleansing role.
  • Making it too far ahead, when the vegetables can release excess water and dilute the dressing.

Cultural Note

Cabbaar is a common accompaniment across Somali meals, its bright, fresh simplicity providing essential balance against the country's typically rich rice and meat dishes.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; refrigerate leftovers for up to 1 day.

Reheating: Not applicable; served cold or at room temperature.

Serve with: Bariis Iskukaris, Suqaar, Maraq
