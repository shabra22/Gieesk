🌿 Tanzanian Boiled Cassava

Mihogo ya Kuchemsha

Mihogo ya Kuchemsha — cassava boiled in salted water until completely tender, served with a pinch of salt, pili pili sauce, or a drizzle of coconut cream. The simple, filling, everyday snack and side dish of Tanzania — available at every roadside stall.

Boiled cassava is one of Tanzania's most widely eaten foods — affordable, filling, and available everywhere. It is eaten as a morning snack, a lunch side, and a between-meal filler. Served with pili pili or coconut cream, it transcends its simplicity.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan

  • 600g fresh cassava — peeled and cut into chunks
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Pili pili sauce or coconut cream to serve

Method

  1. Peel cassava and remove the woody central fibre. Cut into chunks.
  2. Place in a pot with cold salted water.
  3. Bring to a boil. Cook for 20—25 minutes until completely tender.
  4. Drain and serve with pili pili sauce or coconut cream.

Chef Tips

  • Remove the woody central fibre completely — it does not soften during cooking.
  • Cassava is done when a fork slides in without resistance.
  • Fresh cassava is vastly better than frozen.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not removing the central fibre.
  • Under-cooking.

The Science

Cassava must be properly cooked — raw cassava contains linamarin, a cyanogenic compound that breaks down harmlessly during cooking and draining. Properly boiled and drained cassava is completely safe to eat.

Cultural Note

Boiled cassava is the affordable, reliable staple that has sustained Tanzanian communities through droughts and lean seasons for generations — cassava's drought resistance has saved lives across East Africa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Steam briefly or fry.

Serve with: Pili Pili Sauce, Coconut Cream, Fried Fish, As a standalone snack
