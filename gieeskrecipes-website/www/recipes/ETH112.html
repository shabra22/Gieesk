🫘 Ethiopian Chickpea Stew

ሽምብራ ወጥ — Shimbra Wat

Ethiopian Shimbra Wat — whole chickpeas slow-cooked in a berbere sauce with onion, garlic, and tomato. The whole-chickpea fasting stew — more textured than Shiro Wat (which uses chickpea flour), with the chickpeas providing a pleasing firmness in the sauce.

Shimbra Wat uses whole chickpeas where Shiro uses chickpea flour — the result is a stew with distinct texture, the chickpeas holding their shape in the berbere sauce. It requires pre-soaking and longer cooking but rewards with a more satisfying texture.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot or pressure cooker

  • 2 cups dried chickpeas — soaked overnight
  • 3 onions — finely diced
  • 4 tbsp oil
  • 3 tbsp berbere
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tomatoes — diced
  • 2 cups water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Boil soaked chickpeas 45 minutes until tender (or pressure cook 25 minutes).
  2. Dry caramelise onions — 18 minutes.
  3. Add oil and berbere. Fry 3 minutes.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, tomatoes. Cook 5 minutes.
  5. Add cooked chickpeas and their liquid.
  6. Simmer 15—20 minutes until sauce is thick.
  7. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Pre-soak 12 hours — reduces cooking time significantly.
  • The chickpea cooking liquid adds body to the sauce — use it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not soaking — very long cooking time.
  • Discarding cooking liquid.

The Science

Chickpea starch partially dissolves into the cooking liquid during the long boiling — this starchy liquid is used as the sauce base, naturally thickening without added starch.

Cultural Note

Shimbra Wat is the textured fasting stew — where Shiro is smooth and Misir Wat is silky, Shimbra provides the satisfying bite of whole chickpeas. Together they demonstrate the remarkable textural variety of Ethiopian legume cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), On the fasting platter
