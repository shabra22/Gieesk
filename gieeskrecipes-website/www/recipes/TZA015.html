🌾 Tanzanian Sorghum Porridge

Uji wa Mtama

Uji wa Mtama — traditional Tanzanian sorghum porridge, lighter in colour and slightly sweeter than finger millet porridge, sweetened with sugar or honey. The daily breakfast of central and western Tanzania — nutritious, gluten-free, and deeply traditional.

Sorghum porridge is the breakfast of Tanzania's drier inland regions where sorghum thrives in conditions that would challenge other cereals. Lighter and milder than finger millet porridge, it has its own distinct character and is among the most drought-resilient breakfast foods in East Africa.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium saucepan, Wooden spoon

  • 1 cup sorghum flour
  • 4 cups water
  • 2—3 tbsp sugar or honey
  • Pinch of salt
  • ½ cup milk — optional

Method

  1. Mix sorghum flour with 1 cup cold water to a smooth slurry.
  2. Bring remaining 3 cups water to a boil.
  3. Pour slurry into boiling water, stirring constantly.
  4. Cook 8—10 minutes over medium heat until smooth and thick.
  5. Add sugar and salt. Finish with milk if using.
  6. Serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Sorghum makes a lighter, less viscous porridge than finger millet — adjust water for desired consistency.
  • Add milk for a creamier result.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not making a slurry.
  • Under-cooking — raw flour taste.

The Science

Sorghum has a lower tannin content than many traditional varieties of finger millet, producing a lighter, milder-flavoured porridge. Modern food-grade sorghum flour is specifically bred for low tannins and maximum palatability.

Cultural Note

Sorghum porridge is the breakfast of Tanzania's resilient inland communities — the cereal that keeps producing when rains are scarce and other crops fail. Its importance in food security cannot be overstated.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best consumed fresh.

Reheating: Warm gently with splash of water.

Serve with: As a standalone breakfast, With groundnuts, With honey
