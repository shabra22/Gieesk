🥓 Frittole Calabresi

Frittole

Calabria's own version of pork cracklings, mixed pig scraps slowly rendered in their own fat for hours until deeply crisp, historically prepared right after the seasonal pig slaughter.

Frittole makes thorough use of odds and ends from the pig — skin, ears, and various trimmings — slow-cooked in a large copper pot until the fat renders out and the meat turns intensely crisp, a genuinely nose-to-tail tradition.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot

  • 2 kg (4.4 lb) mixed pork scraps and trimmings (skin, ears, fatty cuts)
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 dried chili, crumbled
  • Salt, to taste
  • Orange zest (optional)

Method

  1. Cut the pork scraps into manageable pieces.
  2. Place in a large heavy pot with the bay leaves and a small amount of water to start the process.
  3. Cook over low heat for 2.5–3 hours, stirring occasionally, as the fat gradually renders out and the pieces slowly crisp in their own fat.
  4. In the final 30 minutes, increase the heat slightly and add the chili, stirring frequently until everything turns deeply golden and crisp.
  5. Season generously with salt, and orange zest if using.
  6. Drain briefly and serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • This is a genuine slow process — the fat needs time to render fully before the pieces become properly crisp.
  • Stirring occasionally throughout, especially toward the end, prevents burning as the fat reduces.
  • Traditionally prepared in large batches following the seasonal pig slaughter and shared among family and neighbors.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the process over high heat, which can burn the pieces before the fat properly renders.
  • Not stirring frequently enough in the final stage, risking scorching.
  • Discarding the rendered fat, which is traditionally saved and used in other cooking.

Cultural Note

Frittole reflects Calabria's resourceful nose-to-tail pork tradition, historically prepared in large communal batches following the seasonal pig slaughter.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 1 week, or store covered in the rendered fat for longer preservation.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a dry skillet to re-crisp.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A robust Calabrian red wine
