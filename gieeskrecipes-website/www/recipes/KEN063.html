🍛 Kenyan Chicken Curry

Mchuzi wa Kuku wa Curry

Kenyan Chicken Curry — chicken pieces simmered in a warmly spiced tomato and onion sauce with curry powder, garam masala, turmeric, and fresh ginger until tender and deeply aromatic. Kenya's most popular curry — lighter than goat or beef versions but equally satisfying.

Kenyan chicken curry occupies the sweet spot between quick weeknight meal and impressive dinner party dish. It cooks faster than beef or goat curry, delivers equally complex flavour from the spice blend, and pairs perfectly with rice, chapati, or ugali. It is one of the most frequently prepared dishes in Kenyan homes nationwide.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Wooden spoon

  • 1.2 kg chicken pieces — bone-in
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 cup chicken stock or water
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Brown the chicken pieces in hot oil in batches. Remove and set aside.
  2. Cook onions until golden — 8 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add all spices except garam masala. Stir 1 minute.
  6. Return chicken. Add stock.
  7. Cover and simmer for 35—40 minutes until chicken is completely tender.
  8. Stir in garam masala. Taste and adjust.
  9. Garnish with coriander and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Blooming the spices in the thick tomato base before adding the chicken dramatically intensifies the flavour.
  • Bone-in chicken gives a richer sauce — boneless works but the result is lighter.
  • A dollop of natural yogurt stirred in at the end creates a creamier version.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking on high heat — the chicken becomes tough.
  • Adding garam masala at the start — it loses its fragrance.

The Science

The tomato-onion base (known in Indian cooking as masala) acts as both a flavour foundation and a tenderising environment. The acidity from tomatoes helps break down the chicken proteins slightly, contributing to a tender result.

Cultural Note

Chicken curry is one of the most frequently cooked dishes across Kenyan homes — quick enough for a weekday, impressive enough for guests. It reflects the deep Indian-Kenyan culinary influence that has shaped Kenyan cooking for generations.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on stovetop with a splash of water.

Serve with: Basmati Rice, Chapati, Ugali, Kachumbari, Natural Yogurt, Wali wa Nazi
