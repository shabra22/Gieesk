🍝 Spaghetti Carbonara

Spaghetti alla Carbonara

Spaghetti Carbonara is Rome's most fiercely defended pasta dish — silky egg and Pecorino coating each strand, studded with crisp guanciale, with absolutely no cream in sight.

The magic of true carbonara lies entirely in technique: eggs and cheese are whisked together off the heat and tossed with hot pasta and rendered guanciale fat, the residual warmth alone gently cooking the eggs into a glossy sauce rather than scrambling them.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large skillet, Mixing bowl, Tongs

  • 400 g (14 oz) spaghetti
  • 150 g (5 oz) guanciale, cut into small strips
  • 4 large egg yolks, plus 1 whole egg
  • 80 g (1 cup) finely grated Pecorino Romano, plus more for serving
  • Freshly ground black pepper, generous amount
  • Fine sea salt, for the pasta water

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of generously salted water to a boil for the spaghetti.
  2. While the water heats, whisk the egg yolks, whole egg, and Pecorino Romano together in a bowl until smooth. Season generously with black pepper.
  3. Cook the guanciale in a cold, dry skillet over medium heat, letting the fat render slowly until crisp and golden, about 6–8 minutes. Remove from heat.
  4. Cook the spaghetti until al dente. Reserve a cup of pasta water before draining.
  5. Add the hot, drained pasta directly to the skillet with the guanciale and its fat, tossing to coat, off the heat.
  6. Let the pasta cool for about 30 seconds, then pour in the egg and cheese mixture, tossing vigorously and continuously — the residual heat will gently cook the eggs into a creamy sauce.
  7. Add splashes of reserved pasta water as needed to loosen the sauce to a silky, coating consistency.
  8. Serve immediately with extra Pecorino and black pepper.

Chef Tips

  • Take the pan off the heat before adding the egg mixture — direct heat scrambles the eggs instead of creating a silky sauce.
  • Guanciale is traditional and gives the best flavor; pancetta is the closest substitute if unavailable.
  • Toss constantly and vigorously when combining the egg mixture — this is what emulsifies the sauce.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding cream — authentic carbonara never contains cream; the creaminess comes purely from egg, cheese, and starchy pasta water.
  • Cooking the eggs directly over heat, resulting in scrambled egg bits instead of a smooth sauce.
  • Using pre-grated cheese, which often contains anti-caking agents that prevent a smooth emulsion.

Cultural Note

Romans consider carbonara close to sacred, and adding cream or garlic is regarded by purists as a culinary offense. Its exact origins are debated, with theories ranging from Roman charcoal workers (carbonai) to American soldiers combining eggs and bacon rations during WWII.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately — the egg-based sauce does not reheat or store well.

Reheating: Not recommended; carbonara is meant to be eaten fresh, as reheating can scramble the eggs.

Serve with: A simple green salad, Crusty bread, A glass of Frascati
