🥬 Tanzanian Spinach Curry

Mchicha wa Curry

Tanzanian Mchicha wa Curry — spinach or amaranth leaves sautéed in a fragrant onion, tomato, and spice base with cumin, turmeric, and coconut milk. The Indian Ocean-influenced spiced spinach preparation — more aromatic and deeply flavoured than plain sautéed greens.

The spiced spinach curry reflects Tanzania's Indian Ocean trading heritage — the same spice tradition that perfumes the pilau and the biriani is applied to the everyday spinach, creating a vegetable dish of unusual depth and fragrance.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 500g fresh spinach or mchicha — washed and roughly chopped
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 200ml coconut milk
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden.
  2. Add garlic, ginger, and all spices. Stir 1 minute.
  3. Add tomatoes. Cook 6 minutes until thick.
  4. Add spinach in batches. Stir until wilted.
  5. Pour in coconut milk. Simmer gently 6—8 minutes.
  6. Season. Serve.

Chef Tips

  • Add spinach in batches — the first batch must wilt before the next goes in.
  • Coconut milk makes this richer than plain sautéed spinach.
  • Do not boil the coconut milk hard.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all spinach at once — too much volume to stir.
  • Boiling coconut milk.

The Science

Spinach contains oxalic acid which inhibits calcium absorption. The vitamin C in the tomatoes simultaneously increases iron absorption — the two effects partially balance each other, resulting in a dish with better overall mineral bioavailability than spinach alone.

Cultural Note

Spiced spinach curry reflects the multilayered food culture of Tanzania's coast — where African vegetables meet Indian Ocean spice in daily cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Wali wa Nazi (TZA002), Chapati (TZA004), Ugali (TZA001)
