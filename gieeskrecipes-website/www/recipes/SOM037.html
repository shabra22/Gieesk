🍜 Bariis Shaciiriyo Leh

Bariis Shaciiriyo Leh

Short-grain rice steamed with vermicelli browned in butter and oil first — plain, quick, and the thing Somali families eat at suhoor with yogurt and sugar before a day of fasting.

This is the undemanding end of Somali rice cookery: no stock, no spice blend, six ingredients. Browning the vermicelli in butter before the rice goes in is the whole technique, and it gives the pot a nutty flavour that plain rice does not have. Somali cooks first met it in Cairo, served with a tomato and vegetable stew; it came home and settled into Ramadan, eaten before dawn with yogurt and sugar stirred through.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a tight lid

  • 400 g (2 cups) Calrose or other short-grain rice
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 60 g (1/2 cup) short-cut vermicelli, or capellini broken small
  • 710 ml (3 cups) hot water
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Wash the rice until the water runs clear, then soak it for 15 minutes and drain.
  2. Put the oil, butter and vermicelli in the pot over medium heat and fry, stirring continuously, until the vermicelli turns nut brown. Watch it — it goes from brown to burnt in seconds.
  3. Add the drained rice and fry for one minute, stirring all the time.
  4. Pour in the hot water carefully, standing back from the steam.
  5. Add the salt and stir once. Do not keep stirring, or the rice releases its starch and turns sticky.
  6. Cover, turn the heat to low and cook for 15 minutes without lifting the lid once.
  7. Fork through gently and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Short-grain Calrose is the traditional choice here and behaves quite differently from basmati. Basmati will work, but the dish loses the soft, slightly clinging texture it is meant to have.
  • One stir after the water goes in, and no more. This is the single thing that decides whether the pot comes out fluffy or gluey.
  • At suhoor in Ramadan it is eaten with yogurt and sugar rather than as a savoury side — a different dish from the same pot.
  • The vermicelli is browned, not toasted lightly. Pale vermicelli adds texture but none of the flavour that makes this worth doing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Burning the vermicelli, which makes the entire pot bitter and cannot be salvaged.
  • Stirring the rice repeatedly after the water is in, which is how it turns sticky.
  • Opening the lid during the 15 minutes to check, releasing the steam that is cooking the top layer.
  • Adding cold water, which stalls the pot and leaves the rice unevenly done.

Health Benefits

  • A simple, low-fat carbohydrate base when made with the oil and butter as given.
  • Pairs with pulses or stew to make a complete-protein meal.
  • Easy on the stomach, which is part of why it suits a pre-dawn meal.

Cultural Note

Rice with vermicelli travels: it is eaten across Egypt, the Levant and the Gulf as well as Somalia, and Somali families picked it up in Cairo. In Somali kitchens it has become a Ramadan dish above all, eaten at suhoor when nobody wants to cook something complicated at four in the morning.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Sprinkle with water, cover and steam over low heat until hot through.

Serve with: Suugo Suqaar, Maraq Ari, Plain yogurt and sugar
