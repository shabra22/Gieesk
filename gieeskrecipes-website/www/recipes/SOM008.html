🥟 Sambuusa

Sambuusa

Crisp, triangular fried pastries filled with spiced ground meat, a beloved Somali snack especially abundant during Ramadan, when they're often the first bite to break the fast.

Sambuusa reflects Somalia's centuries-old trade ties across the Indian Ocean, sharing clear lineage with samosas found throughout South Asia and the Gulf, though the Somali version's precise, sharp-cornered fold and confident use of xawaash spice give it a distinct regional character.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large skillet, Deep frying pan

  • 20 sambuusa or spring roll wrappers
  • 400 g (14 oz) ground beef
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 1-2 green chilies, finely chopped
  • 2 tsp xawaash spice blend
  • 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil, plus more for frying
  • 1 egg, beaten, for sealing
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the oil in a skillet and saute the onion until softened.
  2. Add the garlic, ginger, and green chilies, cooking briefly until fragrant.
  3. Add the ground beef and cook, breaking it up, until browned and cooked through.
  4. Stir in the xawaash spice blend and season with salt.
  5. Cook for a few more minutes until the mixture is fairly dry, then stir in the cilantro and let cool completely.
  6. Cut the wrappers into strips if using spring roll wrappers, or keep as triangular sheets if using pre-made sambuusa wrappers.
  7. Place a spoonful of filling near one end and fold into a tight triangle, continuing to fold along the strip, sealing the final edge with beaten egg.
  8. Heat vegetable oil to 180C (350F) and fry the sambuusa in batches until deeply golden and crisp.
  9. Drain on paper towels and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Let the filling cool completely before assembling, as warm filling can make the wrappers soggy and difficult to seal.
  • Fold the triangles tightly, with no gaps, so the filling does not leak out during frying.
  • These freeze very well uncooked, making it easy to prepare a large batch ahead of Ramadan.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Filling with warm meat, which can cause the wrappers to become soggy and tear.
  • Not sealing the edges well enough, allowing the filling to escape into the frying oil.
  • Frying at too low a temperature, resulting in greasy rather than crisp sambuusa.

Cultural Note

Sambuusa is especially significant during Ramadan, when Somali families often serve them as the very first bite to break the daily fast at iftar.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate uncooked, assembled sambuusa for up to 1 day, or freeze for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Reheat cooked sambuusa in a hot oven to re-crisp; avoid the microwave.

Serve with: Basbaas (hot sauce), A wedge of lime
