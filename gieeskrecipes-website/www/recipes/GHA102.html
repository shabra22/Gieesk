🍗 Jollof Fried Chicken

Jollof fried chicken

Buttermilk-marinated strips in a cornflour and nutmeg coating, fried no more than three to four minutes — and the bread-cube oil test.

A declared invention that works: jollof spice mix and buttermilk on chicken strips, a cornflour coating with nutmeg, and a short hot fry. Two tablespoons of the dry spice mix with salt, black pepper and a tablespoon of rapeseed oil go onto four chicken breasts cut into strips, with two hundred and fifty millilitres of buttermilk, and marinate at least one to two hours, preferably overnight. The oil goes to 180 to 190 degrees, or until a cube of bread browns in thirty seconds, and the strips fry no more than three to four minutes — succulent and juicy yet cooked through, golden and crispy but not burnt.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep pot or fryer, Thermometer, or a cube of bread, Shallow dish, for the coating, Slotted spoon and kitchen paper

  • 4 boneless, skinless chicken breasts, cut into strips
  • 2 tbsp jollof dry spice mix
  • 1/2 tsp sea salt
  • 1/2 tsp coarsely ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp rapeseed oil
  • 250 ml buttermilk
  • 500 ml-1 litre vegetable oil, for deep frying

Coating

  • 150-200 g cornflour
  • 1/2 tsp coarsely ground black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp sea salt
  • 1 tsp ground nutmeg

Method

  1. Mix the jollof dry spice mix, salt, black pepper and rapeseed oil.
  2. Coat the chicken strips, then add the buttermilk.
  3. Marinate at least 1 to 2 hours, preferably overnight.
  4. Combine the cornflour, black pepper, salt and nutmeg in a shallow dish.
  5. Heat the oil to 180 to 190 degrees, or until a cube of bread browns in 30 seconds.
  6. Dip each chicken strip into the seasoned cornflour — try to do 4 or 5 pieces in quick succession, as you need to drop them into the hot oil straight away.
  7. Fry in batches for no more than 3 to 4 minutes, to keep them succulent and juicy yet cooked through, and golden and crispy but not burnt.
  8. Drain on kitchen paper and keep hot.

Chef Tips

  • The bread-cube test is the best oil-temperature cue in this collection: a cube of bread should brown in thirty seconds. That is 180 to 190 degrees without a thermometer.
  • Four or five pieces coated in quick succession, then straight into the oil. Her instruction, and the reason is that cornflour on a wet marinade goes pasty if it sits.
  • Three to four minutes maximum. Strips, not pieces — they cook through fast and dry out faster.
  • Cornflour rather than wheat flour gives the shatter-crisp coating. It also makes the dish gluten-free if your spice mix is.
  • A teaspoon of nutmeg in the coating is what makes this taste Ghanaian rather than generic fried chicken.
  • Overnight beats the one-to-two-hour minimum. Buttermilk tenderises slowly.
  • It is a declared invention, not a traditional dish — fried chicken with a West African spice system. Enjoy it as that.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Letting coated strips sit before frying.
  • Frying past four minutes.
  • Using wheat flour instead of cornflour.
  • Oil below temperature, which gives a soggy coating.
  • A short marinade.

Health Benefits

  • Chicken breast is very lean and high in protein.
  • Buttermilk tenderises without added fat.
  • Nutmeg and the spice mix contribute antioxidant compounds.
  • A short fry absorbs less oil than a long one.

Cultural Note

Fried chicken with a Ghanaian spice system is exactly the kind of thing diaspora cooking produces, and the bread-cube oil test is a reminder that useful technique travels regardless of which cuisine it starts in. That this is the one recipe of hers whose two printings agree perfectly is a small pleasure in a collection full of contradictions.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best at once. 2 days refrigerated, though the coating softens.

Reheating: A hot oven to re-crisp. Never the microwave.

Serve with: Jollof One-Pot Rice, Shito, Mango and pineapple salad
