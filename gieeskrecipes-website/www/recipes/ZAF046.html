🍗 Braaied Peri-Peri Chicken

Peri-peri hoender

A chicken spatchcocked flat, basted with half a batch of peri-peri and turned every five minutes over medium coals for forty-five minutes — with the other half of the sauce finished in coconut milk as a dip.

Flattening the bird is the whole method: spatchcocked, it cooks evenly and it gets maximum surface against the coals, which is where the sauce caramelises. The discipline is in the heat and the turning — medium coals and a turn every five minutes, because a sauce with this much sugar and pepper in it burns on high heat long before the chicken is done. Half the sauce goes on raw chicken; the other half becomes the dip, which keeps it safe.

Ingredients

Equipment: Braai with medium coals, Blender, Saucepan, Basting brush, Sharp scissors or a heavy knife, to spatchcock

    The chicken

    • 1 whole free-range chicken, spatchcocked

    The peri-peri sauce

    • 2 red onions, halved
    • 2 red peppers, halved and seeded
    • 1 whole head garlic, halved horizontally
    • 8 to 10 bird's-eye or serrano chillies, chopped
    • 2 tsp smoked paprika
    • 3 tbsp olive oil
    • 3 to 4 tbsp red wine vinegar
    • 1 lemon, zested and juiced
    • Sea salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste

    For the dipping sauce

    • 120 ml (1/2 cup) coconut milk

    Method

    1. Braai the onion and pepper halves and the halved head of garlic over medium coals, turning every 5 minutes, for about 15 minutes until softened and charred at the edges.
    2. Let them cool slightly, then blend with the chillies, smoked paprika, olive oil, vinegar, lemon zest and juice until smooth. Season.
    3. Simmer the sauce in a saucepan for 5 minutes.
    4. Baste the spatchcocked chicken with half the sauce and braai it over medium coals for 45 minutes, turning every 5 minutes, until cooked through.
    5. Add the coconut milk to the remaining sauce and simmer for 5 minutes to make the dip.
    6. Serve the chicken with the dipping sauce alongside.

    Chef Tips

    • Medium coals and a turn every five minutes. High heat burns the sauce black while the thigh is still raw at the bone, and that is the only real way to ruin this.
    • Spatchcock it properly — cut out the backbone and press the bird flat. A whole round chicken will not cook evenly on a grid.
    • Half the sauce for basting, half for the table. Never serve back sauce that has been brushed onto raw chicken.
    • Blistering the vegetables on the coals rather than in the oven gives the sauce its smoke, which is worth the extra fifteen minutes.
    • The thigh should read 75C at the bone. Forty-five minutes is a guide and birds vary.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cooking it over hot coals, which chars the baste and leaves the meat underdone.
    • Basting with the whole batch of sauce.
    • Leaving it undisturbed on the grid, so one side blackens.
    • Skipping the coconut milk in the dip — it is what takes the edge off enough to dunk.

    Health Benefits

    • Chicken provides lean protein, niacin and selenium.
    • Grilled over coals, so most of the fat renders away.
    • The sauce is built on roasted vegetables rather than oil or cream.

    Cultural Note

    Flame-grilled peri-peri chicken is one of the very few dishes that reaches nearly every South African through a restaurant chain as well as a home kitchen. The chain version is a descendant of the Portuguese-Mozambican original rather than the source of it, and the difference shows most in the smoke.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. The dip keeps 4 days.

    Reheating: Warm in a moderate oven, covered. The skin will not re-crisp fully.

    Serve with: Garlic bread, Slap chips with vinegar, Peri-Peri Sauce, extra
