🥞 Ethiopian Layered Pancake

ሙልሙል — Mulmul

Ethiopian Mulmul — a slightly fermented wheat pancake cooked like injera (never flipped, covered to steam) producing a soft, holey, spongy bread similar to injera but made from wheat. The Harari breakfast bread — lighter than injera with a milder sourness.

Mulmul is Harar's answer to injera — a slightly fermented wheat pancake cooked by the same covered, single-side method. It produces a softer, less sour, more tender result than teff injera, and is the traditional breakfast bread of eastern Ethiopia's Harari community.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl for fermentation, Non-stick pan (25cm), Lid

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • ½ tsp active dry yeast
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Dissolve yeast and sugar in warm water. Add flour and salt. Whisk smooth.
  2. Cover and rest 1 hour at room temperature until slightly bubbly.
  3. Heat pan to medium. Pour a ladleful — thin layer.
  4. Cover immediately. Cook 2—3 minutes until surface is dry and holes have formed.
  5. Never flip — slide off and cool.

Chef Tips

  • Thinner batter than injera — mulmul should be even more holey.
  • The 1-hour fermentation is a minimum — 2 hours produces more holes and flavour.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Flipping — never flip.
  • Too thick.

The Science

See ETH001 for the hole-formation science. Wheat-based mulmul forms holes more readily than teff injera because wheat gluten creates a more elastic foam structure that holds CO2 bubbles more effectively before they burst and form holes.

Cultural Note

Mulmul is the morning food of Harar — the ancient walled city that is one of Islam's holiest cities in Africa. The Harari breakfast tradition of mulmul with honey and spiced tea is one of Ethiopia's most refined morning rituals.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten same day. Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Damp cloth. Microwave 20 seconds.

Serve with: Honey, Butter, Spiced tea, Eggs
