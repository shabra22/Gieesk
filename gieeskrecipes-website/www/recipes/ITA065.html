Passatelli in Brodo

Passatelli in Brodo are short, ridged noodle-like strands made from breadcrumbs, Parmigiano, and egg, pushed through a special tool directly into simmering broth for a warming, deeply savory dish.

Not quite pasta in the traditional sense, passatelli is closer to a dense, savory noodle formed by pressing a firm dough through a wide-holed press (traditionally called a ferro) straight into hot broth, where it cooks in mere minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Passatelli press or a potato ricer with large holes, Large pot

  • 150 g (2 cups) fine dry breadcrumbs
  • 150 g (2 cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 3 eggs
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, a generous pinch
  • Zest of ½ lemon
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1.5 L (6 cups) good-quality beef or capon stock

Method

  1. Combine the breadcrumbs, Parmigiano, eggs, nutmeg, lemon zest, and salt in a bowl, mixing into a firm, cohesive dough — it should hold together well without being wet.
  2. Let the dough rest for 15 minutes to allow the breadcrumbs to fully absorb moisture and firm up.
  3. Bring the stock to a gentle simmer in a large pot.
  4. Working over the simmering pot, press the dough through a passatelli press or wide-holed ricer directly into the broth, cutting the strands to about 4–5cm as they emerge.
  5. Cook for 2–3 minutes until the passatelli float to the surface and are just cooked through — they cook very quickly.
  6. Ladle into bowls with plenty of broth and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • The dough should be quite firm — if it feels too soft or sticky to hold together when pressed, add a touch more breadcrumbs.
  • Passatelli cook extremely fast — watch closely and don't overcook, or they can become mushy.
  • A good-quality, well-seasoned stock genuinely makes or breaks this simple dish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Making the dough too wet, causing the strands to fall apart in the broth.
  • Overcooking, since passatelli only need a couple of minutes once pressed into the simmering broth.
  • Using a weak or bland stock, which leaves little to enjoy in such a minimal dish.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of protein from the egg and cheese.
  • The broth provides warming hydration, especially soothing in cold weather.

Cultural Note

Passatelli is a beloved dish throughout Romagna and the Marche region, especially popular during the winter months and often served at Sunday family lunches.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The dough keeps refrigerated for up to 1 day before pressing; the broth keeps for up to 4 days.

Reheating: Best pressed and cooked fresh; passatelli do not hold or reheat well once cooked.

Serve with: Nothing more — this is a complete dish served simply in its broth
