🫘 Bean Stew with Smoked Fish

Bean stew

The earlier, plainer version — no plantain, no name, a twenty-minute simmer and a printed question mark where the chilli quantity should be.

Two years before she published red red, the same author published this: a cup of dried cowpeas washed, soaked and cooked, a medium onion, an eight-ounce tin of tomato sauce, half a pound of smoked fish, a quarter to a third of a cup of palm oil and half a teaspoon of salt, simmered approximately twenty minutes. The phrase red red does not appear anywhere in it, and there is no fried plantain. It is the same stew without the elements that make it red red — and it carries a printed question mark where the chilli quantity should be, which is reproduced rather than filled in.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, for the beans, Heavy frying pan, Wooden spoon

  • 1 cup dried cowpeas, washed, soaked and cooked
  • 1 medium onion
  • 1 x 8 oz tin tomato sauce
  • 1/2 lb smoked fish
  • About 1/4-1/3 cup palm oil
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • Dried red pepper — the source prints '1/8 -? tsp'. See the note.

Method

  1. Wash and soak the cowpeas, then cook them until tender.
  2. Sauté the onion.
  3. Add the tomato sauce and the palm oil.
  4. Add the smoked fish and the dried red pepper.
  5. Add the cooked cowpeas.
  6. Simmer approximately 20 minutes.
  7. Season with the salt and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Twenty minutes here against about ten in her later red red. The longer simmer gives a deeper, more melded stew.
  • Her storage note is the best reason to make this version: it freezes well, and it tastes even better the next day.
  • The chilli quantity is genuinely missing — the source prints a question mark. Start at an eighth of a teaspoon and go up.
  • A quarter to a third of a cup of palm oil, slightly less than the later version's third of a cup. Leaner.
  • Cowpeas and black-eyed peas are the same family and the two recipes use the two names interchangeably.
  • No plantain in this version, which makes it a stew rather than a plate. If you want red red, add fried plantain and you have essentially Red Red.
  • Soak properly rather than quick-boiling. This version assumes a conventional soak.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Guessing high on the missing chilli quantity.
  • Cutting the twenty-minute simmer short.
  • Eating it the day it is made, when it is better the next.
  • Using a refined oil instead of palm oil.
  • Expecting it to be red red without the plantain.

Health Benefits

  • Cowpeas are high in plant protein, folate, iron and soluble fibre.
  • Leaner than the fried-plantain version.
  • Red palm oil provides provitamin-A carotenoids.
  • Smoked fish adds omega-3 fats and iodine.

Cultural Note

Seeing a cook publish the plain stew first and the named, plantain-accompanied version two years later is a small record of how a dish gets recognised — the stew was always there, and red red is what it becomes when you put the plantain beside it and give it a name.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, and it freezes well — her own note. Better the next day.

Reheating: Gentle heat.

Serve with: Rice, Fried plantain, Gari
