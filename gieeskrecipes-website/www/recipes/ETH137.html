🫘 Ethiopian Ful Medames

ፉል — Ful

Ethiopian Ful — cooked fava beans mashed with olive oil, lemon, garlic, cumin, and tomato. The breakfast tradition that crossed the Nile from Egypt and Sudan into Ethiopian urban eating culture — served with injera or bread.

Ful Medames is not indigenous Ethiopian but has become deeply embedded in Ethiopian urban breakfast culture through the country's connection to Egypt and Sudan via the Nile. The fava bean preparation, seasoned with lemon, garlic, and cumin, is served in tej houses and breakfast spots across Addis Ababa.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan

  • 2 cans (800g) cooked fava beans — or 2 cups home-cooked
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 4 tbsp lemon juice
  • 3 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tomatoes — diced
  • ¼ cup fresh parsley or coriander
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Chilli flakes to taste

Method

  1. Warm fava beans in a pan over medium heat.
  2. Mash roughly — about half should be mashed, half whole.
  3. Add olive oil, lemon juice, garlic, and cumin. Stir well.
  4. Top with diced tomatoes and herbs.
  5. Season. Drizzle extra olive oil.

Chef Tips

  • Half-mash — the mixture of mashed and whole beans is the characteristic texture.
  • Lemon is generous — the acidity is essential.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-mashing to a paste — wrong texture.

Cultural Note

Ethiopian ful is a beautiful example of how a food culture absorbs external influences — the ancient Nile Valley fava bean tradition, carried south into Ethiopia through trade and migration, has become part of the urban Ethiopian breakfast without ever losing its North African character.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), Pita-style bread, As an urban breakfast
