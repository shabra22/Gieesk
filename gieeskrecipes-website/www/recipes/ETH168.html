🥟 Ethiopian Meat Sambusa

ሥጋ ሳምቡሳ — Siga Sambusa

Ethiopian Meat Sambusa — the thin crispy sambusa pastry filled with spiced minced beef or lamb, onion, jalapeño, and berbere. The prestige sambusa — more expensive than lentil or potato versions, reserved for Ramadan iftar and celebrations.

Meat sambusa is the celebration sambusa — during Ramadan, iftar tables are laden with meat sambusa fried at sunset. The minced beef filling, spiced with berbere, berbere, and jalapeño, is more intensely flavoured than the lentil version and produces the most satisfying bite.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pan for filling, Rolling pin, Deep pan

    Sambusa Pastry (same as ETH161)

    • 2 cups all-purpose flour
    • ½ tsp salt
    • ½ cup water
    • 2 tbsp oil

    Meat Filling

    • 300g minced beef or lamb
    • 1 onion — very finely diced
    • 3 garlic cloves — minced
    • 1 green jalapeño — finely minced
    • 1 tsp berbere
    • 1 tsp cumin
    • ¼ cup fresh coriander
    • 1 tsp salt
    • 1 tbsp oil for cooking
    • Oil for frying

    Method

    1. Cook filling: heat oil. Cook onion until golden. Add garlic, jalapeño, berbere, cumin.
    2. Add mince. Brown completely — break up all clumps. Cook until completely dry.
    3. Add coriander. Season. Cool completely.
    4. Make pastry as per ETH161.
    5. Shape, fill, seal, and fry as per ETH161.

    Chef Tips

    • The meat filling must be completely cooked and completely dry — raw or wet filling is dangerous.
    • Mince should be well-seasoned — the pastry is neutral.
    • Make the filling the day before — it firms in the fridge and is easier to handle.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Undercooked filling — food safety risk.
    • Wet filling — breaks pastry.

    The Science

    See ETH161 for pastry science. Minced beef filling must be cooked to a minimum of 75°C (ground meat internal temperature) for food safety. The complete drying of the filling during cooking is doubly important — it both ensures food safety and prevents pastry breakage.

    Cultural Note

    Meat sambusa at Ramadan iftar is the pinnacle of Ethiopian Muslim street food culture — the sound of the call to prayer, the date in one hand and the meat sambusa in the other, breaking the day's fast with the most beloved fried snack. It is a moment of profound community and celebration.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately. Freeze uncooked.

    Reheating: Oven at 190°C.

    Serve with: At Ramadan iftar, With Silsi (ETH146), With dates at iftar
