🍟 Moos Shiilan

Moos Shiilan

Plantain cut on the bias, tossed in xawaash and seasoning, and fried two minutes a side — somewhere between chips and a sweet depending entirely on how ripe the fruit was.

The simplest thing in the southern Somali repertoire and the most adaptable. Fry semi-ripe plantain and it eats like a well-seasoned chip; fry it very ripe and it caramelises at the edges and tastes almost like a fried banana. Same recipe, two dishes, and the only variable is what was in the fruit bowl.

Ingredients

Equipment: Frying pan, Paper towels

  • 2 ripe plantains
  • 1/4 tsp xawaash spice blend
  • 1/4 tsp vegetable seasoning, or a good pinch of salt
  • 60 ml (1/4 cup) canola oil, for frying

Method

  1. Peel the plantains.
  2. Cut them on a bias into slices about 1.25 cm thick — the angle gives you more surface to brown.
  3. Toss the slices with the xawaash and the seasoning until every piece is coated.
  4. Heat the oil in a pan over medium heat.
  5. Fry for 2 minutes on the first side, then turn.
  6. Fry for 1 1/2 minutes on the second side and drain on paper towels. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Ripeness decides the dish. Semi-ripe gives you something like a chip; very ripe gives you something close to a dessert. Choose on purpose rather than by accident.
  • Cutting on the bias is not decorative — it gives each slice more contact with the pan and a better crust.
  • Very ripe plantain has more sugar and will brown much faster. Drop to a lower heat and watch it.
  • Serve straight from the pan. Fried plantain goes limp within a few minutes of resting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cutting the slices straight across and thin, so they cook through before they colour.
  • Frying very ripe plantain at the same heat as semi-ripe, which burns the sugars.
  • Crowding the pan, which drops the temperature and steams the slices.
  • Seasoning after frying, when the xawaash has nothing to stick to.

Health Benefits

  • Plantain supplies potassium, vitamin A and vitamin C.
  • Greener fruit is higher in resistant starch and lower in sugar.
  • Plant-based and, at four servings, a modest portion of fried food.

Cultural Note

The banana on the side of a Somali plate is not a garnish and is not a joke: it is a structural part of the meal, balancing salt and acid. Fried plantain is the cooked version of that same instinct.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Eat at once. It does not keep well.

Reheating: Crisp briefly in a hot dry pan; a microwave makes it soft.

Serve with: Maraq Moos Bukiini, Bariis Iskukaris, Suugo Suqaar
