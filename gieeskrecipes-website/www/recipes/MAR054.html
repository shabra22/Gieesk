🌿 Chermoula (Herb and Spice Marinade)

Chermoula / charmoula (شرمولة)

A fresh paste of coriander, parsley, garlic, cumin, paprika, olive oil and lemon, used to marinate fish and meat or spooned over grilled food.

15mTotal time
15mPrep
8Servings
70Calories
EasyDifficulty

The cook chops the herbs properly, crushes the garlic, then either pulses everything in a food processor or pounds it in a mortar until she is happy with the texture. The same base takes a spoonful of tomato paste (double for a red chermoula), turmeric for a yellow one, or a small preserved lemon for a lemony version. The author says the best and most authentic chermoula in Morocco is made with fresh herbs and garlic.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor or mortar and pestle, Jar

  • 3 cups fresh coriander (cilantro), roughly chopped
  • 1 cup fresh flat leaf parsley, roughly chopped, leaves only
  • 6 to 8 cloves garlic, peeled and roughly chopped
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp sweet paprika, powder or paste
  • 1/4 cup extra virgin olive oil
  • 2 tbsp water (optional, not needed if using a pestle and mortar)
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste (double it for a red chermoula)
  • 5 tbsp lemon juice, freshly squeezed
  • 1 tsp harissa paste, or to taste
  • 1/2 tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric (for a yellow chermoula)
  • 1 small preserved lemon, seeds removed (for a lemony chermoula)

Method

  1. Roughly chop the herbs and crush the garlic.
  2. Food processor: place the herbs and the rest of the ingredients in the bowl and pulse until you are satisfied with the texture. Thin with a few tablespoons of olive oil, water or a mix if needed.
  3. Mortar and pestle: start by pounding garlic and salt, then add the chopped herbs and spices in batches and pound to the texture you want. Chop the herbs properly first or the chermoula will be stringy.
  4. Use immediately or store in a jam jar or similar sealed container. If the paste is quite thick, top it off with olive oil. Keep in the fridge for up to a week.

Chef Tips

  • Cue: pound or pulse until you are happy with the texture; the page describes the target as pasty but coarse and runny.
  • For a red chermoula, double the tomato paste (the page also mentions a roasted peeled red pepper).
  • The author says the name comes from the Arabic verb meaning to rub or marinate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the chopping before pounding.
  • Using dried herbs, which are not authentic here.
  • Leaving it uncovered, which darkens it.

Cultural Note

The author writes that the name comes from the Arabic verb chermel, in reference to rubbing or marinating something with a spice mix, and that the best and most authentic chermoula in Morocco is made with fresh herbs and garlic.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Up to a week in the fridge, topped with olive oil.

Reheating: Not heated.

Serve with: Fish and meat marinades
