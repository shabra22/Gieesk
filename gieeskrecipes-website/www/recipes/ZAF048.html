🥖 Prego Roll

Prego

Rump seared hard and rested, piled onto toasted bread spread with onion marmalade, with a garlic, chilli, red wine and tomato sauce spooned over — twenty-five minutes from start to finish.

Prego means nail in Portuguese, and the name refers to the garlic studded into the steak. It arrived in South Africa through the Portuguese-Mozambican community and is now on every roadhouse and pub menu in the country. The whole dish turns on two things: not overcooking the steak, and toasting the bread so it can take the sauce without collapsing. It is a fast dish and it is at its best made to order.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Large heavy frying pan, Griddle pan or toaster, for the bread

    The sauce

    • 2 tbsp olive oil
    • 125 ml red wine
    • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
    • 1 red chilli, chopped
    • 2 medium tomatoes, roughly chopped
    • Salt and pepper, to taste

    The rest

    • 4 rump steaks
    • 1 tbsp olive oil, for the pan
    • 1 French loaf, cut into thick slices
    • 4 tbsp onion marmalade

    Method

    1. Heat 2 tbsp of the olive oil with the red wine, crushed garlic and chopped chilli in a saucepan over high heat.
    2. Add the chopped tomatoes and cook for 10 minutes, until the sauce starts to thicken. Season.
    3. Meanwhile heat the remaining 1 tbsp of oil in a large pan and sear the steaks until well browned on both sides — no more than 2 minutes on the first side and no more than 4 on the second, depending on thickness and how you want them.
    4. Rest the steaks on a board for a few minutes, then slice them.
    5. Toast the slices of French loaf in a hot pan, under the grill or in a toaster until golden, and spread them with the onion marmalade.
    6. Top with the sliced steak and a generous spoonful of the prego sauce.

    Chef Tips

    • Rest the steak before slicing, even for a few minutes. Sliced straight from the pan it loses its juice onto the board rather than into the bread.
    • Toast the bread properly. Untoasted bread under this sauce is soggy before it reaches the table.
    • Two minutes and then four, no more. These are steaks in a roll, not a braise.
    • A spoonful of peri-peri in the sauce is the common South African addition and makes it a peri-peri prego.
    • The onion marmalade is optional but it is what balances the chilli and the wine.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Overcooking the steak, which is the only real failure here.
    • Slicing it straight out of the pan.
    • Skipping the toasting.
    • Reducing the sauce too far, so it becomes a paste rather than something that soaks in.

    Health Benefits

    • Rump is a lean, high-protein cut with iron, zinc and B12.
    • The sauce is tomato, garlic and chilli rather than cream or butter.
    • A quick cook, so very little added fat.

    Cultural Note

    The prego is ubiquitous in South Africa and almost nobody thinks of it as Portuguese any more, which is a reasonable measure of how completely this strand has been absorbed. The Madeiran espetada — garlic and bay beef on a long skewer — travelled the same route and is still recognisably foreign; the prego is not.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Not a keeping dish. The sauce keeps 3 days refrigerated.

    Reheating: Reheat the sauce and cook fresh steak.

    Serve with: Slap chips, Peri-Peri Sauce, A cold beer
