🌴 Palm Nut Cream from Scratch

Banga sauce — palm nut butter, akwu

Four kilos of palm nuts boiled fifteen to twenty minutes until the flesh slides off the nut, bruised in a mortar without cracking the kernels, and worked by hand to extract the cream.

This is the base of banga, ofe akwu and abak atama, and it is worth knowing how it is actually made rather than only how to open a tin. Palm nuts are boiled until the hard fibrous flesh softens and slides off the nut, bruised in a mortar carefully enough not to crack the kernel inside, then covered with just-boiled water and left a few minutes while the water pulls the essence out and colours itself into a loose sauce. When it is cool enough to handle, you work the nuts and chaff by hand to extract as much thick juice and cream as you can, and strain. Four kilos gives about eight litres.

Ingredients

Equipment: Very large pot, Sieve or colander, Mortar and pestle, Large bowl, Tray, for air-drying the kernels

  • 4 kg palm nuts
  • 4 litres water, room temperature — see the note on the two water figures
  • 5 litres just-boiled water, for the extraction

Method

  1. Wash the palm nuts in a sieve or colander to remove dirt, then place them in a large pot with the water.
  2. Bring to the boil, then turn the heat down to medium-low.
  3. Cook 15 to 20 minutes, until the hard, fibrous flesh softens and slides easily off the nut.
  4. Remove from the heat.
  5. In a mortar and pestle, bruise the palm nuts, being careful not to crack the kernel which houses the nuts.
  6. Put the bruised nuts in a large bowl or pot — you may need to work in batches.
  7. Pour the just-boiled water over the nuts and leave a few minutes for the water to pull out the essence of the palm, colouring and flavouring the water till a loose sauce forms.
  8. When the temperature is workable, work the nuts and chaff by hand to extract as much thick juice and cream as you can.
  9. Strain through a sieve and reserve the liquid.
  10. Discard the chaff, and air-dry the nuts for a few days to make the kernels easier to extract.

Chef Tips

  • Fifteen to twenty minutes and the cue is tactile: the flesh should slide easily off the nut. Under-boiled and it will not release; over-boiled and you lose flavour to the water.
  • Bruise, do not crush. Cracking the kernel puts palm kernel — a completely different oil — into your sauce.
  • The extraction is done by hand and there is no substitute. Working the chaff is what gets the cream out.
  • Air-dry the spent nuts for a few days and the kernels come out easily. They are palm kernel, used for a different oil entirely.
  • This is palm nut pulp, not palm oil and not palm kernel oil. One named source is explicit that banga is made with the rich pulp of the palm fruit, not to be confused with palm oil or palm kernel oil.
  • Four kilos gives about eight litres of sauce. Freeze it in meal-sized portions — it is a long job and worth doing once.
  • A tinned concentrate is the usual shortcut and is much oilier; decant the excess oil off once it cools and keep it for banga rice.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cracking the kernels in the mortar.
  • Trying to extract the cream with a blender instead of by hand.
  • Boiling until the nuts fall apart.
  • Straining while it is too hot to work properly.
  • Confusing this with palm oil or palm kernel oil.

Health Benefits

  • Palm nut pulp is exceptionally rich in carotenoids and tocotrienols.
  • A whole-fruit product rather than a refined oil.
  • Vegan and gluten-free.
  • No added salt or sugar.

Cultural Note

Making palm nut cream from scratch is a half-day job and it is why the tinned concentrate exists. But the fresh version tastes of the fruit in a way the tin does not, and the by-products matter too — the spent nuts dry out to give palm kernel, and the oil decanted off the top of a tin goes into banga rice. Nothing in the palm fruit is wasted.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 5 days refrigerated, 6 months frozen in portions.

Reheating: Bring to a simmer. It separates when cold and recombines with heat and stirring.

Serve with: Banga Soup, Ofe Akwu, Abak Atama
