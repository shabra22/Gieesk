🍞 Pane Pugliese

Pane di Altamura

Pane Pugliese is a golden, durum-wheat bread from Puglia with a thick, crackling crust and a dense yet airy crumb, prized for staying fresh far longer than ordinary bread.

Made with semolina rimacinata (finely milled durum wheat), this bread — the most famous version of which, Pane di Altamura, holds Protected Designation of Origin status — gets its deep golden color and slightly sweet, nutty flavor from the durum flour, and its long shelf life from a natural sourdough starter.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Banneton or floured towel, Baking stone, Sharp blade for scoring

  • 400 g (2⅔ cups) fine semolina flour (semolina rimacinata)
  • 100 g (¾ cup) bread flour
  • 150 g (⅔ cup) active sourdough starter
  • 320 ml (1⅓ cups) warm water
  • 10 g (2 tsp) fine sea salt

Method

  1. Combine the semolina, bread flour, starter, and water in a large bowl and mix to a shaggy dough. Let rest 20 minutes (autolyse).
  2. Add the salt and knead for 10–12 minutes until smooth, elastic, and slightly tacky.
  3. Cover and bulk ferment for 4–5 hours at room temperature, folding the dough over itself every hour for the first 3 hours.
  4. Shape into a round or the traditional 'cornetto' crescent shape, place in a floured banneton, and proof for 2–3 hours until puffy.
  5. Preheat the oven to 240°C (465°F) with a baking stone inside.
  6. Turn the loaf out, score the top, and bake for 15 minutes at high heat, then reduce to 220°C (425°F) and bake a further 30–35 minutes until deeply golden and hollow-sounding.
  7. Cool completely on a rack — at least 2 hours — before slicing.

Chef Tips

  • Real semolina rimacinata (fine durum flour, not coarse semolina) is what gives this bread its signature golden crumb — don't substitute regular flour if you can help it.
  • The long, slow fermentation is what gives Pane Pugliese its exceptionally long shelf life.
  • Traditionally baked in a wood-fired oven — a very hot, well-preheated stone gets you closest at home.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using coarse semolina instead of finely milled semolina rimacinata — the texture will be gritty rather than tender.
  • Rushing the fermentation — the flavor and keeping quality both depend on a long, slow rise.
  • Cutting into the loaf before it has fully cooled.

Health Benefits

  • Durum wheat semolina has a lower glycemic index than many refined flours.
  • Natural sourdough fermentation improves mineral absorption and digestibility.

Cultural Note

Pane di Altamura was the first bread in Europe to receive Protected Designation of Origin (DOP) status in 2003, a recognition of its centuries-old, tightly regulated production method unique to the town of Altamura.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keeps at room temperature wrapped in cloth for up to a week — genuinely one of the longest-keeping Italian breads.

Reheating: Refresh in a hot oven for 5–8 minutes to restore crust crispness.

Serve with: Extra virgin olive oil, Burrata, Orecchiette dishes, Puglian olives
