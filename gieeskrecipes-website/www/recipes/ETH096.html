🫙 Ethiopian Fresh Cheese

አይብ — Ayib

Ethiopian Ayib — fresh milk heated with lemon juice or vinegar until curdled, then drained through cloth into a soft, mild fresh cheese. The essential companion to kitfo, gomen, and the celebration platter — its cool mildness tempers the heat of berbere and mitmita.

Ayib is the cool counterbalance on the Ethiopian table — the mild, fresh cheese that tempers the intense heat of berbere and mitmita. Served alongside kitfo, it is eaten together in every bite, the lactic freshness cutting through the mitmita's fierce heat. Made in 2 hours from milk and a souring agent.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan, Muslin cloth or fine sieve, Bowl

  • 2 litres whole milk
  • 4 tbsp lemon juice or white wine vinegar
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Heat milk over medium heat to 85°C — steaming but not boiling.
  2. Add lemon juice or vinegar. Stir once.
  3. Remove from heat. Rest 10 minutes — the curds will separate from the whey.
  4. Line a sieve with muslin cloth.
  5. Pour curdled milk into the cloth.
  6. Let drain for 1—2 hours.
  7. The collected solids are Ayib.
  8. Season lightly with salt.

Chef Tips

  • The milk should be at 85°C — too cool and curds won't form; too hot and they become rubbery.
  • Do not stir after adding the souring agent — disturbing the curds produces a smaller yield.
  • Drain for 1 hour for soft ayib; 2 hours for firmer.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cold milk — curds don't form.
  • Stirring after adding acid.

The Science

Ayib is an acid-precipitated cheese — the lemon juice's citric acid drops the milk's pH below 4.6 (the isoelectric point of casein protein). At this pH, the casein micelles destabilise and aggregate into curds. Unlike rennet-set cheese, acid-set cheese coagulation is rapid and temperature-dependent — above 70°C, the acid coagulation is most complete.

Cultural Note

Ayib is the peacemaker on the Ethiopian table — between the fierce heat of mitmita on kitfo and the tongue, it stands as the cool, mild intermediary. No Ethiopian celebration platter is complete without it, and no piece of kitfo is eaten without a bite of ayib alongside.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days.

Reheating: Serve at room temperature.

Serve with: With kitfo (ETH029), With gomen (ETH106), On the celebration platter alongside all wats, As a cooling accompaniment to hot dishes
