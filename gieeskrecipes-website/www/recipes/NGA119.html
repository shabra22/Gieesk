🥫 Gbegiri from Canned Beans

Gbegiri

The thirty-minute gbegiri — canned beans blended with iru and half a cup of water, cooked covered, and total cooking time should not be more than twenty minutes.

This is gbegiri with the two longest steps removed, and the author says so plainly: the recipe takes away the first two steps, which are the longest, so you only need to blend and cook. Canned haricot or black-eyed beans are drained, rinsed and blended with the locust beans and half a cup of water until smooth, then go into a saucepan with palm oil, ground prawns, pepper and salt, covered on reduced heat. Total cooking should not be more than twenty minutes. There is no peeling and no sieving — which is a real departure.

Ingredients

Equipment: Colander, Blender, Saucepan with a lid

  • 1 can haricot or black-eyed beans
  • 1 tbsp ground dried prawns
  • 1 tsp fermented locust beans (iru)
  • 1 tsp pepper flakes or ground pepper powder
  • 1 tbsp red palm oil
  • 1/2 cup water, for blending
  • 1 bouillon cube
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Drain and rinse the beans in a colander.
  2. Blend them with the locust beans and half a cup of water until smooth.
  3. Pour into a saucepan with the palm oil, ground prawns, pepper and salt.
  4. Cover and reduce the heat.
  5. Stir regularly.
  6. Total cooking time should not be more than 20 minutes.
  7. Serve.

Chef Tips

  • Twenty minutes maximum. The author states it directly and there is no benefit to longer — the beans are already cooked.
  • Blend the iru in with the beans rather than adding it to the pot. It disperses completely that way.
  • Rinse the canned beans properly. The canning liquid is salty and slightly metallic.
  • No peeling and no sieving here, which is the real departure. If the texture is too coarse for you, pass it through a sieve after blending — that is the one step worth adding back.
  • A tablespoon of palm oil for six servings makes this much lighter than the traditional versions, which use three tablespoons to a quarter cup.
  • Stir regularly. Blended beans catch on the base fast, and there is no long cooking stage to hide it.
  • This is a declared shortcut, not a traditional gbegiri. For the full version with soaking, peeling and sieving see Gbegiri.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking it for longer than twenty minutes.
  • Not rinsing the canned beans.
  • Adding the iru whole to the pot.
  • Leaving it unstirred, so it catches.
  • Expecting the velvet texture of the peeled-and-sieved version.

Health Benefits

  • Cowpeas and haricot beans both supply plant protein, folate and soluble fibre.
  • Much lower in fat than the traditional versions at one tablespoon of oil for six servings.
  • Low glycaemic index.
  • Dried prawns add protein and iodine.

Cultural Note

A thirty-minute gbegiri is not what anyone in Ibadan would make, and its author is honest that she has cut the two longest steps out. But it is the version a Nigerian cook abroad on a weeknight will actually produce, and knowing exactly which steps were removed — the soak and the peel — tells you what the long version is buying you.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Low heat with water to loosen. It sets firm cold.

Serve with: Amala, Ewedu, Abula
