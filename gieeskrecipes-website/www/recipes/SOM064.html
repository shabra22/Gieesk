🧆 Kutuleeti Digaag

Kutuleeti Digaag

Chicken beaten thin, dipped in garlic egg wash and pressed into breadcrumbs mixed half and half with parmesan, fried three minutes total — the Somali cutlet, whose name comes straight from the Italian cotoletta.

Kutuleeti is a loanword: Italian cotoletta, worn down by Somali mouths into the word for any meat or chicken coated in breadcrumbs and fried. It is one of the clearest traces the colonial period left on the Mogadishu kitchen, alongside baasto and doolshe. Served here deconstructed rather than smothered — the sauce on toasted bread beside the cutlet, so the crust stays crisp and the fresh mozzarella stays fresh.

Ingredients

Equipment: Meat mallet or rolling pin, Two wide dishes, for the egg and the crumbs, Mortar and pestle, Heavy frying pan

    For the cutlets

    • 454 g (1 lb) skinless boneless chicken breast, 4 halves
    • 1 clove garlic
    • 1 tsp salt, heaped if the salt is coarse
    • 2 eggs
    • 2 tbsp milk
    • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
    • 125 g (1 cup) plain dry breadcrumbs
    • 110 g (1 cup) grated parmesan
    • Canola oil, for frying

    For the marinara

    • 3 tbsp olive oil
    • 1 onion, finely diced
    • 3 cloves garlic, finely chopped
    • 1/2 tsp salt
    • 6 tomatoes, peeled, seeded and diced
    • 1/4 tsp sugar
    • 1 tbsp fresh basil, chopped
    • 2 tbsp olives, chopped

    To serve

    • 4 slices Calabrese or other good bread
    • 250 g fresh mozzarella, in 8 slices
    • 2 tomatoes, sliced

    Method

    1. Trim any fat from the chicken, slice each breast half horizontally, then lay each piece between two sheets of cling film and pound it to an even thickness.
    2. Crush the garlic with the salt in a mortar.
    3. Beat the eggs in a wide dish, add the milk, then the crushed garlic and the black pepper, and mix well.
    4. In a second dish, mix the breadcrumbs and grated parmesan together.
    5. Dip each piece of chicken in the egg, then press it into the crumb mixture, patting firmly so the coating holds.
    6. For the marinara, soften the onion in the olive oil for 2 minutes, add the garlic for a minute more, then the diced tomatoes, salt and sugar. After 7 minutes stir in the basil and olives.
    7. Heat the frying oil over medium heat. Drop in a few breadcrumbs to test it — when they foam, it is ready.
    8. Fry the cutlets for 2 minutes on the first side, then flip and give them 1 minute more. Drain on paper towels.
    9. Toast the bread, spoon the marinara over it, and serve the cutlets alongside with slices of fresh mozzarella and tomato.

    Chef Tips

    • Pound the chicken genuinely thin. A thick cutlet needs longer in the oil and the crumbs are too dark by the time it is cooked through.
    • Breadcrumbs and parmesan in equal measure is what makes the crust taste of something. Use a hard gratable cheese — Parmigiano Reggiano, Grana Padano or Pecorino Romano.
    • Test the oil with a few crumbs rather than guessing. When they foam, it is at temperature.
    • Serve the sauce beside, not on top. Marinara poured over a fried cutlet softens the crust within a minute, and fresh mozzarella is at its best unmelted.
    • The meatless marinara has its own Somali name, dallac bilaash, the worthless sauce — because there is no meat in it.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Leaving the chicken thick, which forces you to choose between raw inside and burnt crumbs.
    • Using pre-grated hard cheese from a shaker, which does not melt into the crust.
    • Frying in oil that is not yet at temperature, so the coating drinks it up.
    • Baking the cutlet under sauce and cheese, which is a different dish and a soggy one.

    Health Benefits

    • Chicken breast is lean and high in protein.
    • Parmesan contributes calcium and a great deal of flavour for its weight.
    • Fresh tomato marinara rather than a cream sauce keeps the plate light.

    Cultural Note

    Italy governed southern Somalia for most of the first half of the twentieth century and left its vocabulary in the kitchen: baasto for pasta, doolshe from dolce for cake, jallaato from gelato, and kutuleeti from cotoletta. What Somalis did with them is the interesting part — the pasta got a xawaash stew instead of a sauce, and the cutlet got served beside its marinara rather than under it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days; keep the sauce separate.

    Reheating: Re-crisp in a hot oven for 8 minutes. Never in a microwave.

    Serve with: Dallac Bilaash, Isbaggeti Somali, Green salad
