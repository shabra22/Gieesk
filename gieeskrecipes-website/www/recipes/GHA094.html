🐠 Grilled Tilapia

Grilled tilapia

Four medium tilapia marinated as little as five minutes, over charcoal burnt down half an hour — and Ghanaians feel cheated if you don't leave the head on.

Grilled tilapia with banku is the Ghanaian plate most likely to be eaten outdoors, and the recipe is simple enough to be mostly about the fire. Two pounds of charcoal is lit and allowed to burn down for about half an hour. Four medium tilapia, about half a pound each, are marinated at least five to fifteen minutes in grated shallots, oil, salt, cayenne, about a quarter cup of fresh chilli and grated ginger. After three to five minutes the fish is turned, and it should cook in about ten to fifteen minutes. The tip that matters most is cultural: leave the head on.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal grill, Fish basket or wide spatula, Grater, Bowl

  • 4 medium tilapia, about 1/2 lb each
  • 2 tbsp grated shallots or onion
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil, like canola
  • About 1 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp hot ground cayenne pepper, or to taste
  • About 1/4 cup (4 tbsp) fresh chilli pepper
  • About 1/2 tsp peeled, grated fresh ginger

For the fire

  • About 2 lb charcoal

Method

  1. Light the charcoal — about 2 pounds — and allow it to burn down for about half an hour.
  2. Clean and score the tilapia, leaving the heads on.
  3. Mix the grated shallots, oil, salt, cayenne, fresh chilli and ginger.
  4. Marinate the fish at least 5 to 15 minutes.
  5. Lay the fish on the grill.
  6. After 3 to 5 minutes, turn the fish over.
  7. It should cook in about 10 to 15 minutes in total.
  8. Serve with banku and shito.

Chef Tips

  • Leave the head on. Barbara Baeta's line, as reported, is that Ghanaians feel cheated if you don't — and it is also the best-eating part of the fish.
  • Charcoal burnt down for half an hour means grey coals, not flames. Tilapia skin on open flame is a disaster.
  • Five to fifteen minutes is a very short marinade and it is deliberate — fresh tilapia does not want to be sat in salt and acid.
  • Turn once, after three to five minutes. Repeated turning tears the skin.
  • Ten to fifteen minutes total for a half-pound fish. No sensory cue is published, so go by the flesh at the thickest point near the bone.
  • A quarter cup of fresh chilli for four fish is generous. It is a wet marinade rather than a dry rub.
  • Score the fish before marinating so the mixture reaches the flesh — not in the source, but standard practice.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Grilling over flames rather than burnt-down coals.
  • Removing the heads.
  • Turning the fish repeatedly.
  • A long marinade, which makes the flesh mushy.
  • Overcooking past fifteen minutes.

Health Benefits

  • Tilapia is lean, high in protein and low in mercury.
  • Grilled with only a tablespoon of oil for four fish.
  • Ginger and chilli both carry documented bioactive compounds.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.

Cultural Note

Grilled tilapia with banku and a pot of shito, eaten at a plastic table with a cold drink, is as close to a Ghanaian national meal as anything. The insistence on leaving the head on is not sentiment — the cheeks and the collar are the best eating, and serving a headless fish reads as cheating someone.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. Grilled fish is best eaten hot.

Reheating: Briefly under a hot grill, or eat cold.

Serve with: Banku, Shito, Green Shito
