Crostini Toscani

Crostini Toscani are small toasted bread rounds topped with a rich, savory chicken liver pâté — a classic Tuscan antipasto found on nearly every trattoria table in the region.

This pâté, known as fegatini, is built on a base of sautéed onion, anchovy, and capers cooked down with chicken livers and a splash of Vin Santo or Marsala wine, then blended smooth. Spread warm on Pane Toscano, it's one of the defining flavors of Tuscan cuisine.

Ingredients

Equipment: Skillet, Food processor or knife for chopping, Baking sheet

  • 1 baguette or Pane Toscano, thinly sliced
  • 300 g (10 oz) chicken livers, trimmed
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 anchovy fillets
  • 2 tbsp capers, rinsed
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) Vin Santo or dry Marsala wine
  • 30 g (2 tbsp) butter
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) olive oil, plus more for the bread
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • 1 bay leaf

Method

  1. Heat the olive oil and half the butter in a skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and bay leaf, and cook gently until soft, about 8 minutes.
  2. Add the chicken livers and cook until browned on the outside but still slightly pink inside, about 5–6 minutes.
  3. Add the anchovies and capers, stirring until the anchovies dissolve into the mixture.
  4. Pour in the wine and simmer until mostly evaporated, about 3–4 minutes. Remove the bay leaf.
  5. Transfer to a food processor with the remaining butter. Pulse to a coarse, spreadable pâté — traditional Tuscan crostini topping is rustic, not perfectly smooth. Season with salt and pepper.
  6. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F). Brush the bread slices with olive oil and toast on a baking sheet for 5–8 minutes until golden and crisp.
  7. Spread the warm liver pâté generously over the toasted bread and serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Don't overcook the livers — they turn grainy and chalky if cooked too long.
  • A rustic, slightly chunky texture is traditional; avoid over-processing into a completely smooth pâté.
  • Serve the crostini while the bread is still warm and crisp for the best contrast against the pâté.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the chicken livers, resulting in a dry, grainy pâté.
  • Skipping the anchovies — they add crucial savory depth without tasting fishy once cooked down.
  • Assembling too far ahead, so the toast loses its crispness.

  • Chicken liver is an excellent source of iron, vitamin A, and B vitamins.

Cultural Note

Crostini Toscani are a staple starter across Tuscany, often the first thing served at a traditional multi-course Tuscan meal alongside a glass of Chianti.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The pâté keeps refrigerated in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Toast bread fresh each time.

Reheating: Gently warm the pâté before spreading; toast bread fresh rather than reheating.

Serve with: Chianti or other Tuscan red wine, Antipasto platters, Pane Toscano
