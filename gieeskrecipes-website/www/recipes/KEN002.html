Sukuma Wiki

Sukuma Wiki — Swahili for "push the week" — is Kenya's most beloved everyday vegetable dish. Collard greens wilted with tomatoes, onion, and garlic into a bright, flavourful side that stretches any meal further without sacrificing nutrition or taste.

You'll find Sukuma Wiki simmering on nearly every stove in Kenya — from Nairobi apartments to rural homesteads, from roadside mama mbogas to city restaurants. When piled beside a dome of steaming white ugali and scooped up by hand, it becomes something greater than the sum of its humble ingredients.

Ingredients

Equipment: Frying pan or sufuria, Wooden spoon, Knife, Chopping board, Measuring spoons

  • 500g sukuma wiki (collard greens) — washed and thinly sliced
  • 1 medium onion (120g) — finely chopped
  • 2 medium tomatoes (200g) — diced
  • 2 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable or sunflower oil
  • 1 tsp salt — adjust to taste
  • ¼ tsp black pepper — optional
  • 1 tsp Royco or beef seasoning — optional
  • ¼ cup (60ml) water — if needed during cooking

Method

  1. Wash the sukuma wiki thoroughly under running water, inspecting each leaf carefully.
  2. Remove any tough central stems if desired, then stack the leaves and slice into thin, even strips.
  3. Heat the oil in a sufuria or frying pan over medium heat until shimmering.
  4. Add the chopped onion and cook for 3–4 minutes, stirring, until soft and translucent.
  5. Stir in the minced garlic and cook for about 30 seconds until fragrant — don't let it brown.
  6. Add the diced tomatoes and cook for 3–4 minutes until they break down into a light sauce.
  7. Season with salt, black pepper, and Royco if using.
  8. Add the sliced sukuma wiki in batches, stirring continuously as each addition wilts down.
  9. If the pan looks dry, add a small splash of water to keep things moving.
  10. Cook for 5–7 minutes until tender but still vibrantly green.
  11. Taste, adjust seasoning, and serve immediately alongside ugali.

Chef Tips

  • Don't overcook — the greens should stay vibrant and have a gentle bite. Dull, khaki-green sukuma wiki is overcooked.
  • Young, fresh leaves are sweeter and more tender. Avoid yellowing or wilted bunches.
  • A small knob of butter stirred in at the very end adds richness without overwhelming the greens.
  • For extra protein, stir in cooked kidney beans or a few scrambled eggs just before serving.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking — the greens lose their colour, nutrients, and texture completely.
  • Too much water — the dish becomes watery and loses its concentrated flavour.
  • Skipping the tomatoes — they provide balance, moisture, and a gentle acidity that lifts the dish.
  • Cooking on high heat — scorches the onion and garlic before the tomatoes have time to soften.

Health Benefits

  • Rich in vitamins A, C, and K — supports immunity, vision, and bone health.
  • Excellent source of dietary fibre — promotes healthy digestion.

  • Naturally low in calories (95 kcal per serving) while delivering valuable micronutrients.

Cultural Note

Sukuma Wiki appears on menus from roadside mama mbogas to family restaurants across Kenya. Its Swahili name — "push the week" — reflects how it has helped generations of Kenyan families eat nutritiously on a budget.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Freezing not recommended — texture becomes soft after thawing.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a pan over medium heat with a teaspoon of water, or microwave until warmed through.

Serve with: White Ugali, Brown Ugali, Beef Stew, Nyama Choma, Fried Tilapia, Goat Stew, Chapati, Rice, Githeri
