🥥 Swahili Coconut Beef Curry

Mchuzi wa Nyama ya Nazi

Swahili Coconut Beef Curry — tender beef simmered in a rich, fragrant sauce of coconut milk, tomatoes, and coastal spices. Where mainland beef curry uses water-based stock, this coastal version adds thick coconut milk for a creamier, sweeter, more aromatic result.

Swahili coconut beef curry is what happens when the inland beef curry tradition meets the coast's beloved coconut milk. The result is richer, creamier, and more complex than either alone — the coconut's natural sweetness balancing the warm spices while the beef's depth enriches the coconut sauce. Served with coconut rice, it is the coastal celebration meal for meat lovers.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Wooden spoon

  • 800g beef — stewing cuts, cubed
  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 5 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 cup beef stock
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Brown beef in batches in hot oil. Remove and set aside.
  2. Cook onions until deeply golden — 10 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 8—10 minutes until very thick.
  5. Add all spices except garam masala. Stir 1 minute.
  6. Return beef. Add stock. Cover and simmer 60—70 minutes until tender.
  7. Add coconut milk. Stir gently. Simmer on low for 15 minutes — do not boil hard.
  8. Stir in garam masala. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Add coconut milk only after the beef is tender — too early and the long cooking splits it.
  • The sauce should be thick before adding coconut milk.
  • Gentle final simmer after coconut milk — 15 minutes maximum.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding coconut milk too early.
  • Boiling hard after adding coconut milk.

The Science

Coconut milk is added at the end because extended boiling breaks the emulsion. Brief gentle simmering integrates the flavours without splitting.

Cultural Note

Coconut beef curry is a celebrated dish of the Swahili coast — the meeting of two great culinary traditions: the East African beef stew and the coastal coconut milk that transforms everything it touches.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop — add splash of coconut milk if needed.

Serve with: Wali wa Nazi, Chapati, Kachumbari, Coconut Chutney
