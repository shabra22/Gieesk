🫓 Panelle

Panelle

Palermo's beloved chickpea flour fritters, cooked into a thick paste, cooled and sliced, then fried until crisp — classic Sicilian street food, often stuffed into fresh bread.

Panelle require patience rather than skill: the chickpea batter must be cooked and stirred until thick as polenta, then chilled completely before it can be sliced and fried, at which point the outside turns delicately crisp while the inside stays soft.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed saucepan, Whisk, Baking sheet or tray, Deep pot for frying

  • 250 g (2 cups) chickpea flour
  • 750 ml (3 cups) water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley, finely chopped
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • Lemon wedges, for serving

Method

  1. Whisk the chickpea flour into the water gradually in a saucepan, working out any lumps.
  2. Add the salt and cook over medium heat, whisking constantly, until the mixture thickens into a smooth, heavy paste, about 15–20 minutes.
  3. Stir in the parsley in the final minute of cooking.
  4. Pour the hot mixture onto a lightly oiled baking sheet or tray and spread to an even thickness of about 5mm.
  5. Let cool completely, then refrigerate for at least 2 hours until firm.
  6. Cut the firm chickpea paste into rectangles or triangles.
  7. Heat vegetable oil to 180°C (350°F) and fry the pieces in batches until golden and crisp on both sides.
  8. Drain on paper towels and serve hot with lemon wedges, traditionally tucked into soft bread rolls.

Chef Tips

  • Whisk constantly while the batter thickens to prevent lumps and scorching on the bottom of the pan.
  • The mixture must be completely cold and firm before slicing, or it will fall apart when handled.
  • Traditionally served inside a soft bread roll (pane e panelle) as a filling street food snack in Palermo.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not cooking the batter long enough, leaving it too soft to hold its shape once chilled and sliced.
  • Slicing before it's fully chilled and set, causing the pieces to crumble.
  • Frying at too low a temperature, resulting in oily rather than crisp panelle.

Health Benefits

  • Chickpea flour provides plant-based protein and fiber, and the dish is naturally vegan and gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Panelle are a defining street food of Palermo, sold from small kiosks throughout the city and traditionally eaten as a quick, inexpensive snack or light meal.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Uncooked chilled paste keeps refrigerated for up to 2 days; fried panelle are best eaten fresh.

Reheating: Reheat in a hot oven to help re-crisp; avoid the microwave.

Serve with: Soft bread rolls, for a classic pane e panelle sandwich, Lemon wedges
