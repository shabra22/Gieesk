☕ Tanzanian Zanzibar Coffee

Kahawa ya Zanzibar

Zanzibar Kahawa — finely ground dark coffee brewed with ginger, cardamom, and cloves in a small pot, served in tiny cups without milk. The ancient Swahili coffee ceremony — more cardamom than coffee, more ritual than beverage.

Zanzibar spiced coffee is one of the oldest traditions of the Swahili coast — the coffee served from brass pots by vendors in Stone Town since the Arab era. The spices are as important as the coffee; many Zanzibaris consider it primarily a spice beverage that uses coffee as its medium.

Ingredients

Equipment: Small pot or jebena (Ethiopian coffee pot), Tiny cups

  • 4 tbsp finely ground dark roast coffee
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp ground ginger
  • ¼ tsp ground cloves
  • 500ml water
  • 2—3 tsp sugar (to taste)

Method

  1. Combine water, coffee, cardamom, ginger, and cloves in a small pot.
  2. Bring to a boil, stirring.
  3. Reduce heat. Simmer 3—4 minutes.
  4. Remove from heat. Rest 2 minutes for grounds to settle.
  5. Add sugar. Stir gently.
  6. Pour slowly into tiny cups, leaving grounds in the pot.
  7. Serve without milk.

Chef Tips

  • Pour slowly — don't disturb the settled grounds.
  • No milk in Zanzibar kahawa.
  • The coffee should be intense but the spices should be equally present.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding milk.
  • Pouring too fast — muddy coffee.

The Science

The spices in Zanzibar kahawa — particularly cardamom — have volatile aromatic compounds that are released during the brewing boil. Unlike extraction-based brewing, boiling the spices directly extracts both water-soluble and slightly fat-soluble aromatics, producing a more integrated flavour.

Cultural Note

Zanzibar spiced coffee is the island's most ancient social ritual — served from brass pots in tiny cups at every gathering, offered to every guest, sipped slowly while the conversation turns. It has been the coffee of Zanzibar's merchants, scholars, and fishermen for centuries.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Brew fresh each time.

Reheating: Not recommended.

Serve with: Vitumbua (TZA126), Kashata (TZA127), Mahamri (TZA132)
