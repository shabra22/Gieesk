🫓 Konso Sorghum Flatbread

ማሻ — Masha

Konso Masha — sorghum flour flatbread cooked on a clay griddle. The staple bread of the UNESCO Heritage terraced farmers of southern Ethiopia. Made from the sorghum that grows on the famous Konso terraces — the bread of one of Africa's oldest agricultural traditions.

Konso Masha is the bread of UNESCO-protected terraces — the sorghum that grows on the ancient stone-walled terraces of the Konso people is ground into flour and cooked into simple flatbreads that have sustained this community for over 400 years.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Konso people — UNESCO World Heritage terraced landscape

The Konso people are one of the most remarkable agricultural communities in Africa — their UNESCO World Heritage terraced landscape in the southern Ethiopian highlands demonstrates over 400 years of sophisticated land management. The Konso grow sorghum and maize in their terraces and use them to make Masha, a simple sorghum flatbread that is their staple food. Konso society is highly organised with age-grade systems (Gada-like), and their traditional foods reflect a culture of communal work and shared eating.

Ingredients

Equipment: Clay griddle or flat pan

  • 2 cups sorghum flour
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ¾ cup warm water (approximately)

Method

  1. Combine sorghum flour and salt.
  2. Add water gradually — mix to a firm but pliable dough.
  3. Divide into 6 balls. Press flat.
  4. Cook on a dry clay griddle or pan over medium heat.
  5. 3—4 minutes per side until cooked through and slightly charred.

Chef Tips

  • Sorghum has no gluten — the bread will be denser and less pliable than wheat bread.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expecting wheat-like texture.

The Science

Sorghum has no gluten proteins — the flatbread holds together through starch gelatinisation and naturally occurring beta-glucans.

Cultural Note

Konso Masha is baked on the terraces that UNESCO designated as a World Heritage Site in 2011 — one of Africa's most remarkable agricultural achievements. The bread of a people who built an agricultural civilisation without modern tools.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Room temperature 2 days.

Reheating: Warm on griddle.

Serve with: With Konso stews, At communal Konso meals
