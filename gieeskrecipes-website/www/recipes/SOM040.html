🥣 Fuud Ari

Fuud Ari

Lamb bones simmered two hours to a clear, aromatic broth, finished with diced potato and carrot, black pepper, coriander and a squeeze of lemon — the small bowl a Somali restaurant puts in front of you before you have ordered.

Walk into a Somali restaurant anywhere in the world and a cup of broth arrives before the menu does. Somali soup is deliberately thin and aromatic — nothing thickened, no cream, no cheese — and traditionally it is not a recipe at all but the liquid lifted off a pot of goat or lamb already cooking for the main course, seasoned at the last moment with pepper, coriander and lemon. Made from the bones of a shoulder you have already grilled, it costs nothing.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large stockpot, Skimming spoon, Sieve

  • 900 g (2 lb) lamb bones
  • 3.3 L (14 cups) water, or enough to cover the bones
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 1 potato, peeled and finely diced
  • 1 carrot, peeled and finely diced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable seasoning, or to taste
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper, or to taste
  • 1 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
  • Lemon wedges, to serve

Method

  1. Put the bones in a large pot and add enough cold water to cover them.
  2. Bring up to a gentle simmer — never a rolling boil — and cook for one hour, skimming the surface as scum rises.
  3. Add the sliced onion, the garlic and the vegetable seasoning, and simmer gently for one hour more.
  4. Lift out the bones, picking off any meat worth keeping and returning it to the pot.
  5. Add the diced potato and carrot, cover, and cook for 15 minutes until just tender.
  6. Finish with freshly ground black pepper and the chopped cilantro. Serve hot with crusty bread and a wedge of lemon to squeeze in at the table.

Chef Tips

  • A gentle simmer gives clear broth; a boil emulsifies the fat and turns it cloudy and greasy. This is the only real technique in the dish.
  • Skim in the first half hour, when most of the scum rises. After that it looks after itself.
  • The lemon is squeezed in at the table, not in the pot. It lifts the whole bowl and it needs to be fresh.
  • Traditionally there is no separate stock to make: ladle the broth off a pot of lamb or goat already cooking, and finish it with pepper, coriander and lemon.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling instead of simmering, which is what makes a cloudy, fatty broth.
  • Adding the vegetables at the start, so they cook to nothing over two hours.
  • Thickening it. Somali soup is thin on purpose, and flour or cream turns it into something else entirely.
  • Salting heavily early on — the broth reduces over two hours and concentrates whatever salt is in it.

Health Benefits

  • Long-simmered bone broth provides collagen, gelatine and minerals.
  • Low in calories and easy to digest, which is why it is offered to the unwell.
  • Chilling it lets you lift off the fat, leaving a very lean broth.

Cultural Note

The welcome bowl of broth is a fixture of Somali hospitality, in restaurants and in homes. It costs the household nothing — it is a by-product of the meat already cooking — which is exactly what makes it a gesture that can always be made.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days; the fat sets on top and lifts off easily. Freezes for 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat to a simmer on the hob. Add the cilantro and lemon fresh each time.

Serve with: Crusty bread, Cad iyo Caano, Lemon wedges
