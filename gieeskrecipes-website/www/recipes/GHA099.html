🥘 Jollof One-Pot Rice

Jollof rice

A three-stage build on a ready-made jollof sauce — and if the sauce isn't hot enough before the rice goes in, the rice soaks up cold water and goes soggy.

This is jollof built from components: a jollof sauce and a dry spice mix made in advance, both published separately. The chicken simmers twenty minutes in half the spice mix, then bakes twenty to twenty-five minutes until crisp while the broth is reserved. The remaining onions are softened with the rest of the spice mix, chilli powder, curry powder and three hundred and fifty millilitres of sauce, and the broth goes back in. Washed rice is stirred in, the reserved sauce ladled over without stirring, and the pan covered with foil and a lid for fifteen to twenty minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pan with a lid, Foil, Roasting tray, Ladle

  • 300 g basmati or long-grain white rice
  • 500 ml jollof sauce
  • 2 tbsp jollof dry spice mix
  • 6-8 bone-in chicken portions, thighs or drumsticks
  • 2 large onions, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, very finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp thyme leaves
  • 1 large bay leaf
  • 5-6 guinea peppers, crushed
  • 1 scotch bonnet chilli, deseeded and diced
  • 1 tsp crushed sea salt
  • 4 tbsp groundnut or sunflower oil
  • 1 tsp hot chilli powder
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • Olive or groundnut oil, for the first stage

To serve

  • Chopped parsley or coriander
  • Shito or green kpakpo shito salsa

Method

  1. Sauté half the onions in oil, add the chicken with the garlic, thyme, bay leaf, guinea peppers, scotch bonnet and 1 tablespoon of the spice mix.
  2. Cover with water and simmer 20 minutes, until tender.
  3. Meanwhile heat the oven to 180 degrees and bake the chicken 20 to 25 minutes, until crisp. Reserve the broth.
  4. Heat the 4 tablespoons of oil and sauté the remaining onions until soft.
  5. Add the remaining spice mix, the chilli powder, the curry powder and 350 ml of the jollof sauce.
  6. Add the reserved broth.
  7. Wash the rice in three changes of cold water, until it runs clear.
  8. Stir the rice into the sauce.
  9. Ladle in the reserved sauce WITHOUT stirring.
  10. Cover with foil and the lid and cook 15 to 20 minutes, until the liquid absorbs and the rice is tender.
  11. Garnish and serve with shito.

Chef Tips

  • The warning is the most useful line in any jollof recipe: if the sauce isn't hot enough before adding the rice, the rice will soak up the cold water and become soggy. Everything must be at a simmer when the rice goes in.
  • Three changes of cold water until clear. More thorough than most recipes ask and it matters at this hydration.
  • Ladle the reserved sauce in WITHOUT stirring. That is what gives the top and bottom different characters rather than one uniform rice.
  • Foil AND the lid, which is the same trick as the Nigerian party-jollof method. It traps steam far better than a lid alone.
  • Three hundred and fifty millilitres of the five hundred goes in with the aromatics; the rest is ladled over at the end. Keep the split.
  • Simmer the chicken then CRISP it in the oven. Boiled-then-baked gives you cooked-through meat with a browned exterior.
  • Add water gradually if the rice dries out — her own note, and it is the recovery move if the ratio is off.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding rice to a sauce that is not hot.
  • Stirring after the reserved sauce is ladled in.
  • Washing the rice once rather than three times.
  • Lid without foil.
  • All the sauce in at the start.

Cultural Note

Building jollof from a standing sauce and a standing spice mix is a restaurant approach, and it is how this cook made a party dish reproducible for a London kitchen. The three-stage method — simmer, crisp, then cook the rice in the broth — is more work than a one-pot, and the result is closer to what a good party jollof actually tastes like.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Steam, or microwave with a splash of water under a damp cover.

Serve with: Shito, Green Shito, Kelewele
