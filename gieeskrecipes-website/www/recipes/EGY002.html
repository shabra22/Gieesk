🥙 Ta'meya (Egyptian Fava Bean Falafel)

Ta'amia / Ta'meya — الطعمية

Split fava beans soaked, ground with leeks, spring onions and a fistful of herbs, whipped, coated in sesame and fried until puffy, golden and green inside.

9hTotal time
30mPrep
30mCook
10Servings
210Calories
MediumDifficulty

Egyptian ta'meya uses split fava beans rather than chickpeas, which gives a green interior from leeks, spring onions, parsley, cilantro and dill. The beans soak at least 8 hours until they crush between two fingers, are ground to a fine paste with the aromatics and spices, then beaten for 3 to 5 minutes so the batter turns light. Patties are coated in sesame and fried in 350°F oil until puffed and golden.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor, Stand or hand mixer, Deep frying pan, Thermometer, Slotted spoon

  • 500 grams split fava beans
  • 2 leeks, chopped (including white and green parts)
  • 2-3 cups mixed parsley, cilantro and dill, cilantro, and dill leaves, stems removed, (see notes)
  • 4-5 spring onions, see notes
  • 1 green pepper, optional
  • 5 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1-3 tablespoons tap water, (see notes)
  • 2 teaspoons ground coriander
  • 2 tablespoons ground cumin
  • 1 ½ teaspoons sweet paprika
  • 1-2 teaspoon(s) harissa paste, (optional)
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder, optional (see notes)
  • salt and pepper to taste
  • 1 tablespoon coarsely crushed coriander seeds, optional
  • 2-3 tablespoons sesame, for coating
  • 1 cup unflavored frying oil, such as canola

To serve

  • warm pita bread or Egyptian Aish Baladi
  • tahini sauce
  • assortment of pickles
  • chopped fresh tomatoes, cucumber and lettuce

Method

  1. Soak the split fava beans in cold water for at least 8 hours or overnight, until a bean can be crushed between two fingers with no effort. Rinse and drain.
  2. Process the beans to a fine, smooth paste. Add the leeks, spring onions, garlic, herbs and spices, adding water by the spoonful only if needed, until the batter is sticky and smooth and a vivid green with a pistachio shade.
  3. Beat the batter on medium speed for 3 to 5 minutes (no more than 5) until it is light, about the consistency of whipped cream.
  4. Shape patties no thicker than ½ inch and press the surface into sesame or crushed coriander seeds.
  5. Heat the oil to 350°F and fry the patties until puffy and golden all over, in batches.
  6. Serve in warm bread with tahini sauce, pickles and chopped salad vegetables.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: beans crush with two fingers after soaking; batter smooth and vivid green; fry until puffy and all golden. The page does not print a frying time per batch.
  • Fry one test patty first; if it falls apart the batter needs a little more beating or a few more minutes of resting (our tip).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using whole dried fava with the skins on instead of split, peeled beans.
  • Over-beating past about 5 minutes, which the page warns against.
  • Oil that is too cool, so the patties soak up fat.

Cultural Note

Nermine Mansour writes that ta'amia was more than breakfast, 'an experience', and that it differs from falafel by using fava rather than chickpeas. She names a favourite Alexandria shop established in 1957 (single reading).

Storage & Serving

Storage: Fried patties are best fresh; the batter can be kept covered in the fridge for a short time (our estimate; the page gives no hold time).

Reheating: Re-crisp in a hot oven for a few minutes (our suggestion).

Serve with: Aish baladi, Egyptian tahini sauce, Salata baladi (tomato-cucumber salad)
