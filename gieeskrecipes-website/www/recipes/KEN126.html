🍞 Mkate wa Sinia

Mkate wa Sinia

Mkate wa Sinia is one of the oldest traditional breads of the Swahili coast — a fragrant, slightly dense coconut rice bread made from rice flour, coconut milk, eggs, and cardamom, steamed until set. A unique Swahili coastal preparation found nowhere else in Kenya.

Mkate wa Sinia stands apart from all other Kenyan breads — it is neither fried like mandazi nor griddled like chapati. It is steamed, producing a uniquely moist, slightly dense texture with a subtle coconut fragrance and the characteristic slightly sticky surface of a rice flour preparation. In Lamu and Mombasa Old Town, it has been prepared for celebrations for centuries.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Lamu and Mombasa Old Town — one of the oldest breads of the Swahili coast

Mkate wa Sinia is a traditional Swahili steamed coconut rice bread — one of the most ancient preparations in coastal Kenyan cooking. The word sinia refers to the large round tray it is traditionally steamed in. Made from rice flour and coconut milk, it is denser than mahamri, with a unique steamed texture unlike any other Kenyan bread. It is prepared for Eid celebrations, weddings, and special coastal gatherings.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large round baking tin or sinia, Large pot for steaming, Mixing bowl, Whisk

  • 2 cups rice flour
  • 400ml thick coconut milk
  • 2 large eggs
  • ½ cup sugar
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp salt
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 2 tbsp butter — melted

Method

  1. Whisk eggs and sugar until pale and slightly thickened.
  2. Add coconut milk, melted butter, and vanilla. Mix well.
  3. Sift in rice flour, baking powder, cardamom, and salt. Whisk until smooth — no lumps.
  4. Rest the batter for 30 minutes — allows rice flour to hydrate.
  5. Pour batter into a greased round tin.
  6. Place tin in a large pot with about 5cm of boiling water. Cover tightly.
  7. Steam for 20—25 minutes until a skewer inserted in the centre comes out clean.
  8. Allow to cool in the tin for 10 minutes before unmoulding.
  9. Slice into wedges and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Resting the batter is important — rice flour needs time to hydrate fully.
  • Ensure the steaming pot has enough water and is well sealed — steam escaping produces an uneven bread.
  • The surface should be set and slightly springy when done — not wet.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not resting the batter.
  • Insufficient water in the steaming pot.
  • Opening the lid during steaming — steam escapes and the bread does not set.

The Science

Rice flour lacks gluten, so the structure of mkate wa sinia comes from the egg proteins setting during steaming rather than gluten network formation. This is why the texture is denser and more cake-like than wheat breads, and why over-beating the batter is not a concern.

Cultural Note

Mkate wa Sinia is a living piece of Swahili cultural heritage — one of the few truly ancient coastal breads still prepared in traditional Kenyan households. Its preparation signals a celebration along the coast.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in airtight container for 2 days. Refrigerate in hot weather.

Reheating: Steam briefly for 5 minutes to restore the soft texture.

Serve with: Kenyan Masala Chai, Honey, Fresh Tropical Fruit, Coconut Chutney
