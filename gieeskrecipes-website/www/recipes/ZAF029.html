🍫 Peppermint Crisp Tart

Peppermint crisp tert

Caramel folded into whipped cream, layered with whole Tennis biscuits and crushed mint chocolate, and left in the fridge for seven hours — no oven, four ingredients, and completely unembarrassed about being modern.

This is a twentieth-century dessert and openly brand-dependent, which is unusual enough to be worth stating plainly. The Peppermint Crisp bar was invented in South Africa by Wilson Rowntree in the 1960s and is now made by Nestle; the tart is built on it, on tinned caramel, and on Tennis biscuits, a coconut biscuit that has no real substitute. It turns up at every braai, church fete and family gathering in the country, and nobody pretends it is heritage cooking.

Ingredients

Equipment: Stand mixer or electric beaters, 30 x 22 x 6.5 cm dish, Fork, Spatula

  • 750 ml (3 x 250 ml tubs) whipping cream
  • 4 x 360 g tins caramel treat
  • 2 x 200 g packets Tennis biscuits, or another coconut biscuit
  • 3 x 150 g slabs Peppermint Crisp, or another mint-filled chocolate, roughly broken

Method

  1. Whip the cream to soft peaks — about 3 minutes on medium in a stand mixer, then faster for the last minute.
  2. In a separate bowl, beat the caramel with a fork until it is smooth and loose.
  3. Fold the caramel gently into the whipped cream. Fold, do not beat, or the mixture slackens and will not hold its layers.
  4. Add most of the broken chocolate, keeping a cupful back for the top.
  5. Line the dish with a single layer of whole Tennis biscuits.
  6. Spread over half the caramel mixture, add another layer of biscuits, then the rest of the caramel mixture.
  7. Scatter the reserved chocolate over the top, cover with foil and chill for at least 7 hours before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Loosen the caramel with a fork before it goes anywhere near the cream. Straight from the tin it is too stiff to fold and you will knock the air out trying.
  • Whole biscuits, not crushed. They soften into a cake-like layer overnight, which is the texture the tart is built on.
  • Seven hours is the floor. Overnight is better, and it is genuinely better on day two.
  • Assembly takes half an hour at most. Everything else is waiting.
  • Leftovers keep well, which is more than can be said for most cream desserts.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Beating the caramel into the cream instead of folding it, which gives a runny tart that will not set.
  • Crushing the biscuits, which turns the layers to mush.
  • Cutting it after three hours, when it has not set and slides apart on the plate.
  • Over-whipping the cream to stiff peaks before folding, after which the mixture turns grainy.

Health Benefits

  • Cream and caramel supply calcium and protein, which is about as far as this goes.
  • Cut into twelve, and a smaller portion than it looks is usually enough.
  • No baking and no added sugar beyond what is in the tins and the chocolate.

Cultural Note

The tart is the clearest example of a South African classic that is entirely modern and entirely commercial, and it is loved without apology. If you are cooking outside South Africa the Tennis biscuit is the hard part to replace — any plain coconut biscuit gets you close, and a digestive does not.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate, covered, up to 4 days. It improves on the second day.

Reheating: Never. Served cold from the fridge.

Serve with: Coffee, Nothing — it is rich enough, A braai, which is where it usually appears
