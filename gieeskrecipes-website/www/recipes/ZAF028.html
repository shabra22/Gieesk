🥮 Hertzoggie Bake

Hertzoggies — tweegevrietjies

Apricot jam over a coconut-flour base under a coconut meringue, baked as one tray and sliced into squares — the tray-bake version of a tartlet with a genuinely political history.

Hertzoggies were made by Cape Muslim women in honour of Prime Minister J.B.M. Hertzog, who had promised women the vote and the coloured community equal rights. When the Women's Enfranchisement Act of 1930 made plain that only the first promise would be kept, the women baked a new version with brown and pink icing and called it a tweegevrietjie — a two-faced cake. This recipe is a modern tray bake rather than the traditional individual pastry cases, which is a real difference and worth saying: same three layers, one dish, sliced.

Ingredients

Equipment: 34 x 24 cm baking tray, well greased, Electric whisk, Palette knife

    The base

    • 240 g coconut flour
    • 100 g caster sugar
    • 125 g butter, at room temperature
    • 2 extra-large free-range eggs
    • 1 tsp vanilla paste

    The filling

    • 240 ml (1 cup) apricot jam — or raspberry

    The coconut meringue topping

    • 2 free-range egg whites
    • 100 g caster sugar
    • 160 g desiccated coconut

    Method

    1. Heat the oven to 180C and grease a 34 x 24 cm baking tray very well.
    2. Rub the coconut flour, caster sugar and butter together with your fingertips until the mixture looks like breadcrumbs.
    3. Add the eggs and the vanilla paste and mix to a dough.
    4. Press the dough evenly into the tray, prick it lightly all over with a fork, and bake for 10 minutes.
    5. Take it out and spread the apricot jam over the warm base, right to the edges.
    6. For the topping, whisk the egg whites with the caster sugar until light and fluffy, then fold in the desiccated coconut.
    7. Spread the topping over the jam, return the tray to the oven and bake for 25 minutes, until golden.
    8. Cool completely in the tray before slicing into squares.

    Chef Tips

    • Coconut flour drinks liquid, so the dough will look crumbly when you press it in. Two extra-large eggs is the right amount; one leaves it too dry to hold.
    • Spread the jam while the base is still warm. It goes on far more easily and seals the base against the wet meringue.
    • Grease the tray very well. Jam that reaches the edge will glue itself to bare metal.
    • The traditional form is individual pastry cases with the same three layers — a shortcrust shell, apricot jam, coconut meringue. If you want the real thing, that is the shape to make.
    • Cool fully before cutting, or the layers slide apart.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cutting into it warm, which separates the jam layer from the base.
    • Spreading jam on a cold base, which tears the pastry.
    • Overbaking the meringue topping, which goes leathery rather than crisp.
    • Calling it a hertzoggie without qualification — the tartlet is the original and this is a tray-bake adaptation.

    Health Benefits

    • Coconut flour is high in fibre and naturally gluten-free.
    • Egg whites and coconut give the topping protein as well as sweetness.
    • Cut into twenty-six pieces, so portions are small by design.

    Cultural Note

    The tweegevrietjie is one of the sharpest pieces of political commentary in South African baking: a cake renamed and re-iced to say, publicly and deniably, that a promise had been broken. The women who did it were Cape Muslim, and the dish is still named after the politician who let them down.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Airtight at room temperature up to 4 days.

    Reheating: Not needed. Served at room temperature.

    Serve with: Rooibos tea, Coffee, Koe'sisters
