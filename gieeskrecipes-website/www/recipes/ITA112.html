🍚 Arancini al Ragù

Arancini al Ragù

Sicily's iconic fried rice balls, encasing a rich meat ragù and melting mozzarella inside a saffron-tinted rice shell, coated in breadcrumbs and fried until deeply golden.

Real Sicilian arancini require a genuinely sticky, well-seasoned risotto-style rice that holds its shape around the filling, and the true test of a good arancino is a crisp, greaseless shell giving way to a filling that's still hot and cohesive at the center.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan for risotto, Saucepan for ragù, Deep pot for frying

  • 400 g (2 cups) Arborio or Carnaroli rice
  • 1 L (4¼ cups) chicken or vegetable stock
  • Pinch of saffron threads
  • 60 g (⅓ cup) unsalted butter
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) ground beef
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 200 g (7 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 100 ml (scant ½ cup) dry red wine
  • 80 g (½ cup) frozen peas
  • 150 g (5.3 oz) mozzarella, cut into small cubes
  • 150 g (1½ cups) breadcrumbs
  • Flour, for dredging
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Cook the rice risotto-style: toast briefly in butter, then gradually add the saffron-infused hot stock, stirring frequently, until tender and quite sticky, about 18 minutes.
  2. Stir in the Parmigiano, spread onto a tray, and let cool completely, ideally chilled for at least 2 hours.
  3. For the ragù, sauté the onion, then brown the ground beef, breaking it up as it cooks.
  4. Add the wine and let it reduce, then add the crushed tomatoes and simmer for 30–40 minutes until thick. Stir in the peas near the end.
  5. Let the ragù cool slightly, then set up a station with the cooled rice, ragù, mozzarella cubes, beaten eggs, flour, and breadcrumbs.
  6. Take a portion of rice in your palm, make a well, and fill with a spoonful of ragù and a cube of mozzarella.
  7. Cover with more rice and shape firmly into a ball or classic cone shape, packing tightly so it holds together.
  8. Dredge each arancino in flour, then beaten egg, then breadcrumbs, coating thoroughly.
  9. Heat oil to 170°C (340°F) and fry the arancini until deeply golden and heated through, about 5–6 minutes, turning as needed.
  10. Drain and let rest for a few minutes before serving hot.

Chef Tips

  • Chilling the rice thoroughly before shaping is essential — warm rice is too loose to hold together properly.
  • Pack the rice firmly around the filling, leaving no air gaps, so the arancini don't fall apart during frying.
  • Maintaining a steady oil temperature is key to a crisp shell without a greasy result.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Shaping the arancini with warm rice, which falls apart during frying rather than holding a firm shape.
  • Underpacking the rice, leaving weak spots that burst open in the hot oil.
  • Frying at too low a temperature, resulting in greasy rather than crisp arancini.

Cultural Note

Arancini are so beloved in Sicily that shapes vary meaningfully by region — round in Palermo, cone-shaped in Catania, said to evoke Mount Etna — and disputes over the 'correct' shape remain a point of local pride.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate uncooked, shaped arancini for up to 1 day, or freeze for up to 1 month before frying.

Reheating: Reheat cooked arancini in a hot oven to re-crisp; avoid the microwave, which leaves them soggy.

Serve with: A simple side salad, Lemon wedges
