🔴 Red Red

Red red

Twice red — the stew from red palm oil, the plantains ripe and fried — with black-eyed peas, smoked fish and a tin of tomato sauce.

Red red is Ghana's famous bean stew with fried plantain, and the name is doubly earned: the stew is red from the tomato and the palm oil, and it is served with ripe red-gold plantains. A cup of dried black-eyed peas is quick-boiled two minutes, stood an hour and cooked until tender, about an hour or so. A large onion, half a pound of smoked fish, an eight-ounce tin of tomato sauce and a third of a cup of red palm oil make the stew, which simmers about ten minutes with the salt left until the end because the smoked fish is salty. Plantains fry in a quarter to half an inch of oil.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, for the beans, Heavy frying pan, Second frying pan, for the plantain, Slotted spoon

  • 1 cup black-eyed peas, uncooked — or about 3 cups cooked or frozen
  • Several nicely ripened plantains, firm not overly ripe
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • About 1/2 lb smoked fish
  • 1 x 8 oz tin tomato sauce
  • 1/3 cup red palm oil
  • 1/2 tsp salt, or to taste — the smoked fish is salty
  • Chilli to taste — begin with about 1/2 tsp dried
  • Vegetable oil, for frying the plantain

Optional

  • Ginger, garlic, herbs, powdered shrimp, dry gari, 1-2 tbsp tomato paste

Method

  1. Quick-boil the black-eyed peas 2 minutes, then stand 1 hour.
  2. Cook them until tender, about an hour or so.
  3. Sauté the onion about 10 minutes.
  4. Add the tomato sauce, palm oil, chilli and smoked fish.
  5. Simmer about 10 minutes.
  6. Add the cooked beans and the salt at the end.
  7. Heat the frying oil to about a quarter to half an inch deep.
  8. Fry the plantain slices, turning when they are well-browned on one side.
  9. Serve the stew with the fried plantain alongside.

Chef Tips

  • Salt at the end, not the beginning. The smoked fish is salty and you cannot take it back out.
  • Firm, not overly ripe plantains. Her own specification — they need to hold their shape in the pan and still be sweet.
  • A quarter to half an inch of oil for the plantain. Shallow frying, not deep.
  • Turn when well-browned on one side, which is the only frying cue given. Once per side.
  • A third of a cup of palm oil, and the red of the dish comes as much from the oil as from the tomato.
  • The quick-boil-and-stand-an-hour is a shortcut for an overnight soak. Then about an hour of real cooking.
  • A lighter alternative she offers: boiled or roasted plantain instead of fried. It makes a much leaner plate and is a genuine option rather than a compromise.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Salting before the smoked fish has gone in.
  • Over-ripe plantains that disintegrate in the pan.
  • Deep-frying rather than shallow-frying the plantain.
  • Turning the plantain repeatedly.
  • Substituting another oil for red palm oil.

Health Benefits

  • Black-eyed peas are high in plant protein, folate, iron and soluble fibre.
  • Red palm oil supplies provitamin-A carotenoids and vitamin E tocotrienols.
  • Smoked fish adds omega-3 fats, protein and iodine.
  • Beans and plantain together give a low-glycaemic, high-fibre plate.

Cultural Note

Red red is sold from roadside pans with a mound of fried plantain and a sprinkle of gari, and it is probably the single most-eaten cooked meal in southern Ghana. The doubled name is a good joke about a dish where every element is some shade of red or orange.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. Fry the plantain fresh.

Reheating: Gentle heat. The stew improves overnight.

Serve with: Fried plantain, Gari, Kelewele
