🥗 Insalaaddo

Insalaaddo — Ansalaato

Romaine, underripe tomato, radish, cucumber and green onion, dressed at the last moment with a shaken emulsion of lemon, vinegar, olive oil, garlic and a quarter teaspoon of sugar — the salad that comes with the rice.

A plate of bariis without a salad beside it is an incomplete meal in Somali eating, and this is the composed version — lettuce-based, cut into bite-size pieces, with a dressing shaken in a jar until it emulsifies and poured on only as it goes to the table. The ratio is three parts oil to one of total acid, and the quarter teaspoon of sugar is there to take the edge off the vinegar rather than to sweeten anything. Underripe tomatoes are specified on purpose: they hold their shape and keep their bite.

Ingredients

Equipment: Glass jar with a lid, for the dressing, Salad spinner or clean tea towel, Serving bowl

    The salad

    • 8 cups romaine lettuce, cut into bite-size pieces
    • 2 underripe tomatoes, sliced
    • 1 English cucumber, sliced
    • 3 radishes, sliced
    • 2 green onions, chopped

    The dressing

    • 30 ml (2 tbsp) fresh squeezed lemon juice
    • 15 ml (1 tbsp) white vinegar
    • 90 ml (6 tbsp) extra virgin olive oil
    • 1 clove garlic, finely minced
    • 1.25 ml (1/4 tsp) salt
    • 1.25 ml (1/4 tsp) sugar
    • A pinch of freshly ground black pepper

    Method

    1. Wash the romaine under cold running water, cut it into bite-size pieces and dry it thoroughly — spin it or pat it dry.
    2. Wash and slice the tomatoes, radishes and cucumber. English cucumber needs no peeling; peel a thick-skinned variety.
    3. Wash and chop the green onions.
    4. Combine the tomatoes, radishes, cucumber and green onions in a serving bowl with the lettuce.
    5. Put all the dressing ingredients in a glass jar and shake well until an emulsion forms.
    6. Add the dressing only just before serving, so the lettuce stays crisp.

    Chef Tips

    • Dress it at the table, not in the kitchen. Dressed early, the lettuce wilts and the whole thing goes limp within ten minutes.
    • Dry the lettuce properly. Wet leaves refuse to hold dressing and you end up with a puddle at the bottom of the bowl.
    • Underripe tomatoes, as specified. Ripe ones bleed and soften and turn the dressing pink.
    • Shake the dressing in a jar rather than stirring it in a bowl — you want a proper emulsion, and shaking is what gets you there.
    • Make the dressing fresh. Leftover dressing keeps 2 to 3 days refrigerated and no longer.
    • There is a second, quite different Somali salad under the same name: no lettuce at all, everything diced small — firm tomato, seeded cucumber, carrot, green onion, parsley — with just salt, half a lemon and a tablespoon of olive oil. It is sharper and leaner, and some households add diced pear, apple or watermelon to it.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Dressing it in advance.
    • Not drying the lettuce.
    • Using ripe tomatoes.
    • Leaving out the sugar and wondering why the vinegar is so sharp.
    • Mincing the garlic coarsely, so someone gets a whole piece.

    Health Benefits

    • Romaine supplies folate, vitamin K and vitamin A.
    • Extra virgin olive oil brings monounsaturated fat and polyphenols.
    • Raw and no-cook, so nothing is lost to heat.
    • Entirely plant-based and very low in calories.

    Cultural Note

    The name gives away the route: insalaaddo is the Italian insalata, one of a long list of Somali food words carried in during the colonial period and kept — like isbaggeti for spaghetti and isbinaasho for spinach. The dish arrived as a loan and settled into the meal so completely that rice without it now looks bare.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Undressed components keep 2 days refrigerated. Dressing keeps 2 to 3 days.

    Reheating: Not applicable.

    Serve with: Bariis Iskukaris, Hilib la Solay, Digaag la Foorneeyay
