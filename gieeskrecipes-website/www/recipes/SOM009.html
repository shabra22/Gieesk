🍮 Xalwo

Xalwo

Somalia's beloved sweet, jellied confection, cornstarch and sugar cooked for a long stretch into a dense, fragrant treat perfumed with cardamom and studded with nuts, essential at weddings and Eid.

Making genuine xalwo demands real patience and strong arms - the mixture must be stirred continuously for the better part of an hour as it thickens from a liquid into a dense, glossy, pull-apart confection, and getting the timing right is considered a genuine mark of skill.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed pot, Shallow dish for setting

  • 400 g (2 cups) granulated sugar
  • 600 ml (2 1/2 cups) water
  • 100 g (3/4 cup) cornstarch
  • 80 ml (1/3 cup) vegetable oil or ghee
  • 4 cardamom pods, crushed
  • 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
  • 1 tsp rosewater or vanilla extract
  • 60 g (1/2 cup) roasted almonds or cashews, chopped

Method

  1. Combine the sugar and water in a heavy pot and bring to a simmer, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
  2. In a separate bowl, whisk the cornstarch with a little cold water to form a smooth slurry.
  3. Gradually whisk the cornstarch slurry into the simmering sugar syrup.
  4. Add the cardamom and nutmeg, and continue cooking over medium-low heat, stirring constantly.
  5. After about 20 minutes, the mixture will begin to thicken; continue stirring as it transforms into a thick, glossy paste.
  6. Gradually stream in the oil or ghee while stirring, allowing it to fully incorporate - this step takes patience and continuous stirring.
  7. Continue cooking and stirring for another 20-30 minutes, until the mixture pulls away cleanly from the sides of the pot and becomes dense and elastic.
  8. Stir in the rosewater and half the chopped nuts.
  9. Pour into an oiled shallow dish, smooth the top, and scatter with the remaining nuts.
  10. Let cool and set for at least 2 hours before cutting into pieces.

Chef Tips

  • Continuous stirring throughout the cooking process is non-negotiable - stopping even briefly can cause the mixture to scorch or set unevenly.
  • Adding the oil gradually, while stirring constantly, is what gives xalwo its signature glossy, elastic texture.
  • The xalwo is ready when it pulls cleanly away from the sides of the pot rather than sticking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stopping stirring even briefly, which risks scorching the bottom of the pot.
  • Adding the oil too quickly, which can prevent it from fully incorporating.
  • Undercooking, leaving the xalwo too soft to hold its shape once cooled.

Cultural Note

Xalwo is inseparable from Somali celebrations, playing a central role at weddings, Eid, and other major gatherings, where its lengthy preparation is itself considered a gesture of hospitality.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 weeks.

Reheating: Not applicable; served at room temperature.

Serve with: Somali coffee or tea, As part of a wedding or Eid dessert spread
