🐖 Porchetta

Porchetta

A whole deboned pork roast, generously seasoned with garlic, rosemary, and fennel, rolled tight and slow-roasted until the skin turns impossibly crackling and the meat falls apart.

Porchetta is central Italy's great street food and festival dish, traditionally prepared by specialist porchettari and sold in thick, herb-flecked slices from vans and market stalls, though the home version rewards the same long, careful roasting.

Ingredients

Equipment: Kitchen twine, Roasting pan, Sharp knife for scoring

  • 1 whole pork belly with skin, about 3kg (6.6 lb), deboned
  • 8 cloves garlic, minced
  • 3 tbsp fresh rosemary, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp fennel seeds, toasted and lightly crushed
  • 2 tbsp coarse sea salt
  • 1 tbsp black pepper
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Kitchen twine

Method

  1. Lay the pork belly skin-side down and score the meat side lightly in a crosshatch pattern.
  2. Combine the garlic, rosemary, fennel seeds, salt, pepper, and olive oil into a paste and rub it thoroughly over the meat side, working it into the scored cuts.
  3. Roll the pork belly tightly into a cylinder, skin-side out, and tie securely at intervals with kitchen twine.
  4. Refrigerate uncovered overnight to dry out the skin, which helps it crackle properly during roasting.
  5. Bring to room temperature before cooking. Score the skin in a diamond pattern and rub generously with salt.
  6. Roast at 150°C (300°F) for about 3–3.5 hours until the meat is very tender.
  7. Increase the oven temperature to 220°C (425°F) for the final 20–30 minutes to crisp and crackle the skin, watching closely to avoid burning.
  8. Rest for at least 20 minutes before slicing into thick rounds.

Chef Tips

  • Drying the skin uncovered in the refrigerator overnight is the real secret to achieving proper crackling.
  • Roll the pork as tightly as possible and tie it securely so it holds its shape and cooks evenly.
  • Resting the meat properly after roasting makes it much easier to slice into neat rounds.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the overnight skin-drying step, resulting in chewy rather than crisp, crackling skin.
  • Rolling the roast too loosely, leading to uneven cooking and an untidy final shape.
  • Slicing immediately after roasting, before the juices have had time to redistribute.

Cultural Note

Porchetta is a beloved fixture of central Italian sagre (food festivals) and markets, traditionally associated with the towns of Ariccia in Lazio and Norcia in Umbria, both famous for their versions.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in the oven; the skin will lose some crispness but the meat remains excellent.

Serve with: Crusty bread, traditionally as a sandwich filling, A robust red wine
