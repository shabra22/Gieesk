🍛 Kenyan Chicken Pilau

Pilau ya Kuku

Pilau ya Kuku — fragrant basmati rice cooked with tender chicken pieces, deeply caramelised onions, and the classic Kenyan pilau spice blend of cardamom, cinnamon, cumin, cloves, and pepper. Quicker than goat or beef pilau but equally aromatic and celebratory.

Chicken pilau has become the most accessible version of Kenya's beloved pilau — it cooks faster than beef or goat, making it practical for weekday celebrations and last-minute gatherings. The deeply caramelised onion base and whole pilau spices give it the same aromatic character as the meat versions, but with a lighter, more delicate result.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot with lid, Wooden spoon

  • 1 kg chicken pieces — bone-in
  • 2 cups basmati rice — rinsed
  • 3 large onions — thinly sliced
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 5 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 4 cups chicken stock
  • 4 cardamom pods — crushed
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 4 whole cloves
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 6 black peppercorns
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Heat oil. Fry onions for 15—20 minutes until deeply golden brown.
  2. Add whole spices. Fry 1 minute.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes. Cook for 8 minutes until thick.
  5. Add chicken pieces. Brown for 5 minutes.
  6. Add 2 cups stock. Cover and simmer for 25—30 minutes until chicken is cooked through.
  7. Add rinsed rice and remaining 2 cups stock. Stir once.
  8. Bring to a boil, reduce to lowest heat. Cover tightly and cook for 20 minutes.
  9. Rest covered for 10 minutes. Fluff and garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Deep caramelisation of onions is the foundation — 15—20 minutes minimum.
  • Chicken cooks faster than beef or goat — do not overcook during the initial simmering stage.
  • Remove the chicken pieces and debone before serving if preferred — makes it easier to eat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Pale onions — produces flat-tasting pilau.
  • Overcooking chicken before adding rice — it becomes dry in the finished dish.

The Science

Bone-in chicken releases gelatin from the bones during simmering, enriching the stock that then cooks the rice. This gives chicken pilau a slightly richer, more complex base than boneless preparations.

Cultural Note

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days. Freeze for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Sprinkle with water and reheat gently in a pot or microwave covered.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Natural Yogurt, Coconut Chutney, Mango Achar
