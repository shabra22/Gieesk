🟡 Krakro

Krakro — kaklo, kakro, plantain fritters

Overripe plantain blended with ginger, cloves and selim grains and deep-fried — no flour at all, and usually eaten with bambara nuts.

Krakro is the Akan name and kaklo the Ga one for deep-fried overripe plantain balls, and this Ewe version uses no flour whatsoever: two overripened plantains, twenty grams of ginger, ten cloves, five grains of selim, half a habanero, an onion and a teaspoon of salt, blended and fried in half a litre of oil. The spicing is what distinguishes it — hwentia and cloves are unusual in a fritter — and her note is that it is usually paired with bambara nuts. The method is video-only, so the steps here are reconstructed and labelled as such.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender or mortar, Deep pot or fryer, Slotted spoon, Paper towels

  • 2 overripened plantains
  • 20 g ginger
  • 10 cloves — see the note on the ambiguity
  • 5 grains of selim (hwentia)
  • 1/2 habanero chilli
  • 1 medium onion
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 500 ml vegetable oil, for frying

Optional

  • 10 g chopped coriander
  • 10 g spring onions

Method

    The published method for this recipe is video-only. No written steps exist in the source.

    1. Peel the overripe plantains.
    2. Blend or pound them with the ginger, cloves, selim grains, habanero, onion and salt to a thick batter.
    3. Stir through the coriander and spring onions if using.
    4. Heat the oil for deep frying.
    5. Drop spoonfuls of the batter into the oil.
    6. Fry until deep golden brown all over, turning so they colour evenly.
    7. Drain on paper towels and serve warm with bambara nuts.

    The order above is inferred from the other documented version of this fritter, not from this source.

      Chef Tips

      • No flour at all in this version, which makes it softer and more intensely of plantain than the versions that add flour and baking powder.
      • Overripened, not just ripe. Black-skinned plantains are what you want — the sugar is what browns and the softness is what lets it blend.
      • Grains of selim — hwentia — in a fritter is unusual and it is the signature of this version. Smoky and faintly bitter against the sweet plantain.
      • Note the ambiguity: the source prints '10 individual cloves', which could mean spice cloves or garlic cloves. Ten spice cloves is a lot but plausible with the selim; ten garlic cloves would dominate. Neither reading is asserted.
      • Her serving note: usually paired with bambara nuts. The starchy, slightly bitter bean against the sweet fritter is the point.
      • No fry temperature or time is published in either documented version of this fritter. Medium heat is what the other source advises, to avoid a dark exterior with a raw interior.
      • The method here is reconstructed from the other documented version. Nothing in the step order is this author's.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Using merely ripe rather than overripe plantain.
      • Oil too hot, giving a dark outside and raw middle.
      • Treating the reconstructed step order as the author's own.
      • Adding flour, which belongs to the other version.
      • Leaving out the selim, which is what makes this version distinct.

      Health Benefits

      • Overripe plantain is high in potassium, vitamin A and vitamin B6.
      • Flourless and therefore naturally gluten-free.
      • Ginger and selim both carry documented antimicrobial aromatic compounds.
      • Vegan as written.

      Cultural Note

      Fried plantain balls are sold at roadsides all over Ghana and the bambara nut pairing is the detail that makes them a meal rather than a sweet — a handful of the hard, earthy beans alongside the soft sweet fritter. Spicing them with selim and cloves is an Ewe habit and it turns a simple snack into something far more aromatic.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Same day. They go soft and greasy.

      Reheating: A hot oven briefly. Never the microwave.

      Serve with: Bambara nuts, Shito, Rice Water
