🦀 Ekpang Nkukwo with Water Yam

Ekpang nkukwo

Four pounds of cocoyam and water yam together, wrapped a tablespoon at a time, with one and a half to two cups of palm oil — forty-five minutes on medium heat.

This is the richer, larger version. Four pounds of cocoyam and water yam go in together — the water yam is this recipe's own addition and it makes the paste stickier and easier to wrap — and the wraps are a tablespoon each rather than a teaspoon. The palm oil is dramatically more generous at a cup and a half to two cups, which gives a deeply red, oily pot rather than a lightly coloured one. Three cups of shelled periwinkle and a tablespoon of ground country onions are the other markers. The whole thing takes forty-five minutes on medium heat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grater or blender, Large pot with a lid, Cocoyam leaves, Ladle

  • 4 lbs cocoyam and water yam
  • 4-5 bunches cocoyam leafy greens
  • 3 cups shelled periwinkle (optional)
  • 1/2 lb boiled beef
  • 1-2 cups dried fish
  • 1/2 cup ground crayfish
  • 1/2 medium onion, chopped
  • 1 1/2 - 2 cups palm oil or red oil
  • Seasoning cubes
  • 1 tbsp ground country onions
  • 1 tbsp white pepper
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper
  • 1 tbsp bitter leaf
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Shred the cocoyam and water yam, or pulse them with a blender to give that grated feel. Add a little salt and leave until ready to use.
  2. Tear the cocoyam leaves into usable pieces.
  3. Put one tablespoon of the grated mixture onto a torn leaf and wrap it up firmly, so the cocoyam is not coming out at all.
  4. Layer the wraps into the pot.
  5. Make a stock with the boiled beef, dried fish, crayfish, onion, seasoning, country onions, white pepper and scotch bonnet, and cook it 10 minutes.
  6. Pour the stock over the wraps and add the periwinkle.
  7. Cook 45 minutes on medium heat, shaking rather than stirring.
  8. Add the palm oil partway through, lifting gently so it distributes.
  9. Finish with the bitter leaf and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Wrap firmly, so the cocoyam is not coming out at all. A loose wrap disintegrates and thickens the pot instead of staying a parcel.
  • Water yam alongside the cocoyam makes the paste stickier and markedly easier to wrap. That is this version's own addition.
  • A tablespoon per wrap here, against a teaspoon in the other documented version. Larger parcels, longer cooking — forty-five minutes rather than about twenty before the first shake.
  • One and a half to two cups of palm oil is an enormous amount relative to the other version's two cooking spoons. It gives a very different, much richer dish. Start lower if you are unsure.
  • Country onions — not onions at all, but a West African spice seed. A tablespoon, ground.
  • Tear the leaves rather than cutting them. Torn edges wrap more willingly.
  • Shake, never stir, exactly as in the other version. That instruction is consistent across both sources.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Wrapping loosely so the parcels burst.
  • Using only cocoyam, which makes a drier paste that is harder to wrap.
  • Stirring the pot.
  • Adding all the palm oil at the start.
  • Cutting the leaves with a knife.

Health Benefits

  • Cocoyam and water yam are both high in potassium and resistant starch.
  • Three cups of periwinkle makes this exceptionally high in iron.
  • Red palm oil supplies carotenoids and vitamin E.
  • A complete one-pot meal.

Cultural Note

The two documented ekpang nkukwo recipes differ so sharply on palm oil — two cooking spoons against two cups — that they are effectively two dishes under one name. One is a light cocoyam parcel stew; the other is a deep red, oil-rich pot. Both come from named sources writing about the same Efik and Ibibio tradition, and the gap between them is a fair measure of how much range a Nigerian dish name can cover.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of water, no stirring.

Serve with: On its own, as a one-pot, Cold palm wine, Boiled plantain
