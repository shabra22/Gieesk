🫛 Iimbotyi

Iimbotyi

Speckled sugar beans soaked overnight and boiled soft in stock, then finished in a pan with softened onion and garlic — plain, patient, and one of the seven colours on a Sunday plate.

Sometimes the beans are the dish. Iimbotyi is sugar beans done properly: an overnight soak, an hour and a half at a rolling boil, and then a brief second act in a pan with onion and garlic that lifts them out of being merely boiled. They turn up beside pap, beside rice, and as one of the components on a seven colours plate, where their job is to be the brown among the beetroot and the pumpkin.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium heavy saucepan, Frying pan, Colander

  • 360 g (2 cups) speckled sugar beans, soaked overnight
  • 950 ml (4 cups) chicken or vegetable stock
  • Salt, to taste
  • 2 tbsp canola oil
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • 240 ml (1 cup) water, to top up if needed

Method

  1. Drain the soaked beans and put them in a saucepan with the stock and a little salt.
  2. Bring to a rolling boil and cook for 1 1/2 hours, stirring occasionally, until the beans are soft all the way through.
  3. Top up with the cup of water if the liquid gets low — beans that cook dry will catch and split.
  4. Drain the beans and set them aside.
  5. Heat the oil in a frying pan and fry the onion gently until softened, then add the garlic and fry for 3 minutes more.
  6. Add the beans, stir them through the onion and garlic, check the seasoning and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Do not salt the soaking water, and go lightly on the salt in the boil. Heavily salted water toughens bean skins.
  • A rolling boil rather than a simmer is what the recipe calls for, and it works — but it drinks liquid, so keep the kettle nearby.
  • Use vegetable stock to keep it plant-based. The version with chicken stock is the commoner one but the beans do not need it.
  • They are better made a day ahead. The skins relax and the beans take up more of the onion.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the overnight soak, which adds an hour to the cooking and still gives unevenly done beans.
  • Letting the pot boil dry, so the beans split and the bottom layer scorches.
  • Salting hard at the start, which leaves the skins tough.
  • Frying the onion until brown. It should be soft and sweet, not caramelised — there is nothing else here to balance a bitter edge.

Health Benefits

  • Sugar beans are high in plant protein, fibre, folate and iron.
  • Low glycaemic index, so they release energy slowly.
  • Naturally low in fat before the oil goes in.

Cultural Note

Beans are the protein that does not need refrigeration, which is why every South African culinary strand has a version of them — iimbotyi in isiXhosa, ubhonsisi in isiZulu, dinawa in Setswana, and boontjiebredie in Afrikaans. The bag of dried beans in the cupboard is the most democratic ingredient in the country.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days. Freezes for 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a pan with a splash of water.

Serve with: Uphuthu, Umngqusho, A seven colours plate
