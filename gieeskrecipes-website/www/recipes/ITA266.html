🧀 Burrata con Pomodorini

Burrata con Pomodorini

Puglia's famous cream-filled mozzarella, its delicate shell revealing a luxuriously soft, stringy interior when cut, served simply with sweet cherry tomatoes and good olive oil.

Burrata was invented in Puglia in the early 20th century as a clever way to use up mozzarella scraps and cream, and its dramatic reveal when sliced open - a burst of soft, creamy stracciatella inside a firmer shell - has since made it one of Italy's most sought-after fresh cheeses.

Ingredients

Equipment: Serving platter

  • 2 balls fresh burrata, about 125g/4.4oz each
  • 300 g (10.5 oz) mixed cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, plus more for drizzling
  • A few fresh basil leaves
  • Coarse sea salt and black pepper
  • Crusty bread, for serving

Method

  1. Bring the burrata to room temperature before serving for the best flavor and texture.
  2. Toss the halved cherry tomatoes with a little olive oil, salt, and pepper.
  3. Place the burrata balls on a serving platter and scatter the dressed tomatoes around them.
  4. Cut into the burrata just before serving to reveal the creamy interior.
  5. Drizzle generously with good olive oil and finish with torn basil, a little more salt, and black pepper.
  6. Serve immediately with crusty bread.

Chef Tips

  • Bringing burrata to room temperature before serving is essential - its flavor and texture are much duller straight from the refrigerator.
  • Cut into the cheese only right before serving, so the dramatic creamy reveal is at its best.
  • The freshest burrata you can find makes all the difference in this simple, ingredient-driven dish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Serving the burrata cold, which mutes its delicate flavor considerably.
  • Cutting into it too far ahead of serving, losing the visual and textural impact.
  • Using an overly acidic or heavy dressing that overwhelms the cheese delicate character.

Cultural Note

Burrata originated in Puglia in the early 20th century as a resourceful use of mozzarella scraps and cream, and has since become one of the region's most internationally celebrated culinary exports.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten fresh; burrata does not keep well once cut, ideally within a day of purchase.

Reheating: Not applicable; always served cold or at room temperature, never heated.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A chilled white wine
