🫘 Dan Wake

Dan wake — danwake, bean dumplings

Bean flour, cassava flour and baobab powder bound with beef stock into a sticky dough, moulded by fingertip and boiled fifteen to twenty minutes.

Dan wake means son of beans and it is the north's dumpling. Two small party cups of bean flour, one of cassava flour, two teaspoons of baking soda or potash and two tablespoons of kuka powder are mixed dry, and then beef stock is added bit by bit until the mixture becomes a soft sticky dough. Rough shapes are moulded with the fingertips and dropped into boiling water, stirred at the ten-minute mark, and done in fifteen to twenty minutes. The interesting thing is the kuka — here it is a binder rather than a soup thickener.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Large pot with a lid, Sieve, if using potash, Slotted spoon

  • 2 small party cups bean flour
  • 1 small party cup cassava flour
  • 2 tsp baking soda, or 2 tsp edible potash
  • 2 tbsp kuka powder (baobab)
  • Beef stock — see the note on the missing quantity
  • Water, for boiling

Method

  1. Mix all the dry ingredients in a bowl: bean flour, cassava flour, baking soda and kuka powder.
  2. If you are using edible potash instead of baking soda, mix it with a small quantity of water, sieve it, and add only the liquid.
  3. Start adding the beef stock bit by bit, mixing as you go, till you get a soft sticky dough.
  4. Set the dough aside.
  5. Boil water in a pot with adequate space for the dumplings.
  6. Mould the dough using your fingertips into rough shapes and drop them into the boiling water.
  7. Cover the pot and continue cooking on medium heat.
  8. After 10 minutes, stir and continue cooking. It should be done in 15 to 20 minutes on low to medium heat.

Chef Tips

  • Add the stock bit by bit. Soft and sticky is the target and you cannot take liquid back out.
  • Sieve the potash and use only the liquid, leaving the sediment. Same rule as every other alkali step in this collection.
  • Mould with the fingertips and do not aim for neat. Rough shapes are correct and they hold together better than rolled balls.
  • Do not stir for the first ten minutes. The dumplings need to set before anything touches them.
  • Plenty of space in the pot. Crowded dumplings stick together into one mass.
  • Kuka powder here is a binder, not a thickener. Same ingredient as miyan kuka, completely different structural role.
  • The beef stock is the hydrating liquid and it carries no quantity on the source page. Expect to need somewhere around a cup to a cup and a half for this quantity of flour, added gradually.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the stock at once.
  • Including the potash sediment.
  • Stirring before ten minutes.
  • Crowding the pot.
  • Rolling smooth balls, which cook unevenly.

Health Benefits

  • Bean flour is high in plant protein, folate and soluble fibre.

Cultural Note

Dan wake is cheap, filling northern street food, sold from a basin with palm oil, pepper and kuli kuli crumbled over the top. Son of beans is a fair name for it — it is what bean flour becomes when you want something to chew rather than something to swallow, and the baobab powder in the dough is a distinctly northern touch that no southern dumpling has.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Brief boil or steam.

Serve with: Yaji, Palm oil and pepper, Kuli-Kuli
