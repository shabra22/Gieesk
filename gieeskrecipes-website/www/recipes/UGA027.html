🐠 Mputa Stew

Mputa — Nile perch

Deep-fried Nile perch simmered in a thick groundnut sauce with eggplant, carrot and green pepper — the stew Ugandans make when the fish is good enough to deserve it.

Nile perch is mputa, and it runs from a palm's length to enormous. It is fleshy and aromatic enough that Ugandans have a stew built specifically around it: thick groundnut paste, the fish already deep-fried so it holds together, and vegetables that go in almost raw and wilt in the sauce. The groundnut paste must be loosened with water and mashed to a thick but runny paste before it goes in, which is the same rule that governs every groundnut sauce in the country.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Bowl and wooden spoon, for the groundnut paste

  • 5 pieces deep-fried Nile perch
  • 60 ml (4 spoonfuls) groundnut paste
  • 2 large eggplants, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 4 medium tomatoes, diced
  • 2 medium green peppers, diced
  • 2 medium onions, diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • A handful of green onions, to garnish
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) oil
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Chilli oil (optional)
  • 480 to 720 ml (2 to 3 cups) water

Method

  1. Put a saucepan over high heat, add the oil and the garlic, and let the garlic brown a little.
  2. Add the onions, tomatoes and salt, then add the fish pieces.
  3. Add the green pepper, the eggplants, the carrots and the chilli oil if using.
  4. Stir until the vegetables wilt a little.

The groundnut sauce

  1. In a bowl, put the groundnut paste with a little water and mash it with a wooden spoon until you have a thick but runny paste.
  2. Pour the paste into the saucepan with the fish and stir well.
  3. Add 2 to 3 cups of water and bring the stew to the boil.
  4. Once it has boiled, add the black pepper, stir, and let it simmer 20 to 30 minutes.
  5. Take it off the heat, garnish with chopped green onions and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Fry the fish before it goes into the stew. Raw perch falls apart in a groundnut sauce and you lose the pieces.
  • Mash the groundnut paste with water first, to thick-but-runny. Spooned in neat it sits in lumps and never disperses.
  • Half a teaspoon of oil is all the recipe uses — the groundnuts carry the fat.
  • The vegetables only wilt at that stage. They finish in the simmer.
  • Twenty to thirty minutes of simmering is what marries the groundnut and the fish. Rushed, it tastes like two things in a pan.
  • Tilapia works if perch is not available, and it is what many Ugandan households actually use.
  • This is the quantified version of a dish the author showed on television and then never published on her blog — it only ever appeared in her ebook.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using raw fish.
  • Adding groundnut paste undiluted.
  • Cooking the vegetables down before the sauce goes in.
  • Cutting the simmer short.
  • Adding all the water at once before the paste is in.

Health Benefits

  • Nile perch is high in protein, omega-3 and vitamin D.
  • Groundnuts add plant protein, niacin, folate and vitamin E.
  • Eggplant, carrot, tomato and pepper make it a complete plate on its own.
  • Contains peanuts and fish.

Cultural Note

Nile perch was introduced into Lake Victoria in the mid twentieth century and remade the lake's ecology and its economy at the same time — it is both a major export and a genuine environmental story. The stew is what happens domestically with a fish that is mostly filleted for Europe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of water. Groundnut sauces thicken as they sit.

Serve with: Matooke, Posho, Rice
