Boerewors

Beef and pork in equal parts with a fifth again of pork fat, coarsely ground with crushed coriander, clove, nutmeg, vinegar and Worcestershire, stuffed into one continuous spiral and rested a full day before it meets the coals.

Boerewors is defined in South African law, which is unusual for a sausage: at least ninety per cent meat and fat, no more than thirty per cent of the meat content as fat, no offal but the casing, no mechanically separated meat, and traditionally formed into a continuous spiral. The spice is coriander first and everything else a long way behind it. Two things separate a good home wors from a mediocre one, and neither is the recipe: chilling the equipment as well as the meat, and giving it a full twenty-four hours in the fridge before braaiing so the vinegar and spice actually go somewhere.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mincer, Sausage filler with a nozzle, Large bowls, Fridge space for the meat and the equipment

    The meat, 7.75 kg total

    • 3 kg beef topside, cubed
    • 3 kg pork loin, cubed
    • 1.75 kg pork fat, cubed

    The seasoning

    • 65 g coriander seeds, crushed
    • 65 ml salt
    • 10 ml ground black pepper
    • 5 ml ground cloves
    • 5 ml ground nutmeg
    • 65 ml Worcestershire sauce

    Added after grinding

    • 90 ml spirit vinegar

    The casing

    • 9 m hog or sheep casing

    Method

    1. Toast and crush the coriander seeds.
    2. Combine the cubed beef, pork and pork fat and season with the salt, coriander, pepper, cloves, nutmeg and Worcestershire sauce.
    3. Chill the meat mixture, the grinder parts and the bowls together for about 1 1/2 hours.
    4. Grind the chilled mixture, then sprinkle over the spirit vinegar and mix it lightly through.
    5. Refrigerate for about 30 minutes.
    6. Soak the casings in warm water, then slide one onto the filler nozzle leaving a 15 cm tail.
    7. Stuff the casing, controlling how fast it slips off the nozzle with your hand, and coil it into one continuous spiral.
    8. Tie both ends in a double knot.
    9. Refrigerate for 24 hours before cooking.
    10. Braai on medium-warm coals — see the wors-braai method for the timings.

    Chef Tips

    • Chill the mincer and the bowls, not just the meat. Warm equipment smears the fat and you get a greasy, dense wors instead of a coarse one.
    • The 24-hour rest is the step most home recipes skip and it is the one that makes the difference. The vinegar and spice need the time.
    • Coriander is the dominant spice and everything else is a supporting note. Any recipe that buries it is not boerewors.
    • Thirty per cent fat is the legal ceiling and this sits just under twenty-three, which is about right. Much leaner and it dries out on the coals.
    • Sheep casings are the traditional and narrower choice; hog casings give a fatter sausage. No published source gives a millimetre spec, so buy what your butcher calls boerewors casing.
    • Add the vinegar after grinding, not before. It firms the protein and makes the mince harder to work.
    • Mix lightly at every stage. Boerewors should be coarse and open, not emulsified.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Working with warm meat and warm equipment.
    • Braaiing it the same day.
    • Over-mixing into a smooth paste.
    • Making it too lean.
    • Stuffing the casing so tight it splits on the fire.
    • Cutting the coil into lengths before braaiing, which lets the juices out.

    Health Benefits

    • High in protein, iron, zinc and B12.
    • No carbohydrate and no fillers — the law forbids mechanically separated meat and limits non-meat content to ten per cent.
    • High in fat and salt by design. It is braai food.

    Cultural Note

    Boerewors is one of very few foods with its own legal definition in South Africa, which happened because the name was worth protecting. The spiral is not decorative either — a continuous coil is easier to turn on a grid and loses less juice than cut lengths, which is why the shape survived.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Raw, refrigerated, 2 days after the 24-hour rest. Freezes for 3 months.

    Reheating: Braaied wors is best eaten straight off the fire. Sliced cold into a salad is the honest second use.

    Serve with: Braaibroodjie, Pap and Chakalaka, A roll and tomato relish
