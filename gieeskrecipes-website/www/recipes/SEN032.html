🍋 Kaldou, Red Snapper with Onions and Lemon

Caldou — kaldou

Two whole red snappers poached in a lemony onion broth, with the lemon added in two stages — a bare-bones southern Senegalese classic.

50 minTotal time
15 minPrep
35 minCook
3Servings
490Calories
EasyDifficulty

The cleanest-sourced recipe in the Senegal set. Two 1½-pound red snappers are rubbed with a spoonful of lemon juice and salt. A quarter cup of peanut oil and two sliced yellow onions go into a wide pot over medium-high, and cook about 16 minutes until soft and golden. A pricked habanero and four cups of water come to the boil, the fish go in and simmer covered about ten minutes until the flesh is nearly opaque, and the rest of the half cup of lemon juice goes in for five minutes more, until the fish is fully cooked. Serve hot over rice.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium bowl, Pot wide enough to hold the fish

  • Two 1 1/2-lb red snappers, cleaned, heads on
  • 1/2 cup fresh lemon juice, divided
  • 2 tsp kosher salt, plus more
  • 1/4 cup peanut oil
  • 2 large yellow onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 habanero chile, pricked with a fork
  • White rice, for serving — quantity not printed

Method

  1. In a medium bowl, rub both fish inside and out with 1 tablespoon of the lemon juice, then season with the salt.
  2. To a pot wide enough to hold the fish, add the oil and onions and turn the heat to medium-high.
  3. Cook, stirring frequently, until soft and golden brown — about 16 minutes.
  4. Add the chile and 4 cups of water and bring to a boil.
  5. Add the fish, turn the heat to medium-low, cover, and simmer until the flesh is nearly opaque — about 10 minutes.
  6. Add the remaining lemon juice and continue cooking until the fish is fully cooked — about 5 minutes more.
  7. Season to taste with salt and serve hot over rice.

Chef Tips

  • The staged fish cue is the best in the set: ‘nearly opaque’, then the lemon goes in, then ‘fully cooked’.
  • Brown the onions properly — soft and golden brown, 16 minutes — before any liquid goes in.
  • Prick the habanero, don’t chop it; remove it earlier for a milder broth since it keeps releasing heat.
  • Some cooks add carrot, peeled tomato or small okra; that is the page’s own aside, not part of the recipe.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the lemon at the start — the acid goes in late.
  • Crowding the fish in a small pot.
  • Stopping the onions before they are golden.

Cultural Note

Harris printed this recipe in her essay ‘Senegal, 1972’ (Saveur, Fall/Winter 2024), and the headnote places it in the southern Casamance. It sits close to yassa — onions, citrus, chile — but the fish poaches in the broth rather than finishing in a caramelised onion sauce.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 1 day refrigerated, fish only once.

Reheating: Gently, just to hot.

Serve with: Steamed white rice
