🍚 Party Jollof Rice

Jollof rice — party jollof

The deliberate-burn version: long grain parboiled twelve to fifteen minutes, foil under the lid twice, and three to five minutes on high at the end to make the smoke.

This is party jollof written down as a technique rather than an accident. The rice is parboiled in stock until it is still hard, a bit white and only partly cooked, the pepper base is fried down until the raw tomato taste has gone, and the pot is covered with a double layer of foil or parchment under the lid — twice, once for each stage. Then comes the part that makes it party jollof: with the rice cooked, the heat goes up with the lid on and the rice is burnt for three to five minutes until it crackles and smells toasted. Rest thirty minutes minimum.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a tight lid, Foil, baking or parchment paper, Blender, Tea cloth

  • 4 cups uncooked long-grain rice — not basmati
  • 6 cups stock (vegetable, chicken or beef) or water, divided
  • 6 medium fresh plum or Roma tomatoes, chopped, or a 400 g tin
  • 6 fresh red poblano peppers, or 4 large red bell peppers, seeds discarded
  • 3 medium red onions — 1 sliced thinly, 2 roughly chopped, divided
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper, yellow preferred, to taste
  • 1/3 cup oil — vegetable, canola or coconut, not olive
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tsp Caribbean or Jamaican-style curry powder
  • 1 heaping tsp dried thyme
  • 2 dried bay leaves
  • 2 tsp unsalted butter (optional), divided
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Rinse the rice, then parboil it with 2 cups of stock, covered, 12 to 15 minutes on medium heat. It should still be hard, a bit white rather than translucent, and only partly cooked.
  2. Blend the tomatoes, poblano or bell peppers, chopped onions and scotch bonnet until smooth — about 4 cups in total.
  3. Heat the oil, add the sliced onion with a pinch of salt and stir 1 to 2 minutes.
  4. Add the tomato paste, curry powder, thyme and bay leaves and stir 2 minutes.
  5. Add the blended mixture and cook 10 to 12 minutes on medium heat, until the raw tomato taste disappears.
  6. Add 2 cups of stock and 1 teaspoon of butter, then the parboiled rice.
  7. Stir, and cover with a double piece of foil, baking or parchment paper, then the lid. Cook on low 15 minutes.
  8. Stir, adjust the seasoning, and add the remaining 1 cup of stock.
  9. Re-cover with foil or parchment and the lid, and cook 15 to 20 minutes more, stirring every 10 minutes, until the grains separate. Add stock or water by the half-cup if needed.
  10. For party jollof: with the rice cooked, turn the heat up with the lid on and burn it 3 to 5 minutes — the rice will crackle and smell toasted.
  11. Turn off the heat, cover the top with a tea cloth, and rest a minimum of 30 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • Long grain, not basmati — the source rules it out explicitly. Note that the same author's other jollof prefers basmati, so this is a real disagreement between two recipes by one person.
  • Parboil to still hard and a bit white. If it looks cooked at this stage the finished jollof will be mush.
  • A double piece of foil or parchment under the lid, and twice — once for each cooking stage. It traps the steam far better than a lid alone.
  • The burn is deliberate. Heat up, lid on, three to five minutes, listening for the crackle. Longer under the lid means smokier rice.
  • Thirty minutes' rest, minimum, with a tea cloth over the top. The cloth absorbs the condensation that would otherwise drip back and wet the rice.
  • Caribbean or Jamaican-style curry powder is specified, and it is not the same product as Nigerian curry powder. Neither source treats them as substitutable.
  • Stock rather than water throughout, added in three stages — two cups, two cups, one cup, then half-cups as needed. That staging is what keeps the grains separate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Parboiling the rice until it is actually cooked.
  • Using a lid without the foil or parchment layer.
  • Adding all six cups of stock at once.
  • Skipping the thirty-minute rest.
  • Burning it with the lid off, which dries it instead of smoking it.

Health Benefits

  • Red bell pepper and tomato together give a high vitamin C and lycopene load.
  • Vegetarian as written.
  • Lower in fat than most party rice dishes at a third of a cup of oil for eight servings.
  • Gluten-free.

Cultural Note

Party jollof is a genuinely different thing from household jollof, and the smoke is the difference — the burnt layer at the bottom of a huge pot cooked over firewood is what people queue for at a Nigerian party. That this recipe turns the accident into an instruction is its real contribution: heat up, lid on, three to five minutes, listen for the crackle.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Steam, or microwave with a splash of water and a damp cover.

Serve with: Moi Moi, Dodo (fried plantain), Coleslaw and grilled chicken
