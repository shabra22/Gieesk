🍖 Yassa Yapp

Yassa yapp — yassa à l'agneau

A leg of lamb done as a méchoui in the pot — the stuffing divided in three, two hours on low heat, done when the leg is tender and nearly all the water has evaporated.

2h 40mTotal time
30mPrep
2h 10mCook
6Servings
620Calories
MediumDifficulty

Her lamb yassa, and her own framing is that it is a méchoui done in a pot rather than over a fire. The stuffing is pounded garlic, pepper, salt, half a bell pepper and bird's eye chillies, divided in three. The leg is stuffed through incisions, one portion of the stuffing spread on it with two tablespoons of mustard and the juice of a lemon. Half a diced onion and the other half-pepper brown in oil, the lamb goes in with a full glass of water, and it simmers covered on low heat for two hours. The sauce takes the last third of the stuffing with the juice of a large lemon and, optionally, some of the braising liquid, finished with mustard near the end.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mortar and pestle, Heavy pot with a lid — marmite, Pan, for the sauce, Sharp knife, for the incisions

    Quantities printed only inside the method

    • Leg of lamb — gigot, quantity not printed
    • 2 tbsp mustard
    • 1 well-filled glass of water
    • 1/2 an onion, diced
    • 1/2 a bell pepper
    • The juice of 1 lemon, for the lamb
    • The juice of 1 large lemon, for the sauce
    • 1 bay leaf
    • A little water, a little oil

    Named without quantity

    • Cloves of garlic
    • Pepper
    • Salt
    • Bird's eye chillies
    • Onions, for the sauce

    Method

    1. Pound the garlic, pepper, salt, half the bell pepper and the bird's eye chillies into a stuffing, and divide it in three.
    2. Stuff the leg of lamb through incisions.
    3. Spread one portion of the stuffing over the lamb with 2 tablespoons of mustard and the juice of one lemon.
    4. Brown half the diced onion and the other half of the pepper in oil, until the onion is well browned.
    5. Add the lamb and a well-filled glass of water, cover, and leave it to simmer on low heat for 2 hours.
    6. Watch the cooking closely. It is done when the leg is tender and nearly all the water has evaporated.

    The sauce

    1. Soften the onions in hot oil with a bay leaf and a chilli.
    2. Once the onions turn translucent, add the remaining stuffing, salt, pepper and the juice of a large lemon.
    3. Add a little water, and some of the lamb braising juices if you like.
    4. Simmer a few minutes and add the mustard near the end. The sauce must have a good golden colour.

    Chef Tips

    • Tender, and nearly all the water evaporated — two conditions, and both have to be true. That is the clearest braising endpoint in the Senegalese material.
    • Divide the stuffing in three. One third into the incisions, one third spread on the outside with the mustard and lemon, one third into the sauce.
    • A well-filled glass of water for a two-hour braise is very little liquid, and that is the point — it is a méchoui in a pot, not a stew.
    • Watch it closely is her own instruction, and with that little water it is not optional.
    • Mustard near the end of the sauce, not at the start. Same rule as her other yassas.
    • Your sauce must have a good golden colour — the second cue, for the sauce rather than the meat.
    • Her own note: lamb yassa is as good as chicken yassa and nobody thinks of it, especially for people who like meat. She is right, and the two-hour braise is why.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding more water, which turns it into a stew.
    • Stuffing the lamb without also spreading stuffing on the outside.
    • Leaving it unattended through the two hours.
    • Mustard into the sauce early.
    • Pulling it when tender but still wet.

    Health Benefits

    • Lamb is high in protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
    • A long low braise with very little liquid retains the meat's own juices.
    • Lemon juice at this quantity supplies vitamin C.
    • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

    Cultural Note

    Méchoui is the whole roast lamb of North and West African feasts, and a cook proposing to do one in a pot on a Thursday is exactly how a festival dish becomes a family one. The two-hour braise in barely a glass of water is the closest a kitchen gets to a fire.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

    Reheating: Gentle covered heat with a splash of water.

    Serve with: White rice, Kaani, Attaya
