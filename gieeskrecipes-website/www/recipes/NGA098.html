Kunun gyada — groundnut and rice gruel

A hundred and fifty grams of raw groundnut to fifty of rice and six hundred millilitres of water — a cooked gruel, not a fermented drink.

Kunun gyada is a light porridge rather than a drink in the kunu family's usual sense, and the important thing to know is that there is no fermentation in it at all — only soaking. Rice soaks at least eight hours, raw groundnuts three, tamarind in warm water until soft. The groundnut milk goes into a pot on medium heat and is stirred constantly to prevent sticking and lumps; when it boils the rice blend is added slowly, and when it boils again the tamarind juice goes in. It is ready once it heats through, and the ratio is unusually clean at three parts groundnut to one of rice.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender, Medium pot, Wooden spoon, Sieve

  • 150 g raw groundnuts (peanuts)
  • 50 g soft rice variety
  • 600 ml cool water
  • Tamarind (tsamiya), to taste

Method

  1. Soak the rice at least 8 hours.
  2. Soak the raw groundnuts 3 hours.
  3. Soak the tamarind in warm water until soft, then strain out the juice.
  4. Blend the soaked groundnuts with some of the water to a milk, and blend the rice separately.
  5. Pour the groundnut milk into a pot on medium heat, stirring constantly to prevent sticking and lumps.
  6. When it comes to the boil, slowly add the rice blend while stirring.
  7. When it boils again, add the tamarind juice.
  8. Keep stirring until it is heated through. Once it heats up again, it is ready to be served.

Chef Tips

  • There is no fermentation here. Only soaking — which makes kunun gyada quite different from kunun zaki despite the shared name.
  • Stir constantly from the moment the groundnut milk goes in the pot. Groundnut catches and lumps faster than almost anything.
  • Add the rice blend slowly, into liquid that is already boiling. Dumped in, it seizes.
  • Three parts groundnut to one part rice, by weight — 150 g to 50 g — in 600 ml of water. A clean, reproducible ratio, which is rare in this part of the record.
  • Eight hours on the rice and only three on the groundnuts. They do not need the same soak.
  • Tamarind to taste at the very end. It is the sour note that makes it kunun gyada rather than a groundnut porridge.
  • Kunu is a category rather than a recipe — kunun gyada is groundnut, kunun aya is tiger nut, kunun zaki is millet or sorghum. They are genuinely different drinks.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expecting it to ferment.
  • Stirring intermittently and getting lumps.
  • Adding the rice blend to liquid that is not yet boiling.
  • Soaking the groundnuts as long as the rice.
  • Adding the tamarind early, where its acidity can curdle the groundnut milk.

Health Benefits

  • Groundnut supplies plant protein, niacin and healthy fats.
  • Tamarind is high in vitamin C and organic acids.
  • No dairy and no gluten.
  • Energy-dense, which is why versions of it are used as weaning food.

Cultural Note

Kunun gyada sits between a drink and a meal, and the United Nations University studied versions of it precisely because it is used as a weaning food — groundnut and cereal together give a protein profile neither does alone. The tamarind is not decoration: the acidity is what makes a very rich gruel drinkable.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. It thickens considerably.

Reheating: Low heat with a little water, stirring.

Serve with: Kosai or akara, Masa, On its own, warm
