🌾 Matured Sorghum Porridge

Muwemba — mugusha

Half a kilo of sorghum flour stirred into two litres of boiling water until the colour turns black and a foam rises, then diluted, covered and left to stand twelve hours before drinking.

Sorghum is a northern Ugandan staple, and this porridge is matured rather than just cooked: after the boil it is diluted, covered and left to sit for twelve hours, which changes it. The doneness cue for the cooking stage is precise and unusual — you stir until the colour changes to black and a foamy liquid rises to the top. Then it rests, and it is that resting that gives the drink its character.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan, Bowl, Medium pot with a lid, Wooden spoon

  • 2 litres water
  • 500 g sorghum flour
  • Cold water, for mixing and for diluting

Method

  1. Bring the 2 litres of water to the boil.
  2. Mix the sorghum flour into cold water until it is lump-free.
  3. Add that to the hot water, stirring, until the colour changes to black and a foamy liquid rises to the top.

The maturing

  1. Pour it into a medium pot and dilute with cold water.
  2. Cover it and let it sit for 12 hours before serving.

Chef Tips

  • The colour turning black and the foam rising are the two cues together. That is when the cooking stage is finished.
  • Twelve hours covered is the recipe, not a suggestion. It is what makes this matured sorghum porridge rather than sorghum porridge.
  • Mix the flour into cold water until genuinely lump-free before it goes anywhere near the heat.
  • Dilute after cooking and before the rest, not at the end.
  • It sits at room temperature, covered. That is what the source describes.
  • Sorghum grains are smaller than maize but have a similarly starchy endosperm, and the grain features in many staple dishes in northern Uganda.
  • Half a kilo of flour to two litres makes a substantial batch, which suits something that has to sit overnight anyway.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cutting the twelve-hour rest.
  • Adding dry flour to the boiling water.
  • Stopping before the colour change and the foam.
  • Diluting it after the rest rather than before.
  • Leaving it uncovered while it sits.

Health Benefits

  • Sorghum is high in fibre, iron, magnesium and B vitamins, and is naturally gluten-free.
  • The long rest begins a light fermentation, which makes the minerals more available.
  • No fat, no dairy and no added sugar.
  • A drought-tolerant grain, which is why it anchors diets in the drier north.

Cultural Note

Sorghum does the work in northern and north-eastern Uganda that banana does in the centre — it grows where little else will, and it turns up as stiff porridge, as this drink, and as the base of the region's beers. The twelve-hour rest is the same instinct that produces obushera and enturire: time doing work that heat cannot.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days after its rest.

Reheating: Drink it as it is, warm or cool. Do not re-boil.

Serve with: Roasted Simsim, Simsim Sesame Snaps, Malakwang
