🦃 Tamarind and Honey-Glazed Roast Turkey

Dinde glacée au tamarin

A 12-lb turkey roasted 1 hour at 450°, 1 hour at 350° under foil, then 30 minutes more basted every 10 minutes with a tamarind, honey and fish-sauce glaze until the thigh reads 160°; the pan juices thickened into a gravy.

The glaze: 6 oz of coarsely chopped tamarind pulp is covered with 1/2 cup of boiling water and left 10 minutes, broken up with the fingers, left 5 minutes more, pressed through a fine sieve and the solids discarded; honey, fish sauce, garlic and chile are stirred in until smooth; you should have 1 1/2 cups. The turkey is set on a rack, seasoned inside and out, stock poured in the pan, and roasted at 450° about 1 hour until golden brown; the oven is lowered to 350°, the bird covered with foil and roasted about 1 more hour until almost cooked through; the foil comes off and it roasts about 30 more minutes, basted with the glaze every 10 minutes, until an instant-read thermometer in the thigh reads 160°. It rests 20 minutes under foil. The pan juices are boiled with the cornstarch slurry about 5 minutes until thickened and strained. Use fresh ‘wet’ tamarind pulp; the page says concentrate or syrup is too sweet.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large roasting pan with rack, Fine sieve, Instant-read thermometer, Foil

  • 6 oz tamarind pulp, coarsely chopped (fresh ‘wet’ pulp sold in blocks with seeds mixed in)
  • 1/2 cup boiling water
  • 3/4 cup honey
  • 1/4 cup Vietnamese or Thai fish sauce
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 Scotch bonnet or habanero chile, stemmed, seeded and finely chopped
  • One 12-lb turkey, rinsed and dried thoroughly
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper — quantities not printed
  • 2 cups chicken stock
  • 2 tablespoons cornstarch
  • 2 tablespoons cold water

Method

    Glaze

    1. Cover the tamarind with 1/2 cup boiling water and let soften 10 minutes. Break it apart with your fingers and let sit 5 more minutes. Press through a fine sieve over a bowl and discard the solids. Stir in the honey, fish sauce, garlic and chile until smooth. You should have 1 1/2 cups.

    Roast

    1. Heat the oven to 450°. Set the turkey on a rack in a large roasting pan and season the cavity and outside generously. Pour the stock into the pan and roast about 1 hour, until golden brown.
    2. Lower the oven to 350°, cover with foil and roast about 1 more hour, until almost cooked through.
    3. Remove the foil and roast about 30 more minutes, basting with the tamarind glaze every 10 minutes (the page prints the basting quantity as ‘12 cup’), until an instant-read thermometer in the thigh reads 160°.

    Rest and gravy

    1. Move the turkey on its rack to a cutting board, tent with foil and rest 20 minutes. Pour the pan juices into a small saucepan. Stir the cornstarch into the 2 tablespoons of cold water and stir the slurry into the juices. Boil over medium-high heat, stirring, about 5 minutes, until thickened. Strain through a fine sieve and serve with the turkey.

    Chef Tips

    • Printed cues: ‘golden brown’, ‘almost cooked through’, thigh at 160°, gravy ‘thickened’.
    • The glaze makes 1 1/2 cups; the page says to baste with ‘12 cup’ — decide the quantity yourself, since it is not determinable from the page.
    • Use wet tamarind pulp, not concentrate.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using tamarind concentrate or syrup, which the page says is too sweet.
    • Basting before the foil comes off.
    • Skipping the 20-minute rest.

    Cultural Note

    Tamarind pulp is ‘a prized ingredient in Senegal’, made into candies, mixed into a cooling drink or spooned over grilled fish; the glaze ‘lend[s] an umami depth to this unexpected Thanksgiving roast turkey’. A Pierre Thiam holiday recipe, not a traditional Senegalese dish.
