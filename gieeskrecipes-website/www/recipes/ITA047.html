🍝 Baked Ziti

Ziti al Forno

Baked Ziti layers tender pasta tubes with a rich meat-tomato sauce and plenty of melted mozzarella and ricotta — a comforting, crowd-feeding baked pasta perfect for family gatherings.

Rooted in Southern Italian baked pasta traditions and popularized further by Italian-American home cooks, ziti al forno is prized for being easy to assemble ahead, generously portioned, and universally loved at the table.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Large skillet, Mixing bowl, Large baking dish

  • 500 g (18 oz) ziti or penne
  • 400 g (14 oz) ground beef
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 700 g (25 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 250 g (1 cup) ricotta
  • 1 egg
  • 300 g (3 cups) shredded mozzarella, divided
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, divided
  • Handful of fresh basil, torn
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Olive oil

Method

  1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the ziti until just shy of al dente, since it will finish cooking in the oven. Drain.
  2. Heat a drizzle of olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and cook until soft, about 5 minutes.
  3. Add the garlic, cooking for 1 minute, then add the ground beef, browning thoroughly, about 8 minutes.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes and basil, season with salt and pepper, and simmer for 15 minutes.
  5. Meanwhile, mix the ricotta with the egg, half the Parmigiano, and a pinch of salt in a bowl.
  6. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F).
  7. Toss the drained pasta with about two-thirds of the meat sauce.
  8. Spread half the sauced pasta in a large baking dish. Dollop spoonfuls of the ricotta mixture over the top, then scatter with half the mozzarella.
  9. Add the remaining pasta, top with the rest of the meat sauce, and finish with the remaining mozzarella and Parmigiano.
  10. Bake for 25–30 minutes until bubbling and golden on top.
  11. Rest for 10 minutes before serving.

Chef Tips

  • Slightly undercooking the pasta before baking prevents it from turning mushy in the oven.
  • Layering the ricotta in dollops rather than spreading it evenly creates pockets of creaminess throughout the dish.
  • This dish can be fully assembled a day ahead and baked just before serving.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Fully cooking the pasta before baking, resulting in an overly soft final texture.
  • Skipping the rest time after baking, making it difficult to portion cleanly.
  • Using pre-shredded mozzarella with anti-caking agents, which can affect how well it melts — freshly shredded is best.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of protein and calcium.
  • Best enjoyed as a hearty, occasional main course given its richness.

Cultural Note

Baked ziti is especially popular in Italian-American communities, often served at large family gatherings, potlucks, and holidays for its ability to easily feed a crowd.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days, or freeze baked or unbaked for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat covered in a 180°C (350°F) oven for 20–25 minutes until warmed through.

Serve with: Garlic bread, A simple green salad, A medium-bodied red wine
