🍪 Ethiopian Crispy Bread Rings

ዳቦ ቆሎ — Dabo Kolo

Ethiopian Dabo Kolo — small pieces of spiced dough (sweet with honey and cinnamon, or savoury with berbere) baked until completely crunchy and dry. The coffee ceremony accompaniment — Ethiopia's most ubiquitous snack, eaten throughout the day at every social gathering.

Dabo Kolo is the snack that Ethiopians eat while drinking coffee, while talking, while travelling, and while waiting — its complete dryness makes it shelf-stable for weeks, its crunchiness makes it satisfying, and its two versions (sweet and savoury) ensure it suits every occasion.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: All of Ethiopia — the ancient snack bread of the coffee ceremony

Dabo Kolo (literally 'hard bread') is one of Ethiopia's most ancient snacks — small, crunchy pieces of spiced bread that are baked until completely hard and dry. They have the shelf life of a biscuit without any processing and are the traditional accompaniment to Ethiopian coffee ceremonies, passed around in baskets while coffee is served. They come in sweet versions (with honey and cinnamon), savoury-spiced (with berbere), and plain. Sold in every market, every school, and at every bus stop.

Ingredients

Equipment: Oven at 180°C, Baking sheet

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour

Sweet Version

  • 2 tbsp honey
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ¼ tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp salt
  • ½ cup warm water
  • 2 tbsp oil

Savoury Version (alternative)

  • 1 tsp berbere
  • ½ tsp garlic powder

(same flour, water, oil, salt — substitute spices)

    Method

    1. Combine flour and salt. Add honey (sweet) or berbere (savoury).
    2. Add oil. Add water gradually. Knead 5 minutes until a firm dough.
    3. Roll into long ropes 1cm thick.
    4. Cut into 1cm pieces.
    5. Spread on a baking sheet — spaced apart.
    6. Bake at 180°C for 20—25 minutes until completely golden and hard throughout.
    7. Cool completely before storing — they must be very hard.

    Chef Tips

    • They must be completely hard when cooled — if still soft, bake longer.
    • Roll ropes evenly — uneven thickness produces uneven baking.
    • Make large batches — Dabo Kolo keeps for weeks.
    • The sweet and savoury versions are equally traditional.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Underbaking — soft inside.
    • Too large pieces — hard to bake through.

    The Science

    Dabo Kolo achieves its crunchiness through complete dehydration — the extended baking at 180°C removes virtually all moisture from the dough, producing a material with a water activity below 0.6 (where no spoilage bacteria can grow). This is why it keeps for weeks without preservation.

    Cultural Note

    Dabo Kolo is the food of togetherness — passed around the coffee circle, shared between friends, eaten on long Ethiopian bus journeys. Its hardness and longevity mean it is always available, always ready to be shared. To offer Dabo Kolo is to invite conversation.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Airtight container at room temperature — 3—4 weeks.

    Reheating: Not applicable.

    Serve with: During the Ethiopian coffee ceremony, As a travel snack, Alongside tea
