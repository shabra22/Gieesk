🍽️ Kenyan Traditional Feast Platter

Sahani ya Karamu ya Kienyeji

Kenyan Traditional Feast Platter — the grandest expression of Kenyan hospitality. A communal platter combining nyama choma, pilau, ugali, sukuma wiki, kachumbari, beans, avocado, and pili pili — every essential Kenyan dish on one magnificent table. The feast that marks the greatest occasions.

The traditional feast platter is not a single recipe — it is the culmination of Kenyan culinary knowledge and hospitality. When a Kenyan family hosts a truly grand occasion, the table holds everything: charcoal-grilled nyama choma, fragrant pilau, smooth ugali, wilted sukuma wiki, fresh kachumbari, creamy avocado, thick bean stew, and pili pili on the side. This is the recipe for assembling the complete Kenyan feast. Every component is referenced to its individual recipe entry.

Ingredients

Equipment: Multiple cooking stations, Large communal platters, Sufurias of varying sizes

    Component dishes — each referencing its own recipe

    • Nyama Choma — 1.5 kg goat or beef
    • Kenyan Pilau — enough for 12 servings
    • White Ugali — large pot
    • Sukuma Wiki — large pan
    • Kachumbari — large bowl
    • Beans — large pot
    • Avocado — sliced, 4 ripe avocados
    • Pili Pili Sauce — small bowl
    • Coconut Chutney — small bowl
    • Kenyan Masala Chai — for 12

    Method

    1. Plan and stage the feast cooking 2 days ahead:
    2. Day 1: prepare and refrigerate pilau (sauce only) and beans.
    3. Day 2 morning: complete pilau assembly and rice cooking.
    4. Day 2: prepare sukuma wiki, kachumbari, and pili pili sauce.
    5. Day 2: prepare charcoal fire 1 hour before guests arrive.
    6. Grill nyama choma while guests gather — this is the arrival ritual.
    7. Warm ugali and beans. Final seasoning check on all dishes.
    8. Assemble communal platters: pilau and nyama choma at centre, ugali to one side, sukuma wiki, kachumbari, beans, avocado, and condiments surrounding.
    9. Serve family style — everything in the centre, everyone serves themselves.
    10. Bring chai at the end.

    Chef Tips

    • The nyama choma fire is the heart of the feast — start it first and let guests gather around it.
    • Stageing the feast over 2 days makes it manageable — the beans and pilau sauce both improve overnight.
    • Family-style serving is the Kenyan tradition — all dishes on the table simultaneously, everyone serving themselves.
    • More food than you think you need. Always.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Trying to cook everything on the day — impossible at this scale.
    • Insufficient quantity — a Kenyan feast must have abundance.
    • Plating individually — the feast platter is communal.

    The Science
