🐟 Ofe Nsala with Utazi

Ofe nsala — white soup

The short version — one big catfish, eight small pieces of white yam, five utazi leaves — done when the pounded yam dissolves to a medium consistency.

This is nsala stripped to its essentials and it is described by its own source as the quickest and easiest Igbo soup to prepare. One catfish, eight small pieces of white yam boiled until they soften and go moist then pounded until smooth and stretchy, five utazi leaves, a handful of crayfish, a small piece of ogiri okpei and habanero to taste. The yam goes into the pot in small lumps and the soup is ready when they have dissolved and it has reached a medium consistency. There is no palm oil, which is what keeps it white.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Mortar and pestle, Separate pot for the yam

  • 1 big catfish
  • 8 small pieces white yam
  • 5 utazi leaves
  • 2 small seasoning cubes
  • Small piece of ogiri okpei
  • A handful of crayfish
  • Habanero pepper, to taste
  • Salt if necessary

Method

  1. Clean the catfish, cutting it into steaks, and set aside.
  2. Boil the white yam pieces until the yam softens and becomes moist.
  3. Pound the cooked yam in a mortar till smooth and stretchy.
  4. Cook the catfish with the seasoning cubes, crayfish, ogiri okpei and habanero pepper.
  5. Add the pounded yam into the pot of soup in small lumps.
  6. Cook until the yam dissolves and the soup achieves a medium consistency.
  7. Add the utazi leaves, taste, and add salt if necessary.
  8. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Small lumps, not one mass. The yam dissolves from the surface inwards and small pieces get there much faster.
  • Softens and becomes moist is the boiling cue for the yam, and smooth and stretchy is the pounding cue. Both are worth watching for.
  • Five utazi leaves is a small number on purpose. Utazi is bitter and is used sparingly — one named reference calls it an excellent garnish used in small quantities.
  • No palm oil. Nothing that would colour the soup goes anywhere near it.
  • Cut the catfish into steaks rather than small chunks. It holds together better and it is how the dish is served.
  • This version has no stockfish and no smoked fish, so it is genuinely fast — about an hour from a standing start.
  • No alternative thickener is mentioned on this page at all. If you have no yam, the fuller version offers achi, yam flour or cocoyam.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the pounded yam as a single ball.
  • Using more utazi than the five leaves, which turns it harshly bitter.
  • Adding palm oil.
  • Under-pounding the yam so it stays lumpy.
  • Boiling hard once the catfish is in.

Health Benefits

  • No palm oil, so much lower in fat than most Nigerian soups.
  • Catfish is a lean source of protein and omega-3 fats.
  • Utazi is traditionally used for digestion and blood sugar.
  • Low in carbohydrate.

Cultural Note

That the simplest Igbo soup is also one of the most prized says something about how the repertoire works: nsala has no palm oil, no egusi, no ogbono and no long list of leaves, and it is still a soup you would serve a guest. The catfish carries it, and the three dried spices do the rest.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with a little water.

Serve with: Iyan, Eba or garri, Akpu or fufu
