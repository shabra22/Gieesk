🫘 Kenyan Bhajia

Bhajia

Kenyan Bhajia — wafer-thin potato slices in a spiced gram flour batter, fried until irresistibly crispy. One of Kenya's most popular street snacks. See KEN018 for the full recipe with complete heritage and cultural detail.

Bhajia is the Kenyan street snack that stops you in your tracks — the smell of spiced gram flour batter frying, the sight of golden crispy slices piled up at the stall. The secret is the thin slice and the right batter thickness. Found everywhere from Mombasa Old Town to Nairobi food courts.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Sharp knife or mandoline, Deep frying pan, Slotted spoon

  • 4 large potatoes — peeled and very thinly sliced (2—3mm)
  • 2 cups gram flour (besan)
  • ¼ cup rice flour
  • 2 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tsp ginger — grated
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — finely chopped
  • 1 green chilli — finely chopped (optional)
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • 1½ cups water
  • Oil for deep frying

Method

  1. Slice potatoes 2—3mm thin. Pat completely dry.
  2. Combine all batter ingredients except water. Mix well.
  3. Gradually whisk in water to a medium-thick batter. Rest 5 minutes.
  4. Heat oil to 175°C.
  5. Dip potato slices in batter, let excess drip, and fry in batches for 3—4 minutes until golden.
  6. Drain on a rack. Serve with tamarind chutney or pili pili.

Chef Tips

  • Thin slices are essential — 2—3mm maximum.
  • Rice flour in the batter is the secret to crispiness.
  • Always fry in small batches.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Thick slices — do not cook through.
  • Wet potato — batter does not adhere.
