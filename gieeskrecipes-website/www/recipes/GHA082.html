🍗 Grilled Guinea Fowl

Akonfem — smoky grilled guinea fowl

A kilo and a bit of guinea fowl with five tablespoons each of suya spice and melted shea butter, finished with dried mint.

Guinea fowl is the northern Ghanaian bird and this is the grilled version, rubbed with five tablespoons of suya spice mix and five of melted unrefined shea butter. Shea butter rather than oil is the northern choice and it is what makes this taste regional rather than generic. Dried mint leaves, two sliced onions, a teaspoon of chilli flakes, a teaspoon of vinegar and salt complete it. The method is video-only, so no grilling times or temperatures are published and the steps here are reconstructed and labelled as such.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grill or barbecue, Basting brush, Mixing bowl, Tongs

  • 1.2 kg guinea fowl
  • 5 tbsp suya spice mix (yaji) — see Tankora Powder
  • 5 tbsp melted unrefined shea butter, or coconut or groundnut oil
  • Dried mint leaves
  • 2 large onions, peeled and sliced
  • 1 tsp chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp vinegar
  • Salt, to taste

Method

    The published method for this recipe is video-only. No written steps exist in the source.

    1. Joint or spatchcock the guinea fowl.
    2. Combine the suya spice, melted shea butter, chilli flakes, vinegar and salt.
    3. Rub the mixture all over the bird and under the skin.
    4. Marinate, covered, at least two hours.
    5. Grill over moderate heat, turning and basting, until cooked through and well browned.
    6. Scatter the sliced onions over the bird toward the end so they soften and char.
    7. Rest briefly, then finish with the dried mint leaves.

    The order and timings above are inferred from the documented grilled dishes in this collection, not from this source.

      Chef Tips

      • Shea butter rather than oil is the northern signature here. It is nutty and slightly bitter and it behaves differently on a grill — it browns fast, so watch it.
      • Five tablespoons of suya spice to a 1.2 kg bird is a heavy crust and it is correct. Suya is meant to coat.
      • Dried mint at the end, not during. It is a finishing herb and heat destroys it.
      • Guinea fowl is tougher and leaner than chicken, so it needs a real marinade and moderate rather than fierce heat.
      • Be aware the method here is reconstructed. The author publishes a full ingredient list and no written steps or temperatures.
      • A teaspoon of vinegar in the rub is unusual and useful — a little acid helps tenderise a tough bird.
      • If you cannot get guinea fowl, use a free-range chicken and accept it will be milder. Do not reduce the spice to compensate.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Treating the reconstructed step order as the author's own.
      • High heat, which burns the shea butter and the spice crust.
      • Adding the mint before grilling.
      • A short marinade on a tough bird.
      • Reducing the suya spice.

      Health Benefits

      • Guinea fowl is leaner than chicken and higher in protein per gram.
      • Shea butter supplies vitamins A and E and stearic acid.
      • The suya spice contributes peanut protein, ginger and capsaicin.
      • Very low in carbohydrate.

      Cultural Note

      Guinea fowl is the bird of northern Ghana — kept semi-wild, harder to catch and far better eating than chicken — and grilling it over coals with a suya crust and shea butter is how it is best served. The shea is the detail that places the dish: a southern cook would reach for palm oil without thinking.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: 3 days refrigerated cooked.

      Reheating: A hot oven briefly, or back on the grill.

      Serve with: Shito, Jollof Rice, Kelewele
