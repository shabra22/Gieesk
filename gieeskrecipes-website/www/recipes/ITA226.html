Pasta e Patate

A comforting Neapolitan pasta-and-potato soup, thick and starchy, simmered together with pancetta and finished with plenty of Parmigiano and a crack of black pepper.

This humble dish sits somewhere between a soup and a pasta, the starches from both the pasta and potatoes combining into a naturally thick, almost creamy broth without any cream at all — a genuine Neapolitan comfort food classic.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 300 g (10.5 oz) mixed short pasta (ditalini or broken spaghetti)
  • 4 medium potatoes, peeled and diced
  • 100 g (3.5 oz) pancetta, diced
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 carrot, chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, chopped
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 L (4¼ cups) water or vegetable stock
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for serving
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the olive oil in a large pot and cook the pancetta until it renders some fat and turns lightly golden.
  2. Add the onion, carrot, and celery, and sauté until softened.
  3. Stir in the tomato paste and cook briefly.
  4. Add the potatoes and enough water or stock to cover generously, and simmer for 15 minutes until the potatoes begin to soften.
  5. Add the pasta directly to the pot and cook until al dente, stirring occasionally to prevent sticking.
  6. The dish should be thick and starchy — mash a few potato pieces against the side of the pot if a thicker consistency is desired.
  7. Season to taste and serve hot with a generous grating of Parmigiano and black pepper.

Chef Tips

  • Cooking the pasta directly in the potato broth is what gives this dish its naturally thick, starchy consistency.
  • Mashing a portion of the potatoes against the pot is a simple way to thicken the dish further without any cream.
  • This dish continues thickening as it sits, so add a little extra water if reheating.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using too much liquid, resulting in a thin soup rather than the traditionally thick, almost stew-like consistency.
  • Not stirring the pasta enough as it cooks in the starchy broth, risking it sticking to the pot.
  • Skipping the Parmigiano and pepper finish, which is essential to the dish's final flavor.

Cultural Note

Pasta e patate is a beloved Neapolitan comfort dish, exemplifying the city's talent for turning humble, inexpensive ingredients into something deeply satisfying.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; thickens further as it sits.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding water to loosen as needed.

Serve with: Crusty bread
