🌽 Tanzanian Corn and Bean Stew

Makande

Makande — Tanzanian whole corn and bean stew. Dried maize kernels and beans slow-cooked together with onions, tomatoes, and spices until completely tender and creamy. The traditional dish of the Chagga people of Kilimanjaro — nutritious, filling, and deeply satisfying.

Makande is one of Tanzania's most ancient and complete foods — the combination of maize and beans creates all essential amino acids in one pot. Slow-cooked until both are completely tender and creamy, makande is the food of Kilimanjaro's slopes and the sustenance of farmers who work the fertile mountain land.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Northern Tanzania — Kilimanjaro and Arusha regions, Chagga community tradition

Makande is a traditional stew of whole dried maize kernels and beans, slow-cooked together until both are completely tender. It is the traditional dish of the Chagga people of Kilimanjaro and is widely eaten across northern Tanzania. The combination of maize and beans creates a nutritionally complete protein — the ancient food wisdom of combining grains and legumes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot or pressure cooker

  • 1 cup dried whole maize kernels — soaked overnight
  • 1 cup dried kidney or pigeon beans — soaked overnight
  • 2 medium onions — diced
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 2 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander

Method

  1. Drain soaked maize and beans.
  2. Cook together in fresh water for 60—90 minutes until both are completely tender (or 30 minutes in pressure cooker).
  3. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden.
  4. Add garlic, tomatoes, curry powder, salt, and pepper. Cook 8 minutes.
  5. Add cooked maize and beans with some cooking liquid.
  6. Simmer together 15—20 minutes until thick and well combined.
  7. Garnish with coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Overnight soaking is essential — it reduces cooking time and improves digestibility.
  • Both maize and beans must be completely tender — al dente is not acceptable here.
  • The cooking liquid from the maize and beans is flavourful — add it to the stew.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not soaking overnight.
  • Under-cooking — chewy maize and beans.

The Science

Soaking reduces phytic acid (an anti-nutrient) by up to 50% and allows initial water absorption, dramatically reducing cooking time. The combination of maize (high in methionine) and beans (high in lysine) creates a complete protein profile that neither provides alone.

Cultural Note

Makande is the food of Kilimanjaro — the ancient sustenance of the Chagga people who have farmed the fertile slopes of Africa's highest mountain for centuries. It represents Tanzania's deep agricultural wisdom.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 4 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently on stovetop.

Serve with: Ugali, Sukuma Wiki, Pili Pili Sauce
