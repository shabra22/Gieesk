🍠 Ethiopian Roasted Sweet Potato

ስኳር ድንች ቆሎ — Suqar Dinich Qolo

Ethiopian Ember-Roasted Sweet Potato — whole sweet potatoes placed directly in glowing charcoal embers and roasted until the skin chars and the inside becomes sweet, caramelised, and fluffy. The most affordable street snack in Ethiopia — sold by vendors whose braziers glow at every street corner.

Ember-roasted sweet potato is the sweet snack that no Ethiopian street corner is without — the blackened whole sweet potatoes piled in large baskets beside charcoal braziers, sold by vendors who peel them to order. The natural sweetness is intensified by the charcoal heat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal brazier or open fire with glowing embers

  • 4 medium orange-fleshed sweet potatoes — scrubbed but unpeeled

Pinch of salt for serving

    Method

    1. Place whole sweet potatoes directly into the glowing charcoal embers.
    2. Cover with more embers.
    3. Roast 40—45 minutes, turning every 10—12 minutes.
    4. The skin should be completely charred and black.
    5. Test with a skewer — no resistance means done.
    6. Remove. Allow to cool slightly.
    7. Peel off the charred skin.
    8. The inside should be fluffy, sweet, and orange.
    9. Serve with a pinch of salt.

    Chef Tips

    • The skin must be completely charred — this is not burning, this is the cooking method.
    • The interior should be almost molten-sweet — this is the goal.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Not leaving long enough — undercooked sweet potato is starchy.

    The Science

    Ember roasting creates a thermal gradient — the exterior chars at 400°C+ while the interior slowly rises to about 100°C. At 100°C, the sweet potato's amylase enzymes (still active below 80°C during the slow heat-up) convert starch to maltose and glucose — a process called 'enzyme-driven gelatinisation' that produces the characteristic extreme sweetness of properly roasted sweet potato.

    Cultural Note

    Ember-roasted sweet potato is the most democratic street food in Ethiopia — at one birr per potato, it is affordable for everyone. The vendor's brazier on a cold highland evening, the stack of blackened sweet potatoes, and the warmth of holding one while peeling it — this is Ethiopian street food at its most elemental.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Eat immediately.

    Reheating: Oven briefly.

    Serve with: As a standalone street snack, With a pinch of mitmita for heat
