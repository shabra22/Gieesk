🐟 Catfish Stew, Nupe Style

Catfish stew

Catfish arranged in a single layer, seasoning sprinkled over rather than stirred in, covered with warm water and cooked twenty minutes to a thick smooth stew.

This is the one Nupe-attributed recipe in the collection, written by an author who identifies herself as Nupe and explains why catfish is central to the cooking of Niger, Kogi and Kwara states — rivers and swampy ground. The method respects the fish: it is cut into five or six pieces, slimed with hot water, patted dry, and then arranged in a single layer over fried onions with the stock cube and dried pepper sprinkled over the top rather than mixed in. Warm water to cover, twenty minutes, and the cue is a thicker smooth stew.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Sharp knife, Kettle

  • 1 catfish
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp dried chilli powder
  • 1 bouillon cube
  • 4 tbsp palm oil
  • Warm water, to cover
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Chop the catfish into five to six pieces.
  2. Pour hot water over the pieces to remove the slime, then rinse in cold water and pat dry.
  3. Heat the oil in a saucepan and fry the onions for one minute.
  4. Arrange the fish in a single layer over the onions.
  5. Sprinkle the bouillon cube and the dried pepper over the fish.
  6. Pour in warm water to cover and cook on medium heat approximately 20 minutes, until you notice a thicker smooth stew.
  7. Add salt to taste and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Hot water over the fish first for the slime, then cold to rinse, then pat dry. Catfish slime will make the whole stew cloudy and slightly muddy.
  • A single layer, and leave it alone. Catfish falls apart the moment you start moving it.
  • Sprinkle the seasoning over the top rather than stirring it in. It dissolves down through the liquid without you having to disturb the fish.
  • Warm water, not cold. Cold water into a hot pan with fish in it will seize the flesh.
  • A thicker smooth stew is the cue, at about twenty minutes. The fish releases gelatine and the liquid tightens on its own.
  • Only one minute on the onions. This is a quick dish and the onions are there for aroma, not for a browned base.
  • Four tablespoons of palm oil for one fish is generous and correct — it is what makes the stew read as a Nigerian stew rather than a poach.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the slime removal.
  • Stacking the fish or stirring it.
  • Adding cold water.
  • Mixing the seasoning in and breaking up the fish.
  • Cooking the onions until browned.

Cultural Note

The Nupe live along the Niger in Niger, Kogi and Kwara states, and their cooking reflects it — catfish is an everyday ingredient rather than a special one. That this is the only documented Nupe recipe reachable anywhere, written by an author claiming the tradition as her own, says more about the state of the written record than about the cuisine.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat, no stirring.

Serve with: Boiled white rice, Tuwo Shinkafa, Boiled yam
