🐠 Poisson braisé (Hartmann, Ma cuisine d’Afrique)

Poisson braisé

Bream or carp marinated 2 hours in a lemon, mustard and oil vinaigrette, then cooked 20 minutes a side with a sautéed tomato, celery, garlic and parsley mixture, until the flesh comes away easily.

The page byline is Amina Savoura; the recipe is credited to Isabelle Hartmann’s Ma cuisine d’Afrique (Éditions de la Réunion des Musées Nationaux, 2007), and described as coming from Cameroon. A vinaigrette of salt, pepper, lemon juice and the crumbled stock cube is made; the fish marinates 2 hours, turning from time to time. Half the parsley, the pepper, onion, garlic, tomatoes and celery are chopped and sautéed at medium heat about 15 minutes. The fish is salted, peppered and oiled; the marinade is added off the heat to the crushed aromatics; each side cooks about 20 minutes, basted with the back of a fork. The cue: check with the point of a knife — the flesh should come away easily. Sprinkle with the rest of the parsley.

Ingredients

Equipment: Grill, barbecue or oven, Pan, Mortar

  • 4 medium bream (daurades) or 1 carp
  • 200 g fresh tomatoes
  • 1 stalk celery
  • 1 onion
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 20 g ginger
  • 1 lemon
  • 1 bunch chopped parsley
  • 1 mild green pepper (printed ‘piment doux vert’; the method says ‘poivron’)
  • 1 stock cube
  • 3 tablespoons peanut oil
  • Salt and pepper

Vinaigrette

  • 6 tablespoons peanut oil
  • 2 tablespoons vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon mustard

Method

  1. Wash the gutted fish.
  2. Make a vinaigrette with salt, pepper, lemon juice and the crumbled stock cube. Marinate the fish 2 hours, turning from time to time.
  3. Chop half the parsley, the pepper, onion, garlic, tomatoes and celery and sauté at medium heat about 15 minutes.
  4. Salt and pepper the fish and oil both sides.
  5. Off the heat, add the marinade to the crushed aromatics.
  6. Cook each side about 20 minutes, basting with the back of a fork. Check with the point of a knife: the flesh should come away easily. Sprinkle with the remaining parsley.

Chef Tips

  • The printed doneness cue is that the flesh ‘doit se détacher facilement’ when tested with a knife.
  • The page does not say which heat source to use (barbecue, oven or pan).
  • The page suggests serving it with miondo.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the 2-hour marinade.
  • Adding the marinade to the aromatics while the pan is still on the heat.
  • Not testing with a knife.
