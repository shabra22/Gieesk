🥘 Kenyan Beef Tripe Stew

Matumbo ya Mchuzi

Matumbo ya Mchuzi is one of Kenya's most beloved traditional stews — beef tripe slowly cooked until tender in a rich tomato, onion, and garlic sauce. Patient, deeply flavourful, and deeply Kenyan.

Matumbo — beef tripe — is a dish that rewards patience. The stomach lining requires long, slow cooking to become tender, but when it does, it absorbs the surrounding sauce beautifully and develops a rich, gelatinous quality unlike any other cut. In Kenya, matumbo stew is found at every nyama choma joint, food stall, and family kitchen.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Pressure cooker (optional), Wooden spoon

  • 800g cleaned beef tripe — cut into 5cm pieces
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 4 garlic cloves — minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1½ cups beef stock
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Wash the tripe thoroughly under running water. Rub with lemon juice and salt, rinse well.
  2. Place tripe in a large pot. Cover with water. Boil for 45—60 minutes until beginning to soften. Drain.
  3. In a fresh pot, heat oil and cook onions until golden — 8 minutes.
  4. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  5. Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 8—10 minutes until thick.
  6. Add all spices. Stir 1 minute.
  7. Add the par-cooked tripe and stock.
  8. Cover and simmer on low heat for 45—60 minutes more until the tripe is completely tender.
  9. Season and serve garnished with fresh coriander.

Chef Tips

  • Cleaning tripe thoroughly before cooking is essential — rinse multiple times and rub with salt and lemon.
  • A pressure cooker cuts the total cooking time to about 45 minutes.
  • The tripe is ready when it has a pleasant, slightly chewy (not rubbery) texture.
  • A splash of white vinegar in the initial boiling water helps clean and deodorise.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not cleaning properly — the smell during cooking will be unpleasant.
  • Not cooking long enough — undercooked tripe is tough and unpleasant.
  • Cooking at too high a heat — the collagen needs slow, gentle heat to dissolve properly.

The Science

Tripe is the stomach lining, composed primarily of smooth muscle and collagen. Long, slow cooking converts the collagen to gelatin, which gives the stew its characteristic rich, silky body and transforms the tripe texture from tough and rubbery to pleasantly tender.

Cultural Note

Matumbo stew is a beloved part of Kenyan food culture — found at nyama choma joints, roadside food stalls, and family kitchens across the country. Its affordability and deep flavour have made it a firm favourite across all communities.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on stovetop with a splash of water.

Serve with: White Ugali, Chapati, Steamed Rice, Kachumbari, Sukuma Wiki
