🥩 Muqmad

Muqmad

Sun-dried, richly spiced strips of meat, historically preserved for long journeys across the Somali countryside, rehydrated and fried in ghee for a deeply savory, slightly chewy dish.

Muqmad reflects Somalia's pastoral heritage, developed as a practical way to preserve meat without refrigeration for herders traveling long distances, and though modern households often skip the lengthy sun-drying in favor of a quick pan-dry, the dish's intense, concentrated flavor remains unchanged.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wire rack, Large skillet

  • 600 g (1.3 lb) lean beef, cut into thin strips
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp xawaash spice blend
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 100 g (7 tbsp) ghee
  • 1 onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced

Method

  1. Toss the beef strips with turmeric, xawaash, and salt, coating evenly.
  2. Arrange the strips on a wire rack and let dry in a warm, low oven (around 70C/160F) for 3-4 hours, or in direct sun if available, until firm and mostly dried but not brittle.
  3. Melt the ghee in a large skillet and saute the onion and garlic until softened.
  4. Add the dried meat strips and fry, stirring frequently, for 10-15 minutes until deeply browned and tender.
  5. Serve hot, with the flavorful ghee spooned over.

Chef Tips

  • The initial drying step, whether in a low oven or the sun, is what gives muqmad its concentrated, intense flavor.
  • Frying the dried meat in a generous amount of ghee is essential to rehydrating it into a tender, richly flavored dish.
  • This dish keeps very well once dried, reflecting its traditional role as a preserved, portable food.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Drying the meat until brittle rather than just firm, making it difficult to properly rehydrate when fried.
  • Skimping on the ghee during frying, leaving the meat tough rather than tender.
  • Rushing the frying step, which does not give the meat enough time to properly rehydrate and soften.

Cultural Note

Muqmad reflects Somalia's nomadic pastoral heritage, historically preserved by sun-drying for herders traveling long distances without access to refrigeration.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Dried, unfried muqmad keeps at room temperature for several weeks; refrigerate the finished fried dish for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a pan with a little extra ghee.

Serve with: Canjeero, Bariis Cad (plain rice)
