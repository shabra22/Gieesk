Strangozzi al Tartufo Nero

A thick, hand-cut eggless pasta from Umbria, traditionally tossed simply with olive oil, garlic, and the region's celebrated black truffle for a dish that lets Umbria's finest ingredient speak for itself.

Named for their resemblance to the frayed strands of a torn shoelace, strangozzi are made without egg, giving them a firmer, more rustic bite than tajarin, and pair beautifully with the black truffles Umbria is famous for.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Rolling pin, Sharp knife, Large pot, Sauté pan

  • 400 g (3 cups) 00 flour
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) water, approximately
  • Pinch of salt
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 40 g (1.4 oz) fresh black truffle, plus more for shaving
  • 1 anchovy fillet, mashed (optional)
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the flour, water, and salt into a firm, smooth dough, kneading for 10 minutes. Wrap and rest for 30 minutes.
  2. Roll the dough out to about 2mm thickness and cut into long, slightly irregular strands roughly 4mm wide.
  3. Toss the cut strangozzi in a little flour and set aside on a floured tray.
  4. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the strangozzi for 3–4 minutes until tender but firm.
  5. Meanwhile, warm the olive oil gently in a wide pan and sauté the garlic and mashed anchovy, if using, until fragrant, taking care not to brown the garlic.
  6. Grate most of the truffle finely into the warm oil off the heat, allowing its aroma to infuse.
  7. Drain the pasta, reserving some cooking water, and toss directly in the truffle-infused oil with a splash of the water.
  8. Plate immediately and finish with a few extra shavings of truffle over the top.

Chef Tips

  • Keep the truffle away from direct high heat, which destroys much of its aroma — infuse it gently off the heat instead.
  • The dough should be firm enough to roll thick and hold a substantial bite, distinct from thinner egg pastas.
  • A small amount of truffle goes a long way when properly infused into warm oil.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking the truffle by adding it too early to a hot pan, which dulls its distinctive aroma.
  • Rolling the pasta too thin, losing the characteristically rustic, chewy bite strangozzi are known for.
  • Overpowering the truffle with too much garlic or other strong flavors.

Health Benefits

  • A simple, largely plant-based dish that can be made fully vegan by omitting the anchovy.
  • Truffles provide antioxidants alongside their prized aroma.

Cultural Note

Strangozzi is closely tied to the town of Spoleto and the surrounding Umbrian countryside, a region renowned for its black truffles, which are hunted through much of the year in the region's oak forests.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Fresh, uncooked strangozzi can be dried for a day or frozen for up to 1 month.

Reheating: Best made and eaten fresh; truffle aroma fades quickly on reheating.

Serve with: Nothing more — the dish is meant to showcase the truffle simply
