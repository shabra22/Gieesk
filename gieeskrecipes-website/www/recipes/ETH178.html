🍠 Ethiopian Sweet Potato Fries

ስኳር ድንች ጥብስ — Suqar Dinich Tibs

Ethiopian Sweet Potato Fries — thick-cut sweet potato sticks deep-fried and seasoned with mitmita and salt. The modern Ethiopian street food — the berbere and mitmita tradition applied to the globally familiar French fry format, producing something completely distinct.

Sweet potato fries with mitmita seasoning are a modern Ethiopian street food addition — the globally familiar format given an unmistakably Ethiopian character by mitmita and berbere salt. Popular at Addis Ababa's modern food stalls.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep pan

  • 4 large orange sweet potatoes — peeled, cut into thick sticks
  • Oil for frying

Mitmita Salt: 1 tsp salt + ½ tsp mitmita (ETH142)

    Method

    1. Dry sweet potato sticks thoroughly.
    2. Heat oil to 175°C.
    3. Fry in batches 4—5 minutes until golden and slightly caramelised.
    4. Drain. Season immediately with mitmita salt.
    5. Serve immediately.

    Chef Tips

    • Sweet potato burns faster than white potato — 175°C maximum, not higher.
    • Season while hot — sticks to the surface.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Too high temperature — sugar burns.
    • Seasoning when cold.

    The Science

    Sweet potatoes contain much more sugar (naturally occurring fructose and glucose) than white potatoes — this is why they caramelise faster and why the oil temperature must be lower. The Maillard reaction and caramelisation work together to produce the characteristic deep orange-brown crust.

    Cultural Note

    Sweet potato fries with mitmita are the new generation's Ethiopian street food — the fusion of global form and Ethiopian spice tradition that defines the modern Addis Ababa food scene.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten immediately.

    Reheating: Oven at 200°C briefly.

    Serve with: With silsi (ETH146), As a standalone street snack
