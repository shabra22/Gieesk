🌾 Gwote Acha

Gwote acha — gote, fonio, hungry rice

Half a cup of fonio into three cups of broth with vegetables — five minutes and it is done, because acha cooks faster than any other grain in Nigeria.

Acha is fonio, the ancient West African grain of the Jos Plateau, and the one thing everyone agrees on is that it cooks fast. This savoury porridge builds a broth with smoked meat, onion, garlic, bay and thyme, adds peppers and cabbage, then stirs in half a cup of fonio for about five minutes and finishes with spring onion and spinach. The author is open that she adapted the Plateau original to what she could buy — smoked turkey for biscuit bones, bouillon for dawadawa and dried prawns — so this is a documented adaptation rather than a traditional gwote.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Wooden spoon, Sieve, for rinsing the acha

  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 medium onion
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 3 small bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp thyme
  • 4 medium pieces smoked turkey
  • 1 tsp ground pepper
  • 1 red pepper
  • 1 green pepper
  • 1/2 small cabbage
  • 1/2 cup fonio (acha)
  • 3 cups water or broth
  • 3 spring onions
  • 1 cup spinach
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Heat the oil and soften the onion and garlic.
  2. Add the bay leaves, thyme and smoked turkey, and build a broth — about 15 minutes.
  3. Add the ground pepper, the red and green peppers and the cabbage, and cook about 10 minutes.
  4. Rinse the acha and stir it in with the broth.
  5. Leave to cook around 5 minutes. If the porridge becomes too thick, add some water to get your preferred consistency.
  6. Stir in the spring onions and the spinach.
  7. Taste, adjust the salt, and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Five minutes. Acha cooks faster than any other grain in Nigerian cooking and it will turn to paste if you treat it like rice.
  • Half a cup of acha to three cups of liquid gives a porridge. That is the only acha ratio published by any named source, and it is a porridge ratio rather than a swallow ratio.
  • Rinse the acha before it goes in. The grain is tiny and carries dust.
  • Add water at the end if it tightens. The author says so directly, and acha keeps absorbing after the heat is off.
  • A peer-reviewed study puts the optimum cooking time at 7.5 minutes at 100°C, judged on moisture uptake and thermal properties. That is slightly longer than this recipe's five and it is the most rigorous figure available.
  • This is a declared adaptation. Smoked turkey replaces biscuit bones and bouillon replaces dawadawa and dried prawns — the author says so — and the bay leaves and thyme are not traditional Plateau seasonings.
  • Acha is fonio, also called hungry rice, and it is one of the oldest cultivated grains in West Africa.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking it like rice, for twenty minutes.
  • Not rinsing it.
  • Letting it tighten without adding water.
  • Expecting a traditional Plateau flavour from a dish seasoned with bay and thyme.
  • Adding the spinach early.

Health Benefits

  • Fonio is gluten-free and unusually rich in the amino acids methionine and cysteine.
  • Very low glycaemic index, which is why it is recommended for diabetic diets.
  • High in fibre, iron and magnesium.
  • Cooks in minutes, which preserves more of its nutrients.

Cultural Note

Acha is one of the oldest cultivated grains in West Africa and the Jos Plateau is its Nigerian home. It is called hungry rice in English, which is unkind to a grain with a better amino acid profile than most cereals — the name comes from its reliability in lean seasons rather than from any deficiency. It is badly under-documented, and anything written down about it is worth having.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with plenty of water — it thickens hard when cold.

Serve with: On its own, as a porridge, Miyan Taushe, Fried fish
