🍚 Pilau

Pilau

Cold cooked rice separated grain by grain with the hands and stir-fried into whole cardamom, cumin and pepper — a pilau made like a fried rice, not by absorption.

This is the pilau you make with yesterday's rice, and it is worth knowing that it is a genuinely different technique from the absorption pilau. Nothing cooks in stock here: the rice is already cooked and cold, the grains are separated by hand, and everything is stir-fried together until the cardamom comes through strongly. The finished rice should have a brownish hue and smell of pilau masala even though no pilau masala goes in — the individual spices do the work.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large frying pan or wok, Wooden spoon

  • 1 tumpeco white rice, cooked and preferably cold
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 10 ml (2 tsp) crushed garlic
  • Ginger, sliced
  • Chilli, if desired
  • Cardamom, whole
  • Cumin
  • Black pepper
  • Cooked peas
  • Boiled potatoes, quartered
  • Meat, preferably boneless, cooked
  • Tomato paste
  • Vegetable oil
  • Fresh cilantro

Used in the method but absent from the printed list

  • Salt

Listed in the source but never used in its method

  • Ground cinnamon

Method

  1. Separate the rice grains by hand. Cold rice works best.
  2. Set the cooked meat aside.
  3. Heat the pan and fry the onions, garlic and ginger until translucent.
  4. Add the chilli, cumin, whole cardamom, black pepper, potatoes, peas and tomato paste, and stir-fry about 5 minutes. Season with salt.
  5. Add the rice to the mixture and stir-fry until hot, with the cardamom coming through strongly.
  6. Add the cilantro, heat through and adjust the seasoning.
  7. The rice should have a brownish hue and a fragrant pilau aroma.

Chef Tips

  • Cold rice, separated by hand. Warm rice clumps and you end up with a paste rather than a pilau.
  • Whole cardamom, not ground. It perfumes the oil and you can pick the pods out, and the recipe's finishing cue is a strong cardamom presence.
  • The brownish hue is the doneness cue, along with the aroma. There is no time given for the final stir-fry.
  • This is a stir-fry pilau, not an absorption one. Nothing here cooks in liquid, which is why it takes twenty minutes rather than an hour. The absorption version is Chicken Pilau.
  • Salt is used in the method and does not appear in the ingredient list.
  • Ground cinnamon is listed and never used. Half a teaspoon with the other spices would fit the spirit of it; the source does not say.
  • The recipe refers to a pilau masala aroma while listing the spices individually rather than using a blend. That is the point — the individual spices should add up to it.
  • A tumpeco is a Ugandan enamel mug and is a real measure in a Ugandan kitchen. One tumpeco of cooked rice; the source gives no conversion.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using warm or freshly cooked rice.
  • Using ground cardamom.
  • Adding raw meat.
  • Adding liquid and turning it into a wet rice.
  • Stir-frying it so long the grains break.

Health Benefits

  • A complete meal in one pan with rice, meat and vegetables.
  • Cardamom, cumin, ginger and garlic all go in whole or fresh.
  • Uses leftover rice, which cuts waste.
  • Cooled and reheated rice carries more resistant starch than freshly cooked.

Cultural Note

Pilau came to East Africa along the Indian Ocean trade routes and is now standard at Ugandan functions, alongside the matooke and the meat. That there are two entirely different Ugandan techniques for it — absorption and stir-fry — is the sort of thing that only shows up when you read several sources rather than one.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Stir-fry again in a hot pan.

Serve with: Kachumbari, Beef Samosas, Fresh fruit juice
