🫀 Ethiopian Beef Tripe Wat

ጨጓ ወጥ — Chegua Wat

Ethiopian Chegua Wat — beef tripe cleaned and slow-cooked in a berbere and kibbeh wat sauce for 2 hours until tender. The tripe wat of Ethiopian celebration cooking — honouring the whole animal in the most complete way.

Tripe wat is the other side of Ethiopian whole-animal cooking — while tendons produce the stickiest sauce, tripe produces the most distinctively textured meat. Properly cleaned and long-cooked, tripe becomes tender, absorbs the berbere sauce, and produces a wat of singular character.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 600g beef tripe — thoroughly cleaned
  • 2 onions
  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • 3 tbsp berbere
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 1 cup water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Clean tripe thoroughly: rub with salt and lemon. Rinse many times.
  2. Blanch tripe 15 minutes. Drain. Cut into pieces.
  3. Dry caramelise onions. Add kibbeh and berbere. Toast.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, and tripe. Add water.
  5. Simmer covered 90—120 minutes until completely tender.

Chef Tips

  • Cleaning tripe thoroughly is the most important step — shortcuts produce an unpleasant flavour.
  • Tripe must be genuinely tender — test with a fork.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Insufficient cleaning.
  • Under-cooking.

The Science

Tripe is the stomach lining — primarily smooth muscle with a very different protein composition from skeletal muscle. It requires longer cooking than muscle meat to denature its proteins and become tender.

Cultural Note

Tripe wat is the dish of those who understand that the honour of an animal lies in using every part of it with skill and care.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days.

Reheating: Fully heated through.
