🌿 Ethiopian Spinach Stew

ስፒናጭ ወጥ — Spinach Wat

Ethiopian Spinach Stew — baby spinach or fresh spinach cooked with onion, garlic, turmeric, and tomato in the Ethiopian style. A modern addition to the Ethiopian vegetable repertoire — faster-cooking than collard greens, ideal for urban kitchens.

Spinach wat is the urban cook's quicker alternative to Gomen — baby spinach needs no blanching and wilts in minutes, while still absorbing the Ethiopian aromatic flavours of garlic, turmeric, and onion.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 400g fresh spinach — washed
  • 1 onion — diced
  • 2 tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Heat oil. Cook onion until golden.
  2. Add garlic and turmeric. Stir 30 seconds.
  3. Add tomatoes. Cook 4 minutes.
  4. Add spinach in batches. Stir until each batch wilts.
  5. Cook 3 more minutes.
  6. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Add spinach in batches — it seems like a huge amount before wilting.
  • Do not over-cook — spinach needs only 5 minutes total.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-cooking — turns grey-green.

The Science

See ETH106 for green vegetable cooking science. Spinach wilts much faster than collard greens because it has no fibrous stem tissue and very high water content — the cells rupture quickly, releasing their water and collapsing.

Health Benefits

  • Very high in iron, vitamins A, C, and K.
  • Folate from spinach.
  • Very low calorie.

Cultural Note

Spinach wat is modern Ethiopia's practical concession to speed — where Gomen requires blanching and longer cooking, spinach delivers green nutrition in 15 minutes for the urban working cook.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 2 days.

Reheating: Briefly.

Serve with: On the fasting platter, As a light side
