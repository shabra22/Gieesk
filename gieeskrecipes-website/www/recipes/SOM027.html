🐟 Taraaqad la Shiilay

Taraaqad la Shiilay

Kingfish steaks rubbed with garlic, ginger, turmeric and lemon, then fried in olive oil for five minutes and three — on the table in under a quarter of an hour, and the everyday fish dish of the Somali coast.

Somalia has one of the longest coastlines in Africa and kingfish is its workhorse: firm, meaty and forgiving, cut into steaks across the bone. This is deliberately not a xawaash dish. The rub is turmeric alone, with garlic, ginger and lemon, because the fish is fresh enough to be the flavour and the spice is there to frame it rather than cover it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Skillet or heavy frying pan, Small bowl, Fish slice

  • 900 g (2 lb) kingfish steaks, about 1 cm thick
  • 2 tsp garlic paste
  • 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 1/4 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 60 ml (1/4 cup) olive oil, for frying

Method

  1. Mix the garlic paste, grated ginger, turmeric, black pepper, salt and lemon juice into a loose paste.
  2. Rub the paste over both sides of each fish steak, getting it into the cut surfaces.
  3. Heat the olive oil in a skillet over medium heat until it shimmers.
  4. Lay the steaks in and fry for 5 minutes without moving them, so a crust forms.
  5. Flip once and fry for 3 minutes more.
  6. Lift onto a warm plate and serve at once, with tomato sauce, fried onions and muufo.

Chef Tips

  • Steaks about a centimetre thick are what the 5-and-3 timing assumes. Thicker cuts need longer and thinner ones will be dry before the crust forms.
  • Everything that goes alongside — the tomato sauce, the fried onions — can be made ahead, which is what makes this a weeknight dish.
  • Turmeric rather than xawaash is the coastal instinct with very fresh fish. Save the spice blend for older fish or for a stew.
  • Kingfish has few small bones compared with most fish, but it still has some, and it is worth telling guests so.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Moving the fish around in the pan during the first 5 minutes, which tears the surface and prevents any crust.
  • Turning the steaks more than once — each flip loses more of the crust than it gains.
  • Frying in a pan that is not properly hot, so the fish stews in the oil and the flesh goes woolly.
  • Overcooking. Kingfish goes from firm to tough within a minute, and there is no recovering it.

Health Benefits

  • Kingfish is a lean source of high-quality protein.
  • Provides omega-3 fatty acids, selenium and vitamin B12.
  • Cooked in olive oil rather than a batter, so it stays a light dish.

Cultural Note

Fish is eaten far more on the Somali coast than the interior, and in the coastal towns it appears at breakfast, lunch and supper without anyone finding that strange. In Mogadishu, kalluun is ordinary weekday food rather than an occasion.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten straight from the pan. Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a dry pan. Avoid a microwave, which turns the flesh rubbery.

Serve with: Muufo, Dallac Bilaash, Bariis Iskukaris
