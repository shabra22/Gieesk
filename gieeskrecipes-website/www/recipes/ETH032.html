🫓 Ethiopian Beef Firfir

ፍርፍር — Firfir (Beef)

Ethiopian Beef Firfir — yesterday's injera torn into pieces and tossed with beef Key Wat sauce, kibbeh, berbere, and onions until every piece is coated in spiced sauce. The brilliant Ethiopian solution to leftover injera — transformed into a completely different and extraordinary dish.

Firfir (meaning 'torn and mixed') is the Ethiopian tradition of using leftover injera — torn and mixed with yesterday's wat sauce, it becomes a completely new dish. Beef firfir uses Key Wat sauce; the injera pieces absorb the sauce and become tangy, spiced, and deeply satisfying. It is eaten for breakfast at Ethiopian restaurants and represents the genius of Ethiopian zero-waste cooking.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 3 pieces day-old injera — torn into small pieces
  • 1 cup Key Wat sauce — warmed
  • 2 tbsp kibbeh
  • 1 small onion — finely diced
  • 1 tsp berbere
  • 1 jalapeño — sliced
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Heat kibbeh in a pan.
  2. Cook onion until golden — 5 minutes.
  3. Add berbere. Stir 30 seconds.
  4. Add warmed Key Wat sauce.
  5. Add jalapeño.
  6. Add torn injera pieces. Toss to coat every piece.
  7. Cook 3—4 minutes, stirring, until injera has absorbed the sauce.
  8. Season. Serve immediately.

Chef Tips

  • Day-old injera is better than fresh — it absorbs sauce more readily.
  • Every piece of injera must be coated — toss constantly.
  • Serve immediately — firfir waits for no one.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Fresh injera — absorbs poorly, too soft.
  • Under-tossing.
  • Waiting to serve.

The Science

Day-old injera has undergone partial starch retrogradation — the gelatinised starch has recrystallised slightly, forming a firmer structure that absorbs liquid more slowly and evenly than fresh injera. This produces firfir pieces that are coated in sauce but retain some structural integrity, rather than dissolving completely as fresh injera would.

Cultural Note

Beef firfir is the dish that Ethiopian restaurants serve for breakfast — the previous night's Key Wat sauce, the previous day's injera, combined in the morning into something that tastes deliberate rather than improvised. Ethiopian zero-waste cooking at its most satisfying.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Ayib (ETH096), With a soft-boiled egg on top for breakfast
