🌱 Tanzanian Cowpea Leaves

Kunde

Tanzanian Kunde — tender young cowpea leaves sautéed with onions, tomatoes, and garlic. The protein-rich green that complements the cowpea bean — Tanzania's whole-plant vegetable where nothing is wasted.

Kunde refers to both the cowpea plant's leaves (the vegetable dish) and its beans. The leaves are harvested young and tender and cooked like mchicha — the taste is slightly more earthy and nutty than amaranth, with a higher protein content.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide pan

  • 400g young cowpea leaves — washed and chopped
  • 2 onions — diced
  • 2 tomatoes — diced
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. Heat oil. Cook onions until golden.
  2. Add garlic and curry powder. Stir 30 seconds.
  3. Add tomatoes. Cook 4 minutes.
  4. Add kunde leaves. Stir to combine.
  5. Cover and cook 5—6 minutes until wilted and tender.
  6. Season. Serve.

Chef Tips

  • Young leaves only — mature leaves are tough.
  • Do not add water.
  • Brief cooking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mature leaves.
  • Overcooking.

The Science

Cowpea leaves contain more protein than most leafy greens — approximately 5g per 100g cooked. The slight bitterness of raw cowpea leaves is reduced by brief cooking, which also increases iron bioavailability.

Health Benefits

  • High protein for a leafy green.

Cultural Note

Kunde leaves represent Tanzania's whole-plant philosophy — in Tanzanian cooking, every part of the cowpea plant is eaten and valued.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Briefly.

Serve with: Ugali (TZA001), Beans, Meat or fish stew
