🍟 Lemongrass Palm Oil Potato Wedges

Irish wedges

Palm oil cooked out first until the yellow foam on top disappears, then lemongrass root browned in it before the potatoes go in — five to seven minutes of patience that changes everything.

The first instruction in this recipe is the one worth the whole dish: put the palm oil in a dry pan on high heat and wait for it to fully cook, five to seven minutes, until the yellow foam on top is gone. Raw palm oil tastes raw, and almost nobody outside West and Central Africa knows to do this. Then the lemongrass root goes in and browns, releasing a sweet flavour, and the potatoes cook in that. Green beans go in near the end.

Ingredients

Equipment: Clean dry saucepan with a lid, Wooden spoon

  • 1 kg Irish potatoes, washed and cut into wedges
  • 480 ml (2 cups) green beans, julienned
  • 2 large onions, thinly sliced lengthwise
  • 3 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) lemongrass root, finely chopped
  • 120 ml (1/2 cup) palm oil
  • Ground black pepper and salt

Method

    Cooking out the palm oil — do not skip this

    1. Put the palm oil in a clean dry saucepan on high heat.
    2. Wait for the oil to fully cook. This takes 5 to 7 minutes, or until the yellow foam on top of the oil is gone.

    The dish

    1. Add the lemongrass root and let it brown a little — it will release a sweet flavour.
    2. Add the garlic and let it brown a little, then add the potatoes.
    3. Stir occasionally. After five minutes, add salt and stir.
    4. After another five minutes, add the onions and black pepper and stir well.
    5. Cover the pan and let the potatoes cook on high heat, stirring occasionally.
    6. Once the potatoes are starting to become tender, add the green beans and stir well.
    7. Let them cook until the potatoes are soft when a fork goes in.
    8. Take the pan off the heat, let it cool a little, and serve.

    Chef Tips

    • Cook the palm oil out first. Five to seven minutes until the yellow foam is gone, in a dry pan, before anything else goes in. Raw palm oil has a flavour that dominates everything and this step removes it.
    • A clean dry pan. Water in the pan will spit violently in hot palm oil.
    • Lemongrass root, not the blades. The root is where the sweet flavour is and it browns rather than infusing.
    • The timings are staged deliberately — potatoes, five minutes, salt, five minutes, onions. Following the order matters more than the clock.
    • Green beans go in near the end so they stay green and firm.
    • High heat with the lid on. It is part frying and part steaming and that is what cooks a wedge through.
    • Half a cup of palm oil for a kilo of potatoes is a lot and it is the dish. This is not a low-fat side.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using the palm oil straight from the tin.
    • Putting the oil into a wet pan.
    • Using lemongrass blades instead of root.
    • Adding the green beans at the start.
    • Cooking uncovered so the wedges never cook through.

    Health Benefits

    • Palm oil is high in vitamin E and carotenoids, and is also most of the calories here.
    • Potatoes provide potassium and vitamin C, and green beans add fibre and vitamin K.
    • Lemongrass, garlic and onion all go in fresh.
    • Entirely plant-based.

    Cultural Note

    Cooking out palm oil before using it is knowledge that travels with the oil rather than with cookbooks, and it is the single most common reason a palm oil dish tastes wrong when made by someone unfamiliar with it. That the instruction survives here in writing is genuinely useful.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Warm in a pan — palm oil dishes reheat well.

    Serve with: Fried Party Irish Potatoes, Classic Bean Stew, Grilled fish
