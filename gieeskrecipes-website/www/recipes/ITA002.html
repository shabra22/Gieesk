🍞 Ciabatta Bread

Ciabatta

Ciabatta — 'slipper' bread — is prized for its irregular, wide-holed crumb and thin, crackling crust, achieved through a very wet dough and a long, slow overnight fermentation.

Invented relatively recently, in 1982, as an Italian answer to the French baguette, ciabatta relies on a biga (a stiff overnight pre-ferment) and an extremely hydrated dough handled with a light touch rather than kneaded hard, which is what produces its famously open, airy structure.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large mixing bowl, Dough scraper, Floured kitchen towel, Baking stone or sheet

    Biga (pre-ferment, made the night before)

    • 200 g (1⅔ cups) bread flour
    • 130 ml (½ cup) cool water
    • ¼ tsp active dry yeast

    Final dough

    • All of the biga
    • 300 g (2½ cups) bread flour
    • 250 ml (1 cup) warm water
    • 1 tsp active dry yeast
    • 10 g (2 tsp) fine sea salt
    • 2 tbsp olive oil

    Method

    1. The night before, mix the biga ingredients into a stiff dough, cover, and leave at room temperature for 12–16 hours until bubbly and domed.
    2. The next day, combine the biga with the final dough ingredients in a large bowl. Mix with a wooden spoon or stand mixer — the dough will be very wet and sticky.
    3. Beat or mix vigorously for 8–10 minutes until the dough becomes glossy and stretches into long strands — this develops structure without traditional kneading.
    4. Cover and let rise for 1 hour, then gently fold the dough over itself a few times without deflating it, and let rise 1 more hour.
    5. Generously flour a work surface and turn the dough out. Gently divide into 2–3 rectangular loaves without pressing out the air.
    6. Transfer to a floured towel or parchment, cover, and proof for 45 minutes.
    7. Preheat the oven with a baking stone (or sheet) to 230°C (450°F).
    8. Carefully transfer the loaves to the hot oven and bake for 20–25 minutes until deep golden and hollow-sounding when tapped.
    9. Cool completely on a rack before slicing.

    Chef Tips

    • The wetter the dough, the more open the crumb — trust the process even though it feels unmanageable at first.
    • Handle the dough as little as possible after the bulk rise to preserve the air bubbles.
    • A pizza stone gives the best crust; if you don't have one, preheat a heavy baking sheet.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Adding too much flour to make the dough easier to handle — this kills the open crumb structure.
    • Skipping the overnight biga, which builds most of the flavor.
    • Deflating the dough while shaping — handle it gently with a light touch.

    Health Benefits

    • Long fermentation improves digestibility compared to quick breads.
    • Naturally free of dairy and added fats beyond a small amount of olive oil.

    Cultural Note

    Despite its rustic, ancient appearance, ciabatta is a relatively modern invention created specifically to compete with imported French baguettes — and quickly became one of Italy's most exported breads.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten the day it's made. Store at room temperature in a paper bag for up to 2 days, or freeze for up to 2 months.

    Reheating: Refresh in a 200°C (400°F) oven for 5 minutes.

    Serve with: Extra virgin olive oil and balsamic, Italian cold cuts, Caprese Salad, Minestrone
