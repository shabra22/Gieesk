🦐 Garden Egg Stew with Dried Shrimp

Garden egg stew

Skinned and de-seeded rather than mashed, with a cup each of palm oil and dried shrimp, and a final thirty to forty minutes on low.

The other way to make garden egg stew: boil the eggs twelve to fifteen minutes, then remove the skin and the seeds and dice the flesh, so you get distinct pieces rather than a paste. A cup of palm oil is heated three to five minutes, the onion fried three, then red pepper powder and nutmeg for three more before a cup of dried shrimp goes in. Blended tomato simmers fifteen minutes, a cup of water twenty, then the fish and garden eggs return for a long final thirty to forty minutes on low heat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, Blender, Heavy frying pan, Knife

  • 9 garden eggs
  • 8 large tomatoes, or 1 tin tomato purée
  • 2 large white onions
  • 1 tbsp red pepper powder
  • 1 habanero pepper
  • 1 tsp nutmeg
  • 1 small ginger root, grated
  • 1/2 lb steamed or dried fish
  • 1 cup dried shrimp
  • 1 cup palm oil
  • 1 cup water
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Boil the garden eggs 12 to 15 minutes.
  2. Remove the skin and seeds and dice the flesh.
  3. Heat the palm oil 3 to 5 minutes.
  4. Fry the diced onion 3 minutes.
  5. Add the red pepper powder and nutmeg and cook 3 minutes.
  6. Add the dried shrimp.
  7. Add the blended tomato mix and simmer 15 minutes.
  8. Add 1 cup of water and simmer 20 minutes.
  9. Add the fish and the diced garden eggs.
  10. Let the stew simmer another 30 to 40 minutes on low heat.

Chef Tips

  • Skin AND de-seed, then dice. That is what makes this stew chunky where the other documented version is a paste — the two are genuinely different dishes.
  • The seeds are where most of garden egg's bitterness sits, so removing them gives a much milder stew. Keep them in if the bitterness is what you want.
  • A cup of dried shrimp is a large amount and it is the backbone of the dish — it supplies all the savoury depth that dawadawa does in the other version.
  • Heat the palm oil three to five minutes before the onion. That drives off the raw edge without bleaching it.
  • Thirty to forty minutes on low at the end, after everything else. It is a long finish and it is where the stew comes together.
  • Nutmeg here but no aniseed, against both in the other version. A teaspoon is a lot of nutmeg and it will be prominent.
  • No doneness cue is printed anywhere on this page — only times. Judge the end by the oil sitting clear on the surface.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mashing rather than dicing, which makes it the other recipe.
  • Leaving the seeds in if you want it mild.
  • Adding the onion to cold palm oil.
  • Cutting the final low simmer short.
  • Reducing the dried shrimp, which leaves the stew flat.

Health Benefits

  • Dried shrimp is very high in protein, calcium and iodine.
  • Garden egg provides fibre, folate and polyphenols.
  • Red palm oil supplies provitamin-A carotenoids.
  • High in lycopene from the long-cooked tomato.

Cultural Note

Whether you mash garden eggs or dice them is the kind of household decision that never gets argued about in print but splits kitchens completely. The diced version keeps the vegetable visible on the plate; the mashed one turns it into the sauce itself. Both are called garden egg stew and nobody in Ghana would be confused by either.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat until the palm oil re-melts.

Serve with: Boiled yam, Ampesi, Rice
