Bariis Maraq

Basmati cooked in a deeply aromatic vegetable and bone stock with cinnamon, cardamom and clove, then streaked with red and orange at the last minute — the rice Somali families make for weddings and Eid.

This is festive rice, and the tell is the liquid: two cups of stock to every cup of rice, twice what an everyday pot would use, so every grain carries the flavour of the broth rather than simply being cooked in water. Traditionally it was made with goat broth over charcoal, and the streaks of colour stood in for saffron, which was far too expensive for a wedding-sized pot. Somali households still make the stock the day before.

Ingredients

Equipment: 6-litre pot, for the stock, Heavy pot with a tight lid, Sieve

    For the stock — best made the day before

    • 1 onion, sliced
    • 7 cloves garlic
    • 2 carrots
    • 1 tomato
    • 1/2 green pepper
    • 50 g (1/2 bunch) fresh parsley
    • 50 g (1/2 bunch) fresh cilantro
    • 450 g (1 lb) chicken, beef or goat bones (optional)
    • 3 L cold water
    • 1 tbsp coriander seeds
    • 1 tbsp cumin seeds
    • 1 tsp black peppercorns
    • 1 tbsp vegetable seasoning salt

    For the rice

    • 840 g (4 cups) basmati rice
    • 120 ml (1/2 cup) canola or vegetable oil
    • 1 onion, chopped
    • 3 cloves garlic, minced
    • 2 cinnamon sticks
    • 1 tsp xawaash spice blend
    • 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
    • 10 whole cloves
    • 1.4 L (6 cups) stock, hot
    • 2 tbsp vegetable seasoning salt

    For the colour

    • 1/4 tsp red food colouring, loosened with 1 tsp water
    • 1/4 tsp orange food colouring, loosened with 1 tsp water

    Method

    1. For the stock, put the onion, garlic, carrots, tomato and green pepper in a 6-litre pot with the parsley, cilantro and bones if using.
    2. Add the cold water, the coriander and cumin seeds, the peppercorns and the seasoning salt. Cover and simmer for 3 hours, then strain and keep hot.
    3. Wash the rice until the water runs clear, then soak it for 30 minutes and drain.
    4. Soften the chopped onion in the oil over medium heat, then add the garlic and stir.
    5. Add the cinnamon sticks, xawaash, ground cardamom and cloves, stirring after each addition.
    6. Pour in the hot stock, add the seasoning salt and stir.
    7. Add the drained rice, stir once and bring to a boil.
    8. Cover and cook on low heat for 10 minutes.
    9. Stir well, cover again and cook for a further 10 minutes.
    10. Dot the two food colourings over the surface in patches — do not stir them through.
    11. Cover and leave on the very lowest heat for 5 minutes, then fork through gently so the colour streaks rather than blends.

    Chef Tips

    • Six cups of stock to four of rice is the everyday ratio for ordinary basmati. Parboiled Sella basmati is the traditional choice and takes more — eight cups — and it will stand a four-hour soak that would turn ordinary basmati to mush.
    • Make the stock the day before. A Somali cook usually lifts it straight from the pot of goat already cooking for the same meal.
    • The colouring is dotted on, not mixed in. Streaks of white, red and orange through one pot of rice is what the dish is supposed to look like.
    • A pinch of saffron steeped in a little hot water does the same job as the food colouring and tastes of something as well.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Soaking ordinary basmati for hours as the traditional recipe says — that timing belongs to parboiled Sella rice and will collapse anything else.
    • Stirring the colouring through the pot, which gives uniformly orange rice instead of the streaked look.
    • Adding cold stock, which stops the pot and leaves the rice unevenly cooked.
    • Lifting the lid during the first ten minutes, letting out the steam that is doing the cooking.

    Health Benefits

    • A vegetable-rich stock carries most of the flavour, so less salt is needed.
    • Basmati has a lower glycaemic index than most white rice.
    • Naturally gluten-free and dairy-free.

    Cultural Note

    Bariis maraq is occasion food. It is cooked in quantity for weddings, Eid and the arrival of guests, traditionally over charcoal in a pot wide enough to feed a courtyard, and served with a fried topping and a whole banana on the side of each plate.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days. Freezes well for a month.

    Reheating: Sprinkle with water, cover and steam over low heat or in a microwave until hot through.

    Serve with: Korkasaarka Bariiska, Hilib Ari Karis, Basbaas
