🍯 Ngalakh, Thiakry Couscous with Neuteri Sauce (peko-peko)

Ngalakh / sauce neuteri

100 g of thiakry couscous softened with boiling water, mixed with butter, salt and raisins, then folded into a chilled sauce of 70 g baobab powder, 100 g peanut paste, sugar, vanilla and nutmeg whisked into 500 ml of water.

Published on peko-peko.fr on 30 June 2020 as ‘Recette de ngalakh’, dessert sénégalais au baobab et beurre de cacahuète. Total time printed 30 minutes; 4 to 6 servings. THE COUSCOUS: 100 g of thiakry (medium-grain millet couscous, or standard couscous), 5 g of butter or oil, a pinch of salt and 15 g of raisins. The page tells you to cook the thiakry as the packet says or, failing that, in a microwave in 1- to 2-minute intervals with boiling water, stirring and adding water as needed; add the butter, salt and raisins while it is still hot; cool completely. THE SAUCE (neuteri): 70 g of baobab powder (or 100 g of whole baobab fruit), 100 g of peanut paste, 50 g sugar or to taste, half a teaspoon of vanilla extract or a sachet of vanilla sugar, an optional teaspoon of Nutella, one eighth of a teaspoon of nutmeg and 500 ml of water. If you use whole grains or a powder full of lumps, boil the baobab in the 500 ml of water and simmer 10 minutes, then strain through a fine sieve; the step is skipped with a ready-to-dilute powder. Mix the peanut paste, sugar, Nutella, vanilla and nutmeg, whisk in the baobab juice little by little, and chill until well cold. Mix couscous into the sauce at serving time.

Ingredients

Equipment: Whisk, Fine sieve (chinois), Microwave (optional)

    Couscous

    • 100 g thiakry (medium millet couscous, from an African grocer) or standard couscous, large grain if possible
    • 5 g butter or oil
    • 1 pinch salt
    • 15 g raisins

    Neuteri sauce

    • 70 g baobab powder, or 100 g baobab fruit in grains
    • 100 g peanut paste, smooth peanut butter or peanut puree
    • 50 g sugar, or to taste
    • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract, or 1 sachet vanilla sugar
    • 1 teaspoon Nutella (optional)
    • 1/8 teaspoon nutmeg
    • 500 ml water

    Method

      Sauce

      1. If you are using whole baobab grains, or a powder still full of lumps, boil the baobab in the 500 ml of water and simmer 10 minutes, stirring, then strain through a fine sieve. With a ready-to-dilute powder, skip this step.
      2. Mix the peanut paste, sugar, Nutella (if using), vanilla and nutmeg. Whisk in the baobab juice little by little. Refrigerate until well chilled.

      Couscous

      1. Rehydrate the raisins in a small bowl of boiling water (no time is printed).
      2. Cook the thiakry as the packet says or, failing that, in the microwave in 1- to 2-minute intervals with boiling water, stirring and adding water as needed. The page’s boiling-water step is read two ways (see the notes): about 130 g of boiling water on the 100 g of thiakry, covered and rested 15 minutes, in one reading; no quantity or rest in the other.
      3. Add the butter, salt and the drained raisins while the couscous is still hot. Cool completely.
      4. At serving time, mix the couscous into the chilled sauce.

      Chef Tips

      • Printed cues (single reading): the couscous should be « bien tendre » and « s’écraser facilement entre les doigts »; the baobab pieces should « se désagréger ».
      • The author distinguishes pâte d’arachide, the African peanut puree (often salted and emulsified), from peanut butter.
      • Keep couscous and sauce separate in the refrigerator and reheat the couscous briefly before mixing with the chilled sauce.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Skipping the straining.
      • Mixing couscous and sauce long before serving.
      • Treating the 130 g / 15-minute reading as settled: the second reading of the page omits both.

      Health Benefits

      • Baobab adds vitamin C; peanut paste adds protein.

      Cultural Note

      The page ties ngalakh to Easter: the Christian minority prepares large quantities on Good Friday for neighbours and guests, including Muslims, making it a symbol of interfaith fraternity; in 2020 the health crisis disrupted this sharing. Baobab fruit is ‘pain de singe’ or ‘bouye’ in Senegal.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: Couscous and sauce are kept separately in the refrigerator; no time is printed.

      Reheating: Reheat the couscous briefly before mixing with the chilled sauce.

      Serve with: Chilled neuteri sauce
