🐟 Catfish Pepper Soup

Point and kill

A whole catfish, two cups of stock, a teaspoon of pepper soup spice and a pinch of utazi — covered and simmered twenty-five minutes, and that is the whole recipe.

This is the simplest serious dish in the collection: everything goes into the pot at once, the lid goes on, and twenty-five minutes later it is done. A whole catfish cut and cleaned, two cups of stock or water, a teaspoon of pepper soup spice, a bouillon cube, a tablespoon of oil, salt, and a pinch of utazi or bitter leaf. No frying, no stages, no stirring. Note that the spice blend's components are named on the page but not proportioned, so the teaspoon is not reproducible from the page alone — for a blend with actual proportions see Nigerian Pepper Soup.

Ingredients

Equipment: Medium pot with a lid, Sharp knife

  • 1 whole catfish, cut and cleaned
  • 1 tsp pepper soup spice mix
  • 1 tbsp oil of choice
  • 1 bouillon cube
  • 2 cups stock or water
  • A pinch of utazi or bitter leaf
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Clean the catfish and cut it into steaks. Pour hot water over the pieces to remove the slime, then rinse in cold water.
  2. Place the cleaned fish in a pot with the stock or water.
  3. Add the pepper soup mix, the salt, the bouillon cube, the oil and the utazi.
  4. Cover and simmer 25 minutes.
  5. Serve hot in the broth.

Chef Tips

  • Hot water over the catfish first to deal with the slime, then cold water to rinse. Skip it and the broth will be cloudy and faintly muddy.
  • Cut into steaks, not chunks. Catfish holds together in rounds and falls apart in cubes.
  • Cover it and leave it. Twenty-five minutes without stirring — this is a dish you can ignore.
  • A pinch of utazi, no more. It is bitter and it is there as a counterweight to the richness of the fish.
  • Two cups of stock to one fish gives you a proper brothy pepper soup. Reduce it if you want something closer to a sauce.
  • The page lists the spice blend's components — allspice seed, anise pepper, cloves, coriander seeds, cumin seeds, dried ginger, fennel seeds, tamarind pulp and ground pepper — but gives no proportions, so the teaspoon is not reproducible from that page. Use the proportioned blend at Nigerian Pepper Soup, or a commercial mix.
  • Point and kill is what this is called when the fish is chosen live from a tank at a roadside bar. It is as fresh as Nigerian cooking gets.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the hot-water slime removal.
  • Cutting the fish into small chunks.
  • Stirring during the simmer.
  • Using more than a pinch of utazi.
  • Expecting to reproduce the spice blend from this page's component list.

Health Benefits

  • Catfish is a lean source of protein and omega-3 fats.
  • Very low in carbohydrate and calories.
  • Pepper soup spices are traditionally used as digestives and decongestants.
  • Utazi is used for digestion and blood sugar.

Cultural Note

Point and kill is the name for this at a roadside bar: you pick your catfish live from a tank and it is in a bowl of pepper soup twenty-five minutes later. Pepper soup generally is Nigeria's restorative — eaten when someone has a cold, after childbirth, late at night with a drink — and the catfish version is the one most associated with the Delta and the rivers.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat. The fish will soften further.

Serve with: Onunu, Boiled yam, Agidi or eko
