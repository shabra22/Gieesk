🍲 Cassoeula Milanese

Cassoeula

A hearty Milanese winter stew of pork cuts and sausage slow-braised with savoy cabbage, traditionally reserved for the coldest days and often considered better the following day.

Cassoeula makes use of the less prized cuts of pork -- rind, ribs, and trotters -- braised for hours with plenty of cabbage until everything becomes rich and gelatinous, a genuinely hearty dish built to fuel Milan's cold winter months.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot with lid

  • 800 g (1.75 lb) mixed pork cuts (ribs, rind, trotters), cut into pieces
  • 4 pork sausages (luganega if available)
  • 1 large savoy cabbage, cored and cut into wedges
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 carrots, chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, chopped
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) dry white wine
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Blanch the pork rind and trotters in boiling water for 10 minutes, then drain.
  2. Warm the olive oil in a large heavy pot and brown the pork cuts and sausages well on all sides.
  3. Add the onion, carrots, and celery, sautéing until softened.
  4. Pour in the wine and let it reduce, scraping up any browned bits.
  5. Add the bay leaves and enough water to almost cover the meat.
  6. Cover and simmer gently for 1.5 hours.
  7. Add the cabbage wedges on top, cover again, and continue simmering for another 1-1.5 hours, until everything is very tender and the cabbage has melded into the sauce.
  8. Season to taste and serve hot, ideally the day after making for the best developed flavor.

Chef Tips

  • This dish genuinely improves with a day's rest, allowing the rich flavors to fully develop.
  • The lengthy braise is what transforms the tougher pork cuts into something meltingly tender.
  • Traditional versions use a good quantity of savoy cabbage, which should become soft and almost silky by the end of cooking.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the cooking time, which leaves the tougher cuts of pork insufficiently tender.
  • Skipping the initial blanch of the rind and trotters, which can leave an unwanted odor.
  • Serving immediately rather than letting it rest, missing the flavor development a day's wait provides.

Cultural Note

Cassoeula is a defining winter dish of Milan and Lombardy, traditionally eaten during the coldest months and closely associated with the pig-slaughtering season.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 4 days; flavor genuinely improves the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, covered.

Serve with: Creamy polenta, A robust Lombardy red wine
