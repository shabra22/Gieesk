Coniglio alla Ligure

Rabbit braised in white wine with olives, pine nuts, and herbs, a classic Ligurian preparation reflecting the region's coastal ingredients even in this inland meat dish.

Even inland, Ligurian cooking carries a distinctly Mediterranean character, and this rabbit dish's combination of olives, pine nuts, and white wine reflects the region's pantry more than a heavier northern Italian braise might.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy-bottomed pot with lid

  • 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) rabbit, cut into pieces
  • Flour, for dredging
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 250 ml (1 cup) dry white wine
  • 80 g (½ cup) black olives, pitted
  • 30 g (¼ cup) pine nuts, toasted
  • 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 80 ml (⅓ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Season the rabbit pieces and dredge lightly in flour, shaking off the excess.
  2. Warm the olive oil in a heavy pot and brown the rabbit pieces well on all sides, then set aside.
  3. In the same pot, sauté the onion until softened, then add the garlic and cook briefly.
  4. Return the rabbit to the pot, pour in the wine, and let it reduce slightly, scraping up any browned bits.
  5. Add the olives, pine nuts, rosemary, and bay leaves, and enough water to come partway up the rabbit.
  6. Cover and simmer gently for 50–60 minutes, until the rabbit is very tender.
  7. Uncover for the final 10 minutes if the sauce needs to reduce and thicken further.
  8. Season to taste and serve hot, with plenty of the pan sauce spooned over.

Chef Tips

  • Browning the rabbit thoroughly before braising builds essential depth of flavor.
  • Toasting the pine nuts beforehand brings out more of their flavor than adding them raw.
  • Low, gentle simmering is key to tenderizing the rabbit without drying it out.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Cooking at too high a heat, which can dry out the relatively lean rabbit meat.
  • Skipping the browning step, missing an important layer of flavor.
  • Over-reducing the sauce too early, before the rabbit has had time to become fully tender.

Health Benefits

  • Rabbit is a lean source of protein, lower in fat than many other meats.

Cultural Note

Coniglio alla Ligure showcases how even Liguria's inland, meat-based dishes carry the region's characteristic Mediterranean pantry of olives, pine nuts, and good olive oil.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; flavor improves the next day.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop, covered.

Serve with: Crusty bread, Roasted potatoes, A dry Ligurian white wine
