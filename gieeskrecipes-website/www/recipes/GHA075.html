🌺 Bito Soup

Bito — bira, biire, hibiscus leaf soup

Hibiscus leaf with groundnut paste and locust bean — the only quantified version in print is brand marketing, and it contradicts itself.

Bito is the sour hibiscus leaf of northern Ghana — bito to the Frafra, bira to the Dagomba — and the soup made from it uses groundnut paste and dawadawa. Only one source publishes quantities and it is a seasoning brand's marketing page, which also contradicts itself: its ingredient list says spinach while its method says shuure leaves, and it describes the dish as containing hibiscus leaves while calling it parsley peanut soup. All of that is flagged below. Four tablespoons of groundnut paste, two of locust beans, a teaspoon of grains of paradise, and about forty minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot, Blender, Wooden spoon

  • Beef, cut for stewing — no quantity is printed
  • 1 bunch bito (hibiscus) leaves — the source prints spinach, see the note
  • 4 tbsp groundnut paste
  • 2 tbsp locust beans (dawadawa)
  • 0.5 cup shallots
  • 5 cloves garlic
  • 1 small piece ginger, chopped
  • 1 tsp grains of paradise
  • 1 tsp thyme
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 4 fresh peppers
  • 2 seasoning tablets

Method

  1. Simmer the beef with the shallots, garlic, ginger, grains of paradise and thyme for 15 minutes.
  2. Add the groundnut paste mixture and cook 5 minutes.
  3. Add the tomato paste.
  4. Allow to simmer for 10 minutes.
  5. Add the leaves, the dawadawa and the seasoning tablet.
  6. Stir and let simmer for 3 minutes.
  7. Add the scotch bonnet pepper.
  8. Serve with tuo zaafi.

Chef Tips

  • Bito is hibiscus leaf — Hibiscus sabdariffa, the same plant as sobolo — and it is sour rather than slimy. That is what distinguishes bito soup from ayoyo.
  • Groundnut paste and dawadawa together are the northern leaf-soup formula, documented by the 1968 government survey as well as by the modern sources.
  • Grains of paradise, a teaspoon, is a genuinely northern aromatic and worth sourcing.
  • Be aware the only quantified version is a brand's marketing page, it substitutes spinach for the hibiscus leaf in its ingredient list, and its method names a third leaf entirely. Use real bito if you can get it.
  • The dawadawa and the leaves go in near the END, for three minutes only. Long boiling flattens both.
  • The beef carries no quantity at all in the source. Six hundred grams for six servings is a reasonable reading, offered as such.
  • An ethnographic study describes bra soup as prepared like light soup with groundnut paste and bra leaves, which is a plainer and probably more typical version than this one.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using spinach and expecting bito's sourness.
  • Boiling the leaves and dawadawa for a long time.
  • Leaving out the groundnut paste, which is structural.
  • Substituting black pepper for grains of paradise.
  • Treating the brand page's quantities as authoritative.

Health Benefits

  • Hibiscus leaf is high in vitamin C, calcium and iron.
  • Groundnut paste supplies plant protein, niacin and vitamin E.
  • Dawadawa is a fermented food with documented B-vitamin content.
  • Grains of paradise carry documented antimicrobial compounds.

Cultural Note

Bito is sour where ayoyo is slippery, and the two are the alternating leaf soups of a northern Ghanaian week. That the only written quantities for one of the region's commonest dishes come from a stock-cube advertisement is the clearest single illustration of how unevenly Ghanaian food has been documented.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Gentle heat.

Serve with: Tuo Zaafi, Tuo Zaafi with Ayoyo, Rice
