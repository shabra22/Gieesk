Malloreddus alla Campidanese

Malloreddus alla Campidanese — Sardinia's saffron-scented gnocchetti — are tossed in a rich sausage and tomato sauce, their ridged shape perfectly designed to hold every bit of it.

These small, shell-shaped semolina dumplings get their golden hue and distinct aroma from saffron, a spice Sardinia has cultivated since Phoenician times, paired here in the island's signature dish with crumbled sausage in a tomato ragù.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Gnocchi board or fork for ridges, Large pot, Large skillet

    Malloreddus dough

    • 400 g (3⅓ cups) semolina flour
    • 200 ml (¾ cup) warm water
    • 1 pinch saffron threads, steeped in 2 tbsp warm water
    • Pinch of salt

    Sauce

    • 400 g (14 oz) Italian sausage, casings removed
    • 1 onion, finely chopped
    • 2 garlic cloves, minced
    • 500 g (18 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
    • 60 ml (¼ cup) dry white wine
    • 100 g (1¼ cups) grated Pecorino Sardo
    • Salt and pepper, to taste
    • Olive oil

    Method

    1. Combine the semolina flour, saffron water, warm water, and salt into a firm dough. Knead for 8 minutes. Rest, covered, for 20 minutes.
    2. Roll small pieces of dough into thin ropes and cut into 1cm pieces. Roll each piece over a gnocchi board or the back of a fork to create ridges, curling slightly into a shell shape.
    3. Heat a drizzle of olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and cook until soft, about 5 minutes.
    4. Add the garlic and sausage, breaking it apart, and cook until browned, about 8 minutes.
    5. Pour in the white wine and simmer until reduced.
    6. Add the crushed tomatoes, season with salt and pepper, and simmer for 20 minutes until thickened.
    7. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the malloreddus for 5–8 minutes until tender and floating.
    8. Drain and toss with the sausage sauce and half the Pecorino.
    9. Serve immediately with remaining Pecorino.

    Chef Tips

    • Steep the saffron in warm water first to fully extract its color and aroma before adding to the dough.
    • Rolling the malloreddus over ridges is essential — the texture helps the sauce cling far better than a smooth surface would.
    • Fresh, high-quality Italian sausage makes a noticeable difference in the sauce's depth of flavor.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Skipping the saffron, losing the dish's signature color and aroma.
    • Rolling the malloreddus smooth without ridges, resulting in less sauce clinging to each piece.
    • Overcooking the sausage before adding tomatoes, drying it out.

    Cultural Note

    Malloreddus — sometimes called 'gnocchetti sardi' outside Sardinia — takes its name from 'malloru,' a Sardinian word related to young bulls, referencing the pasta's plump, rounded shape.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate the sauce for up to 3 days; uncooked malloreddus can be dried and stored at room temperature for weeks.

    Reheating: Reheat the sauce gently, then toss with freshly cooked malloreddus.

    Serve with: A robust Cannonau di Sardegna red wine, Crusty bread
