🐟 Grilled Sardines in Spiced Roasted Tomato Sauce

Grilled sardines with Ga kenkey

The sauce ingredients roast twenty-five minutes before the sardines join them — and the kenkey is boiled eight to ten minutes until fork-tender.

Twelve whole fresh sardines in a roasted tomato sauce, served with a ball of Ga kenkey sliced into eight discs. The sauce is built by roasting rather than frying: half a red onion, four large quartered tomatoes, ginger, garlic, black pepper, curry powder, extra-hot chilli powder, chilli flakes, stock, tomato purée and salt go into a 180-degree oven for twenty-five minutes. Then the sardines go in for fifteen to twenty minutes until cooked through. Meanwhile the kenkey is boiled eight to ten minutes until fork-tender and sliced. It is the only documented recipe that cooks with kenkey rather than just serving it alongside.

Ingredients

Equipment: Roasting dish, Oven, Pot, for the kenkey, Knife

  • 12 whole fresh sardines, scaled, gutted and washed
  • 1/2 red onion, sliced
  • 4 large ripe tomatoes, quartered
  • 2.5 cm piece fresh root ginger, grated
  • 1 garlic clove, chopped
  • 1/2 tsp coarsely ground black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp curry powder
  • 1/2 tsp extra-hot chilli powder
  • 1/2 tsp dried chilli flakes
  • 100 ml vegetable or chicken stock
  • 1/2 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1/2 tsp sea salt, plus extra
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • Olive oil, for drizzling

To serve

  • 1 ball Ga kenkey

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 180 degrees.
  2. Put the onion, tomatoes, ginger, garlic, black pepper, curry powder, chilli powder, chilli flakes, stock, tomato purée and salt into a roasting dish.
  3. Roast 25 minutes.
  4. Add the sardines, the lemon juice and a drizzle of olive oil.
  5. Cook 15 to 20 minutes, until the sardines are cooked through.
  6. Meanwhile boil the ball of Ga kenkey 8 to 10 minutes, until fork-tender.
  7. Slice the kenkey into 8 discs.
  8. Serve the sardines and sauce with the kenkey discs.

Chef Tips

  • Roast the sauce for twenty-five minutes BEFORE the fish goes in. Roasting rather than frying the tomato gives a sweeter, deeper sauce and it is why this works.
  • Fork-tender at eight to ten minutes is the kenkey cue, and this is the only documented recipe that gives one for cooking a ready-made kenkey ball.
  • Slicing the kenkey into eight discs rather than serving a lump is a plating choice and a good one — it soaks up the sauce.
  • Fifteen to twenty minutes for whole sardines in a hot sauce. They are small and oily and they go from done to dry quickly.
  • Lemon juice goes in WITH the fish, not at the end. It cooks into the sauce.
  • Half a teaspoon each of curry powder, extra-hot chilli powder and chilli flakes is a restrained hand for this cook. The roasting does more of the work than the spicing.
  • Ga kenkey is bought here, not made. If you want to make it, that is Ga Kenkey and it takes three days.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the sardines at the start.
  • Overcooking the sardines.
  • Serving the kenkey as a lump.
  • Frying the sauce instead of roasting it.
  • Boiling the kenkey past fork-tender, so it disintegrates.

Health Benefits

  • Sardines are among the richest sources of omega-3 fats, vitamin D and calcium, eaten whole with their bones.
  • Roasted tomato is high in bioavailable lycopene.
  • Very low in mercury compared with larger fish.
  • Kenkey's fermentation improves the meal's mineral absorption.

Cultural Note

Kenkey with fried fish is the Accra street meal, and this takes the same pairing and roasts it into a single dish — sardines in a spiced tomato sauce with kenkey discs to mop it up. Sardines are also the most sustainable fish in the recipe book and the cheapest, which is worth saying.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 days refrigerated. Oily fish does not keep well.

Reheating: Gentle oven heat. Do not stir, or the sardines break up.

Serve with: Ga Kenkey, Shito, Green Shito
