🥤 Nectar de tamarin (Tamarind Drink)

Nectar de tamarin

A kilogram of tamarind fruit soaked 10 minutes, macerated, filtered and boiled 20 minutes in 5 litres of water, cooled, sweetened to taste and kept cool.

p. 73 of the October 2023 recipe book: 1 kg tamarind fruit, 3 kg sugar and 5 litres of water. The fruit is soaked 10 minutes to soften the pulp, macerated to separate the pulp from fibres and seeds, filtered through a fine white cloth or sieve, and the juice boiled 20 minutes, removed from the heat and cooled; sugar is stirred in to the desired taste; keep cool. The book calls the drink very rich in vitamins A and C and ‘an excellent laxative’ (unsourced). It notes extracting the pulp is delicate because the pulp is sticky. The diagram says ‘Cuisson (100°, 30 min)’ against 20 minutes in the text; recorded, not reconciled.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, Fine cloth or sieve, Bottles

  • 1 kg tamarind fruit
  • 3 kg sugar (the method says ‘to taste’)
  • 5 litres water

Method

  1. Soak the fruit 10 minutes in drinking water to soften the pulp.
  2. Macerate to separate the pulp from fibres and seeds.
  3. Filter through a fine white cloth or sieve.
  4. Boil 20 minutes. Remove from the heat and cool.
  5. Stir in sugar to the desired taste.
  6. Bottle and keep cool.

Chef Tips

  • The printed note is that pulp extraction is delicate because the pulp is sticky.
  • Sugar is listed as 3 kg but the method says ‘to taste’.
  • The diagram says 100° for 30 minutes; the text says 20 minutes.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all 3 kg sugar without tasting.
  • Skipping the filtering.
  • Taking the laxative claim as established.

  • The book’s description claims vitamins A and C and a laxative effect; it cites no evidence.

Cultural Note

The book attributes its recipes to the northern part of Cameroon and names no community, lamidat or town for any recipe.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Keep cool (‘conserver au frais’). No shelf life is printed.

Reheating: Serve cold.

Serve with: Served cold
