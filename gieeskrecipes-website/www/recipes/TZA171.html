Chips Mayai

Tanzanian Chips Mayai — Tanzania's greatest street food. Freshly fried thick chips combined with beaten eggs in a heavy pan, pressed and set into a crispy-bottomed egg-and-chip omelette, served with tomato sauce and pili pili. Completely original, completely Tanzanian, completely irresistible.

Chips Mayai (chips and eggs) is the dish that defines Tanzanian street food — it has no equivalent anywhere else in the world. A portion of freshly fried chips is poured into a hot oiled pan, beaten seasoned eggs poured over, and everything pressed together until the bottom is crispy and the egg is set. Flipped (sometimes), cut into wedges, and served with ketchup and pili pili — it is the meal of Dar es Salaam's students, workers, and night owls.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Dar es Salaam — Tanzania's greatest street food invention

Chips Mayai is Tanzania's most beloved and original street food creation — a thick omelette made by pouring beaten eggs over a pan of freshly fried chips, pressing everything together until the egg sets around the chips. The result is a square of crispy chips bound in fluffy egg — filling, affordable, and completely irreplaceable. It is sold at every chips stand in every Tanzanian city and town, made to order in the same heavy steel pans that have been in use for decades. No other country has this dish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep frying pan for chips, Heavy 24cm frying pan (the chips mayai pan), Plate or lid for flipping

  • 3 large potatoes — peeled, cut into thick chips
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 medium onion — thinly sliced
  • 1 medium tomato — diced
  • 1 green chilli — sliced
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander
  • ½ tsp curry powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Oil for deep frying the chips
  • 2 tbsp oil for the pan

To serve: tomato ketchup, pili pili (TZA117)

    Method

    1. Fry chips in deep oil at 175°C until golden and cooked through — 8—10 minutes. Drain on paper towels.
    2. Beat eggs with curry powder, turmeric, salt, and a splash of water.
    3. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a heavy frying pan over medium heat.
    4. Add onion and chilli. Fry 2 minutes.
    5. Add fried chips. Spread evenly across the pan.
    6. Pour beaten egg mixture evenly over the chips.
    7. Add diced tomato and coriander on top.
    8. Press down firmly with a spatula.
    9. Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 5—7 minutes until egg is fully set.
    10. Slide onto a plate — or flip for a crispy top — cut into wedges.
    11. Serve with tomato ketchup and pili pili.

    Chef Tips

    • The chips must be freshly fried and hot — never use cold chips.
    • Press down firmly once — then do not disturb until set.
    • The bottom should be crispy and golden — medium-low heat and patience.
    • Flipping is optional — most vendors serve it egg-side up.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cold chips — produce a soggy result.
    • High heat — burns the bottom before egg sets.
    • Stirring — destroys the structure.

    The Science

    The chips provide structural scaffolding for the egg — the egg fills the gaps between chips and sets around them, binding everything into a coherent structure. The oil absorbed by the chips during frying migrates into the egg during the setting stage, creating a more richly flavoured omelette than plain eggs.

    Storage: Best eaten immediately — does not keep.

    Reheating: Not recommended.
