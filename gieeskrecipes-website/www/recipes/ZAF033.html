🧀 Braaibroodjie

Braaibroodjie

Thick white bread, butter on both outsides, boerewors and chutney and onion and two cheeses inside, closed in a hinged grid and cooked slowly over dying coals until golden all over.

The braaibroodjie is served last, over coals that are on their way out, and that timing is not an accident — it needs low, even heat to melt the cheese through before the bread burns. Chutney is not optional in South Africa; Mrs Ball's is the default and its ancestor is blatjang, the dried-fruit and chilli chutney the Cape Muslim kitchen made from the Malay blachan. It is the most argued-about item at any braai, mostly about whether tomato belongs in it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Hinged braai grid (a toastie grid), Pastry brush, Braai with hot coals, then medium

  • 12 slices thick-cut farm-style white bread
  • 200 g butter, melted
  • 600 g thick boerewors
  • Fruit chutney, generously — Mrs Ball's if you have it
  • 1 small red onion, thinly sliced
  • 250 g Gouda, grated
  • 250 g mozzarella, grated
  • Salt and pepper
  • A handful of chives, chopped, to serve

Method

  1. Braai the boerewors over hot coals until the outside is well caramelised but the middle is still pink. Let it cool, then slice it into discs.
  2. Mix the grated Gouda and mozzarella together in a bowl.
  3. Lay out the bread slices and brush them with melted butter.
  4. Spread chutney on six of the slices and lay the boerewors discs on top.
  5. Add the sliced onion and a generous amount of the mixed cheese, season, and close each sandwich.
  6. Brush the top of each with butter, load them into the hinged grid and close it.
  7. Place the grid butter-side down over medium coals, and brush the now-upward side with the rest of the melted butter.
  8. Braai slowly, a few minutes a side, until evenly golden all over and the cheese has melted through.
  9. Cut each in half, scatter with chives and serve with extra chutney for dipping.

Chef Tips

  • Medium coals and patience. The point of serving these last is that the fire has calmed down — over hot coals the bread blackens and the cheese never melts.
  • Butter both outsides generously. This is not the moment for restraint, and it is what gives the even golden crust.
  • Two cheeses for two jobs: Gouda for flavour, mozzarella for the pull.
  • Let the wors cool before slicing. Hot boerewors crumbles under the knife.
  • The tomato question is genuinely contested. Add a slice if you want; expect to be told about it.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Making them over hot coals, which is the single reason most braaibroodjies come out burnt outside and cold inside.
  • Under-buttering, which gives dry, patchy toast.
  • Overfilling, so the grid will not close and the cheese runs into the fire.
  • Rushing them. A few minutes a side, turned more than once, is the whole method.

Health Benefits

  • Half a kilo of cheese across six sandwiches makes this high in protein and calcium.
  • Red onion goes in raw, keeping its compounds intact.
  • There is no arguing this is a light dish.

Cultural Note

The chutney in a braaibroodjie has a longer history than the sandwich. Blatjang comes from the Malay blachan and was made in Cape kitchens from dried fruit, chilli and vinegar; Mrs Ball's is its commercial descendant, and it now sits on the table at braais in every community in the country.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Eaten immediately. They do not keep.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Extra chutney, for dipping, Sosaties, Roosterkoek
