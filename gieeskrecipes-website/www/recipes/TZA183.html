🥜 Tanzanian Boiled Groundnuts

Karanga za Kuchemsha

Tanzanian Karanga za Kuchemsha — fresh raw groundnuts boiled in their shells in heavily salted water until completely tender. The harvest season snack — softer, moister, and more complex than roasted peanuts. Sold at rainy season markets when fresh peanuts are available.

Boiled groundnuts are the harvest season alternative to roasted — when fresh green peanuts are available immediately after the harvest, they are boiled in heavily salted water. The result is completely different from roasted peanuts: soft, moist, creamy, and mild. A seasonal treat that roasted peanuts cannot replicate.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot

  • 500g fresh raw groundnuts in shells
  • 3 tbsp coarse salt
  • Enough water to cover by 5cm

Method

  1. Wash groundnuts thoroughly.
  2. Place in a large pot. Cover with water by 5cm.
  3. Add salt generously — the water should taste very salty.
  4. Bring to a boil. Simmer covered for 40—50 minutes.
  5. Test one — the nut inside should be completely soft and creamy.
  6. Drain. Allow to cool slightly.
  7. Eat by cracking the shell and extracting the soft nut.

Chef Tips

  • The water must be genuinely salty — this seasons the nut through the shell.
  • Fresh green peanuts only — dried peanuts do not boil the same way.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Insufficient salt.
  • Under-boiling — nuts should be creamy soft.

The Science

Salt penetrates the peanut shell through osmosis during boiling — seasoning the nut from the outside in. This is why boiled peanuts are more uniformly seasoned than salted roasted peanuts. The soft texture results from starch gelatinisation at 100°C, which the hard structure of raw peanuts prevents.

Cultural Note

Boiled groundnuts are the taste of harvest season in Tanzania — the brief annual window when fresh peanuts from the fields are available, transformed by salt and water into a completely different snack from the roasted version everyone knows year-round.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 3 days in the shell.

Reheating: Warm briefly in their water.

Serve with: As a standalone snack, With cold drinks
