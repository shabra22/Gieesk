🍯 Ngalakh with Thiakry, Bouye and Peanut Paste (Dumplings And More)

Ngalakh / neteri

200 g of thiakry soaked in hot water, steamed about ten minutes and worked with butter, coconut, raisins and nutmeg, then folded gradually into a strained sauce of 100 g baobab powder, 100 g peanut paste and 50 cl of water until it reaches a porridge-like texture.

The French blog Dumplings And More gives ngalakh for 4 people: 200 g of thiakry, 100 g of bouye powder, 100 g of peanut paste, 150 g of sugar, 10 g of butter, 30 g of grated coconut, 15 to 30 g of raisins, 2 tablespoons of orange blossom water, 2 sachets of vanilla sugar and a generous pinch of nutmeg. The thiakry is soaked in hot water, steamed in a couscous pot about ten minutes, spread in a dish and worked with the butter, coconut, raisins and nutmeg. For the sauce the bouye is dissolved in water (20 cl, then another 30 cl with the peanut paste), strained to remove excess baobab fibre and flavoured with the vanilla sugar and orange blossom water. The sauce is worked into the thiakry gradually ‘until a texture close to porridge’ and the dish refrigerated before serving. The page ties ngalakh to Easter in Senegal and says it ‘symbolises sharing’. Ingredients

Equipment: Couscous pot (couscoussier), Fine sieve (chinois), Large dish

  • 200 g thiakry (medium millet semolina)
  • 10 g butter
  • 30 g grated coconut
  • 15 to 30 g raisins
  • A generous pinch of nutmeg
  • 100 g bouye powder (baobab)
  • 100 g peanut paste
  • 150 g sugar
  • 2 sachets vanilla sugar
  • 2 tablespoons orange blossom water
  • Hot water for soaking — the quantity is not captured in the structured extraction
  • 20 cl water to dissolve the bouye, then 30 cl more with the peanut paste (as in the structured extraction; not character-verified)

Method

  1. Pour hot water over the thiakry and let it absorb. Steam in a couscous pot about 10 minutes.
  2. Spread the cooked thiakry in a large dish and work in the butter, coconut, raisins and nutmeg.
  3. For the sauce, dissolve the bouye powder in 20 cl of water. Add 30 cl more water and the peanut paste. Strain to remove excess baobab fibre.
  4. Flavour with the vanilla sugar and orange blossom water.
  5. Pour the thiakry into a large dish and gradually work in the sauce, until the texture is close to porridge.
  6. Refrigerate before serving.

Chef Tips

  • The cue is « jusqu’à obtenir une texture proche du porridge », which refers to how much sauce goes into the millet.
  • Add the sauce gradually; the amount you need depends on how much the thiakry has absorbed.
  • The 20 cl + 30 cl water split is carried from the structured extraction; check against the original page.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding all the sauce at once.
  • Skipping the straining.
  • Serving it warm.
