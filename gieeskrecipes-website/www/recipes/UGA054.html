🐟 Tomato Fish Stew

Tomato fish stew

The spice blend split in half — one half into a twenty-minute marinade, the other into the sauce — with the fish dusted in maize flour before frying and eight tomatoes half chopped, half blended.

This is the most structured fish dish in the Ugandan repertoire and every part of its structure earns its place. The spices are divided in two: half go into a lemon marinade with the fish, half into the tomato sauce later. The tomatoes are likewise divided: half finely chopped for texture, half blended with a scotch bonnet for body. And the fish is rolled in maize flour before frying, which absorbs the liquid the marinade drew out and keeps it from sticking. Sugar goes in to neutralise the tomatoes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bowl, for marinating, Blender, Deep pan for frying, Second pan, for the stew, Flat plate, for the flour

  • 1 large fish (tilapia or snapper), scaled, cut and thoroughly washed
  • 8 large tomatoes
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 4 cloves garlic, chopped
  • 1 celery stick, chopped
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) ginger, grated
  • 15 ml (1 tbsp) sugar, or more
  • 1/2 scotch bonnet
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • Salt
  • 240 ml (1 cup) corn or maize flour
  • Oil, for frying
  • 480 to 720 ml (2 to 3 cups) water

The spice blend — half to the marinade, half to the sauce

  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) turmeric powder
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) cumin powder
  • 10 ml (2 tsp) coriander powder
  • 10 ml (2 tsp) ground bay leaves
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) ground cloves
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) cinnamon
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) ground black pepper

Method

    The marinade — half the spices

    1. Put the clean pieces of fish in a bowl. Add half of the turmeric, cumin, coriander, ground bay leaves, cloves, cinnamon and black pepper, a pinch of salt and the juice of the lemon.
    2. Mix well until all the pieces are covered, and set aside to marinate 20 minutes.

    Frying the fish

    1. In a separate pan, add enough oil to immerse the fish and put it on high heat to get hot.
    2. Put the maize flour on a flat plate and roll each piece of fish in it until well coated.
    3. Once the oil is hot, gently lower the fish in and fry until golden brown and crispy. Lift out and set aside.

    The tomatoes — half chopped, half blended

    1. While the fish fries, finely chop half the tomatoes and set them aside.
    2. Cut the other half into chunks and blend them with the scotch bonnet until pureed.

    The sauce — the other half of the spices

    1. Add about a tablespoon of oil to a separate pan over medium heat.
    2. Add the garlic, onions, ginger and celery and cook until translucent.
    3. Add the finely chopped tomatoes and cook until tender.
    4. Pour in the pureed tomatoes and cook until reduced to a thick paste and the oil has separated from it.
    5. Add the remaining half of the spices and mix well.
    6. Add 2 to 3 cups of water and the sugar and stir well.

    Together

    1. Add the fried fish to the tomato stew and cover the pan.
    2. Let it boil about 15 minutes.
    3. Reduce the heat and simmer until it has reduced, thickened, and has oil floating on top.
    4. Serve hot.

    Chef Tips

    • Split the spices in half. Marinade and sauce need different things from them, and putting them all in one place gives you a flat dish either way.
    • The maize flour coating absorbs the liquid the marinade drew out of the fish and stops it sticking to the pan. Breadcrumbs do the same job if you have no maize flour.
    • Half the tomatoes chopped, half blended. The chopped half gives texture, the blended half gives body, and you want both.
    • Cook the pureed tomatoes until the oil separates. That is the point at which they stop being raw and start being a sauce.
    • Sugar neutralises the sourness. Tomatoes are naturally sour and you may well need more than a tablespoon.
    • Fry the fish crisp and golden before it goes in. Soft fish disintegrates in the stew.
    • Oil floating on top is the final doneness cue, exactly as in the bean stew and the groundnut sauces.
    • It is eaten with obundu, akaalo or posho depending on where in Uganda you are, and hands go straight into the bowl.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Putting all the spices in the marinade.
    • Skipping the maize flour and having the fish stick and tear.
    • Blending all the tomatoes, so there is no texture.
    • Adding the water before the puree has separated from the oil.
    • Leaving the sugar out and wondering why it is so sour.

    Health Benefits

    • Tilapia and snapper are high in protein, selenium and vitamin B12.
    • Eight tomatoes make this a significant source of lycopene and vitamin C.
    • Turmeric, cumin, coriander, cloves and cinnamon all go in in quantity.
    • The fish is deep-fried, which is where most of the fat comes from.

    Cultural Note

    This is a special-occasion dish in a country full of quick ones, and the structure shows it — three separate operations before anything is combined. The three names for the starch you eat it with, one sentence apart, is a neat illustration of how many regional cuisines sit inside Ugandan food.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Warm gently. The fish will soften but the sauce improves.

    Serve with: Posho, Matooke, Kalo
