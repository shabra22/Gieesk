🐟 Ingelegde Vis

Ingelegde vis — Kaapse kerrievis

Fish segmented on the bone, massaged with a masala paste and fried slowly until crusty, then submerged in warm brown vinegar with onions and toasted whole spice and left two days before anyone touches it.

Pickled fish is the Easter dish of the Cape, eaten in Cape Muslim and Cape Christian households alike, and the reason is practical before it is religious: it keeps, which matters over a long weekend. It is usually made with kabeljou, and snoek works too. The fish is cut across the body with the skin on rather than filleted, because segments hold together in the vinegar where fillets fall apart. Made properly it is better on day three than day one.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy frying pan, Second pot, for the vinegar, Deep glass or ceramic dish with a cover

    The fish

    • 1 kg kabeljou or snoek, cut across into segments, skin on
    • Oil, for slow frying

    The masala paste

    • 4 tsp red masala powder
    • 1 tsp curry powder
    • 3 tsp crushed garlic
    • 1 tsp crushed chillies
    • A squeeze of lemon
    • A pinch of salt

    The pickling liquid

    • 3 tsp crushed garlic
    • 3 bay leaves
    • 6 allspice berries (pimento)
    • 750 ml brown vinegar
    • 3 white onions, sliced lengthwise
    • 100 g (1/2 cup) sugar
    • 1 tsp ground turmeric

    Method

    1. Cut the fish horizontally from head to tail into segments, keeping the skin on. Do not fillet it and do not cube it.
    2. Lay the segments on a board and pat them properly dry.
    3. Mix the masala powder, curry powder, 3 tsp of the garlic, the crushed chilli, the lemon and the salt into a paste.
    4. Massage the paste evenly into the dried fish, getting it into the cut surfaces.
    5. Fry the fish very slowly in a little oil over low heat, turning, until crusty and flaky. Low and slow — masala burns fast and burnt masala makes the whole dish bitter.
    6. In an empty pot, toast the remaining 3 tsp garlic with the bay leaves and allspice over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes, until fragrant.
    7. Keeping the heat very low, pour in the brown vinegar with the sugar and turmeric and heat for 3 to 5 minutes, until warm and the sugar has dissolved.
    8. Turn off the heat and add the sliced onions to the warm vinegar, so they soften without cooking to mush.
    9. Drain the fried fish on paper towel, then pour the vinegar and onions into a deep dish and lay the fish in it.
    10. Make sure the fish is fully submerged, cover, and refrigerate. Eat it about two days later.

    Chef Tips

    • Low heat for the frying is the whole technique. The masala on the outside of the fish will scorch over a hot pan and there is no recovering from that.
    • The vinegar goes on warm, not boiling. Boiling vinegar cooks the onions to nothing and drives off the sharpness the dish needs.
    • Two days minimum. It improves for a week and keeps up to two in the fridge.
    • Eat it cold, with homemade white bread and butter. Cape cooks are firm that it is bread and not hot cross buns.
    • Kabeljou is the usual fish; snoek, yellowtail or any firm white fish will do. Soft fish disintegrates in the vinegar.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Filleting or cubing the fish, which leaves you with flakes floating in vinegar after two days.
    • Frying over anything above low heat and burning the masala.
    • Leaving any piece of fish standing above the liquid, which will spoil and take the rest with it.
    • Serving it the same day. It is not pickled yet — it is just fried fish in vinegar.

    Health Benefits

    • A lean, high-protein fish dish, poached in vinegar rather than finished in fat.
    • Provides omega-3 fatty acids, selenium and vitamin B12.
    • Turmeric and raw onion both go in with their compounds intact.

    Cultural Note

    Pickled fish crosses a line that most South African food does not: it is the Easter dish of both Cape Muslim and Cape Christian households, and the traditions have intermingled for centuries. Cape families used to camp at Macassar Beach over Easter, and by one cook's account every one of up to a hundred tents had a dish of it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerated and fully submerged, up to 2 weeks. The flavour keeps improving for the first week.

    Reheating: Served cold. Some households warm it very slightly, but it is a cold dish by design.

    Serve with: Homemade white bread and butter, Boiled potatoes, Geelrys (yellow rice)
