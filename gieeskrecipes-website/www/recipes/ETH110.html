🫘 Ethiopian Split Pea Stew

ቅቅ ወጥ — Kik Wat

Ethiopian Kik Wat — split peas cooked in a berbere sauce (unlike the mild Kik Alicha). The spiced, red version of the split pea stew — built on the same dry-caramelised onion base as Key Wat, but with peas instead of meat.

Kik Wat is the spiced version of Kik Alicha — where Kik Alicha is mild and golden, Kik Wat is red and spiced with berbere. The split peas dissolve partially into the berbere sauce, producing a thick, rich stew that is as satisfying as Misir Wat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot

  • 2 cups green or yellow split peas — washed
  • 3 onions — finely diced
  • 4 tbsp oil
  • 3 tbsp berbere
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 3 cups water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Dry caramelise onions — 18 minutes.
  2. Add oil and berbere. Fry berbere 3 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add peas. Stir to coat.
  5. Add water. Simmer covered 40—45 minutes.
  6. Peas should dissolve partially.
  7. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Split peas take longer to soften than red lentils — 40—45 minutes minimum.
  • The berbere base is identical to Misir Wat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-cooking — peas must be soft.

The Science

See ETH101 for berbere legume stew science. Split peas have a slightly higher starch content than red lentils — producing a thicker, more starchy sauce when dissolved.

Cultural Note

Kik Wat is the spiced version of the split pea tradition — for those who want berbere heat in their legume stew. It sits naturally alongside Misir Wat on the fasting platter.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days. Freeze 3 months.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), On the fasting platter
