🫓 Tigre Canjeero and Suqaar

ካንጀሮ — Canjeero

Tigre Canjeero with Suqaar — the Tigre-speaking community's fermented sorghum pancake served with a quick-cooked meat and onion scramble. Canjeero is the gateway dish between Ethiopian injera and Somali/Afar traditions — thinner than injera, cooked on both sides.

The Tigre language (distinct from Tigrinya) is spoken by the semi-nomadic communities of northwestern Ethiopia. Their canjeero is a thin, slightly sour sorghum pancake — a bridge between injera and the canjeero of Somalia.

Ingredients

Equipment: Non-stick pan, Bowl for fermentation

  • 2 cups sorghum flour
  • ½ cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp yeast
  • 2.5 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Suqaar filling: 300g minced meat, 1 onion, 1 tsp cumin, salt

    Method

    1. Mix flours, yeast, water, and salt. Ferment 8—12 hours.
    2. Make suqaar: brown mince with onion and cumin.
    3. Cook thin canjeero pancakes on non-stick pan — both sides.
    4. Serve canjeero with suqaar on top.

    Chef Tips

    • Canjeero is cooked on both sides — unlike injera.
    • Thinner than injera.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Too thick — canjeero should be very thin.

    Cultural Note

    Tigre canjeero is the culinary bridge of northwestern Ethiopia — the place where injera culture meets the flatbread traditions of the lowland Saharan fringe.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Canjeero: 2 days. Suqaar: 3 days.

    Reheating: Briefly.

    Serve with: With suqaar, At Tigre community gatherings
