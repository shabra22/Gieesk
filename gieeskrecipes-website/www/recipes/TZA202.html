🍩 Tanzanian Cardamom Doughnut Balls

Kaimati ya Tanzania

Tanzanian Kaimati — soft, round dough balls deep-fried golden then dipped while hot into a cardamom-rose water sugar syrup. The Eid celebration sweet of the Swahili coast — fragrant, sticky, and irresistible.

Kaimati are among the most beloved sweets of the Zanzibar coast — the soft fried dough balls dipped in fragrant sugar syrup are made for every major celebration. The rose water and cardamom syrup is what makes them distinctly Zanzibari — the same aromatics that perfume the biriani and the halwa here perfume the syrup.

Ingredients

Equipment: Deep frying pan, Small saucepan for syrup, Slotted spoon

    Dough

    • 2 cups all-purpose flour
    • 1 tsp active dry yeast
    • 1 tbsp sugar
    • ½ tsp ground cardamom
    • Pinch of salt
    • 200ml warm water
    • Oil for deep frying

    Cardamom-Rose Syrup

    • 1 cup sugar
    • ½ cup water
    • ½ tsp ground cardamom
    • 1 tsp rose water
    • 1 tbsp lemon juice

    Method

    1. Dissolve yeast and sugar in warm water. Rest 10 minutes until foamy.
    2. Mix flour, cardamom, and salt. Add yeast mixture. Mix to a thick, sticky batter.
    3. Cover and rest 60 minutes until bubbled and puffed.
    4. Make syrup: boil sugar and water 5 minutes until slightly thickened. Remove from heat. Add cardamom, rose water, and lemon juice. Keep warm.
    5. Heat oil to 170°C.
    6. Drop tablespoons of batter into oil.
    7. Fry 3—4 minutes, turning gently, until deeply golden all over.
    8. While still very hot, toss kaimati in warm syrup.
    9. Pile on a plate. Serve immediately.

    Chef Tips

    • Dip in syrup while piping hot — the syrup penetrates as the steam escapes.
    • The lemon juice in the syrup prevents it from crystallising.
    • Rose water is the Zanzibar signature — do not omit.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Cold syrup — does not penetrate.
    • Syrup too cold when dipping.
    • Over-crowding the fryer.

    The Science

    The hot kaimati create a pressure differential as they cool — the steam inside condenses, drawing the warm syrup into the surface pores of the dough. This is why hot-into-warm syrup produces a more deeply glazed result than cold-into-cold.

    Cultural Note

    Kaimati at Eid are Zanzibar's most anticipated sweet — made in enormous quantities, shared with the whole neighbourhood, and eaten warm with spiced coffee. The rose water and cardamom smell that drifts from the kitchen is the smell of celebration itself.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best eaten same day.

    Reheating: Warm briefly in oven.

    Serve with: Kahawa ya Zanzibar (TZA142), Chai ya Viungo (TZA144)
