🌿 Njama Njama, Braised Garden Huckleberry Greens (Forager Chef)

Njama njama

Garlic and onion sweated in unrefined palm oil, cooked down with tomato puree until the oil visibly separates, then simmered with garden huckleberry greens and smoked-turkey stock until nearly dry.

Bergo’s version of the Cameroon braised greens uses black nightshade (garden huckleberry) leaves. Garlic is sweated in palm oil until aromatic, onions cooked until translucent about 5 minutes, and the tomato puree cooked 10–15 minutes on medium-low heat or until the palm oil visibly separates. The greens (blanched or fresh) and the stock go in, covered, and cook until the greens are very tender and the pan is nearly dry (10–15 minutes depending on how old the leaves are). Cooked smoked chicken or turkey is stirred in. An optional fufu (flour, water, salt) is whisked over medium-high heat until too thick for the whisk, then stirred 5 minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan with lid, Whisk

  • 1/4 cup unrefined palm oil
  • 4 oz onion
  • 2 tablespoons garlic
  • 1–2 small habanero peppers (optional)
  • 1 cup tomato puree
  • 8 oz garden huckleberry leaves / black nightshade greens
  • 1 cup smoked turkey stock or chicken stock
  • 4 oz leftover smoked chicken or turkey, roughly chopped (optional)

Optional fufu

  • 1 cup fufu flour
  • 1.5 cups water
  • 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt

Method

  1. Sweat the garlic in the palm oil until aromatic. Add the onions and cook on medium heat until translucent, about 5 minutes.
  2. Add the tomato puree and cook 10–15 minutes on medium-low heat, or until the palm oil visibly separates.
  3. Add the blanched or fresh greens and the stock. Cover and simmer until the greens are very tender and the pan is nearly dry (10–15 minutes depending on age).
  4. Stir in the cooked meat and adjust the seasoning.

Optional fufu

  1. Whisk the fufu flour, water and salt over medium-high heat until it becomes too thick for the whisk. Switch to a wooden spoon and stir constantly for 5 minutes, kneading and folding. It will keep setting as it cools.

Chef Tips

  • Printed cues: ‘palm oil visibly separates’; ‘very tender’; ‘pan nearly dry’.
  • The page says to expect 10–15 minutes for the greens depending on how mature the leaves are.
  • The page gives no toxicity guidance for black nightshade — identify the plant carefully.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Misidentifying the nightshade leaves.
  • Pulling the pan before the oil separates.
  • Skipping the cover during the braise.

Cultural Note

Bergo, a non-Cameroonian forager-chef, writes that this is a traditional recipe from Cameroon and that at least three local cultivars are named for places in Cameroon including Bamenda, Buea and Bamoun. His recipe card lists cuisine as ‘Cameroonian, South African’ without explanation.
