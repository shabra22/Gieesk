🍗 Authentic Kenyan Kuku wa Nazi

Kuku wa Nazi

Kuku wa Nazi is the jewel of Swahili coastal cooking — chicken slowly simmered in creamy coconut milk with onions, tomatoes, garlic, ginger, and warm spices until the sauce is rich, golden, and deeply fragrant. Always served with Wali wa Nazi. Always celebrated.

There is a reason Kuku wa Nazi is prepared for every important occasion along the Kenyan coast — weddings, Eid, family homecomings. The combination of chicken simmering in coconut milk with turmeric, coriander, and garlic creates something that is simultaneously comforting and festive. The sauce turns a deep gold, thick and fragrant, clinging to every piece of chicken. Set it next to a bowl of Wali wa Nazi and you have one of the great meal combinations in all of Kenyan cuisine.

Heritage & Origin

Origin: Kenyan Swahili Coast — shaped by centuries of African, Arab, Persian, and Indian culinary exchange

Kuku wa Nazi is one of the defining dishes of the Kenyan coast, born from the same centuries of Indian Ocean trade that shaped all of Swahili cuisine. The use of coconut milk as a braising liquid for meat reflects the Persian and Indian influence on coastal East African cooking. In homes from Mombasa to Lamu, this dish has been prepared for weddings, Eid celebrations, and family gatherings for generations, always served alongside Wali wa Nazi.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large cooking pot or Dutch oven, Wooden spoon, Knife, Chopping board, Measuring spoons

  • 1.5 kg chicken — cut into serving pieces, bone-in preferred
  • 500ml thick coconut milk — fresh or canned
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 5 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste — optional
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped, for garnish

Method

  1. Wash and pat the chicken pieces completely dry. Season lightly with salt and black pepper.
  2. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium-high heat. Brown the chicken pieces for 5–7 minutes, turning, until lightly golden on all sides. This step builds deep flavour. Remove and set aside.
  3. In the same pot, add the onions and cook over medium heat, stirring, until soft and lightly golden — about 6–8 minutes.
  4. Add the garlic and ginger. Cook for 1 minute until fragrant.
  5. Stir in the diced tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 6–8 minutes until the tomatoes completely break down into a thick sauce.
  6. Add the curry powder, turmeric, ground coriander, cumin, and paprika. Stir for 1 minute to bloom the spices in the sauce.
  7. Return the browned chicken pieces to the pot. Stir to coat every piece in the spiced tomato base.
  8. Pour in the coconut milk and stir gently to combine.
  9. Bring to a gentle simmer. Reduce heat to low, cover, and cook for 35–40 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is completely tender and cooked through.
  10. Do not boil vigorously — this causes the coconut milk to separate.
  11. Taste and adjust the seasoning with salt and pepper.
  12. Remove from heat and allow to rest for 10 minutes. Scatter with fresh coriander and serve with Wali wa Nazi.

Chef Tips

  • Bone-in chicken develops a far richer, deeper flavour in the sauce than boneless cuts — always worth it.
  • Brown the chicken properly before simmering — this golden crust adds flavour that infuses the entire curry.
  • Simmer gently, never boil — vigorous boiling causes the coconut milk to split into an oily, grainy sauce.
  • Rest the curry for 10 minutes before serving — the flavours deepen noticeably in this time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling the coconut milk vigorously — it separates and the sauce becomes oily rather than creamy.
  • Skipping the browning step — the chicken and sauce lack depth of flavour.
  • Adding the spices too late — they need time to bloom and cook into the sauce.
  • Overcooking the chicken until it falls apart — it should be tender but still hold its shape.

Cultural Note

Kuku wa Nazi is one of the defining dishes of Kenya's Swahili Coast, where coconuts grow abundantly and feature in virtually every aspect of coastal cooking. Influenced by centuries of African, Arab, Persian, and Indian culinary exchange, it is prepared for weddings, Eid celebrations, and family gatherings across the coast. The combination of Kuku wa Nazi with Wali wa Nazi is one of the great meal pairings of Kenyan cuisine.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Freeze for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently over low heat, adding a splash of coconut milk or water if the sauce has thickened. Stir gently to restore the creamy texture.

Serve with: Wali wa Nazi, Plain Steamed Rice, Chapati, Ugali, Kachumbari, Steamed Greens
