🍛 Thiébou Yap with Senegal River Valley Basmati

Thiébou yap — thiébou yapp

The same dish with a mustard-and-macédoine onion sauce, a nokoss of eight garlic cloves, and the rice given eight minutes in the microwave first.

A second and genuinely different thiébou yap from the same cook, written as an argument for Senegalese-grown rice. Three onions and five centilitres of oil for the onion sauce, with a good tablespoon of mustard, a pot of macédoine and green olives. The nokoss is one onion, half a bell pepper, a teaspoon of pepper, eight cloves of garlic and salt, used in thirds — a third with the browning meat, a third into the water, the rest at the end. The rice gets eight minutes in the microwave or a pre-steam in a couscoussier. Her rice cue is the best in the collection: the water must sit slightly above the rice without drowning it.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Cocotte, for the onion sauce, Blender, for the nokoss, Microwave, or a couscoussier

    For the onion sauce, the only printed list

    • 3 onions
    • 5 cl oil
    • 1 bay leaf
    • 5 cloves garlic
    • 1/2 bell pepper
    • 1 tbsp vinegar
    • 1 good tbsp mustard
    • 2 dried bird's eye chillies (optional)
    • Salt and pepper
    • 1 pot of macédoine
    • Green olives

    For the nokoss, from the method

    • 1 onion
    • 1/2 bell pepper
    • 1 tsp pepper
    • 8 cloves garlic
    • Salt

    From the method

    • About 1.5 litres water

    No quantity is printed for the meat or the rice. See the note.

    • Meat, cut into pieces
    • Rice — basmati

    Optional: 1/2 sugar, unit not printed in the source

      Method

      1. Brown the meat in hot oil.
      2. Meanwhile blend one onion, half a bell pepper, 1 teaspoon of pepper, 8 cloves of garlic and salt into the nokoss.
      3. When the meat is well browned, add a sliced onion and the other half-pepper in small dice.
      4. Add a third of the nokoss and salt, and cook.
      5. Brown the onions to a good brown colour for the rice.
      6. Add about 1.5 litres of water and bring to the boil.
      7. Add a third of the farce, a fresh chilli and a bay leaf, and leave it to simmer 1 hour on low heat.
      8. Wash the rice, then give it 8 minutes in the microwave, or pre-steam it in a couscoussier.
      9. Add the rest of the farce, taste the broth and re-salt.
      10. Reduce the broth as a precaution, then stir in the rice, topping up with broth if needed. The water must sit slightly above the rice without drowning it.

      The onion sauce

      1. Put oil in a pan and add the onions once the oil is hot enough.
      2. Chop the garlic, pepper and chilli into a small farce. Add the bay leaf and stir.
      3. When the onions turn translucent, add the farce, salt and pepper, then a little water and the vinegar, and mix.
      4. Cook a few minutes on low heat, then add the mustard near the end and adjust the salt.
      5. Finish with the macédoine and the olives.

      Chef Tips

      • The water must sit slightly above the rice without drowning it — her phrase, and the single most useful rice instruction in this whole collection. Learn it and most rice dishes get easier.
      • Eight minutes in the microwave is a legitimate shortcut for the pre-steam, and she offers it herself alongside the couscoussier.
      • The nokoss in thirds is the structure: one third with the meat, one third into the water, the last third at the end. It builds in layers rather than all at once.
      • Eight cloves of garlic in the nokoss is a lot and it is correct. This is a garlic-forward dish.
      • Mustard goes in NEAR THE END of the onion sauce, not at the start. Cooked long it loses its point.
      • Reduce the broth a little before the rice goes in, as a precaution. Easier to add broth back than to take it out.
      • This is a different recipe from her 2023 thiebou yaap, not a revision — that one has no mustard and no macédoine. Both are published.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Drowning the rice.
      • All the nokoss in at once.
      • Mustard added early.
      • Skipping the rice pre-steam.
      • Pale onions.

      Health Benefits

      • High in protein and iron.
      • Eight cloves of garlic supply allicin and other organosulfur compounds.
      • Basmati has a lower glycaemic index than broken white rice.
      • Gluten-free and dairy-free.

      Cultural Note

      Senegal imports a great deal of rice while growing good rice in the Senegal River valley, and this recipe is quietly an argument about that — a cook putting her national grain in the title of her own dish and noting how long consumers overlooked it. The food-self-sufficiency line is hers, not a garnish.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

      Reheating: Steam the rice; warm the meat and sauce gently.

      Serve with: Green olives, Kaani, Attaya
