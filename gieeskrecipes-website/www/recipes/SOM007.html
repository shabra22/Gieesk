Maraq

A foundational Somali meat and vegetable soup, gently spiced and slow-simmered, served alongside rice or bread, its broth also spooned over canjeero for a comforting everyday meal.

Maraq is less a single fixed recipe than a category - the base technique for turning meat, bones, and vegetables into a nourishing broth-based dish, and its exact makeup shifts by household, season, and what protein is on hand, from beef and goat to chicken.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot with lid

  • 800 g (1.75 lb) bone-in goat or beef, cut into pieces
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 carrots, chopped
  • 2 potatoes, cubed
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp xawaash spice blend
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1.5 L (6 cups) water
  • Fresh cilantro, chopped
  • Salt, to taste

Method

  1. Warm the oil in a large pot and brown the meat pieces on all sides.
  2. Add the onion and cook until softened.
  3. Stir in the garlic, ginger, and xawaash spice blend, cooking briefly until fragrant.
  4. Add the tomatoes and cook until they begin to break down.
  5. Add the water and bay leaf, bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer.
  6. Cover and cook for 35-40 minutes until the meat begins to tenderize.
  7. Add the carrots and potatoes, and continue simmering for another 20-25 minutes, until the meat and vegetables are fully tender.
  8. Season with salt, finish with fresh cilantro, and serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Bone-in cuts give the broth much more depth and body than boneless meat alone.
  • Browning the meat well at the start builds essential flavor for the whole pot.
  • This soup is versatile - vegetables can be adjusted to whatever is seasonally available.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the browning step, missing an important layer of flavor.
  • Adding the vegetables too early, causing them to overcook and turn mushy by the time the meat is tender.
  • Rushing the simmer, which leaves the meat tough rather than properly tender.

Health Benefits

  • A good source of protein and collagen from the bone-in meat, with vitamins from the vegetables.

Cultural Note

Maraq is a foundational, everyday dish across Somalia, its broth often poured directly over canjeero at breakfast or paired with rice for a heartier meal.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; freezes well for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on the stovetop.

Serve with: Canjeero, Bariis Cad (plain rice), Sabaayad
