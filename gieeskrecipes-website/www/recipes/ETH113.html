🫘 Ethiopian Black-Eyed Pea Stew

ሽምብራ ዓይን ወጥ — Shimbra Ayin Wat

Ethiopian Black-Eyed Pea Stew — black-eyed peas (cowpeas) slow-cooked in a berbere and onion sauce. One of the more affordable fasting legume stews — black-eyed peas require less soaking time than chickpeas and provide a creamy, earthy texture in the berbere sauce.

Black-eyed pea stew is an important part of Ethiopia's fasting cuisine repertoire — cowpeas grow well in Ethiopian lowlands and provide an affordable, highly nutritious protein source for fasting periods.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot

  • 2 cups dried black-eyed peas — soaked 4 hours
  • 3 onions
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 3 tbsp berbere
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 3 tomatoes — diced
  • 2 cups water
  • 2 tsp salt

Method

  1. Boil soaked peas 35—40 minutes until tender.
  2. Dry caramelise onions 15 minutes.
  3. Add oil and berbere. Fry 3 minutes.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, tomatoes. Cook 5 minutes.
  5. Add cooked peas. Add water.
  6. Simmer 15 minutes. Season.

Chef Tips

  • Black-eyed peas require shorter soaking and cooking than chickpeas.
  • They should remain slightly firm — unlike lentils which dissolve.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-cooking to mush.

The Science

Black-eyed peas contain trypsin inhibitors — anti-nutritional factors that are deactivated by boiling. Pre-soaking and boiling removes most of these compounds, making the peas' protein fully bioavailable.

Health Benefits

  • High plant protein.
  • Very high in folate.
  • Good source of potassium.

Cultural Note

Black-eyed pea stew extends the fasting platter's legume variety — providing a different texture and flavour profile from lentils and chickpeas, ensuring that fasting days offer genuine food diversity.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate 5 days.

Reheating: Gently.

Serve with: Injera (ETH001), On the fasting platter
