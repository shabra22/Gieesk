🍲 Braised Matooke with Vegetables

Katogo

Twenty mature matooke fingers simmered an hour or more in unsalted water while eleven vegetables cook down separately for forty-five minutes, then combined and given a final half-hour together.

This is katogo built at the scale and ambition of a Sunday dish: twenty fingers of matooke and a vegetable list that runs to eleven items including garden eggs, courgette, celery and two bunches of dodo. The instruction that matters most is the first one — no salt in the matooke water. Salt firms the banana and stops it going soft. The two pans meet only at the end, and then cook together for a final thirty minutes, which is what makes it katogo rather than matooke with a side.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Second large pan, Wooden spoon

  • 20 very mature peeled fingers of matooke
  • 2 carrots, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 2 medium onions, sliced
  • 1 courgette, sliced
  • 250 g (1/4 kilo) fresh garden peas
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) black pepper
  • 1 celery stalk, chopped
  • 1 large green pepper, sliced
  • 10 garden eggs (entula), chopped
  • 5 medium tomatoes, chopped and pureed
  • 60 ml (4 tbsp) tomato paste
  • 2 bunches dodo, washed and chopped
  • 1 bay leaf
  • Vegetable oil
  • Salt, to taste

Listed in the source but never used in its method

  • 60 ml (4 tbsp) ghee
  • Cilantro sprigs, chopped

Used in the method but absent from the printed list

  • Stock or water

Method

    The matooke

    1. Put the peeled matooke fingers in a heavy saucepan, add water and bring to the boil. Do not add salt.
    2. Reduce the heat and simmer gently for at least 1 hour — longer is better.

    The vegetables

    1. Heat vegetable oil in a separate pan and fry the garlic and onions.
    2. Add the remaining vegetables along with salt and pepper.
    3. Add stock or water and simmer gently about 45 minutes over low heat.
    4. Correct the seasoning.

    Together

    1. Drain the matooke when it is done, reserving some of the water in case you need it.
    2. Add the vegetables to the drained matooke straight away and return the pan to the heat.
    3. Once it boils, reduce the heat immediately and cook a further 30 minutes.
    4. Serve hot.

    Chef Tips

    • No salt in the matooke water. Salt keeps banana firm, and this dish wants it soft enough to take up the vegetables.
    • At least an hour, and longer is better — that is the source's own phrasing and it is right.
    • Keep some of the matooke water back. If the combined pan looks dry in the last half hour, that is what goes in.
    • The final thirty minutes together is the dish. Matooke and vegetables served side by side is not katogo; they have to cook into each other.
    • Reduce the heat the moment it boils after combining. A hard boil breaks the banana up entirely.
    • The printed list includes 4 tablespoons of ghee and the method never says when to add them. Stirring them through at the very end is the sensible reading, and the source does not say so.
    • Cilantro is also listed and never used. Chopped over the top at serving is where it would go.
    • Very mature matooke, as specified. Younger fruit stays firm and the texture never comes right.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Salting the matooke water.
    • Boiling the matooke for twenty minutes.
    • Throwing away all the matooke water.
    • Serving the two pans separately.
    • Leaving it at a hard boil after combining.

    Health Benefits

    • Matooke provides resistant starch, potassium and vitamin B6.
    • Eleven vegetables in one pot make this unusually complete for a starch dish.
    • Garden peas and dodo add plant protein, iron and folate.
    • Contains dairy if you use the ghee.

    Cultural Note

    Katogo means a mixture, and it is defined by the one-pot cooking rather than by any particular ingredient — which is why this collection carries several and they share almost nothing. This is the vegetable one, written for a sixtieth-anniversary column about what Ugandans have actually been eating since independence.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of water.

    Serve with: Sauteed Garlic Dodo, Chapati, Avocado
