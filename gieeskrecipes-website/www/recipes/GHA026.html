🍆 Garden Egg Stew

Garden egg stew — abɔmu

A chef's version learnt from his grandmother — baking powder into the cooling water, dawadawa bloomed in palm oil, and the eggs mashed to a creamy paste.

This is the best-documented garden egg stew in print and it has two unusual moves. The garden eggs cook about twelve minutes until softened, then the pan comes off the heat, baking powder goes into the water, and they cool in it — which keeps them from browning and softens the skins. Separately, a hundred and fifty millilitres of red palm oil is heated with two teaspoons of dawadawa for three to four minutes before anything else, so the fermented locust bean blooms in the fat. Aniseed, nutmeg and black pepper follow, then blended aromatics and tomatoes for twelve minutes, then the mashed eggs and peanut butter.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, Blender, Heavy frying pan, Masher

  • 10 garden eggs, or 2 large aubergines (about 700 g)
  • 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • 150 ml red palm oil
  • 2 tsp dawadawa
  • 3 medium onions — 1 sliced, 2 roughly chopped
  • 1/2 tsp ground aniseed
  • 1/2 tsp ground nutmeg
  • 1/2 tsp ground black pepper
  • 6 garlic cloves
  • 20 g fresh ginger
  • 1/2 scotch bonnet chilli, deseeded
  • 400 g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 50 g smooth peanut butter
  • 1 tbsp all-purpose seasoning

To serve

  • 4 green (unripe) plantains
  • 1 avocado, sliced
  • 4 free-range eggs, boiled

Method

  1. Cook the garden eggs around 12 minutes, or until softened.
  2. Remove from the heat, add the baking powder to the water, and leave them to cool in it.
  3. Heat the palm oil with the dawadawa for 3 to 4 minutes.
  4. Add the sliced onion and the aniseed, nutmeg and black pepper.
  5. Blend the chopped onions, garlic, ginger and scotch bonnet and add.
  6. Add the blended tomatoes and cook a further 12 minutes.
  7. Boil the plantains 20 minutes.
  8. Mash the garden eggs to a creamy, paste-like consistency, add to the pan and simmer another 3 minutes.
  9. Add the peanut butter and all-purpose seasoning and cook about 10 minutes.
  10. Serve with the boiled plantain, sliced avocado and boiled eggs.

Chef Tips

  • Baking powder into the cooking water off the heat, then cool the garden eggs in it. It is the one trick in this recipe you will not find elsewhere, and it keeps them pale and soft-skinned.
  • Bloom the dawadawa in the palm oil first, three to four minutes, before anything else. Fermented locust bean needs fat and heat to release what it has.
  • Creamy, paste-like consistency is the mashing cue. Lumps of garden egg skin in the finished stew are the usual fault.
  • Aniseed and nutmeg together are the Ghanaian aromatic signature here, not an eccentricity — half a teaspoon of each.
  • Fifty grams of peanut butter is a small amount and it is there for body rather than flavour. It goes in near the end with the seasoning.
  • Green unripe plantain, not ripe. The starch and faint bitterness are what the rich stew needs against it.
  • Garden eggs are small pale Ghanaian aubergines and they are more bitter than a large purple one. Two large aubergines at about 700 g is the author's own substitution.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the baking powder and the cool-in-the-water step.
  • Adding the dawadawa with the tomatoes rather than blooming it first.
  • Under-mashing, leaving skin in pieces.
  • Ripe plantain instead of green.
  • Adding the peanut butter at the start, where it will catch.

Health Benefits

  • Garden eggs are high in fibre, folate and polyphenols.
  • Red palm oil supplies provitamin-A carotenoids and vitamin E tocotrienols.
  • Dawadawa is a fermented food with documented B-vitamin content.
  • Vegetarian as written, with complete protein from the eggs and peanut.

Cultural Note

A chef publishing his grandmother's garden egg stew in a British magazine, complete with the baking powder trick and the dawadawa bloomed in palm oil, is the best kind of documentation — the technique survives intact even as the brand names change. Garden egg is one of the few vegetables with no good substitute: the bitterness is the dish.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.

Reheating: Gentle heat. The palm oil re-melts and rises.

Serve with: Boiled green plantain, Sliced avocado, Boiled eggs
