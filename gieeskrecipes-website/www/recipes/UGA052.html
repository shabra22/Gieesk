🥬 Sauteed Spicy Sukuma Wiki

Sukuma wiki

Collard greens folded into ginger, onion, tomato and chilli oil and cooked just five to seven minutes, until they turn bright green and still have a crunch.

Sukuma wiki means to push the week — collard greens are what stretches a household through to the next payday, and the plant is a survivor that comes back green after months of drought and a week of rain. The cooking is fast and the cue is colour: bright green, five to seven minutes, still with a crunchy raw feel if you like it that way. If the tomatoes make it too sour, a sprinkle of sugar is the fix, and that is a real Ugandan kitchen habit rather than a modern adjustment.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pan, Wooden spoon

  • 2 or more bunches sukuma wiki (collard greens), thinly cut
  • 2 large tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 large onion, thinly chopped
  • Ginger, grated
  • Chilli oil
  • Oil
  • Salt

If needed

  • A sprinkle of sugar

To garnish

  • Thinly sliced onion, crushed nuts or simsim

Method

  1. Thinly cut the sukuma wiki and set it aside.
  2. Put a pan on high heat with oil and let it get hot.
  3. Add the ginger and onions and let them fry until almost golden.
  4. Add the tomatoes, chilli oil and salt and stir. Let the tomatoes cook until tender.
  5. Add the sukuma wiki and fold it in well.
  6. Let it cook 5 to 7 minutes, or until it turns a bright green colour.
  7. Take it off the heat.
  8. Garnish with thinly sliced onion and crushed nuts or simsim, and serve with posho.

Chef Tips

  • Bright green is the doneness cue. Past that you get the grey, collapsed greens nobody wants.
  • Five to seven minutes. If you want it more tender, cook it a little longer — but the crunchy raw feel is the preferred texture.
  • Tomatoes add a tangy sourness. To reduce it, sprinkle in some sugar. That is the standard fix and it works.
  • Cut the greens thin. Thick ribbons need longer than the tomatoes can wait.
  • Fold them in rather than stirring hard. You want them coated, not bruised.
  • Sukuma wiki is mostly served alongside posho, and any East African will tell you the same.
  • Crushed nuts or simsim on top is not decoration — it adds the protein and fat the dish otherwise lacks.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overcooking past bright green.
  • Cutting the greens too thick.
  • Adding them before the tomatoes are tender.
  • Ignoring the sourness rather than adding a little sugar.
  • Stirring hard enough to break them up.

Health Benefits

  • Collard greens are very high in vitamins K, A and C, plus calcium and folate.
  • Short cooking preserves far more of the vitamin C than a long braise.
  • Simsim or nuts on top add calcium, protein and healthy fats.
  • Low in calories and high in fibre.

Cultural Note

The name is the whole story — sukuma wiki pushes the week, because it is cheap, it grows fast and it keeps coming back. It is the most-eaten green in East Africa for exactly that reason, and it is served with posho so consistently that the pairing is almost a single dish.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm briefly — it will lose the crunch.

Serve with: Posho, Classic Bean Stew, Matooke
