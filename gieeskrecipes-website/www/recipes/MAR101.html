🥘 Dafina (Shabbat Slow-Cooked Stew)

Dafina / skhina / hamin — Shabbat stew (דפינה)

Short ribs, chicken, potatoes, chickpeas, eggs in their shells and dates cooked together in a slow cooker for 24 hours in a Shabbat tradition.

1d 1hTotal time

Chickpeas go on the bottom of the slow cooker, potatoes are set around the walls, and the meat, chicken, whole eggs and dates sit in the centre. Water is poured over until its top is about 1/4 inch above the ingredients, and the cooker is set on medium for 24 hours. The page notes that Sephardic tradition is not to add any water, even boiling, to the cooker on Shabbat.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large slow cooker (crockpot)

  • 2 lbs flanken meat, on the bone (flanken is short ribs cut across the bones)
  • 4 pieces of chicken, on the bone
  • 12 large red potatoes, peeled
  • 2 cans of chickpeas, rinsed
  • 4 eggs (in the shell)
  • 4 pitted dates
  • 1 tbsp salt
  • 1 tsp pepper
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp of honey
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 3-4 garlic cloves
  • 2 Tbsp of olive oil

Method

  1. Arrange the chickpeas on the bottom of the slow cooker.
  2. Add the potatoes around the interior walls.
  3. Place the meat, chicken, eggs and pitted dates in the centre, with the seasonings, honey and garlic.
  4. Pour in enough water to cover everything; the top of the water should hit around 1/4 inch above the ingredients.
  5. Set the cooker at a medium temperature and cook for 24 hours.

Chef Tips

  • The 24 hours is NO CUE, TIME ONLY; the page calls the cooked egg golden brown.
  • The olive oil is listed in the ingredients but not used in the directions on the page.
  • Keep the slow cooker closed on Shabbat, per the Sephardic tradition the page mentions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding water mid-cook on Shabbat, against the tradition the page notes.
  • Using too little liquid, so the stew dries out.

Health Benefits

  • Chickpeas and potatoes add fibre and carbohydrate; the meats add protein.

Cultural Note

The page calls dafina an iconic slow-cooked Moroccan stew served especially on Shabbat, with a long history in which no two are the same.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate leftovers promptly and reheat until steaming hot.

Reheating: Reheat thoroughly before serving.
