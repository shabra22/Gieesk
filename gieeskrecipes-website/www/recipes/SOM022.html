🥣 Soor iyo Maraq

Soor iyo Maraq

Stiff white corn grits mounded in a dish with a well pressed into the centre and a spiced beef stew spooned in — the most widely eaten staple in the country, and in the south the definition of a proper meal.

Soor goes by several names depending on where you are — gashiisho, shuuro, malalaf — and is close kin to the ugali eaten across East Africa, though the Somali version is served with a deliberate hollow in the middle. The stew is what changes: beef and potato here, but goat, vegetables or okra all belong in that well. Along parts of the coast the grits are cooked with coconut milk in place of half the water, which makes them richer and faintly sweet.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pot with a lid, Deep saucepan, Sturdy wooden spoon

    For the beef stew (maraq)

    • 500 g (1 lb) beef, cut into 2 cm cubes
    • 1 onion, chopped
    • 4 cloves garlic, chopped
    • 2 tomatoes, chopped
    • 2 potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
    • 1 tbsp tomato paste
    • 1 tsp xawaash spice blend
    • 1 green pepper, sliced
    • 120 ml (1/2 cup) vegetable oil
    • 480 ml (2 cups) water
    • Salt, to taste

    For the grits (soor)

    • 1 cup coarse white corn grits
    • 1.2 L (5 cups) water
    • 1 tsp salt

    Method

    1. Heat the oil in a heavy pot and soften the onion and garlic over medium heat.
    2. Stir in the xawaash and cook for about a minute, until it smells fragrant, then add the beef and stir to coat every piece.
    3. Cover and cook for 5 minutes. With a tougher cut, give it longer here — the stew is finished when the meat gives, not when the clock says so.
    4. Add the tomatoes, potatoes, tomato paste and water. Cover and simmer for 10 minutes, or until the potatoes are tender and the meat is done.
    5. Add the sliced green pepper 3 minutes before the end, so it keeps some bite. Season with salt.
    6. For the grits, bring the water to a boil in a deep saucepan and add the salt.
    7. Pour in the grits slowly in a thin stream, stirring hard the whole time so no lumps form.
    8. Cover and cook for 15 minutes over medium heat, stirring well every 5 minutes.
    9. Turn off the heat and let the grits stand, covered, for 5 minutes to firm up.
    10. Mound the grits in a deep dish, press a well into the centre with the back of a wet spoon, and ladle the stew into it. Serve with a banana alongside.

    Chef Tips

    • Use coarse-texture grits, not instant. Fine grits cook faster and need less time than the 15 minutes here, and instant grits will not hold the mound.
    • The banana is not a garnish. Its sweetness is there to balance the salt in the stew and the acidity of the tomatoes, and Somali cooks consider the plate incomplete without it.
    • Substituting coconut milk for half the water gives the coastal version — richer, slightly sweet, and very good with fish stew instead of beef.
    • Leftover soor is eaten a second way: if you have both stew and milk, finish the stew first, then pour milk and sugar over what grits remain.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Tipping the grits into the water all at once, which sets lumps instantly and cannot be fixed by stirring afterwards.
    • Leaving the pot unstirred for the full 15 minutes, so the bottom catches and the whole dish tastes scorched.
    • Serving the grits straight off the heat before the 5-minute rest, when they are still loose and will not hold a well.
    • Cooking a tough cut of beef for only the 10 minutes in the method — the times assume a tender cut and a stringy one needs far longer.

    Health Benefits

    • Whole corn grits provide slow-release carbohydrate and some fibre.
    • A good source of protein and iron from the beef.
    • Naturally gluten-free, as long as the grits are not processed alongside wheat.

    Cultural Note

    Soor is the most common staple food in Somalia and is eaten across the whole country, though it is most closely identified with the south and the riverine farming regions where maize grows. The well in the middle is the point of the dish — it keeps the stew pooled where the fingers of the right hand can reach it.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Keep the stew and the grits in separate containers, refrigerated up to 3 days. Grits stiffen hard when cold.

    Reheating: Reheat the stew gently on the hob. Loosen cold grits with a splash of water over low heat, stirring until soft again.

    Serve with: Basbaas, Fresh banana, Shaah Cadays
