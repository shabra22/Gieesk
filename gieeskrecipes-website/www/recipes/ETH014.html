🥣 Ethiopian Thin Porridge

አጥሚት — Atmit

Ethiopian Atmit — a thin, pourable barley or teff porridge sweetened with honey and flavoured with cinnamon and a touch of spice. Given to the sick, to new mothers, to athletes, and during fasting — Ethiopia's healing drink-porridge of restorative nutrition.

Atmit occupies the space between a porridge and a drink — thin enough to pour, thick enough to be nourishing. Made from roasted and ground barley or teff, sweetened with honey, and spiced gently, it is given to those who are recovering, those who are fasting, and those who have been working hard. It is the Ethiopian equivalent of restorative broth.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Whisk

  • 1 cup roasted barley flour or teff flour
  • 4 cups water
  • 2 tbsp honey
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ¼ tsp ground ginger
  • Pinch of ground cloves
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Whisk roasted barley flour into 1 cup cold water until smooth.
  2. Bring remaining 3 cups water to a simmer.
  3. Pour flour mixture into simmering water, whisking continuously.
  4. Simmer 12—15 minutes, stirring, until it coats the back of a spoon (thin porridge consistency).
  5. Remove from heat. Add honey, cinnamon, ginger, and cloves.
  6. Serve warm in cups or small bowls.

Chef Tips

  • Roasted barley flour gives a deeper, more complex flavour than raw.
  • The consistency should be pourable — thinner than oatmeal.
  • Honey is added off the heat — never boil honey.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too thick — should pour.
  • Boiling the honey.

The Science

Roasting barley before grinding converts some of the starch through Maillard reactions — producing complex aromatic compounds that give roasted barley flour its characteristic malty, slightly caramel flavour. This is the same principle as making coffee or roasting cocoa beans.

Health Benefits

  • Barley beta-glucan supports immune function.
  • Honey provides antimicrobial compounds.
  • Ginger and cinnamon are anti-inflammatory.
  • Highly digestible — perfect for recovery.

Cultural Note

Atmit is the food of Ethiopian care — when someone is sick, the family makes atmit. When a woman has just given birth, the community makes atmit for 40 days. When an athlete trains hard in the highlands, atmit is drunk before dawn. It is nourishment as love.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best consumed immediately.

Reheating: Gently with added water.

Serve with: As a standalone healing drink, With honey on the side, During Ethiopian Orthodox fasting periods
