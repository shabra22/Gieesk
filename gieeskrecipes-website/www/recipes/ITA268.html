Pasta alla Trapanese

Trapani's own uncooked pesto, blending ripe tomatoes, almonds, garlic, and basil into a vibrant, no-cook sauce that predates and likely inspired Genoese pesto through historic maritime trade.

Sicilian trade with Liguria is thought to have carried this almond-based sauce north, where it may have influenced the development of Genoese basil pesto, though Trapanese pesto's addition of ripe tomatoes and almonds instead of pine nuts gives it a character distinctly its own.

Ingredients

Equipment: Food processor or mortar and pestle, Large pot

  • 400 g (14 oz) busiate or fusilli pasta
  • 500 g (1.1 lb) ripe tomatoes, halved
  • 100 g (3/4 cup) blanched almonds
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • A large handful fresh basil
  • 80 ml (1/3 cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • 60 g (3/4 cup) grated pecorino, plus more for serving
  • Salt and chili flakes, to taste

Method

  1. Blend the tomatoes, almonds, garlic, and basil in a food processor until you have a coarse, textured sauce.
  2. With the processor running, drizzle in the olive oil until the sauce comes together.
  3. Stir in the pecorino, and season with salt and chili flakes to taste.
  4. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the pasta until al dente.
  5. Drain the pasta, reserving a little cooking water, and toss with the raw pesto, adding a splash of water to loosen if needed.
  6. Serve immediately with extra pecorino at the table.

Chef Tips

  • This sauce is entirely uncooked - the pasta residual heat is enough to warm it through when tossed together.
  • A slightly coarse, rustic texture is traditional, rather than blending the sauce completely smooth.
  • Ripe, flavorful tomatoes make a genuine difference in this simple, ingredient-driven sauce.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-blending the sauce until completely smooth, losing its traditional rustic texture.
  • Using underripe, bland tomatoes, which this simple sauce depends on entirely for flavor.
  • Serving it cold rather than tossed with hot pasta, which is what gently warms the sauce through.

Health Benefits

  • Almonds provide healthy fats and vitamin E; tomatoes contribute lycopene and vitamin C.

Cultural Note

Pasta alla Trapanese reflects Sicily's historic maritime trade with Liguria, and many food historians believe this almond-tomato sauce may have influenced the later development of Genoese basil pesto.

Storage & Serving

Storage: The sauce keeps refrigerated for up to 2 days.

Reheating: Not recommended; this sauce is meant to be served fresh and uncooked.

Serve with: A chilled Sicilian white wine
