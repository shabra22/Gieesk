🍖 Kenyan Lamb Rack

Mfupa wa Kondoo

Kenyan Lamb Rack — a frenched rack of lamb crusted with Kenyan herbs, pan-seared to a perfect crust, and oven-roasted to a blushing medium-rare. The premium fine dining protein — elegant, precise, and spectacular on the plate.

Lamb rack is the hallmark of a confident kitchen — it requires precise timing, cannot be rushed, and reveals the cook's skill immediately on the plate. At Nairobi's finest hotels, a properly executed lamb rack is a statement dish.

Ingredients

Equipment: Oven-safe pan, Oven (200°C), Meat thermometer, Food processor for crust

  • 1 frenched rack of lamb (8 cutlets, ~700g)
  • 2 tsp coarse salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper

Herb Crust

  • 1 cup fresh coriander and parsley — mixed
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp breadcrumbs
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard

Sauce

  • ½ cup red wine or stock
  • ½ cup beef stock
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Process herbs, garlic, breadcrumbs, and oil into a coarse paste.
  2. Season lamb with salt and pepper.
  3. Sear fat-side down in a very hot pan for 4 minutes until deeply golden.
  4. Sear the other sides briefly.
  5. Remove from heat. Brush with Dijon mustard. Press herb crust firmly onto the fat cap.
  6. Roast at 200°C for 10—12 minutes until internal temperature reaches 55—57°C (medium-rare).
  7. Rest 8 minutes — the temperature will rise to 60°C.
  8. Make jus: deglaze pan with wine and stock. Reduce by half. Whisk in butter.
  9. Slice into double cutlets. Serve with jus.

Chef Tips

  • A thermometer is essential — lamb rack is too expensive to overcook.
  • The rest period is as important as the cook — it redistributes juices throughout the meat.
  • French the bones cleanly — they are part of the visual impact of this dish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • No thermometer — guessing with expensive lamb is too risky.
  • Insufficient resting.
  • Overcooking — well-done lamb rack is a tragedy.

The Science

Lamb fat melts at a higher temperature than chicken or pork fat (around 43°C vs 35—36°C) — this is why searing the fat cap first is more important with lamb. The rendered fat bastes the meat as it drips. At 55—57°C, the muscle proteins are just set — the meat is tender and blush-pink. Beyond 70°C, the proteins over-contract and the meat becomes dry and grey.

Cultural Note

Lamb rack represents Kenya's hotel industry reaching for international fine dining standards — a dish that competes with any served in London, Paris, or New York, using Kenya's own quality lamb.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Warm gently in oven — never microwave.
