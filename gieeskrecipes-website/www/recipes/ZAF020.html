🍛 Chicken Akni

Akni — akhni

Chicken marinated in buttermilk and masala, then cooked into a single pot of basmati with whole spice, potato and green chilli — the Cape Muslim one-pot, and a Ramadan fixture.

Akni is what a Cape Muslim household cooks when there will be visitors: one pot, generous, and enough left over to send home in a barakat, the parcel of food guests leave with. Unlike a breyani it is not layered — the rice goes in with everything else and finishes in the same pot. The buttermilk marinade is what keeps chicken tender through ninety minutes of cooking, and plain yoghurt does the same job.

Ingredients

Equipment: Very large saucepan with a tight lid, Mixing bowl

    The marinade

    • 1.5 kg free-range chicken pieces
    • 180 ml (3/4 cup) buttermilk, or plain yoghurt
    • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
    • 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
    • 1 tsp masala
    • 1/2 tsp salt

    The whole spices

    • 2 tbsp oil
    • 2 star anise
    • 1 cinnamon stick
    • 2 bay leaves
    • 6 cardamom pods
    • 1 tsp cumin seeds
    • 1 tsp fennel seeds
    • 1 allspice berry
    • 8 curry leaves
    • 1 tsp crushed chilli

    The pot

    • 2 onions, chopped
    • 1 jalapeno, chopped (optional)
    • 1 green pepper, chopped
    • 8 cloves garlic, minced
    • 2 heaped tbsp fresh ginger, grated
    • 2 tsp salt
    • 2 tsp ground turmeric
    • 4 tbsp masala
    • 4 to 5 potatoes, peeled and halved
    • 600 g basmati rice, rinsed
    • Fresh coriander, to finish
    • 1.4 L (6 cups) water, added in stages

    Method

    1. Mix the chicken with the buttermilk, garlic, ginger, masala and salt, and marinate for 2 hours or overnight.
    2. Heat the oil in a large saucepan and add the star anise, cinnamon stick, bay leaves, cardamom, cumin, fennel, allspice, curry leaves and crushed chilli. Let them sizzle until fragrant.
    3. Add the onions, jalapeno and green pepper and fry until golden brown.
    4. Add the garlic, ginger and 1/2 cup of water and cook for 5 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
    5. Add the salt, turmeric, masala and another 1/2 cup of water, and cook over medium heat for 6 minutes.
    6. Add the marinated chicken and cook for 25 minutes.
    7. Add the potatoes and 2 cups of water and cook until the potatoes are soft.
    8. Add the rinsed rice and 3 cups of water. Stir once, cover, and cook over medium heat until the water is fully absorbed and the rice has tripled in size.
    9. Scatter over fresh coriander and serve with slaai.

    Chef Tips

    • Marinate overnight if you can. Buttermilk works on the chicken slowly, and two hours is the minimum rather than the target.
    • Bloom the whole spices in the oil before anything else goes in. That first minute is where most of the aroma of the finished pot comes from.
    • Stir the rice once and then leave it alone. Repeated stirring releases starch and the pot turns claggy.
    • For the slaai that goes alongside: a chopped onion, a chopped tomato, fresh coriander, salt and pepper, a cup of brown vinegar and 120 g sugar, mixed and left to stand an hour.
    • Plain yoghurt substitutes for buttermilk with no other change.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Skipping the marinade, which leaves the chicken dry after ninety minutes in the pot.
    • Adding the ground spices at the same time as the whole ones, so the ground masala burns while the pods are still opening up.
    • Stirring after the rice goes in, which is how a one-pot rice dish turns to porridge.
    • Adding the rice before the potatoes are soft — the rice will be done long before they are.

    Health Benefits

    • Chicken provides lean protein, niacin and selenium.
    • Buttermilk contributes calcium and tenderises without added fat.
    • A complete meal in one pot, with potato, pepper and onion built in.

    Cultural Note

    Akni is Ramadan and visitor food, and the barakat — the parcel of blessings that guests take home — is part of the reason it is cooked in quantity. The term comes from Arabic, like boeka for the meal that breaks the fast and sajie for the catering of a communal event, and these words are ordinary domestic vocabulary in the Cape rather than religious formality.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days.

    Reheating: Sprinkle with water, cover and steam over low heat or in a low oven.

    Serve with: Slaai (onion and tomato in sweetened vinegar), Plain yoghurt, Dhaltjies
