🫘 Ethiopian Shiro Powder

ሽሮ ዱቄት — Shiro Duqet

Ethiopian Homemade Shiro Powder — roasted chickpeas, dried onion, garlic powder, berbere, and ground spices ground together into the pre-seasoned flour used for Shiro Wat. Making shiro powder at home produces a dramatically superior result to commercial versions.

Homemade shiro powder is a revelation — where commercial shiro is often bland and one-dimensional, home-made shiro from freshly roasted chickpeas with a custom berbere blend produces a Shiro Wat of extraordinary depth.

Ingredients

Equipment: Dry pan, Grain mill or high-powered blender, Airtight jar

  • 2 cups dried chickpeas (or yellow split peas)

Spice blend

  • 3 tbsp berbere
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 2 tsp onion powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp ground ginger
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • ½ tsp salt

Method

  1. Dry roast chickpeas in a heavy pan over medium heat for 18—20 minutes, stirring constantly, until deeply golden and nutty-smelling.
  2. Cool completely.
  3. Grind roasted chickpeas to a fine powder.
  4. Add berbere, garlic powder, onion powder, turmeric, ginger, cardamom, and salt.
  5. Grind together until uniformly fine.
  6. Store in an airtight jar.

Chef Tips

  • Roast chickpeas very thoroughly — light roasting produces a starchy, flat shiro.
  • The grinding must be fine — coarse shiro produces a grainy Shiro Wat.
  • Home-made shiro powder makes dramatically better Shiro Wat than commercial powder.
  • Yellow split peas can substitute for chickpeas — slightly different flavour.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Light roasting — insufficient depth of flavour.
  • Coarse grinding.

The Science

See ETH102 for shiro cooking science. Home-made shiro retains the volatile aromatic compounds from freshly roasted chickpeas — these begin dissipating immediately after grinding. Commercial shiro, ground weeks earlier, has lost most of these volatile aromatics, which is why home-made is dramatically superior.

Cultural Note

Home-made shiro powder is the mark of the committed Ethiopian cook — the person who takes the time to roast and grind their own chickpeas produces Shiro Wat at a quality level that commercial shiro cannot match. Ethiopian grandmothers make their own shiro powder the same way their grandmothers did.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Airtight jar — 6 months. Freeze for longer.

Reheating: Not applicable.

Serve with: Used to make Shiro Wat (ETH102), Stored as a pantry staple for fasting periods
