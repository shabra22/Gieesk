🍯 Ngalakh, Millet Couscous with Baobab and Peanut Sauce (Daba)

Ngalakh / neteri

Millet couscous made from scratch — flour rubbed with water into granules, rested 15 minutes and steamed — mixed with butter, sugar, nutmeg and coconut and served with a chilled sauce of baobab fruit, peanut paste and 60 cl of water.

Ngalakh is the Easter dessert of Senegal, shared with neighbours of every faith and eaten at Korité. This version from the blog ‘La cuisine de Daba’ (24 February 2015) makes the millet couscous from scratch: millet flour is put in a bowl and water added gradually while the cook rubs and kneads ‘until it forms granules’, then rests 15 minutes. The granules are steamed in a couscoussier over a cloth ‘as soon as it is cooked’, tipped into a bowl, mixed with a knob of butter and a little sugar, cooled, and finished with grated nutmeg and coconut shavings. For the juice, 4 tablespoons of bouye (baobab fruit) and 2 tablespoons of peanut paste are mixed with 60 cl of water until ‘a concentrated juice’ forms; thickness is adjusted if it looks too thin or too thick; it is strained through a fine sieve, sweetened with sugar to taste, nutmeg and vanilla, and chilled. The juice and couscous are combined just before serving. THE PAGE DOES NOT PRINT how much millet flour to use, how long to steam, or how long to chill.

Ingredients

Equipment: Couscoussier and cloth, Fine sieve (chinois), Mixing bowls

    Couscous

    • Millet flour — quantity not printed
    • Water, added gradually
    • 1 knob of butter
    • A little sugar
    • Coconut shavings
    • 1 nutmeg, grated

    Baobab juice (neteri)

    • 4 tablespoons bouye (baobab fruit)
    • 2 tablespoons peanut paste (pâte d’arachide)
    • 60 cl water
    • Sugar, to your taste
    • Vanilla; the author uses vanilla-flavoured cane sugar
    • Nutmeg

    Method

      Couscous

      1. Put the millet flour in a bowl and add water gradually while rubbing and kneading until it forms granules. Rest 15 minutes.
      2. Boil water in a couscoussier, lay a cloth over the steamer section, pour in the granules, cover and cook gently. No steaming time is printed; the only cue is ‘as soon as it is cooked’.
      3. Tip into a bowl, add the butter, mix, add a little sugar and cool. Grate nutmeg over and add the coconut shavings.

      Juice

      1. Combine the baobab and peanut paste, add the 60 cl of water and mix until you have a concentrated juice. If it looks too thin or too thick, adjust. Strain through a fine sieve, then add sugar, nutmeg and vanilla. Chill.
      2. Combine the juice and couscous just before serving. No chilling time is printed.

      Chef Tips

      • Printed cues: knead ‘until it forms granules’; the juice should be ‘concentrated’; adjust if ‘too thin or too thick’.
      • The steaming step has no cue beyond ‘as soon as it is cooked’, which is circular.
      • The author notes ready-made millet couscous can be bought in some grocery stores, and prefers vanilla-flavoured cane sugar.

      Common Mistakes to Avoid

      • Mixing couscous and juice early; the couscous goes soggy.
      • Skipping the straining.
      • Using the second recipe’s ratios with this page’s quantities: the two disagree.

      Health Benefits

      Cultural Note

      Recipes for ngalakh vary by family and generation, sometimes using rice or corn semolina or adding fruit, according to Aïssatou Mbaye. This one’s ratio — 4 tbsp bouye and 2 tbsp peanut paste to 60 cl water — differs substantially from the peko-peko version; the two are not averaged.

      Storage & Serving

      Storage: No storage time is printed.

      Reheating: Not applicable — served cold.

      Serve with: Chilled neteri sauce, Sweet tea
