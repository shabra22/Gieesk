🍞 Bunny Chow

Bunny chow — a bunny, a quarter

Lamb curry with big chunks of potato, spooned into a hollowed quarter of a white loaf with the scooped-out bread set on top as a lid — Durban's most famous export, and it was portable for a reason.

A bunny is a quarter of a white loaf with the middle taken out and curry poured in, and the bread is the plate, the cutlery and the last mouthful. It comes from Durban and it is portable because it had to be: under apartheid Black customers were barred from most food establishments, and a meal you carried away in bread was a way around that. The etymology of the name is genuinely contested — the most credible suggestion traces it to bania, a Gujarati merchant caste, and the tidy bun-plus-achar story is generally regarded as too neat to be true.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy saucepan with a lid, Sharp knife, for hollowing the bread

    The curry

    • 60 ml sunflower oil
    • 1 onion, finely chopped
    • 2 cloves garlic, crushed
    • 3 cm piece fresh ginger, grated
    • 1 red chilli, finely chopped
    • 750 g deboned leg of lamb, cubed
    • 1 tsp ground coriander
    • 1 tsp ground cumin
    • 2 tsp ground turmeric
    • 1 tsp paprika
    • 2 tbsp garam masala
    • 1 cinnamon stick
    • 2 star anise (optional)
    • 1 x 410 g tin chopped tomatoes
    • 2 tbsp sugar
    • Salt, to taste
    • 750 ml water, or enough to just cover the lamb
    • 4 large potatoes, peeled and cut into large chunks

    The bunny

    • 1 unsliced white loaf, cut into quarters

    Method

    1. Heat the oil in a large saucepan and fry the onion, garlic, ginger and chilli until soft.
    2. Add the cubed lamb and brown it for 5 minutes.
    3. Add all the ground spices and stir to coat the lamb thoroughly.
    4. Add the cinnamon stick and the star anise if using, and stir-fry for 2 minutes.
    5. Add the tinned tomatoes and the sugar and stir to coat.
    6. Add just enough water to barely cover the lamb — not the full amount unless you need it.
    7. Cover and cook for 45 minutes, stirring occasionally, then season with salt.
    8. Add the potato chunks, topping up with water if needed, and cook a further 15 to 20 minutes, until the potatoes are done and the lamb is soft.
    9. Fish out the cinnamon stick and the star anise.
    10. Cut the loaf into quarters. Hollow each one out from the cut face, leaving a good base — do not scoop all the way through, or the gravy runs straight out of the bottom.
    11. Spoon the curry into the hollow and set the scooped-out chunk of bread on top as a lid.

    Chef Tips

    • Leave a base in the bread. This is the one structural rule of a bunny chow and it is the one people get wrong the first time.
    • The potatoes go in at forty-five minutes, not at the start. Their job is to soak up gravy while holding their shape — which is why Durban cooks call them gravy soakers.
    • Big chunks of potato, not dice. They are a component, not a thickener.
    • The bread should be a day old if possible. Fresh bread tears while you hollow it and goes soggy faster.
    • A quarter loaf feeds one person and a half loaf feeds two — the order in Durban is by size, and a quarter is the standard.
    • For the vegetarian version, which is older than the lamb one, use sugar beans and sweet potato with the same spicing.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Scooping the bread all the way through the base.
    • Adding all 750 ml of water at once, which gives a thin curry that soaks the bread instead of sitting in it.
    • Putting the potatoes in with the lamb, so they collapse into the gravy over an hour.
    • Assembling it ahead. The bread has minutes, not hours, before it gives way.
    • Forgetting the salt — it is easy to miss when the spice list is this long.

    Health Benefits

    • Lamb provides protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
    • Potato contributes potassium and vitamin C.
    • A genuinely complete meal in one item, which is most of why it exists.

    Cultural Note

    Durban has the largest Indian population of any city outside India, descended from around 150,000 indentured labourers brought between 1860 and 1911, mostly Tamil and Telugu speakers, for the sugar plantations and railways. The bunny chow belongs to that community and became a dish of the whole city. Whether Soweto's kota or Durban's bunny came first is actively argued and nobody has proof either way.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Refrigerate the curry up to 3 days. Never store it assembled.

    Reheating: Reheat the curry on the hob and hollow a fresh quarter loaf.

    Serve with: Carrot salad or ushatini, Fresh coriander, Nothing else — it is a complete meal in bread
