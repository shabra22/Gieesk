🍳 Rafanata Lucana

Rafanata

A rustic Basilicata frittata built on grated fresh horseradish root and potato, its sharp, pungent bite a distinctive feature of this simple, satisfying peasant dish.

Rafanata takes its name directly from rafano (horseradish), whose sharp bite is mellowed somewhat by cooking but still gives this humble egg dish a distinctive character unlike an ordinary frittata found elsewhere in Italy.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large non-stick skillet, Plate for flipping

  • 2 medium potatoes, boiled and grated
  • 60 g (⅓ cup) fresh horseradish root, peeled and finely grated
  • 6 eggs
  • 80 g (1 cup) grated Pecorino cheese
  • 60 ml (¼ cup) extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Combine the grated boiled potato with the grated horseradish.
  2. Beat the eggs with the pecorino, salt, and pepper.
  3. Stir the potato and horseradish mixture into the beaten eggs.
  4. Warm the olive oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat.
  5. Pour in the egg mixture, spreading evenly, and cook for 6–8 minutes until the bottom is set and golden.
  6. Carefully flip using a plate, then slide back into the pan to cook the other side for another 5–6 minutes.
  7. Slide onto a board, let rest briefly, then slice into wedges and serve warm.

Chef Tips

  • Fresh horseradish root, grated just before use, gives the most pungent, authentic flavor — jarred, prepared horseradish is a milder substitute if needed.
  • A good non-stick pan makes the flipping process much easier.
  • This dish is traditionally enjoyed warm rather than piping hot, letting the horseradish flavor come through clearly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using too little horseradish, missing the dish's defining sharp character.
  • Flipping before the bottom has properly set, resulting in a messy transfer.
  • Cooking over heat that's too high, browning the outside before the center sets.

Cultural Note

Rafanata is a humble, distinctive dish of Basilicata, its pungent fresh horseradish setting it apart from ordinary frittate found elsewhere in Italy.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days; excellent eaten cold or at room temperature.

Reheating: Reheat gently in a dry skillet, or enjoy at room temperature.

Serve with: Crusty bread, A simple green salad
