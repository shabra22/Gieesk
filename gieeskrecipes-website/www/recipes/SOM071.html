🥑 Casiir Afokaado

Casiir Afokaado — Casiir Qare

Ripe avocado blended with milk and sugar until it is thick, cold and buttery — the Somali juice-bar standard, and a milkshake that gets its richness from fruit instead of cream.

Avocado grows in Somalia but for a long time most Somalis had little to do with it, and the avocado shake arrived as a juice-bar drink rather than a home tradition. It earns its place: blended with cold milk it comes out thick and buttery with none of the heaviness of an ice-cream shake. Two versions are in circulation — the plain one, which is avocado, milk and sugar and nothing else, and the pineapple and coconut one, which is a more elaborate proposition.

Ingredients

Equipment: Blender

    The plain version

    • 1 ripe avocado (about 160 g)
    • 100 g (1/2 cup) granulated sugar, or honey, to taste
    • 1.25 litres (5 cups) milk — any fat level
    • 8 ice cubes

    The pineapple and coconut version

    • 250 g (1 cup) pineapple chunks, very ripe or canned
    • 1 ripe avocado (about 160 g)
    • 150 g (3/4 cup) granulated sugar, or honey, to taste
    • 474 ml (2 cups) light coconut milk
    • 474 ml (2 cups) milk
    • 8 ice cubes

    Method

    1. Halve the avocado, remove the stone and scoop the flesh into the blender.
    2. Add the remaining ingredients for whichever version you are making.
    3. Blend until completely smooth.
    4. Serve cold, straight away.

    Chef Tips

    • The avocado has to be properly ripe — it should yield gently to thumb pressure. An underripe one gives a thin, grassy shake and no amount of sugar hides it.
    • To ripen a hard avocado, put it in a paper bag with an apple or a banana for a day or two.
    • Use very ripe or canned pineapple in the second version. Underripe fresh pineapple turns the whole thing bitter.
    • Blend and serve. Avocado starts to darken within the hour, so this is not a make-ahead drink.
    • The published source gives 3/4 cup and 1/2 cup of sugar both as 150 g, which cannot both be right. Take 3/4 cup as about 150 g and 1/2 cup as about 100 g.
    • A spoonful of strawberry jam in the bottom of the glass before you pour is a documented Somali juice-bar trick, and a dusting of ground cardamom on top works with it.
    • The banana and date-syrup build you see in some juice bars is not documented in any Somali source I could find, so treat it as your own invention rather than tradition.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Using an underripe avocado.
    • Making it ahead, so it browns and separates before anyone drinks it.
    • Over-blending with the ice, which waters it down.
    • Using underripe fresh pineapple in the second version.

    Health Benefits

    • Avocado supplies monounsaturated fat, potassium, folate and vitamin E.
    • Milk adds calcium, protein and vitamin B12.
    • The fat is entirely from the fruit — no cream or ice cream involved.
    • The sugar is the one thing worth moderating; honey or less sugar both work.

    Cultural Note

    Somali juice bars are a genuine institution, and the avocado shake is their flagship. That the fruit grows locally but was historically unfamiliar to most Somalis is a neat reminder that a national favourite does not have to be old — this one is recent, urban, and entirely at home.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Drink immediately. It browns within an hour.

    Reheating: Not applicable.

    Serve with: Sambuusa, Bajiye, A hot afternoon
