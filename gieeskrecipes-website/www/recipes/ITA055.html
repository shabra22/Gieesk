🍝 Strozzapreti al Ragù

Strozzapreti al Ragù Romagnolo

Strozzapreti — 'priest stranglers' — are hand-rolled, eggless twists of pasta from Romagna, their irregular ridged surface ideal for clinging to a hearty, slow-cooked ragù.

The colorful legend behind the name involves gluttonous priests choking on the pasta after eating too quickly, a tale reflecting the dish's deep roots in rural Romagnolo home cooking, where simple flour-and-water dough was twisted by hand into distinctive shapes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Mixing bowl, Large heavy pot, Large pot for pasta

    Strozzapreti dough

    • 400 g (3⅓ cups) all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
    • 200 ml (¾ cup) warm water
    • Pinch of salt

    Ragù

    • 1 tbsp olive oil
    • 30 g (2 tbsp) butter
    • 1 onion, finely diced
    • 1 carrot, finely diced
    • 1 celery stalk, finely diced
    • 300 g (10 oz) ground beef
    • 200 g (7 oz) ground pork
    • 120 ml (½ cup) dry red wine
    • 500 g (18 oz) canned crushed tomatoes
    • Salt and pepper, to taste
    • Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, for serving

    Method

    1. Combine the flour, water, and salt into a firm, smooth dough. Knead for 8–10 minutes. Rest, covered, for 30 minutes.
    2. Meanwhile, make the ragù: heat the olive oil and butter in a large pot. Add the onion, carrot, and celery, and cook gently until softened, about 10 minutes.
    3. Add the beef and pork, browning thoroughly, about 8–10 minutes.
    4. Pour in the red wine and simmer until evaporated.
    5. Add the crushed tomatoes, season with salt and pepper, and simmer on very low heat, partially covered, for at least 2 hours, stirring occasionally.
    6. To shape the strozzapreti: pinch off small pieces of dough and roll between your palms into short, twisted ropes, about 6cm long — the irregular twist is traditional and desired.
    7. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the strozzapreti for 3–5 minutes until tender and floating.
    8. Drain and toss with the ragù.
    9. Serve immediately with grated Parmigiano.

    Chef Tips

    • The dough should be firm, not soft — this gives strozzapreti its characteristic substantial, chewy bite.
    • Irregular, rustic shaping is traditional — don't worry about perfect uniformity.
    • A long, slow ragù simmer develops far more depth than rushing the process.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Making the dough too soft, resulting in pasta that loses its shape when boiled.
    • Rushing the ragù simmering time, missing out on its full depth of flavor.
    • Overcrowding the pot when cooking the pasta, causing pieces to stick together.

    Cultural Note

    Strozzapreti is a beloved staple across Romagna and parts of Tuscany, with regional variations on the exact shaping technique, though the hand-twisted, eggless dough remains a constant.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: The ragù keeps refrigerated for up to 4 days, or frozen for up to 3 months.

    Reheating: Reheat the ragù gently, then toss with freshly cooked strozzapreti.

    Serve with: Grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, A robust Sangiovese di Romagna wine
