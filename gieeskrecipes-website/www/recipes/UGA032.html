🍩 Mandazi

Mandazi

Flour rubbed with butter and brought together with cold water, rested, rolled a full inch thick and fried in batches of four or five for seven minutes — with lemon zest and ground cloves doing the flavouring.

Mandazi are everywhere in Uganda and almost everybody has their own recipe. This is the version its author calls the perfect one, and the details that make it work are all small: the water must be cold, the dough is rolled an inch thick rather than thin, the oil must not be very hot, and they fry for a full seven minutes. Lemon zest and ground cloves give them a warm citrusy flavour that distinguishes them from the plain cinnamon version.

Ingredients

Equipment: Sieve, Large bowl, Rolling pin, Deep frying pan, Slotted spoon

  • 960 ml (4 cups) all-purpose flour
  • 60 ml (1/4 cup) extra flour, for the surface
  • 120 ml (8 tbsp) sugar
  • 480 ml (2 cups) cold water
  • 30 ml (2 tbsp) butter or margarine, at room temperature
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) baking powder, or 1.25 ml (1/4 tsp) baking soda
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) lemon zest
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) ground cloves (optional)
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) vanilla essence
  • A pinch of salt
  • Oil, for frying

Method

  1. Sift the flour and baking powder into a clean bowl.
  2. Add the salt, cloves, lemon zest and sugar and mix well.
  3. Rub in the butter until the flour becomes coarse.
  4. Mix the vanilla into the cold water, then add the water to the flour and mix until a firm dough forms.
  5. Knead the dough about 2 minutes, until it no longer sticks to your hands.
  6. Set it aside to rest for 10 minutes.
  7. Flour your surface and roll the dough out to about 1 inch thick.
  8. Cut out rectangles — or divide the dough into 10 to 12 equal parts and roll them into balls.
  9. Put a pan of frying oil on medium heat and let it heat through.
  10. Fry the mandazi in batches of 4 to 5 for about 7 minutes, until risen and golden brown.
  11. Drain and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Cold water. Warm water starts working the gluten and softens the butter you just rubbed in.
  • Do not let the oil get very hot. It is the commonest mandazi failure — they burn on the outside before the middle cooks.
  • An inch thick. Mandazi are meant to be substantial; rolled thin they fry into crackers.
  • Seven minutes and batches of four or five. Crowding the pan drops the temperature and you lose the rise.
  • If you are using less oil, flip them occasionally so the dough cooks all the way through.
  • Cloves and lemon zest are what give the warm citrusy flavour. The cloves are marked optional and they are the better half of the pair.
  • Half a teaspoon of ground cinnamon in place of the zest and cloves gives you the other standard Ugandan mandazi, and that version is rolled thicker still, up to two inches.
  • Powdered sugar dusted over at the end is common and entirely optional.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using warm water.
  • Oil too hot.
  • Rolling the dough thin.
  • Frying too many at once.
  • Pulling them out before seven minutes because the outside looks done.

Health Benefits

  • A filling snack that pairs with tea and needs no refrigeration.
  • Contains only two tablespoons of butter across ten or more pieces.
  • No eggs and no milk in the dough.
  • Deep-fried, which is the honest limit on how healthy a mandazi gets.

Cultural Note

Flour is a major component of Ugandan kitchen staples — Ugandans eat a lot of it — and mandazi are found almost everywhere, with nearly everyone having their own recipe. They came up the coast with the Swahili trade and settled inland so thoroughly that arguing about the right thickness is now a Ugandan pastime.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 2 to 3 days in an airtight container.

Reheating: A few minutes in a warm oven.

Serve with: Ugandan tea, Daddies, Kabalagala
