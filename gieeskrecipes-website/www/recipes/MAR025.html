🍝 Seffa Medfouna (Sweet Vermicelli or Couscous with Saffron Chicken)

Seffa medfouna — "buried" seffa (سفة مدفونة)

Steamed vermicelli or couscous sweetened with butter and powdered sugar, domed over saffron chicken and dusted with cinnamon, ground almonds and raisins.

3hTotal time
30mPrep
2h 30mCook
8Servings
820Calories
HardDifficulty

Medfouna means 'buried' because the meat is hidden inside the dome of seffa. The grain is steamed and tossed with butter and sugar; the chicken (or lamb/beef) is simmered with onions, ginger, saffron, cinnamon stick, turmeric and cilantro until very tender and the sauce reduces to thick. The author notes that in Casablanca meat-free seffa is usually a follow-up course, and that a vegetarian nut-free version called seffa touba is served as a light dinner in Meknes and Fez.

Ingredients

Equipment: Couscoussier or steamer, Dutch oven, Very large serving dish

    Couscous or chaariya

    • 2.2 lbs dry broken vermicelli, angel hair pasta or couscous
    • 3 tbsp vegetable or olive oil (for steaming)
    • 2 tsp salt (for steaming)
    • 4 tbsp unsalted butter, room temperature (for sweetening after final steaming)
    • 1/2 cup powdered sugar, or to taste (for sweetening after final steaming)

    Optional raisins

    • 1 cup raisins, soaked in water for 20 minutes
    • 1 tbsp butter (optional; for caramelizing)
    • 1 tbsp sugar or honey (optional; for caramelizing)

    Saffron chicken or meat

    • 1 whole chicken, cut into pieces and skin removed (or 2 lbs. cubed lamb or beef)
    • 2 large sweet onions, chopped medium
    • 1 tbsp ground ginger
    • 1 1/2 tsp salt, or to taste
    • 1 tsp white pepper
    • 1/2 tsp black pepper
    • 2 small pieces (2 to 3") of cinnamon stick
    • 1 tsp saffron threads, crumbled
    • 1 tsp turmeric
    • 4 tbsp unsalted butter
    • 1/4 cup olive oil
    • 1/4 cup chopped fresh cilantro

    To decorate and serve

    • 1 cup almonds (blanched, fried and ground)
    • 1 cup powdered sugar
    • 2 tbsp ground cinnamon
    • Caramelized raisins (optional, if not steamed with the grain)

    Method

    1. Steam the couscous or vermicelli over simmering salted water in rounds, as in the author's linked tutorials, working in oil and salt between steamings.
    2. Add soaked raisins in the final steaming (the fourth for vermicelli), or caramelise them in a small pot with 1 tbsp butter and 1 tbsp sugar or honey and water to cover, until the liquids are absorbed.
    3. Combine the meat, onions, spices, butter, oil and cilantro in a Dutch oven. Cook over medium heat about 10 minutes, stirring frequently.
    4. For chicken, add no water unless necessary; for lamb or beef, add water to just cover. Cover and cook over medium heat until very tender (chicken about 1 hour; beef or lamb longer). Reduce to a thick sauce, discard the cinnamon stick and adjust the seasoning.
    5. Toss the hot grain with the butter and powdered sugar.
    6. Spread a quarter to a third of the grain on a very large dish, put the meat in the centre and cover with sauce. Dome the remaining grain over it and decorate with cinnamon, almonds and raisins. Serve with extra powdered sugar, almonds and cinnamon.
    7. To toast the almonds instead of frying: 350°F (180°C), about 10 minutes, until golden brown.

    Chef Tips

    • Doneness cue: meat very tender; sauce reduced to thick.
    • The author notes it is Moroccan tradition to gather around the seffa and eat from your own side of the dish.
    • Steam grains in separate rounds; do not rush.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Making the sauce too thin, which soaks the dome.
    • Adding all the sugar in advance; taste as you go.
    • Skipping the fluffing between steamings.

    Health Benefits

    • A celebratory dish; high in sugar, so serve in moderation.
    • Chicken and almonds add protein.

    Cultural Note

    The author glosses medfouna as 'buried' and writes that in Casablanca meat-free seffa is usually a follow-up course to a main dish; the vegetarian seffa touba is served as a light dinner in Meknes and Fez.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Best served at once; store meat and sauce separately from the grain for 2 days.

    Reheating: Re-steam the grain and warm the meat gently.

    Serve with: Mint tea
