🌺 Karkade (Hibiscus Tea)

Karkadeh — كركديه

Dried hibiscus flowers boiled 3 minutes then steeped 20 to 30 minutes, strained, sweetened, spiked with lime and chilled to a deep red cranberry-like tea.

Water is boiled and the dried hibiscus and sweetener stirred in; it boils for 3 minutes over high heat, the heat is turned off, and the pot is covered and steeped for 20 to 30 minutes. It is strained, lime juice is added, and it chills a few hours. A hot single serving uses 8 oz of water just below boiling (about 200°F) and a heaping tablespoon of flowers for 5 minutes.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan with lid, Strainer, Pitcher

  • 6 cups filtered water
  • 3/4 cup dried hibiscus flowers
  • 1/4 to 1/2 cup sugar, agave nectar or honey
  • 2 large limes, 1 juiced, 1 cut into thin wheels
  • Fresh mint sprigs, for serving

Method

  1. Bring the water to a boil, stir in the hibiscus and sweetener and boil for 3 minutes over high heat.
  2. Turn off the heat, cover and let the hibiscus steep for 20 to 30 minutes.
  3. Strain, stir in the lime juice and cool, then chill a few hours.
  4. Serve over ice with lime wheels and mint. For a hot single cup, steep a heaping tablespoon of flowers in 8 oz water just below boiling (about 200°F) for 5 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • The steep is NO CUE, TIME ONLY. The page describes a deep red elixir that looks and tastes like cranberry juice, which is descriptive, not a doneness cue.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Steeping too long, which makes the tea bitter (our caution).

Health Benefits

  • Hibiscus is rich in antioxidants and tart in flavour.

Cultural Note

Suzy Karadsheh writes, 'Where I'm from in Egypt, we call hibiscus tea kar-ka-deh,' and recalls a 'karkade man' in Old Cairo selling ice-cold glasses from a carafe at his waist.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 5 days (our estimate).

Reheating: Serve cold or warmed.

Serve with: Mint, lime
