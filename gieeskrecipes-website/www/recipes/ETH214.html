🥤 Ethiopian Healing Barley Drink

አጥሚት — Atmit (Drink)

Ethiopian Atmit — a very thin, pourable barley or teff porridge-drink sweetened with honey and spiced with cinnamon, ginger, and cardamom. Ethiopia's restorative healing drink — given to the sick, to fasting individuals, and to those recovering from illness. More drink than food.

Ingredients

Equipment: Saucepan, Whisk

  • ½ cup roasted barley flour (or teff flour)
  • 4 cups water
  • 2 tbsp honey
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • ¼ tsp ground ginger
  • ¼ tsp ground cardamom
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Whisk barley flour into 1 cup cold water until smooth.
  2. Bring remaining water to a simmer.
  3. Add flour mixture slowly, whisking.
  4. Cook 10—12 minutes, stirring, until very thin porridge consistency.
  5. Remove from heat. Add honey, cinnamon, ginger, and cardamom.
  6. Strain through a fine sieve for a completely smooth drink.
  7. Serve warm in cups.

Chef Tips

  • Thinner than ETH014 (porridge version) — should pour freely.
  • Straining produces a smoother drink.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too thick — should be a pourable drink.

Health Benefits

  • Barley beta-glucan.
  • Honey antimicrobial.
  • Easily digestible.
  • Ginger and cinnamon anti-inflammatory.

Cultural Note

Atmit drink is the care that Ethiopians give to each other — when someone is ill, atmit is prepared and brought to them. When someone is fasting and needs sustenance, atmit is available. It is nourishment as an act of love.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best consumed immediately.

Reheating: Gently with added water.

Serve with: As a morning drink, As a healing drink for the sick, During fasting periods
