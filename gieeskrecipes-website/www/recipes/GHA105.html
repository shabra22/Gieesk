🥬 Spinach and Agushi Curry

Agushi curry

The stew's colour changes from pink to mustard as it cooks — the clearest visual endpoint in the collection.

Melon seed and spinach in a chalé sauce base, and it has two of the best cues in the whole Ghanaian record. The chalé sauce simmers twenty minutes on low, then a hundred grams of agushi goes in and the sauce should turn creamy. Crushed guinea peppers and lime juice follow for ten minutes, and the stew colour changes from pink to mustard — a visual endpoint you cannot mistake. Finally the spinach goes in and you simmer gently until it wilts. Grilled plantain, twelve to fifteen minutes turning halfway, is the optional accompaniment.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy pan, Grill or griddle, for the plantain, Wooden spoon

  • 1 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp chilli powder
  • 350 ml uncooked chalé sauce
  • 100 g (about 2 heaped tbsp) agushi — dried ground melon seeds
  • 8 guinea peppers, crushed (optional)
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • 150-300 ml water or vegetable stock, if required
  • 200 g baby leaf spinach
  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • 1 tsp coarsely ground black pepper

Grilled plantain, optional

  • 4-6 ripe plantains
  • 1 tbsp ground ginger
  • 1/2 tbsp dried chilli flakes
  • Sea salt
  • Melted coconut oil and olive oil, for drizzling

Method

  1. Heat the coconut oil and soften the onion.
  2. Add the curry powder and chilli powder.
  3. Add the chalé sauce and simmer over low heat 20 minutes.
  4. Stir in the agushi — the sauce should turn creamy.
  5. Add the crushed guinea peppers and the lime juice and simmer 10 minutes.
  6. The stew colour changes from pink to mustard.
  7. Add water or stock if required.
  8. Add the spinach and simmer gently until it wilts.
  9. Season with salt and black pepper.
  10. For the plantain: season with ginger, chilli flakes and salt, drizzle with oil, and grill 12 to 15 minutes, turning halfway.

Chef Tips

  • Pink to mustard is the cue and it is the clearest visual endpoint in this collection. Before the colour shifts the melon seed has not cooked out.
  • The sauce should turn creamy as the agushi goes in. Two cues in sequence, and both are worth waiting for.
  • A hundred grams of agushi is given as about two heaped tablespoons, which is a useful conversion for a dense powder.
  • Water or stock only if required, and the range is wide — a hundred and fifty to three hundred millilitres. Judge by thickness.
  • Guinea peppers crushed, eight of them, and they are optional. They are grains of paradise and they lift the whole dish.
  • Spinach in at the very end, simmered gently just until it wilts. Baby leaf is specified and it needs almost nothing.
  • Vegan as written, which is unusual for an agushi dish — most Ghanaian melon-seed stews lean on fish or meat.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Serving before the colour turns from pink to mustard.
  • Adding all the water at once.
  • Boiling the spinach rather than wilting it.
  • Using cooked chalé sauce where uncooked is specified.
  • Skipping the lime juice, which brightens the melon seed.

Health Benefits

  • Melon seeds are high in plant protein, magnesium, zinc and healthy fats.
  • Spinach contributes iron, folate, vitamin K and vitamin A.
  • Vegan and a complete protein when served with rice.
  • Lime juice improves the absorption of the spinach's iron.

Cultural Note

Agushi stews are everywhere in Ghana and almost always carry fish or meat, so a vegan version built on melon seed alone is a genuine diaspora contribution rather than a compromise. The pink-to-mustard colour change is the kind of cue that someone only writes down after watching the pot carefully more than once.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen. Add fresh spinach on reheating.

Reheating: Gentle heat with a splash of water.

Serve with: Grilled plantain, Rice, Banku
