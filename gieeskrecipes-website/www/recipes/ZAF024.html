🍢 Sosaties

Sosatie

Lamb threaded with soaked dried apricots and fresh lemon leaves, marinated a full day or three in caramelised curried onion, lemon and tamarind, then braaied over moderate coals.

Sosatie comes from the Javanese sesate, skewered meat, and the Dictionary of South African English has it in print by 1833. It is a Cape Muslim dish that became the national braai kebab, and what separates it from every other marinated skewer is the marinade being cooked first: the curry powder, coriander and allspice are bloomed in butter with onions, the sugar is caramelised, and only then is it let down with lemon juice and vinegar or tamarind water. Then it waits.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large saucepan, Non-metallic container — glass, ceramic or plastic, 12 bamboo skewers, Braai with moderate coals

    The marinade, cooked first

    • 3 tbsp butter
    • 3 large onions, sliced
    • 3 tbsp curry powder
    • 3 tbsp whole coriander seeds
    • 3 allspice berries
    • 3 tbsp soft brown sugar
    • Juice of 3 lemons
    • 375 ml (1 1/2 cups) brown vinegar, or tamarind water

    For tamarind water, if using

    • 100 g compressed tamarind soaked in 500 ml warm water, then strained

    The skewers

    • 1.5 kg leg of lamb, cut into 5 cm blocks
    • 24 dried peaches or apricots, soaked in warm water
    • 24 fresh lemon leaves
    • Salt and freshly ground black pepper
    • 12 bamboo skewers, soaked in water

    Method

    1. Melt the butter in a large saucepan over medium heat and fry the onions until soft.
    2. Add the curry powder, coriander seeds and allspice and fry until fragrant.
    3. Add the brown sugar and fry a further 3 minutes, letting it caramelise.
    4. Add the lemon juice and the vinegar or tamarind water, and simmer for 15 minutes. Then let it cool completely.
    5. Meanwhile trim the sinew and bone from the lamb, leaving as much of the fat as you like — it bastes the meat on the fire.
    6. Thread the meat onto the soaked skewers, alternating with the soaked apricots and the fresh lemon leaves. Season and lay them in a single layer in a non-metallic container.
    7. Pour the cooled marinade over the skewers. Refrigerate overnight, turning them two or three times.
    8. Leave up to 3 days for the best flavour. They also freeze raw in their marinade.
    9. Bring them to room temperature before they go near the fire.
    10. Braai over moderate coals for about 20 minutes, until browned outside and still slightly pink in the middle.

    Chef Tips

    • Cool the marinade completely before it touches the meat. Warm marinade starts cooking the lamb and the texture never recovers.
    • Three days beats overnight, and overnight beats an hour. This is a dish built around waiting.
    • The apricots go on the skewer, not into the marinade. Soaked in warm water first so they do not char to nothing over the coals.
    • A non-metallic container — the vinegar and lemon will react with metal over three days.
    • Tamarind water is the older souring agent and worth the extra step: soak 100 g of compressed tamarind in 500 ml of warm water and strain out the fibres and seeds.

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    • Pouring hot marinade over raw lamb.
    • Marinating for an hour and expecting the same result, which gives seasoned lamb rather than sosaties.
    • Braaiing them straight from the fridge, so the outside chars before the middle is warm.
    • Using unsoaked bamboo skewers, which burn through over twenty minutes on the coals.
    • Cooking them to well done. Slightly pink in the middle is the target.

    Health Benefits

    • Lamb provides protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
    • Dried apricots contribute fibre, potassium and beta-carotene.
    • Grilled over coals, so the rendered fat drips away rather than being eaten.

    Cultural Note

    Sosaties are the clearest single line from the Indonesian archipelago to the South African braai. The word is Javanese, the technique is Javanese, the dish arrived with enslaved and exiled people at the Cape, and it now turns up at every kind of South African gathering without most people knowing where the name came from.

    Storage & Serving

    Storage: Raw in marinade, 3 days refrigerated or 3 months frozen. Cooked, refrigerate 2 days.

    Reheating: Warm briefly over the coals or in a hot pan. They dry out quickly on a second pass.

    Serve with: Geelrys (yellow rice with raisins), Blatjang, Braaibroodjies
