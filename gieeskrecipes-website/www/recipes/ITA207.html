Risotto alla Zucca

A golden autumn risotto built on roasted pumpkin, its natural sweetness balanced by Parmigiano and a hint of sage, an especially comforting dish across northern Italy in fall.

The pumpkin here is often cooked two ways -- some pureed into the risotto base for color and body, some roasted separately and folded in at the end for pockets of caramelized sweetness and texture.

Ingredients

Equipment: Wide, heavy-bottomed pan, Baking sheet, Blender or potato masher

  • 320 g (1½ cups) Carnaroli or Arborio rice
  • 600 g (1.3 lb) pumpkin or butternut squash, peeled and cubed
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1.2 L (5 cups) hot vegetable stock
  • 100 ml (scant ½ cup) dry white wine
  • 80 g (⅓ cup) unsalted butter, divided
  • 60 g (¾ cup) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • Fresh sage leaves
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Toss half the pumpkin cubes with olive oil and roast at 200C (400F) for 20-25 minutes until tender and lightly caramelized.
  2. Meanwhile, simmer the remaining pumpkin cubes in a little of the stock until very soft, then mash or blend into a smooth puree.
  3. Melt half the butter in a wide pan and sauté the onion until soft.
  4. Add the rice and toast for 2 minutes, then add the wine and stir until absorbed.
  5. Stir in the pumpkin puree, then begin adding the hot stock one ladle at a time, stirring frequently, for about 18 minutes.
  6. In the final few minutes, fold in the roasted pumpkin cubes to warm through.
  7. Remove from the heat and beat in the remaining butter and Parmigiano vigorously.
  8. Fry a few sage leaves briefly in a little butter until crisp, and use to garnish each portion.

Chef Tips

  • Using pumpkin two ways, pureed for the base and roasted for texture, gives the best combination of flavor and interest.
  • Roasting the pumpkin first concentrates its sweetness far more than simply boiling it would.
  • Crispy fried sage makes an excellent, easy garnish that adds aromatic contrast.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using only pureed or only roasted pumpkin, missing the textural contrast both together provide.
  • Under-roasting the pumpkin, missing the caramelized sweetness that develops with good color.
  • Making the risotto too sweet by using an overly sugary squash variety without balancing seasoning.

Health Benefits

  • Pumpkin is rich in vitamin A, fiber, and antioxidants.

Cultural Note

Risotto alla zucca is a beloved autumn dish across northern Italy, particularly in Lombardy and the Veneto, where pumpkin has long featured in both sweet and savory regional cooking.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately; does not hold or reheat particularly well.

Reheating: Reheat gently with a splash of stock, stirring constantly.

Serve with: A medium-bodied white wine
