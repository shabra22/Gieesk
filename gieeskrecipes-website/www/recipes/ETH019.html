🫕 Ethiopian Corn Porridge

የበቆሎ ገንፎ — Ye-Beqolo Genfo

Ethiopian Corn Porridge — maize flour cooked into a thick, stiff porridge in the genfo style, served with a well of kibbeh and berbere. The southern Ethiopian equivalent of barley genfo — corn growing in the lowlands where barley cannot.

Corn porridge (ye-beqolo genfo) is the southern and western Ethiopian version of genfo — where the cooler highlands grow barley and teff, the warmer lowlands grow corn, and the corn genfo is made the same way. The Ethiopian technique — the well filled with kibbeh — makes this distinctly Ethiopian rather than a generic corn porridge.

Ingredients

Equipment: Heavy saucepan, Strong wooden spoon

  • 1.5 cups maize flour (white or yellow)
  • 3 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Serving: 2 tbsp kibbeh (ETH092)

  • 1 tsp berbere

Method

  1. See ETH009 (Genfo) for the full method.
  2. Bring water to a boil. Add salt.
  3. Add maize flour in a stream, whisking constantly.
  4. Cook over medium heat, stirring vigorously, 15—18 minutes until very stiff.
  5. Mound in bowls. Press a well in the centre.
  6. Fill with kibbeh and top with berbere.

Chef Tips

  • Corn genfo sets faster than barley — watch carefully.
  • The stiffness should be the same as barley genfo.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-cooking.
  • Insufficient stirring.

Cultural Note

Corn genfo is the southern lowlands' answer to barley genfo — the same preparation, the same eating ritual, the same community significance, but made from what the warmer climate provides. It demonstrates how Ethiopia's food culture adapts seamlessly across its extraordinary ecological diversity.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: With added water.

Serve with: With kibbeh (ETH092) and berbere (ETH091)
