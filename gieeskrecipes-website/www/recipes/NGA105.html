🪵 Okoho Soup

Okoho — dafara, latutuwa, agara

A stem peeled into thin strands, soaked fifteen minutes in hot water and squeezed until the water runs thick — that sap is the thickener, and nothing else in Nigeria works this way.

Okoho is the Idoma soup of Benue and it is the most unusual preparation in this entire collection. The thickener is not a seed or a flour but the sap of a plant stem: the okoho stick is peeled into thin strands, soaked in hot water for fifteen minutes, and then squeezed until the liquid is very thick and slippery. Egusi balls are dropped into the broth and steamed firm, everything is lifted out while the okoho sap simmers ten minutes on low, and then it all goes back in. Two varieties of locust bean, dawadawa and iru, are used together.

Ingredients

Equipment: Sharp knife, Bowl, for soaking, Mortar and pestle, or blender, Large pot with a lid

  • 1 medium stick or stem of okoho plant
  • Hot water, for soaking the strands
  • Dried fish, medium size, preferably dried catfish
  • Seeds of fresh pepper (tarodo)
  • Locust beans — 2 balls of 2 varieties, dawadawa and iru
  • 1 cup egusi (melon seed)
  • 1 medium onion
  • A pinch of crayfish
  • 2 seasoning cubes
  • 1/4 kilo of beef, or dried bush meat
  • Salt

Method

  1. Wash and peel the okoho stem into thin strands, and soak them in hot water for 15 minutes.
  2. Squeeze the sap from the strands until the liquid is very thick, and reserve it.
  3. Parboil the beef or dried meat with the onions, salt and ground pepper.
  4. Add the ground locust beans and the dried fish, and cook 15 minutes.
  5. Blend the egusi until smooth, add the crayfish, and mix with minimal water until the oil forms.
  6. Drop small egusi balls into the boiling broth and cover to steam until firm.
  7. Remove the meat, the egusi balls and the fish from the pot.
  8. Add the okoho sap and simmer on low heat 10 minutes.
  9. Return the meat, egusi and fish, stir briefly, and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Thick and slippery is the squeeze endpoint. Keep working the strands in the water until the liquid visibly changes — that is the whole dish.
  • Beat the stem gently with a pestle first to loosen the bark, then strip the rope-like inner layer and discard the woody stick. The inner layer is what holds the sap.
  • Take everything out before the sap goes in. The sap needs ten minutes on low heat undisturbed and the solids get in the way.
  • Low heat for the sap. Hard boiling will break the mucilage down and you lose the thickening.
  • Two varieties of locust bean, dawadawa and iru, used together. No other recipe in this collection does that.
  • Firm to the touch is the cue for the egusi balls. Steam them covered and do not stir.
  • The hot-water soak is given as fifteen minutes here and as eight to ten minutes by another named source. Both then squeeze, and the endpoint is the same.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling the sap hard, which destroys the thickening.
  • Leaving the meat and egusi in while the sap simmers.
  • Stopping the squeeze before the liquid goes thick and slippery.
  • Keeping the woody stick in rather than stripping the inner layer.
  • Stirring the egusi balls before they are firm.

Health Benefits

  • The okoho sap is a soluble mucilaginous fibre.
  • Melon seed supplies protein, magnesium and zinc.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.
  • Two varieties of fermented locust bean give B vitamins and glutamates.

Cultural Note

Okoho belongs to the Idoma of Benue State and it is Christmas and village-celebration food. The technique — a thickener wrung by hand out of plant bark — has no counterpart in the rest of Nigerian cooking, where thickeners come from seeds, tubers and flours. It is the kind of preparation that survives because nothing else does the same job.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated. The sap's thickening slackens on reheating.

Reheating: Very low heat. Do not boil.

Serve with: Iyan, Eba or garri, Tuwo Shinkafa
