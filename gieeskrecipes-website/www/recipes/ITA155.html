Vincisgrassi

The Marche's own elaborate baked lasagna, layered with a rich ragù of mixed meats and offal, béchamel, and Parmigiano, considered one of the region's grandest celebratory dishes.

Vincisgrassi predates and rivals Bologna's famous lasagna, its ragù traditionally built from a mix of meats including chicken giblets or sweetbreads, giving the finished dish a depth that has made it a fixture of Marche's most important family gatherings.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pasta machine, Large pot, Saucepan for béchamel, Baking dish

  • 400 g (3 cups) 00 flour
  • 4 eggs
  • Pinch of salt
  • 400 g (14 oz) mixed ground meats (beef, pork, chicken giblets)
  • 100 g (3.5 oz) pancetta, diced
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 carrot, chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, chopped
  • 200 ml (¾ cup plus 1 tbsp) dry white wine
  • 500 g (1 lb) canned crushed tomatoes
  • 800 ml (3¼ cups) whole milk, for the béchamel
  • 70 g (5 tbsp) butter, plus more for the dish
  • 70 g (½ cup) flour, for the béchamel
  • Nutmeg, to taste
  • 150 g (1¾ cups) grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Make the pasta dough with flour, eggs, and salt, kneading until smooth. Wrap and rest for 30 minutes, then roll into thin sheets and cut into lasagna-sized rectangles.
  2. For the ragù, render the pancetta in a heavy pot, then sauté the onion, carrot, and celery until softened.
  3. Add the mixed ground meats and giblets, browning well, then pour in the wine and let it reduce.
  4. Add the crushed tomatoes, season, and simmer gently for 1.5–2 hours until rich and thick.
  5. For the béchamel, melt the butter, whisk in the flour, and cook briefly, then gradually whisk in the warm milk, cooking until thickened. Season with nutmeg, salt, and pepper.
  6. Blanch the fresh pasta sheets briefly in boiling water, then lay flat on clean towels.
  7. Butter a baking dish and layer pasta, ragù, béchamel, and Parmigiano, repeating until all ingredients are used, finishing with béchamel and a generous layer of Parmigiano on top.
  8. Bake at 180°C (350°F) for 35–40 minutes until golden and bubbling.
  9. Let rest for 10–15 minutes before slicing and serving.

Chef Tips

  • Let the ragù simmer long enough to become properly thick and deeply flavored — this is not a quick sauce.
  • Resting the baked dish before slicing helps the layers hold together for cleaner portions.
  • Traditional recipes often include chicken giblets in the ragù, giving real depth that a simple ground beef ragù won't quite match.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the ragù, which needs real time to develop its characteristic depth.
  • Making the béchamel too thin, resulting in a watery rather than creamy final dish.
  • Slicing immediately after baking, before the layers have had a chance to set.

Cultural Note

Vincisgrassi is one of the Marche's grandest culinary traditions, often reserved for major holidays and family celebrations, its name possibly linked to an 18th-century Austrian prince.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 3 days; freezes well for up to 2 months.

Reheating: Reheat covered in the oven until warmed through.

Serve with: A robust red wine from the Marche
