🍢 Chichinga

Chichinga — kyinkyinga, tsire suya

Twelve ounces of beef cut into eighteen to twenty-one strips, shaken six at a time in half a cup of tankora, three to a skewer.

Chichinga is the Ghanaian kebab, known as kyinkyinga and as tsire suya in Nigeria, and this is the step-by-step version. Twelve ounces of tender beef is cut into eighteen to twenty-one strips a quarter to three-eighths of an inch thick, marinated about half an hour in tomato paste, pepper, ginger, garlic, grated onion, salt, white pepper and oil, then shaken six pieces at a time in a bag with half a cup of tankora powder. Three pieces per skewer, brushed with peanut oil, broiled five minutes, turned, five minutes more. Serves three.

Ingredients

Equipment: Bamboo skewers, Plastic bag, for shaking, Grill or broiler, Basting brush

  • 12 oz tender beef — top round or London broil
  • 1/2 cup tankora powder
  • Bamboo skewers — not the long ones
  • Peanut oil, for brushing

Marinade

  • 1-2 tsp tomato paste
  • Ground dried red pepper, about 1/3-1/2 tsp
  • About 1/2 tsp fresh grated ginger
  • About 1/2 tsp garlic
  • About 1/4 to 1/3 onion, grated — to get 1 1/2 to 2 tbsp
  • About 1 tsp salt or seasoned salt
  • About 1/2 tsp white or black pepper
  • 2 tsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Soak the bamboo skewers at least 1 hour.
  2. Cut the beef into 18 to 21 strips, a quarter to three-eighths of an inch thick.
  3. Mix the marinade and coat the strips.
  4. Refrigerate about half an hour or longer.
  5. Shake 6 pieces at a time in half a cup of tankora in a bag.
  6. Thread 3 pieces onto each skewer.
  7. Brush with peanut oil.
  8. Broil 5 minutes, turn, and broil another 5 minutes.

Chef Tips

  • Shake six pieces at a time in the bag rather than all at once. The tankora coats evenly that way and you do not end up with bare patches and clumps.
  • Soak the skewers at least an hour or they char through under a broiler.
  • Not the long skewers — her own note. Three pieces of meat on a short skewer is the street-food proportion.
  • A quarter to three-eighths of an inch thick. Thicker and the ten minutes will not cook it; thinner and it dries out.
  • Half an hour in the marinade or longer. This is not an overnight marinade — the tankora does the heavy flavour work.
  • Brush with peanut oil before broiling. It helps the coating stick and crisp rather than scorch.
  • A chicken version is published alongside: twelve ounces of boneless breast or thigh, same procedure, same eighteen to twenty-one strips.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Coating all the meat at once.
  • Unsoaked skewers.
  • Cutting the strips too thick.
  • Skipping the oil brush.
  • Over-broiling past ten minutes total.

Health Benefits

  • Lean beef is high in protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12.
  • The peanut-based tankora adds niacin, vitamin E and monounsaturated fat.
  • Very low in carbohydrate.
  • Grilled rather than fried.

Cultural Note

Chichinga is sold from charcoal grills on Accra street corners after dark, three pieces to a stick, with extra tankora dusted on at the last moment. That the same kebab is tsire suya across the border in Nigeria, with a nearly identical peanut-and-spice coating, is one of the clearest pieces of shared food culture in West Africa.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten at once. 2 days refrigerated cooked.

Reheating: Briefly under a hot broiler.

Serve with: Shito, Kelewele, Sliced onion and tomato
