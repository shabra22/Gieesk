🎃 Pumpkin Greens

Ensujju — pumpkin leaves

Pumpkin leaves stripped of their hairy outer layer, soaked ten minutes in salted water with a little baking soda, then cooked down with garlic, onion, tomato and green pepper.

Pumpkin leaves are eaten across Uganda and cooked differently depending on where you are from — stewed with groundnuts in some regions, lightly sautéed in others. The preparation is the part nobody tells you about: each leaf and stalk carries a thin hairy outer layer that has to be peeled off, and there is no good English word for it. After that a soak in salted water with baking soda draws out impurities and lets the sand settle. Then it is a straightforward pan of greens.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large bowl, for the soak, Pan or pot, Colander

  • 3 to 4 handfuls of pumpkin greens, chopped

The soak

  • Water, to cover
  • 10 ml (2 tsp) salt
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) baking soda

The pan

  • 3 cloves garlic, crushed and chopped
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 small green pepper, diced
  • 5 ml (1 tsp) curry powder
  • 2.5 ml (1/2 tsp) black pepper
  • 30 to 45 ml (2 to 3 tbsp) oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. Peel the thin hairy outer layer off each leaf stalk, then thinly slice the greens.
  2. Put water in a large bowl with the 2 tsp of salt and the 1 tsp of baking soda.
  3. Add the greens and let them sit for about 10 minutes.
  4. Wash the greens thoroughly and drain them completely.
  5. Put a clean pan on high heat, add the oil and let it get hot.
  6. Add the garlic and onions and cook for about 2 minutes.
  7. Add the tomatoes and green pepper and stir well.
  8. Let the tomatoes cook until tender, about 3 to 5 minutes, then mash them with the wooden spoon.
  9. Add the pumpkin greens, mix well, and cook 5 to 10 minutes until tender.
  10. Serve with your meal.

Chef Tips

  • Peel the hairy layer off. It is fiddly and it is what makes the greens soft rather than scratchy, and there is no way round it.
  • Salt and baking soda in the soaking water draws out impurities, and letting the greens stand lets the sand settle to the bottom.
  • Use soft, tender leaves. Older ones stay tough however long you cook them.
  • Mash the tomatoes in the pan once they are tender. It gives you a sauce rather than pieces.
  • Drain them completely after washing. Wet greens water the pan down.
  • Cooked with groundnut paste instead of this treatment they become a stew rather than a side, and that is the other standard regional way of doing them.
  • Five to ten minutes is the range and tenderness is the cue.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the peeling.
  • Missing the soak, so the dish is gritty.
  • Using tough older leaves.
  • Adding the greens before the tomatoes have broken down.
  • Not draining them.

Health Benefits

  • Pumpkin leaves are high in iron, calcium, vitamin A and vitamin C.
  • A genuinely under-used green — more nutrient-dense than most cultivated leaves.
  • Entirely plant-based with a modest amount of oil.
  • High in fibre.

Cultural Note

Growing pumpkins for the leaves as much as the fruit is a habit Uganda shares across the region, and it is a good example of a plant being used whole — leaves as a green, young fruit cooked with beans, seeds roasted as a snack. The technique of peeling the hairy layer is the kind of knowledge passed on in a kitchen rather than in writing.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheating: Warm briefly in a pan.

Serve with: Posho, Matooke, Binyebwa
