🥔 Kenyan Mashed Arrowroots

Nduma ya Kupondwa

Kenyan Mashed Arrowroots — nduma boiled until completely soft, then mashed with butter and milk into a smooth, mildly flavoured mash with a dense, satisfying texture. A traditional Kenyan side dish especially beloved in the highlands.

Mashed arrowroots have a distinctly different character from mashed potatoes — denser, slightly sticky, and with a subtle earthy sweetness. They are deeply comforting and pair beautifully with rich stews and grilled meats. In Central Kenya, nduma ya kupondwa is a traditional side dish that appears at both everyday meals and celebratory occasions.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large pot, Potato masher, Measuring spoons

  • 600g arrowroots — peeled and cubed
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • ¼ cup warm milk
  • 1 tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper

Method

  1. Wear gloves to peel arrowroots — the raw sap can irritate skin. Peel and cube into even pieces.
  2. Boil in salted water for 30—40 minutes until completely soft throughout.
  3. Drain and dry over low heat for 1 minute.
  4. Mash with butter until smooth. Gradually add warm milk.
  5. Season with salt and pepper. Serve hot.

Chef Tips

  • Arrowroots have a slightly sticky texture when mashed — this is normal and traditional.
  • Do not over-mash or they become gluey.
  • Adding a little cream cheese makes them luxuriously rich.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not cooking thoroughly — undercooked arrowroots are unpleasant and difficult to mash.
  • Over-mashing — creates a gluey paste.

The Science

Arrowroots contain a mucilaginous starch that becomes slightly sticky when mashed. Unlike potato starch, arrowroot starch does not become gluey when briefly overworked, but extended mashing will tighten the texture significantly.

Health Benefits

  • Good source of potassium and vitamin E.
  • High in dietary fibre.
  • Easily digestible — suitable for sensitive stomachs.

Cultural Note

Mashed arrowroots are a traditional dish in the Kenyan highlands, particularly in Central Kenya where arrowroots have been cultivated for generations. They appear alongside beef stew and sukuma wiki at family celebrations and everyday meals.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 3 days.

Reheating: Warm gently with a splash of milk.

Serve with: Beef Stew, Kuku Kienyeji, Nyama Choma, Sukuma Wiki
