🍛 Kenyan Lamb Curry

Mchuzi wa Kondoo wa Curry

Kenyan Lamb Curry — tender bone-in lamb simmered in an aromatic, warmly spiced tomato and onion sauce with curry powder, garam masala, turmeric, and fresh ginger. Richer and more aromatic than beef curry, with a depth that makes it one of the most satisfying curries in the Kenyan repertoire.

Lamb curry occupies a special place in Kenyan cuisine — richer than beef, milder than goat, and deeply aromatic when paired with the Kenyan-Indian spice blend. Along the coast and in Nairobi's Indian-Kenyan community, lamb curry is the luxury one-pot meal for special occasions, always served with fragrant rice or freshly made chapati.

Ingredients

Equipment: Large heavy pot, Wooden spoon

  • 1 kg lamb — bone-in shoulder or leg pieces
  • 2 large onions — finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes — diced
  • 5 garlic cloves — minced
  • 2 tbsp fresh ginger — grated
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1½ cups water or stock
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp paprika
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 2 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • ¼ cup fresh coriander — chopped

Method

  1. Brown seasoned lamb in hot oil in batches. Remove and set aside.
  2. Cook onions until deeply golden — 10 minutes.
  3. Add garlic and ginger. Cook 1 minute.
  4. Add tomatoes and tomato paste. Cook for 8—10 minutes until very thick and dark.
  5. Add all spices except garam masala. Stir 1 minute to bloom.
  6. Return lamb. Add stock. Bring to a boil.
  7. Reduce heat to very low. Cover and simmer for 60—70 minutes until the lamb is completely tender.
  8. Stir in garam masala. Taste and adjust seasoning.
  9. Garnish with coriander and serve.

Chef Tips

  • Allow the tomato-onion base to cook down until very dark and thick — this deep base is the foundation of the curry's flavour.
  • Bone-in lamb enriches the sauce far more than boneless.
  • A splash of natural yogurt stirred in at the end creates a creamier, milder version.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-cooking the onion-tomato base — the sauce will taste raw.
  • Boneless lamb — less flavour and the sauce will be thinner.

The Science

Lamb fat contains short-chain fatty acids that are highly aromatic when heated. The longer the lamb cooks in the spiced sauce, the more these flavour compounds integrate into the curry, creating a more unified, complex flavour profile.

Cultural Note

Lamb curry reflects Kenya's long history of Indian-Kenyan cultural exchange, particularly in coastal cities and Nairobi. It is a celebration dish — served at family gatherings, Eid feasts, and restaurant occasions where something special is called for.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Refrigerate for up to 4 days. Freeze for up to 3 months.

Reheating: Reheat gently on stovetop with a splash of water.

Serve with: Basmati Rice, Chapati, Naan, Kachumbari, Natural Yogurt, Wali wa Nazi
