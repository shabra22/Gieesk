🍠 Swahili Roasted Cassava

Muhogo wa Kuchoma

Tanzanian Muhogo wa Kuchoma — cassava peeled and roasted over charcoal until the exterior is lightly charred and the interior is soft, creamy, and slightly smoky. Eaten with chilli and lime — the simplest and most honest Tanzanian street food.

Roasted cassava is the most honest street food in Tanzania — the cassava vendor, the charcoal jiko, the cassava roasting until the exterior chars. Eaten with chilli salt and lime, it is the most direct expression of East African food culture.

Ingredients

Equipment: Charcoal jiko or grill

  • 1 large cassava root — peeled and cut into sections
  • Chilli powder
  • Coarse salt
  • Lime wedges

Method

  1. Peel cassava and cut into 10cm sections.
  2. Roast over charcoal coals 25—30 minutes, turning regularly.
  3. The exterior should be lightly charred in places; interior creamy when pierced.
  4. Sprinkle with chilli and salt.
  5. Serve with lime wedges.

Chef Tips

  • Medium coals — slow roasting produces the creamy interior.
  • Test with a skewer — no resistance means done.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Too high heat — exterior burns before interior cooks.

The Science

The cassava starch gelatinises during roasting — converting from raw starch granules to a soft, digestible gel. The charring on the exterior adds pyrazines — the aromatic compounds responsible for the roasted, slightly smoky flavour.

Cultural Note

Roasted cassava is the street food that needs no introduction — the vendor, the smoke, the charred cassava and the lime. It is Tanzania in its most honest and direct form.

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best eaten immediately.

Reheating: Briefly on grill.
