🍞 Mangauna (Port Said Easter Brioche)

Manga una / Mangauna — منجأونة

An enriched sweet dough braided into portions, risen overnight, baked to a brown fluffy loaf and soaked in a hot lemon syrup.

10h 30mTotal time
40mPrep
50mCook
8Servings
480Calories
HardDifficulty

Yeast is proofed until frothy and foamy; butter, sugar and milk are warmed until warm to touch, then whisked with eggs and yeast and kneaded with flour about 15 minutes. After doubling and a rest in the fridge overnight, the dough is cut into 6 portions, braided and risen 20 minutes, then baked at 170°C for about 40 to 50 minutes until nicely browned and fluffy. A simple syrup is poured hot over the hot loaves.

Ingredients

Equipment: Stand mixer, Baking tray, Saucepan

  • 35g grams butter, at room temperature
  • 135 grams milk, at room temperature
  • 200 grams sugar
  • 4 large eggs, at room temperature
  • 870 grams bread flour
  • 21/0.7 grams/oz dry yeast
  • 1 cup lukewarm water

Egg wash

  • 1 egg, whipped
  • 1 tablespoon milk
  • ¼ teaspoon vanilla
  • Silvered Almonds or sesame seeds

Syrup

  • 1 cup water
  • ½ cup sugar
  • 1 slice lemon

Method

  1. Proof the yeast in lukewarm water with a pinch of sugar until frothy and foamy.
  2. Melt the butter, sugar and milk on very low heat until warm to the touch yet not hot; whisk in the eggs and yeast.
  3. Mix with the flour on medium-high about 15 minutes, until the dough comes together, soft and sticky.
  4. Let rise until doubled, then rest in the fridge overnight. If the room is cold, a closed oven warmed to 30°C works.
  5. Cut into 6 portions, braid, brush with egg wash, add almonds or sesame, rise 20 minutes, then bake at 170°C for about 40 to 50 minutes until nicely browned and fluffy.
  6. Simmer the syrup ingredients 5 minutes and pour hot over the hot loaves.

Chef Tips

  • Cues: yeast frothy; dough doubled; loaf nicely browned and fluffy. The fridge rest and 20-minute rise are NO CUE, TIME ONLY.
  • The method names ground mastic, mahleb and orange zest that the ingredient list omits; the first rise is given as 1 to 2 hours in one place and 2 to 3 in another; the page contains leftovers from a Greek Easter bread recipe.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding the warm milk mixture while hot, which kills the yeast.

Cultural Note

Nermine Mansour describes it as the Port Said brioche for Easter and Sham El-Nessim, with Italian influence from expatriates who left after 1952 and 1954; the name echoes vendors' cries of 'Manga una!' ('eat one').

Storage & Serving

Storage: Best within 2 days; wrap well.

Reheating: Warm briefly.

Serve with: Boiled eggs (page suggestion)
