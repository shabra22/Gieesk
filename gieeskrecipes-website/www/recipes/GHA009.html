🧆 Yam Eto with Spices

Yam eto

A fully quantified yam eto with paprika and turmeric added — the spices are not traditional and the source's own cue is a smooth, reddish-yellow mash.

This is the most completely measured version of eto in print, and the price of that is a spice rack the traditional dish does not have. Five hundred grams of white yam, a quarter cup of dry roasted peanuts, a whole scotch bonnet, garlic, a quarter cup of oil — palm, canola or olive — and then paprika, turmeric and dried thyme, which are additions rather than inheritance. The yam boils only seven to eight minutes and should take a fork without turning mushy. The finished mash is described as smooth and reddish-yellow.

Ingredients

Equipment: Pot, Blender or grinder, Masher, Frying pan

  • 500 g white yams
  • 1/4 cup dry roasted peanuts
  • 1 medium scotch bonnet
  • 1/2 medium onion
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 1/4 cup palm, canola or olive oil
  • 1 1/2 tsp salt, divided
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 2 stalks green onions

Not traditional — see the note

  • 1 tsp paprika
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 1/2 tsp dried thyme

To serve

  • 2 hard boiled eggs
  • 1 avocado

Method

  1. Peel and cube the yam.
  2. Boil until cooked, about 7 to 8 minutes — a fork should pierce it without it turning mushy.
  3. Grind the peanuts, scotch bonnet, onion and garlic.
  4. Drain the yam and mash with half the salt.
  5. Warm the oil and stir in the ground mixture, the paprika, turmeric, black pepper, thyme and remaining salt.
  6. Work the seasoned oil through the mashed yam until the colour is even — smooth and reddish yellow.
  7. Stir through the chopped green onions.
  8. Serve topped with the halved boiled eggs and sliced avocado.

Chef Tips

  • Seven to eight minutes is this source's yam time and it is much shorter than you would expect. Its own cue is better than the clock: a fork should go in without the yam turning mushy.
  • Red palm oil is what makes this eto rather than mashed yam. The source offers canola or olive as alternatives — they will work and the dish will not be eto.
  • Smooth and reddish yellow is the finishing cue, and it tells you when the oil is fully through.
  • Paprika, turmeric and dried thyme are this author's additions, not part of the dish. Leave all three out for the traditional version and you have something close to Yam Oto.
  • Salt is divided deliberately — half into the mash, half into the seasoned oil. Keep the split.
  • A whole medium scotch bonnet for four servings is genuinely hot. Deseed it if that is not what you want.
  • Grinding the peanuts with the aromatics rather than separately is what keeps the texture coarse enough to read as eto.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Boiling the yam past the fork-tender point.
  • Using olive oil and calling it eto.
  • All the salt at once.
  • Leaving the scotch bonnet whole and seeded for a mild household.
  • Treating the paprika and turmeric as traditional.

Health Benefits

  • Yam has a lower glycaemic index than potato and supplies potassium and vitamin C.
  • Peanuts add protein, niacin and monounsaturated fat.
  • Turmeric and paprika both carry antioxidant compounds, even if they are not traditional here.
  • Eggs and avocado make the plate nutritionally complete.

Cultural Note

A fully measured eto is a diaspora artefact — the quantities exist because someone was cooking it away from the person who taught them, with a scale instead of a hand. The paprika and turmeric are the tell, and they are worth leaving in the record rather than editing out, because that is how dishes actually travel.

Storage & Serving

Storage: 3 days refrigerated.

Reheating: Low heat with a splash of water.

Serve with: Boiled eggs, Sliced avocado, Dried smoked herring
